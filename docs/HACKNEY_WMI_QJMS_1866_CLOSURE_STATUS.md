## 1. Institutional existence / public-education context

A separately published pamphlet establishes Hackney Working Men's Institute as an active public-education venue before the 1866 microscopical communication:

Harry Chester, *Education and Advancement for the Working Classes: A Speech Delivered at a Public Meeting at the Hackney Working Men's Institute, on January 20th, 1863* (London: Bell and Daldy, [1863]), 16 pp.

This closes:

`Hackney WMI -> public meeting / educational address -> separately printed pamphlet`

It does **not** establish microscopy at the Institute in 1863.

## 2. QJMS 1866 primary proceedings signal

The 1866 *Quarterly Journal of Microscopical Science* proceedings of the Microscopical Society record, immediately after Dr. Halifax's presentation on specimen preparation and before the Annual Soiree report, that the President:

- announced Society business;
- **read a communication received by the Committee from the Committee of the Hackney Working Men's Institute**;
- directed readers to `p. 194`.

Controlled chain:

`Hackney WMI Committee -> communication -> Microscopical Society Committee -> Society President reads communication -> QJMS proceedings -> separate/destination item p.194`

## 4. Why the negative boundary matters

This case is valuable even without p.194 because it proves a **committee-to-committee communication path** between an adult-education institution and the London Microscopical Society.

That is a stronger relation than generic contemporaneity, but weaker than a closed material/pedagogical transaction.

1. Hackney WMI existed and hosted public educational meetings — `CLOSED`;

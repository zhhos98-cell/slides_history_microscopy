# London case — Working Men's College, Cooke, and the slide economy

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Status:** case study v1; object-first reconstruction.

## 1. Case question

London is the strongest present case for an institutional genealogy from working-men's liberal education into organized popular microscopy. The central question is narrower than whether the Working Men's College encouraged science:

> Can a chain be reconstructed from WMC teachers and pupils into the making, donation, borrowing and exchange of physical microscope slides?

The current answer is **yes at institutional/network level, not yet at individual slide-accession level**.

## 2. Working Men's College -> microscope pedagogy

Nathan E. C. Smith's 2023 reconstruction places Mordecai Cubitt Cooke inside the WMC world and documents his 1861 evening class in **Physiological Botany**. Cooke explicitly treated the microscope as part of the teaching programme. This is the first secure London hinge for this project: microscope use is not only a later Quekett hobby but enters a working-men educational setting.

Source:
- Nathan E. C. Smith, `Fertile substrate: the rise, fall, and succession of popular microscopy in Great Britain`, *Annals of Science* (2023): https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00033790.2023.2181398

Evidence boundary: the class establishes **microscope pedagogy**, not ownership of a WMC slide cabinet. No specific mounted preparation used in Cooke's class has yet been identified.

## 3. Pupil continuity: Alfred Grugeon

The important feature is that Cooke's relation to WMC pupils continued beyond a single short course. Smith identifies Alfred Grugeon as one of the pupils with whom Cooke remained in contact. WMC's own 1866 programme independently lists **Mr Grugeon** as the Botany teacher under `Physical Sciences`, alongside Natural Philosophy and Zoology.

Primary WMC programme:
- *The Working Men's College ... Thirteenth Year, commencing October 22nd, 1866*: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/The_Working_Men%27s_College%2C_founded_1854%2C_45_Great_Ormond_Street%2C_Bloomsbury%2C_W.C._Principal%2C_Rev._F.D._Maurice%2C_M.A._.._%28IA_workingmenscolle188work%29.pdf

This creates a useful educational succession:

```text
Cooke teaches WMC botany with microscope (1861)
    -> pupil/associate network persists
    -> Grugeon later teaches Botany at WMC (1866)
```

It does not yet demonstrate that Grugeon inherited Cooke's exact microscopy practices or slides. That is an archival question.

## 4. WMC pupils -> Society of Amateur Botanists

Smith reconstructs the **Society of Amateur Botanists** as a Cooke-led body formed with former WMC pupils. Its functions included excursions, specimen interchange, papers, library, herbarium and museum. These are important because they transform pedagogy into member-run material infrastructure.

The operative transition is:

```text
teacher/pupil relation
    -> self-organized society
    -> specimen exchange
    -> collective library/herbarium/museum
```

The society is not yet a slide library. But it establishes the social and logistical practices later required for slide circulation.

Supporting contemporary retrospective:
- J. Ramsbottom summary, *Nature* 119 (1927): https://www.nature.com/articles/119104b0

## 5. Society of Amateur Botanists -> Quekett Microscopical Club

The Quekett Club's own institutional history records that Cooke was president of the Society of Amateur Botanists when an amateur microscopical association was proposed; Cooke, Thomas Ketteringham and Witham Bywater were already meeting weekly with their microscopes, examining objects and discussing specimens. W. Gibson's 1865 proposal explicitly sought an inexpensive mutual-help association for amateur microscopists on the model of the botanical society.

Source:
- Quekett Microscopical Club history: https://www.quekett.org/about/who/history
- Simon Cook, `Illustrating natural history`, *BJHS*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-for-the-history-of-science/article/illustrating-natural-history-images-periodicals-and-the-making-of-nineteenthcentury-scientific-communities/8B1DA7E7CA3CE9FBDA3311F5BF5F514F

This is the strongest organizational bridge:

```text
WMC pedagogy
 -> WMC pupil network
 -> amateur botanical mutual aid
 -> weekly domestic microscope practice
 -> Quekett Microscopical Club
```

## 6. The point where physical slides become institutionally explicit

The slide history becomes materially hard at Quekett. A recent collection history notes that the Club established a general microscope-slide collection in its earliest years, allowed members to **borrow** items, had **more than 2,000 preparations by 1873**, and also developed a scheme for exchanging unwanted slides.

Source:
- `Gibson microscope slide collection`, *Journal of the History of Collections* 37.3: https://academic.oup.com/jhc/article/37/3/507/8653474

This transforms an educational genealogy into a material economy:

```text
mounted object
 -> donation/acquisition
 -> catalogue/cabinet
 -> member loan
 -> observation
 -> return

or

member's unwanted slide
 -> exchange scheme
 -> another member
```

The Club's surviving archive and slide collection at the Natural History Museum make this chain unusually recoverable. Current QMC material includes minute books, membership records, catalogues and a very large physical slide collection.

Sources:
- https://www.quekett.org/about/venues/dorothea-bate-room
- https://www.quekett.org/about/citizen/slide-labels

## 7. The unresolved object genealogy

The key missing relation is deliberately specific:

> **Which identified WMC pupil or teacher donated, prepared, borrowed or exchanged which identified Quekett slide?**

At present the project can securely state:

- WMC -> Cooke microscope teaching;
- Cooke -> former-pupil network;
- former-pupil network -> Society of Amateur Botanists;
- that society -> organizational model for Quekett;
- Quekett -> large slide collection, loan and exchange.

It should not compress these into `WMC slides became the Quekett collection`.

## 8. Archival route capable of closing the gap

### Working Men's College archive

The surviving WMC institutional archive should be searched for:

- Cooke class registers, 1861;
- lists of pupils in physiological botany;
- Alfred Grugeon;
- museum donations and natural-history objects;
- apparatus and scientific purchases;
- student occupations;
- clubs and old-student activity.

Catalogue route:
- London Metropolitan Archives / WM College archive, `LMA/4535` (catalogued through AIM25 / National Archives Discovery).

### Quekett archive

Crosswalk the WMC actor set against:

- founding and early membership lists;
- annual slide donations;
- loan registers / slide catalogues;
- exchange notices;
- makers' labels;
- acquisition notes in minutes and journals.

Priority date window: **1865-1875**.

## 9. Analytical reading

London currently supports a strong model of **institutional reproduction**. The crucial operation is not that a college bought microscopes for passive demonstrations. Instead, a pedagogical relation appears to reproduce itself as self-organized clubs, periodical exchange, mutual instruction and eventually a collectively managed slide collection.

Working formulation:

> In London, the material infrastructure of popular microscopy emerged downstream from working-men's pedagogy: the College supplied a social and educational relation; the successor societies converted that relation into specimen and slide circulation.

That formulation remains provisional until contributor-level accession work is completed.

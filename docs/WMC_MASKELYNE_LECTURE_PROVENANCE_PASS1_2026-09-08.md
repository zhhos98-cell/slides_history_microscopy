# Maskelyne lecture provenance — Working Men's College vs other venues

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Purpose:** prevent lecture manuscripts and public courses from being silently assigned to the Working Men's College.

## 1. Secure WMC relations

Two relations are currently secure:

1. The **1866 Working Men's College programme** lists `N. S. MASKELYNE` on the College Council.
2. Maskelyne's **Royal Society obituary** states that he **frequently lectured at the Working Men's College**.

The second statement upgrades his WMC role beyond governance/support. It does **not** identify lecture subjects or dates.

Current WMC lecture-status formula:

```text
Maskelyne -> WMC Council = SECURE (1866 primary programme)
Maskelyne -> repeated WMC lecturing = SECURE/NEAR-PRIMARY (Royal Society obituary)
Maskelyne -> specific WMC lecture subject/date = OPEN
Maskelyne -> taught Davies at WMC = NOT PROVEN
Maskelyne -> taught microscopy/mineralogy at WMC = NOT PROVEN
```

## 2. 1866 programme: useful positive and negative evidence

The thirteenth-year programme places Maskelyne on Council but does not list him among that term's regular Physical Sciences class teachers.

Named Physical Sciences teachers/classes in the programme include:

- Alfred Grugeon — Botany;
- Rev. A. Goalen — Natural Philosophy;
- T. Spencer Cobbold — Zoology.

The Associateship/science syllabus also recognizes Botany, Physiology, Geology/Physical Geography, Mechanics, Hydrostatics, Optics and related physical-science subjects.

Thus the College clearly had a science curriculum in an institution governed in part by Maskelyne, but this programme cannot be used to assign him a regular 1866 science class.

## 3. `DF/MIN/20/1/4/13` — excluded as WMC evidence

NHM catalogue title:

`Visitors to Prof Maskelyne's lecture on crystallography, Nov 23, 1874`, with notes on crystal structure and circular polarisation.

This initially looks attractive for WMC because it is a lecture, is optical/crystallographic, and occurs during Maskelyne's adult-education activity.

However contemporary *Nature* (12 Nov 1874) explicitly announced that Maskelyne had offered a short course of **Crystallography to members of the Chemical Society**, to begin Monday **23 November 1874** and continue on Mondays and Fridays. The NHM/AIM25 finding aid likewise identifies item 13 with Maskelyne's notable 1874 Chemical Society series.

Therefore:

```text
DF/MIN/20/1/4/13
 = Chemical Society lecture-series evidence
 != Working Men's College lecture evidence
```

Do not use this file to reconstruct WMC teaching except as a comparison showing what Maskelyne's advanced crystallographic pedagogy looked like elsewhere.

## 4. `DF/MIN/20/1/4/12` — unidentified lecture manuscript

Title:

`Physical properties of minerals - I. optical`: notes for a lecture, illustrated with diagrams.

The catalogue gives **no date or venue**.

This is a high-value candidate for lecture-provenance work because optical mineralogy is close to the technical world of Davies, meteorite thin sections and polarizing microscopy. But no current evidence connects it to WMC.

Required closure fields:

`paper/watermark | handwriting layer | named audience | institutional heading | internal cross-reference | diagram type | cited instrument | dateable publications | associated envelope/folder | adjacent item provenance`.

Search WMC reports/programmes for matching title/vocabulary before assigning venue.

## 6. 1869 crystallography course — WMC candidacy downgraded; exact venue still not fully closed

The later DNB wording must not be read as evidence for an independently identified **London 1869** course. It says only that Story-Maskelyne gave notable crystallography courses **both in London and Oxford**, followed by the separate statement that **in a course delivered in 1869** he announced an important proof concerning the number and mutual inclinations of possible symmetry planes. Grammatically, this does not assign the 1869 course to London.

A theorem-specific near-contemporary source points in the other direction. An anonymous review of Maskelyne's *The Morphology of Crystals* in *The London, Edinburgh, and Dublin Philosophical Magazine and Journal of Science*, ser. 5, vol. 40, no. 242 (July 1895), p. 145, discusses the same proof and states that Maskelyne used to give it **more than twenty-five years earlier in his lectures at Oxford**. This is currently the strongest venue evidence tied to the theorem itself.

W. J. Lewis, *A Treatise on Crystallography* (Cambridge, 1899), p. 119, is the source later cited for the chronological priority. Contemporary/recently contemporary reviews of Lewis summarize its footnote as saying that the proof was **given by Maskelyne in lectures in 1869**, two years before Gadolin's 1871 publication, but the accessible wording does not itself supply a venue. The 1911 *Nature* obituary likewise says the mathematical result was first given in Maskelyne's **lectures in 1869** without specifying place.

Therefore the controlled relation is:

```text
Maskelyne -> symmetry-plane proof -> lectures in 1869 = STRONG CHRONOLOGICAL TRADITION
Maskelyne -> same proof -> Oxford lectures more than 25 years before July 1895 = STRONG VENUE TRADITION
exact archival event "Oxford 1869 course" = NOT YET CLOSED
1869 course -> London / WMC = NO LONGER A DEFAULT CANDIDATE
```

This is enough to remove **“1869 London crystallography course”** from the active WMC search queue. It is not enough to collapse the two traditions into a single exact archival event without a dated Oxford course notice, programme, or lecture manuscript.

Sources/control:

- anonymous review, “The Morphology of Crystals,” *Philosophical Magazine*, ser. 5, 40.242 (July 1895), 145 — theorem explicitly associated with Maskelyne's earlier **Oxford lectures**;
- W. J. Lewis, *A Treatise on Crystallography* (Cambridge University Press, 1899), p. 119 — later summaries identify the footnote as assigning the proof to Maskelyne's **lectures in 1869**;
- H. A. Miers, review of Lewis, *Nature* 61 (22 Mar. 1900), 485–486 — near-contemporary summary of the 1869 priority claim;
- *Nature* obituary, 1 June 1911 — repeats that the result was first given in lectures in 1869, venue unspecified;
- DNB, Second Supplement (1912) — London/Oxford courses + 1869 course, but no grammatical venue closure.

**Stop rule:** do not spend further broad-web effort trying to make the 1869 theorem into a WMC lecture. Reopen only if a primary 1869 WMC/Oxford programme, dated lecture notice, or manuscript provenance appears independently.

The higher-value WMC-specific target is now `DF/MIN/20/1/4/12` (“Physical properties of minerals - I. optical”) or an independently dated WMC programme/report naming a Maskelyne lecture.

## 7. Why exclusion is analytically useful

The WMC–British Museum problem is not served by maximizing the number of science lectures assigned to WMC. It is served by distinguishing teaching ecologies.

Maskelyne moved among at least:

- Oxford university teaching;
- British Museum custody/research;
- Working Men's College lecturing/governance;
- Royal Institution public lecturing;
- Chemical Society advanced crystallography courses;
- other scientific societies/public forums.

The same actor therefore translated mineralogical expertise across audiences and institutional purposes. The historical problem is to identify **what changed in objects, diagrams, mathematical level, instruments and expected practical work when the venue changed**.

That is more informative than a binary `popularizer / expert` label.

# WMC periodical corpus index v1

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Purpose:** periodical-first control surface for the RSVP/WMC research line, separating bibliographic closure, serial function, material state, and grant-funded delta.

## Governing question

This index treats nineteenth-century periodicals not merely as records of popular microscopy or adult education, but as part of their material and institutional infrastructure.

> How did serial print organize entry into institutions, teaching, reading, reporting, advertising, exchange, verification, reprinting, institutional replication, and the circulation of scientific objects and practices across Working Men's Colleges, institutes, societies, museums, workplaces and domestic networks?

The periodical layer must remain analytically distinct from the actor/object layer already developed elsewhere in this branch.

---

## 00 — Four serial regimes currently visible

The corpus should no longer be organized only by title. Current cases show at least four analytically distinct serial regimes.

### Regime A — College self-publication

Examples:

- *The People's College Journal* (Sheffield, 1846–47);
- *The Working Men's College Magazine* (London, 1859–61 leading run model);
- later *The Working Men's College Journal* (London, from 1890 according to current secondary control).

Functions include institutional identity, orientation, reading guidance, internal argument, curriculum, and popular-education self-definition.

### Regime B — Local scientific sociability in serial form

Primary example:

- *The Circulator: A Magazine of Literature, Science & Art* (Haley Hill/Halifax, 1866–67).

Functions include manuscript-to-print transition, local scientific/literary society self-publication, continued geology/natural-history serialization, and conversion of local observations into stable print.

### Regime C — Federated replication / organizational coordination

Comparative control:

- Working Men's Club & Institute Union *Occasional Papers*;
- *The Working Men's Club and Institute Magazine*;
- *The Workmen's Club Journal and Official Gazette* and successors.

Function: local institutional cases and organizational information are collected and redistributed across a federated field. Keep this institutionally separate from Working Men's Colleges.

### Regime D — Specialist scientific exchange / recoding

Examples:

- QJMS;
- *Monthly Microscopical Journal*;
- JRMS;
- *Hardwicke's Science-Gossip*;
- *Northern Microscopist* / related titles;
- local microscopical-society transactions.

Functions include technical communication, exchange, object circulation, proceedings, advertisements, and local-to-metropolitan specialist recoding.

A title may perform more than one function, but do not collapse the regimes into a generic category of `periodical influence`.

## 02 — Federated working-men institution serials — comparative control

### Working Men's Club & Institute Union

Keep distinct from WMCs.

Current controlled publication sequence:

1. *Occasional Papers of the Working Men's Club and Institute Union, on the formation, progress, and results of working-men's clubs...*, nos. 1–24, bibliographic object dated from 1863;
2. *The Working Men's Club and Institute Magazine*, launched 1864, 3d per issue, discontinued October 1865;
3. *The Workmen's Club Journal and Official Gazette of the Workingmen's Club and Institute Union*, first issue 15 May 1875, suspended 1878; scholarly control describes it as weekly May 1875–Feb. 1878;
4. *Club and Institute Journal*, from 1883;
5. *Club Journal*, from July 1894.

Key comparative function:

`SERIAL_TO_REPLICATION`

The *Occasional Papers* explicitly collected the `formation, progress, and results` of local working-men's clubs/institutes. Contemporary reporting describes short statements on named local clubs. This makes the serial a candidate organizational technology for packaging local institutional cases for a wider federated readership.

Do not use growth of the Union as proof that the serial caused institutional replication.

Dedicated control: `docs/WORKING_MENS_CLUB_UNION_SERIAL_NETWORK_1863_1894.md`.

## 07 — Circulation-chain / function model

Normalize relations as transactions rather than generic `influence`.

Core function codes now include:

- `ORIENT` — print helps a reader enter/understand an institution;
- `READ_GUIDE` — serial guides study/reading;
- `SELF_PUB` — institution/society publishes itself;
- `POPULAR_EDUCATION_SERIAL` — serial explicitly serves popular education;
- `MS_TO_PRINT` — manuscript serial becomes print;
- `SCIENCE_SERIALIZATION` — scientific inquiry appears as continued serial sequence;
- `LOCAL_OBSERVATION_TO_PRINT` — local specimens/observations become serial content;
- `EVENT_TO_PERIODICAL` — lecture/event becomes article;
- `OFFICE_TO_LETTER` — institutional office becomes periodical correspondence;
- `INSTITUTION_TO_SPECIALIST` — adult-education institution enters specialist proceedings;
- `EXCHANGE_TO_OBJECT` — printed exchange mediates physical material circulation;
- `LOCAL_TO_METRO` — local practice is recoded for wider audience;
- `SERIAL_TO_REPLICATION` — local institutional cases/arrangements are packaged for reuse across a federated field;
- `REPRINT`;
- `AD_TO_PURCHASE`;
- `REPORT_TO_VISIT`.

Recommended event schema:

`date | publication | issue/page | genre | function_code | institution | actor | object/practice | transaction | destination/audience | downstream source/object | source_state | material_state | confidence`

## 09 — Evidence states

- `CLOSED_PRIMARY` — exact issue/page or physical item inspected;
- `CLOSED_BIBLIOGRAPHIC` — title/run/issue identity secure;
- `CLOSED_BIBLIOGRAPHIC_ARCHIVE` — archive item directly identifies surviving issue/title;
- `CLOSED_CITATION_SECONDARY` — source-bearing scholarship gives exact issue/page, primary not yet inspected;
- `PREVIEW_ONLY` — search snippet/result only;
- `INDEXED_NOT_READ` — exact source location known, content unrecovered;
- `ISSUE_CONTENT_PENDING` — institutional/title context known, issue content unread;
- `MATERIAL_STATE_PENDING` — text known, serial/binding/wrapper state unresolved;
- `REPRINT_RELATION_PENDING` — apparent reprint/duplicate relation unresolved;
- `RUN_CONFLICT` — incompatible run/date evidence deliberately preserved;
- `NEGATIVE_BOUNDED` — defined search produced no hit, not universal absence;
- `RSVP_TARGET` — grant-funded physical/full-access closure target.

## Stop rule

Do not expand into a general history of Victorian working-class or science periodicals.

A title remains in the working corpus only if it performs at least one of the following:

1. documents/constitutes a working-men/adult-education institution;
2. carries science/natural-history practice tied to those institutions or successor networks;
3. mediates a material transaction;
4. packages institutional arrangements for replication/coordination;
5. visibly recodes local practice into another periodical/public audience;
6. is needed to reconstruct the material/bibliographic state of the grant-funded WMC ecology.

This index is a control surface, not a finished bibliography.

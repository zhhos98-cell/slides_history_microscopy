# WMC periodical corpus index v1

**Date:** 2026-09-09  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Purpose:** reorganize the existing working-men's microscopy research from a **periodical-first** perspective, with explicit separation between pre-award baseline, unresolved source work, and RSVP-funded research delta.

## Governing question

This index treats nineteenth-century periodicals not merely as records of popular microscopy or adult education, but as part of their material and institutional infrastructure.

Core question:

> How did serial print organize teaching, reporting, advertising, exchange, verification, reprinting and the circulation of microscopical objects and practices across Working Men's Colleges, mechanics' institutes, microscopical societies, museums, workplaces and domestic networks?

The periodical layer must remain analytically distinct from the actor/object layer already developed elsewhere in this branch.

## 02 — Specialist microscopy periodicals

### *Quarterly Journal of Microscopical Science* (QJMS)

Role in corpus:

- instrument and method discussion;
- society communications;
- preparation catalogues;
- commercial/technical notices;
- slide-series announcements;
- transfer of local microscopical activity into specialist print.

**Direct WMI case already open:**

- Hackney Working Men's Institute, 1866;
- communication indexed/referenced at QJMS vol. 6, p. 194;
- status remains `B_PENDING` until the actual communication is page-closed.

Do not infer lecture, loan or slide transfer until the text is read.

### *Monthly Microscopical Journal*

Role:

- Royal Microscopical Society meetings;
- apparatus and prepared-object reports;
- method disputes;
- letters and technical comparisons;
- relay between provincial actors and metropolitan specialist audiences.

Existing Sorby work supplies a mature extraction model but should be reused only where it bears directly on adult education / local society / circulation questions.

### *Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society*

Role:

- proceedings;
- bibliography/review infrastructure;
- society affiliation;
- donations/exhibitions;
- provincial society visibility;
- reclassification of local practice into specialist disciplinary print.

### *Hardwicke's Science-Gossip*

**Priority core title.**

Treat not merely as a popular-science magazine but as a circulation mechanism supporting:

- exchange columns;
- specimen requests;
- slide offers;
- maker/dealer visibility;
- reader correspondence;
- local society notices;
- material transactions that later reappear in specialist journals.

Existing repository precedents include slide/gathering exchanges and object-to-periodical bridges. This title is especially important for reconstructing reader-to-reader scientific material exchange.

### *Microscopical News and Northern Microscopist* / *Northern Microscopist*

Role:

- northern/provincial microscopical practice;
- slide and preparation notices;
- practical instruction;
- local-society activity;
- commercial and exchange information.

Existing exact-source lead includes 1884 `Fish Scales` in *Microscopical News and Northern Microscopist* IV, p.109.

### *American Monthly Microscopical Journal*

Control / reception title rather than core British institutional source.

Use to detect transatlantic re-reporting of British provincial society practices, especially Sheffield and other local networks.

### Local society transactions / reports

Targets include:

- Sheffield Microscopical Society transactions and annual reports;
- Quekett proceedings/reports/collection notices;
- South Shields Microscopical Society traces;
- Guildford Natural History and Microscopical Society;
- Manchester Microscopical Society;

Research question: which local practices become serial print, and which printed notices in turn generate object circulation, attendance, exchange or replication?

## 05 — Serial materiality

This is a principal RSVP-funded physical-inspection layer.

For every surviving issue/volume, record where possible:

- wrappers / covers;
- advertisements;
- publishers' lists;
- supplements;
- inserted catalogues;
- exchange columns;
- correspondence columns;
- issue numbering;
- original fascicle boundaries;
- pagination anomalies;
- title changes;
- reprints;
- binding order;
- later rebinding that may erase original serial structure;
- missing/extra issues or supplements.

The object of analysis is not only text content but **how a reader encountered, ordered and acted on serial information**.

## 06 — Circulation-chain model

Normalize periodical relations as explicit transactions rather than generic `influence`.

Candidate chain types:

- `institution -> notice`;
- `class -> report`;
- `lecture -> summary`;
- `maker -> advertisement`;
- `reader -> exchange notice`;
- `periodical notice -> physical object transaction`;
- `slide/preparation -> journal description`;
- `local society -> specialist journal`;
- `specialist journal -> Nature/general science press`;
- `local report -> reprint/abstract elsewhere`;
- `periodical -> archive/catalogue identification lead`.

Recommended event schema:

`date | publication | issue | page | genre | institution | actor | object/practice | action | destination/audience | reprint/source relation | physical issue state | exact source | confidence`

## 08 — Evidence-state vocabulary

Use the following states for the periodical corpus:

- `CLOSED_PRIMARY` — exact issue/page or physical item directly inspected;
- `CLOSED_BIBLIOGRAPHIC` — title/run/issue identity secure, content not fully inspected;
- `PREVIEW_ONLY` — search result/snippet only;
- `INDEXED_NOT_READ` — source location known, text not recovered;
- `MATERIAL_STATE_PENDING` — textual content known but wrapper/binding/issue state unresolved;
- `REPRINT_RELATION_PENDING` — apparent duplicate/reprint relation not yet source-closed;
- `NEGATIVE_BOUNDED` — searched defined corpus produced no hit; not universal absence;
- `RSVP_TARGET` — explicitly reserved for grant-funded physical/full-access closure.

## Stop rule

Do not expand into a general history of Victorian science periodicals.

A title enters the working corpus only if it performs at least one of the following:

1. documents a working-men/adult-education institution;
2. carries microscopy/natural-history practice tied to those institutions or their successor networks;
3. mediates a material transaction (slide, specimen, preparation, instrument, exchange, purchase, donation, exhibition);
4. visibly recodes local practice into a different periodical/public audience;
5. is necessary to reconstruct the material/bibliographical state of the grant-funded WMC periodical ecology.

This index should be updated as the RSVP project proceeds; it is a control surface, not a finished bibliography.

# Bibliography pass 14 — East and Southeast Asia

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: verified bibliography expansion only. The frozen 307 / 155 surviving-object survey is unchanged.

## Admission rule

Pass 14 keeps the existing **physical-slide first** threshold. A source was admitted only when it is substantially about microscope slides, mounted preparations, permanent sections, their production or material system, a bounded slide series, an object-level slide collection, or a documented institutional regime in which physical teaching slides are handled as circulating objects.

Broad institutional histories, optical catalogues, general medical texts, virtual-slide platforms, photographic/lantern slides and museum collections dominated by jars or gross specimens remain outside this bibliography.

## Added records

### Japan — dedicated preparation texts

1. **岩川友太郎, 『プレパラート 第1輯』 (1906).** NDL records a dedicated work compiled/explained by Iwakawa and published by 鰯屋岩本標本部. The digitized book is divided into 動物, 植物 and 生理 units. The source establishes a preparation-specific printed work. It does **not** by itself prove that the digitized copy was issued with, or still retains, physical slides.

2. **田中克己, 『顕微鏡標本の作り方』 (1954).** NDL: 裳華房, 278 pp. Dedicated specimen-making manual.

3. **慶応義塾大学医学部病理学教室 編, 『病理組織標本の作り方』 改訂3版 (1968).** NDL: 医学書院, 290 pp. Dedicated pathology/histology preparation manual.

4. **太田邦夫・畠山茂, 『病理顕微鏡標本の作り方』 (1969).** NDL: 南山堂, 209 pp. Dedicated pathological microscopic-specimen manual.

### Taiwan — Chinese-language preparation and teaching history

5. **林庚欽, 『實用組織切片製作法』 (1978).** National Taiwan University Special Collections records the title, author, year and medical-school provenance. The public result does not expose a publisher; the bibliography therefore does not invent one. This record raises the bibliography to twelve publication languages.

6. **國立臺灣大學醫學院解剖學暨細胞生物學研究所, 「歷史沿革：組織學教學」 (current institutional history, updated 2026).** The institute states that during the Japanese colonial period students worked in pairs, received several section specimens for each practical and returned them afterward. It also records the 2016 digitization/cloud migration of all teaching histology glass slides. The evidentiary value is the explicit loan-return regime and the glass-to-digital afterlife, not a claim that every current slide dates to the colonial period.

### Japan — surviving collections and slide-publication systems

7. **東京大学総合研究博物館, 近代医家三宅一族旧蔵コレクション, slide records.** The object catalogue separately records boxes/card sheets of glass preparations under IMTE_MK0000193–0201 and related slide/coverslip materials. It preserves packaging, breakage, inscriptions, unused slides, coverslips, a W. Stender Leipzig box and a slide rack. Individual catalogue-lot counts remain separate and are not silently converted into one historical total.

8. **国立科学博物館, 『日本産淡水藻類標本集 (Algae Aquae Dulcis Japonicae Exsiccatae)』, issued from 2007.** Each fascicle joins twenty permanent slides to a catalogue and is distributed without charge for exchange to about thirty herbaria. The public site currently exposes eight fascicles, nos. 1–160. This is one of the clearest contemporary Asian slide-as-publication systems found in the project.

9. **国立科学博物館, 微細藻類標本庫, 2025 state.** The collection register separates 21,457 prepared slides from dry, liquid and electron-microscopy material. A separate museum methods page distinguishes temporary, semi-permanent and permanent preparations and states that permanent diatom slides are retained as specimens. No cross-medium total is inferred.

### Japan — object-centred collection scholarship

10. **半田久美子, 「現生植物葉脈標本（横山 章コレクション）図録」 (2015).** The article/catalogue centres on 309 bleached/stained leaf preparations mounted as slides and publishes the collection list and plates.

11. **大西亘・田中徳久・勝山輝男, 「尾崎公彦葉脈標本コレクション目録」 (2017).** Catalogue of 936 leaf-vein specimens enclosed in prepared slides; identifications, digital images and taxonomic metadata were added to a research collection that had remained in museum custody for about thirty years after Ozaki's death.

### Hong Kong — medical-slide afterlife in public history

12. **Ria Sinha / HKU Centre for the Humanities and Medicine / Hong Kong Museum of Medical Sciences, *Fever: The History of Malaria in Hong Kong* (2015).** HKU reports that the research began from a surviving laboratory sink bearing purple marks associated with staining malaria-parasite slides and that old laboratory slides were used among the exhibition's historical artefacts. The entry is admitted for this material reconstruction, not as a generic malaria-history source.

## Screened but excluded / held out

### Singapore

- **NUS Pathology Museum** — historically important, founded in 1907, but the public record is dominated by gross pathological specimens preserved in jars/pots. No dedicated historical microscope-slide register was verified in this pass.
- **NUS Pathweb / virtual slides** — excluded under the existing digital whole-slide imaging rule.
- **Lee Kong Chian Natural History Museum arthropod holdings** — mites and other small taxa are reported as mainly mounted on glass slides, but the available page is a broad entomological collection description. Held out pending a dedicated slide catalogue or object study.

### Hong Kong

- General HKU pathology histories and current virtual pathology resources were screened but excluded where physical glass slides were incidental or replaced by digital whole-slide systems.

### Mainland China

- Broad institutional specimen pages that merely list glass-slide specimens as one possible preservation type were excluded.
- A recent IVPP donation includes hundreds of `切片`, but the public description does not securely establish that these are mounted microscope slides rather than another thin-section category. Held out.

### Korea / Philippines

- Institutional teaching histories and current course pages were screened. They did not supply sufficiently bounded historical slide collections or slide-centred publications for admission in this pass.
- Photographic and lantern-slide collections were excluded as a different medium.

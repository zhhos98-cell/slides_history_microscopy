# WM College archive contact and access state — 2026-09-09

**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`

## 1. Email-derived access state

A July 2026 correspondence with WM College establishes that the College itself retains historical archival material, but that the holdings are not yet comprehensively indexed or arranged for rapid remote reference.

Key points supplied by the College's Head of Governance:

- the College has its own historical archive;
- substantial work remains to establish exactly what records are held and to index, archive and preserve them systematically;
- not all records are on paper;
- the College also holds many glass plates;
- no reliable index has been maintained for the contents of those glass plates;
- the College was willing to look informally for relevant material and to retain the research enquiry for possible future follow-up if further material is identified.

The researcher replied that no substantial search was required at that stage and indicated that a future visit might be more appropriate once the project was better developed.

## 2. Critical provenance distinction

Do **not** collapse the following into one archival corpus:

1. `LMA/4535` — the Working Men's College records represented through the London Metropolitan Archives / National Archives Discovery framework;
2. WM College's own institution-held historical material described in the July 2026 correspondence as incompletely indexed and including glass plates.

The email does not establish that every item in the institution-held archive is additional to LMA/4535, nor does it provide a box-level concordance between the two. Treat the relation as `PROVENANCE_RELATION_PENDING`.

## 3. RSVP relevance

This access state strongly supports the wording of the RSVP microgrant application proposing a bounded survey of:

- scattered publications;
- short-run print;
- internally circulated material;
- institutional holdings not readily identifiable through standard periodical catalogues.

The reason is now explicit: the College itself reports incomplete indexing of its historical holdings.

This means the WMC visit should be conceived as **exploratory institutional reconnaissance**, not as a conventional pre-ordered archival visit based on a complete finding aid.

Recommended on-site workflow if access is arranged:

1. first establish the physical / intellectual arrangement of the institution-held archive;
2. separate print, manuscript, photographic/glass-plate and object material;
3. identify serial or quasi-serial material before deep reading;
4. record title variants, dates, issue numbers, wrappers, supplements, reports, prospectuses, newsletters, circulars and club publications;
5. photograph or note shelf/box/folder context where permitted;
6. avoid attributing unindexed material to a known LMA series without a direct provenance bridge.

## 4. Glass plates as a separate research frontier

The College specifically reports holding many unindexed glass plates.

For the current RSVP periodical project these are **adjacent**, not automatically core. They enter the core only if they can be tied to:

- periodical production or illustration;
- College lectures/classes documented in serial print;
- club excursions or specimen work reported in WMCJ / other periodicals;
- reproductions, lantern projection, microscopy, museum or teaching practices;
- identifiable people/events that can be crosswalked with the serial corpus.

Otherwise they remain a separate visual/archive line.

## 5. Research consequence

The College-held archive creates a useful asymmetry with the British Library leg of the RSVP project:

- **British Library:** comparatively controlled bibliographic corpus; high value in issue materiality, wrappers, supplements, bindings, advertisements and variant runs.
- **WM College on-site holdings:** comparatively uncontrolled discovery environment; high value in identifying previously unmapped institutional serials, short runs, internal publications and associated ephemera.

The two-day design in the grant application therefore remains methodologically sound even if much of the textual research can be done from open sources beforehand.

## 6. Funding implication

The grant does not need to be spent on broad discovery work that open digital resources can already accomplish.

Highest marginal-value uses remain:

- travel for direct inspection of the institution-held archive;
- one night of accommodation if required by a two-day London visit;
- BNA subscription only where it converts specific preview-only press leads into full-text evidence;
- any permitted direct access costs tied to archival research.

Do not spend simply to exhaust the award. Await the formal award/payment terms before making assumptions about unused balances.

## Stop rule

Do not describe the WMC institution-held archive as catalogued, complete, or identical with LMA/4535. Do not claim the glass plates contain microscopy or periodical material until inspected. The current evidence establishes an **unindexed institutional holding environment with high exploratory value**.

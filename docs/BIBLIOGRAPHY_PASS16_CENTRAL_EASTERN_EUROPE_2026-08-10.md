# Bibliography pass 16 — Central and Eastern Europe

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: verified bibliography expansion only. The frozen 307 / 155 surviving-object survey remains closed and unchanged.

## Admission rule

Pass 16 keeps the existing **physical-slide first** threshold. A source enters only when mounted microscope preparations, slide-based collection units, physical teaching preparations, permanent slide-making techniques, or a bounded slide/preparation archive performs substantive evidentiary work.

The module deliberately does not convert broad entomological, herbarium, histology or museum totals into slide totals. Where an institution gives a specimen count inside a slide-mounted collection rather than a count of slides, the row preserves that distinction. Digital atlases are admitted only when their source explicitly identifies a bounded physical teaching-preparation archive as the underlying collection; virtual whole-slide systems with no such physical-archive function remain excluded.

## Added records

### Poland

1. **Henryk Kadyi, `Seife als Einbettungsmasse beim Anfertigen mikroskopischer Schnitte` (1879).** A historical technical paper specifically on embedding material for making microscopic sections. Recent preparatory-history scholarship verifies the citation. The row is confined to the 1879 microscopic-section method and does not equate Kadyi's later paraffin preservation of larger anatomical preparations with microscope slides.

2. **KRAM M myxomycete collection.** The Polish Institute of Botany PAS and RCIN both describe approximately 3,000 permanent microscopic preparations. Historical material includes Rostafiński, Raciborski, Krupa and Krzemieniewska, with additions through exchange or donation involving Lister, Meylan, Schroeter and Torrend. Current Polish and English institute pages disagree on the number of numbered herbarium items, but both support the approximately 3,000-slide figure; no attempt is made to normalize the unrelated herbarium counts.

3. **UMCS Lublin entomological collection.** More than one million scientific specimens overall, with 11,000 microscopic preparations as a separately stated class. Thysanoptera and Aphididae are specifically held as microscopic preparations.

4. **University of Silesia HEMIPTERON.** More than 9,000 aphids and more than 9,000 scale insects are held as microscopic preparations. The digitization workflow scans slides and checks their labels rather than treating digitization as a replacement for the physical collection.

### Czechia

5. **National Museum Prague Diptera.** 30,000 microscopic preparations are separately reported from dry and alcohol material. Jan Ježek's Psychodidae collection includes microscope slides mounted in Canada balsam; the museum gives more than 21,000 specimens across ethanol and slides, including 785 types, so that figure is not converted into a slide count.

6. **National Museum Prague Hemiptera.** 1,900 microscopic slides of scale insects and 781 whitefly preparations. The two slide/preparation counts remain separate.

7. **National Museum Prague small insect orders.** Historical microscopic-preparation holdings explicitly include J. Uzel's Thysanoptera, K. Pfleger's Amblycera/Ischnocera and B. Rosický's Anoplura material. No slide total is published.

8. **Masaryk University Faculty of Medicine, `Histologický atlas LF MU` (2018).** The institute states that the preparations underlying the atlas were made over previous decades and constitute a unique study collection used by generations of medical students. The digital atlas is retained as a publication of that physical teaching archive, not as a generic virtual-slide resource.

### Hungary

9. **Hungarian Natural History Museum, Pantocsek diatom preparations.** József Pantocsek's 918 diatom preparations are identified as the most valuable microscopic component of the algae collection and as only about one fifth of the original corpus, the remainder having been destroyed during the Second World War.

10. **Bakony Museum Tardigrada collection.** A slide catalogue (`tárgylemez kataszter`) whose unit is explicitly the slide: 211 slides, including 11 holotypes and one paratype, from material collected and identified by Gyula Alfonz Iharos in 1943–1967.

11. **Bakony Museum diatom collection.** 499 prepared, identified and documented diatom preparations made by László Vida from Erzsébet Kol's preserved material, separated from 723 liquid algae preparations.

### Romania

12. **Maria Suciu Siphonaptera collection, Grigore Antipa National Museum.** 1,740 microscopic preparations containing 1,999 flea specimens plus 2,074 alcohol-preserved specimens. The whole collection retains Suciu's handwritten evidence register from her doctoral research.

13. **W. K. Knechtel Thysanoptera collection, Grigore Antipa National Museum.** Slide-and-coverslip preparations (`preparate lamă-lamelă`) containing 3,275 specimens, 177 of them types. The institutional record gives specimen count, not slide count.

14. **Botnariuc–Albu Chironomidae collection, Grigore Antipa National Museum.** 1,831 slide preparations (`lame`) plus separately preserved wet larvae and adults.

15. **Constantin Manolache Homoptera collection, Grigore Antipa National Museum.** 4,016 microscopic preparations, separated by the museum from the additional 868 specimens of the Cantoreanu collection.

16. **BUC palynotheca, University of Bucharest Botanical Garden.** The herbarium explicitly maintains a palynotheca of microscopic preparations containing pollen samples and is re-inventorying, databasing and digitizing it. The overall herbarium total is not reused as a slide count.

### Croatia

17. **Croatian National Collection of Diatoms.** Established in 2018 as an institutional collection of permanent microscopic preparations. The dated 2019 launch page reported more than 6,000 preparations; a current departmental history page says more than 4,000. The bibliography preserves the 2019 state and flags the discrepancy instead of silently choosing a normalized total.

### Slovakia

18. **Domoráková et al., `Základy histológie II` (2023).** The 433-page Slovak textbook/atlas explicitly states that all histological preparations come from the archive of the Institute of Histology and Embryology, where they are used for practical classes and teaching presentations. It is admitted for the documented physical preparation archive underlying the publication.

## Screened but excluded / held out

- **Julian Steinhaus, `Histologia patologiczna, Cz. 1. Metody badania mikroskopowego tkanek i płynów patologicznych` (1905)** — bibliographically verified, but the accessible catalogue record does not establish that mounted preparations are sufficiently central rather than one element of a broader pathological microscopy manual. Held out pending contents-level verification.
- **Henryk Kadyi's later paraffin anatomical-preservation papers (1891/1895)** — historically important preparatory work, but accessible histories describe durable anatomical sectional preparations rather than securely slide-mounted microscopic preparations. Kept outside the slide bibliography.
- **`Jak hledáme, usmrcujeme a pro sbírky upravujeme hmyz`** — Czech collecting/preparation handbook whose catalogue subjects include microscopic preparations, but the book is broadly entomological rather than preparation-specific.
- **Masaryk / Slovak virtual or image-only histology resources** without an explicit physical archive basis — excluded under the existing digital boundary.
- **Sofia University geological microscope-preparation laboratory** — directly documents preparation of petrographic thin sections and polished microscopic preparations, but geological thin sections have not yet been admitted as a separate medium-domain in this bibliography. Held out rather than silently widening scope.
- **Bulgarian current school-slide retailers** — physically real slide sets, but ordinary retail listings add little research value and are not admitted merely to create Bulgarian-language coverage.
- **Serbian and Slovenian broad museum/teaching pages** found in this pass did not close a bounded physical slide collection or dedicated preparation publication.

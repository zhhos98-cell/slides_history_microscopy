# Bibliography pass 15 — Latin America

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: verified bibliography expansion only. The frozen 307 / 155 surviving-object survey is unchanged.

## Admission rule

Pass 15 keeps the existing **physical-slide first** threshold. A source was admitted only when permanent or temporary microscope slides, mounted preparations, slide-making materials, bounded microscopic-preparation collections, or the conservation/case-linkage of glass slides does substantive evidentiary work.

The pass deliberately separates slide/preparation counts from anatomical pieces, paraffin blocks, wet lots, pinned specimens, accessions, images and type counts.

## Added records

### Brazil — pathology collections and the material archive of diagnosis

1. **Instituto Oswaldo Cruz / Museu da Patologia, CSAP.** The original pathology-museum collection is reported by IOC as more than 850 anatomical pieces and about 10,000 slides, covering the pathology tradition from 1903 into the 1970s. The dedicated history and recovery pages document surviving histological slides, loss/dispersal, cleaning, barcoding and continued conservation.

2. **Instituto Oswaldo Cruz / Museu da Patologia, Coleção de Febre Amarela (CFA).** Approximately 498,000 case units generated through yellow-fever work. The collection's case architecture links preserved liver material, a paraffin block and stained histological section(s) on glass to diagnostic paperwork. The museum records deterioration/loss of some blocks and slides and a conservation programme involving technical cleaning, slide boxes, labels, barcodes, digitization and research access.

3. **Instituto Oswaldo Cruz / Museu da Patologia, CDEPAT.** Research material generated from 1984 onward through the pathology department and Histotechnology Sector. IOC reports 65,000 paraffin blocks and 240,000 stained histological slides on glass, with registration numbers linking cases, pieces, blocks, slides, documentation and resulting publications. Current IOC pages expose different case-count states (>16,000, c.20,000, >21,000); the bibliography therefore does not normalize a single case total.

4. **FMUSP Department of Pathology scientific archive.** The 2025 digitization project identifies more than 200,000 histological slides in a climate-controlled `blocoteca` alongside approximately 277,500 autopsy reports dating from 1921–2000. The project treats slides as a separate conservation/digitization class.

### Brazil — botanical/anatomical slide collections

5. **Jardim Botânico do Rio de Janeiro, Xiloteca RBw.** A separately registered anatomical-slide collection linked to the wood collection. Current official JBRJ pages expose live count states of 26,000, approximately 35,000 and approximately 36,000 slides. This discrepancy is preserved as versioned institutional metadata rather than resolved by choosing one number. The collection has a numerical slide register and ongoing imaging in JABOT.

6. **Herbário SPF, Universidade de São Paulo, Laminoteca.** A dedicated subcollection of anatomical preparations explicitly defined as histological or palynological slides used to study plant cells, tissues and pollen. No slide total is supplied by the public page.

### Brazil — slide-making research

7. **Paiva et al. 2006, `Verniz vitral incolor 500®`.** Direct materials study of mounting media for permanent stained plant slides. The paper compares a lower-cost Brazilian synthetic varnish with Canada balsam, Euparal, Entellan and Permount and evaluates preservation of tissue and staining.

8. **Quadros & Monteiro-Filho 2006.** Preparation study for a mammal-hair reference collection. Cuticular impressions are made on glass slides with clear nail varnish; permanent mounts use Entellan and temporary preparations glycerin. Slide construction is part of the experimental method.

### Argentina — microscopic-preparation collections and teaching systems

9. **Museo de La Plata, División Entomología, Sección Diptera.** Approximately 10,000 microscopic preparations are reported as a distinct preservation class, separately from >20,000 pinned specimens and >16,000 alcohol-preserved larvae/adults.

10. **Museo Argentino de Ciencias Naturales, BA Pal.** The national paleopalynology collection began in 1965 and now reports >6,600 microscopic preparations and >200 type specimens. A closed CIRGEO collection incorporated in 2000 is documented as 1,279 preparations and 160 types; these historical component figures are not added again to the current total.

11. **CECOAL-CONICET-UNNE, CTES-PMP.** The `Dr. Rafael Herbst` paleontological repository explicitly separates `Preparados micropaleontológicos` from paleobotany, paleozoology and ichnology. Its approximately 20,000-object overall count is not converted into a CTES-PMP total.

12. **INIMEC-CONICET-UNC, Curioscopio.** A contemporary teaching kit combining permanent preparations with an atlas and activity guides. Its institutional description explicitly gives the material workflow: fixation, solid embedding, thin sectioning, staining and final permanent mounting.

13. **Bodnar et al. 2025, Museo de La Plata paleobotanical collections.** One of the two central collections studied in the article is a microscopic-preparation collection. The paper reports approximately 1,600 microscope-slide preparations of fossil woods, leaf cuticles and pollen/spores, traces their historical growth and describes their university teaching/research functions.

### Mexico and Peru — helminth slide repositories

14. **UNAM Colección Nacional de Helmintos (CNHE).** Founded in 1930; the current material architecture explicitly includes a permanent-preparation (`laminillas`) collection, wet collection and display specimens. The public page supplies no permanent-slide total, so none is inferred.

15. **UNMSM Museo de Historia Natural, Colección Científica de Helmintos e Invertebrados Relacionados.** The collection reports 3,138 accessions deposited in alcohol lots and permanent-mount slides, with database fields for host, locality, provenance and references. The 3,138 figure remains a mixed accession count, not a slide count.

## Screened but excluded / held out

### Brazil

- **IOC Entomological Collection / 2008 collection-recovery article.** The collection includes specimens mounted in Canada balsam, but the article and collection are materially mixed and primarily organized around the entomological collection as a whole. Held outside this slide bibliography until a dedicated slide subset or object study is closed.
- Broad anatomy/pathology papers that only report routine histological slide preparation were excluded.

### Mexico

- **Colección Nacional de Ácaros (CNAC).** The institutional page explicitly mentions microscopic slides as one preservation form, but this pass retained the more clearly preparation-structured CNHE and held CNAC for a possible dedicated catalogue/count.
- **Rafael Lamothe Argumedo, *Manual de técnicas para preparar y estudiar los parásitos de animales silvestres* (1997).** Bibliographic records verify the 43-page manual, but the accessible records do not establish how much of the book is specifically about mounted microscope preparations. Held out rather than admitted from title alone.

### Uruguay

- Udelar virtual-microscope pages acknowledge physical teaching preparations but are organized around digital whole-slide access. Excluded under the standing digital-whole-slide rule unless a separate physical-collection record is found.

### Chile

- Search results for `láminas anatómicas` referred to anatomical teaching plates/canvases, not microscope slides. This is a Spanish false-friend and was explicitly excluded.

### General Latin American collection literature

- Broad museum/division histories were excluded when microscopic preparations appeared only as one minor preservation mode without a dedicated collection, count, catalogue, conservation problem or preparation-centred argument.
- The existing bibliography already contains Sutton de Licitra & Damborenea's 2000 catalogue of the Museo de La Plata helminth microscopic preparations; it was not duplicated.

## Analytical result

This pass makes several distinct Latin American slide regimes visible without collapsing them:

- campaign diagnosis as a case-linked glass-slide archive (CFA);
- research histotechnology as a registered slide/block/publication system (CDEPAT);
- pathology slides as institutional heritage requiring recovery and digitization (CSAP, FMUSP);
- anatomical/palynological reference slide collections attached to herbaria and xylotheques (RBw, SPF);
- permanent mounting as a materials and cost problem (Paiva et al.);
- slide preparation as reference-collection methodology (Quadros & Monteiro-Filho);
- micropaleontological/paleopalynological preparations as national research collections (BA Pal, CTES-PMP, Museo de La Plata);
- permanent preparations as teaching kits (Curioscopio);
- helminth slides as curated biodiversity repositories (CNHE, UNMSM).

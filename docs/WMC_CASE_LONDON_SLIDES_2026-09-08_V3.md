# London case v3 — WMC object culture, Cooke, museum labour, and slide circulation

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Supersedes for current analysis:** `WMC_CASE_LONDON_SLIDES_2026-09-08_V2.md`.

## 1. Revised claim

London now supports three distinct mechanisms by which Working Men's College education entered material science:

1. **internal object culture** — a College Museum, natural-history collecting and later microscope teaching;
2. **institutional reproduction** — Cooke/former pupils -> Society of Amateur Botanists -> Quekett slide collection/loan/exchange;
3. **occupational material mobility** — WMC students such as Thomas Davies and Harry Govier Seeley combine general/museum education with scientific workplace careers.

These mechanisms overlap but should not be collapsed.

## 2. WMC object culture precedes Cooke's microscope class

Marcella Pellegrino Sutcliffe's study records that the **College Museum was founded in 1856** through contributions by students and teachers and contained fossils, minerals, shells and antiquities. Papers and scientific objects were presented and country walks complemented natural-history classes.

Source:
- `The origins of the 'two cultures' debate in the adult education movement`: public/repository version and indexed text.

The museum establishes object handling and collective custody within the College before the 1861 physiological-botany course. It does not by itself establish microscope-slide holdings.

## 3. Seeley makes the museum a labour-training edge

Harry Govier Seeley studied English and mathematics at WMC in the late 1850s and became **secretary to the College museum**. He supported himself by copying documents in the British Museum library, where Samuel Pickworth Woodward encouraged geology. In 1859 he entered Sidney Sussex College and was soon employed by Adam Sedgwick as assistant in the Woodwardian Museum, cataloguing fossils and lecturing.

Authority:
- Science Museum Group person record: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap28054/seeley-harry-govier

The chain is unusually concrete:

```text
WMC study
 -> WMC museum secretary
 -> British Museum library/document work
 -> Cambridge Woodwardian Museum assistant
 -> fossil cataloguing / collection work
```

The moving competence is not only `knowledge of geology`; it includes museum organization and documentary/object handling.

## 4. Cooke places microscopy in WMC botany

Nathan E. C. Smith documents M. C. Cooke's 1861 WMC class in Physiological Botany. Cooke explicitly emphasized microscope use in his teaching plan.

Source:
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00033790.2023.2181398

The direct edge is:

`WMC -> Cooke -> microscope pedagogy`.

Physical slides/preparations used in the class remain unidentified.

## 5. Grugeon gives pupil-to-teacher continuity, but slide provenance remains open

Smith identifies **Alfred Grugeon** as one of Cooke's former WMC pupils with whom he remained in contact. A WMC programme for 1866 lists Grugeon as Botany teacher under Physical Sciences. Later botanical sources also identify him as a London botanical lecturer/locality contributor.

This closes:

`Cooke pupil -> WMC botany teacher`.

The public-web pass has not yet located a specific Quekett slide, accession or donation attributable to Grugeon. Do not promote him into the slide ledger without that object-level evidence.

## 6. Former pupils -> Society of Amateur Botanists -> Quekett

The Society of Amateur Botanists was formed in 1862 by Cooke and former WMC pupils around mutual assistance, excursions, specimen interchange, papers, library, herbarium and museum.

Quekett's own history records that this association was the organizational blueprint for the Quekett Microscopical Club in 1865. Cooke, Thomas Ketteringham and Witham Bywater were already meeting weekly with microscopes, examining objects and discussing specimens.

Sources:
- https://www.quekett.org/about/who/history
- Smith 2023 above.

By 1873 the Quekett general collection held **more than 2,000 preparations**, with member borrowing and slide exchange.

This gives a secure institutional slide economy but not yet WMC provenance for individual preparations.

## 7. Ketteringham shows the craft ideology adjacent to the Quekett founders

Thomas Ketteringham's appendix on `The Preparation and Mounting of Objects`, published in editions of Edwin Lankester's *Half-Hours with the Microscope*, explicitly argues that the beginner should learn to prepare specimens for himself. The text treats transparency, permanent mounting, preservation and repeated exhibition as advantages of practical preparation skill.

Public scan:
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Half-hours_with_the_microscope_-_a_popular_guide_to_the_use_of_the_microscope_as_a_means_of_amusement_and_instruction_%28IA_halfhourswithmic00lank_1%29.pdf

Ketteringham was one of the small weekly microscope circle with Cooke and Bywater immediately before Quekett's formation.

This does not make Ketteringham a WMC pupil unless separately sourced. It does show that **self-preparation craft was embedded in the immediate Quekett founding milieu**.

## 8. Thomas Davies (1837–1892): WMC education beside workplace thin-section apprenticeship

The strongest named worker case is British Museum mineralogist Thomas Davies.

Fletcher's obituary states:
- Davies entered the British Museum Mineralogy Department in 1858 under Maskelyne;
- Maskelyne, while studying meteorite thin sections, initiated Davies into microscopic recognition of rock-forming minerals;
- Davies became an exceptional practical petrologist;
- Davies attended WMC evening classes and acquired French and German.

Source:
- L. Fletcher, *Mineralogical Magazine* 10 (1893), 161–164: https://rruff.info/doclib/MinMag/Volume_10/10-46-161.pdf

This produces a `dual curriculum` rather than a WMC microscopy class:

```text
WMC -> language/general education
British Museum -> thin-section technical apprenticeship
```

Both meet in one occupational trajectory.

## 9. Davies's object labour is archive-reconstructable

NHM Archives preserves Davies's papers and official diaries. Key targets include:

- `DF/MIN/20/2/1–12` — official diaries recording daily Museum duties;
- `DF/MIN/20/2/13` — chemical/mineralogical notebook and incoming collections;
- `DF/MIN/20/2/14` — minerals removed to laboratory for analysis;
- `DF/MIN/10/1/6` — Davies's list of meteorites in British Museum, 1860–62;
- adjacent `DF/MIN/10/1/5–8` meteorite lists and exchange/purchase material.

NHM authority:
- https://www.nhm.ac.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=PX1373&src=CalmView.Persons

A British Museum history states that in 1861 a microscope with graduated revolving stage and eyepiece goniometer was constructed under Maskelyne's direction for polarized-light examination of meteorite thin sections, in a department constrained by lack of chemical laboratory facilities.

Thus Davies can potentially be reconstructed day-by-day as an **object worker** rather than known only from retrospective reputation.

## 10. Critical identity correction: two Thomas Davieses

The 1863 *Preparation and Mounting of Microscopic Objects* was written by **Thomas Davies of Warrington (c.1831–1876)**, not the British Museum mineralogist.

The Warrington Davies was active in the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society's Microscopical Section and worked experimentally with crystal growth on glass slides, polarized light and practical preparation.

Sources:
- Project Gutenberg authority: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60225
- disambiguation/evidence synthesis: https://microscopist.net/DaviesT.html

This correction prevents a false chain:

`WMC student -> Maskelyne assistant -> famous mounting-manual author`.

That chain is not historically valid.

## 11. London WMC archive can now test names directly

London Metropolitan Archives collection `LMA/4535` preserves:

- Council Minutes from 1854;
- Directors Minutes 1857–78;
- College ledgers 1858–1905;
- **lists of students 1854–73**, with occupations in the earliest list;
- student-club records;
- correspondence and scrapbooks.

Catalogue:
- https://atom.aim25.com/index.php/working-mens-college
- https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/c/F157579

This permits a bounded prosopographical pass rather than inference from obituaries:

1. locate Thomas Davies;
2. locate Harry Govier Seeley;
3. locate Alfred Grugeon;
4. extract occupations, addresses, class attendance and dates;
5. reconstruct Cooke's 1861 pupil cohort;
6. crosswalk to Society of Amateur Botanists / Quekett membership and slide transactions.

## 12. The unresolved slide-provenance edge

Despite the stronger social/material reconstruction, the most valuable missing closure remains:

> **named WMC person -> named Quekett preparation / donation / loan / exchange**.

The public-web pass did not close this. The absence is now localized to two record layers:

- WMC class/student membership identification;
- Quekett early accession/donation/slide catalogues.

This is a good archival gap because both sides of the join have surviving institutional archives.

## 13. London working model

The London case now looks like:

```text
                 WMC museum / object culture
                       |
       +---------------+----------------+
       |                                |
Cooke microscope pedagogy          general education
       |                                |
former pupils                      Davies / Seeley
       |                                |
amateur botanical society         museum workplaces
       |                                |
Quekett slide economy              technical / curatorial expertise
```

The College's importance is therefore not restricted to direct science teaching. It supplies a social and educational interface through which material competence can be reproduced in clubs or combined with occupational apprenticeship elsewhere.

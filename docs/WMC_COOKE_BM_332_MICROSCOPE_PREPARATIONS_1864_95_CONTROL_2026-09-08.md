# M. C. Cooke: 332 British Museum microscope preparations, purchased 1864–1895

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Status:** institutional acquisition corpus closed at 1904 retrospective level; individual accessions, object identities and maker attribution remain open.

## Question

Can Mordecai Cubitt Cooke — the 1861 Working Men's College Physiological Botany teacher whose course explicitly integrated the microscope — be connected to a named institutional corpus of microscope preparations rather than only to publications, club formation, or general microscopical practice?

Yes, at British Museum (Natural History) collection-history level.

## 1. Official 1904 British Museum collection history

The British Museum (Natural History), *The History of the Collections Contained in the Natural History Departments of the British Museum*, vol. I (London: printed by order of the Trustees, 1904), Botany section, p. 141, gives the following entry under **Cooke (Mordecai Cubitt)**:

- *Fungi Britannici exsiccati* (1865–72), 700 specimens;
- 850 British and 300 exotic fungi;
- **332 “preparations for the microscope”**;
- the entry states that this material was **purchased 1864–95**;
- a second edition of *Fungi Britannici*, 600 specimens (1875–77), formed part of C. E. Broome's herbarium.

Source: British Museum (Natural History), *History of the Collections*, vol. I (1904), p. 141, Botany.

**Evidence level:** `A_INSTITUTIONAL_RETROSPECTIVE`.

The source is an official Museum collection history published after the acquisition period. It is not the contemporaneous accession register itself.

## 2. Claim ceiling

The 1904 wording securely establishes a **Cooke-associated British Museum corpus of 332 preparations for the microscope acquired by purchase within the 1864–95 acquisition span**.

It does **not** by itself establish that:

- Cooke personally mounted all 332 preparations;
- all 332 were standard glass microscope slides;
- all 332 were fungi, despite their placement in a predominantly fungal Cooke entry;
- the preparations were all acquired in one transaction;
- 1864 and 1895 are the first and last dates specifically for the microscope preparations rather than the acquisition span of the grouped Cooke material;
- any of these preparations had been used in Cooke's 1861 WMC class;
- any preparation moved from WMC or the Society of Amateur Botanists into the Museum.

Use the source for **institutional acquisition/custody of a substantial Cooke-associated microscope-preparation corpus**, not for personal manufacture or WMC provenance.

## 3. Why this is stronger than forcing a Quekett donation

A bounded Quekett pass did not produce a secure direct `Cooke -> slide donation` edge.

One early Quekett report records Cooke describing a large collection of mounted spider-palpus preparations belonging to a **medical friend**. The friend, while mounting a set, offered to present them to the Club cabinet. The grammar assigns the preparation and intended donation to the unnamed friend; Cooke is the mediator/reporter. It must not be rewritten as a Cooke slide donation.

Other early Quekett donation lists recovered in the bounded pass show Cooke donating books, while microscope slides in the same lists are attributed to other donors.

Therefore:

```text
Cooke -> QMC named slide donation = OPEN
Cooke -> mediation of another preparer's QMC slide gift = SECURE AT PRINTED-MEETING LEVEL
Cooke -> BM(NH) corpus of 332 microscope preparations purchased 1864–95 = SECURE AT OFFICIAL 1904 COLLECTION-HISTORY LEVEL
```

The British Museum corpus is the stronger object-history edge because it names both a quantity and an institutional acquisition relation without requiring donor/maker inference.

## 4. Contemporary accession layer survives

The Natural History Museum Archives catalogue identifies:

`DF/BOT/413` — **Botany Accessions, Acquisition and Exchange Records**.

The series description states that:

- accessions for **1829–1880** are recorded on catalogue slips (`DF413/1–11`);
- these slips record new collection additions and sometimes non-specimen material;
- from **1880** the slips are replaced by accession registers (`DF413/15–26` for the surviving register sequence described by the catalogue);
- the series is open;
- formal accession registration at the British Museum followed the 1836 Select Committee recommendation that each departmental head register new acquisitions in detail.

Source: NHM Archives catalogue, `DF/BOT/413`.

**Evidence level:** `A_ARCHIVE_ROUTE` for the existence and structure of the contemporaneous accession series.

Public indexing does not currently resolve `DF413/1–11` or `DF413/15–26` into a trustworthy Cooke-specific child reference or a sufficiently narrow 1864–95 sub-range. Do not invent one.

## 5. Minimal archive gate

The next question should be one identification request, not a thirty-one-year accession sweep:

> The official 1904 Botany collection history records, under Mordecai Cubitt Cooke, **332 “preparations for the microscope,” purchased 1864–95**. Can this corpus be matched to an entry or entries in the Botany accession records (`DF/BOT/413`)? If so, could you provide only the relevant accession date(s) and slip/register reference(s)?

If **YES**:

1. request only the identified accession entry/entries;
2. record exact object wording, quantity, date, seller/source, price if present, and accession reference;
3. only then ask whether surviving specimen/slide labels or catalogue numbers can be matched.

If **NO**, or if locating the entries would require broad paid research across the entire 1864–95 sequence, stop. Preserve the 1904 official collection-history claim without escalating into an open-ended acquisition hunt.

## 6. Effect on the WMC material-practice argument

The Cooke trajectory can now be stated more concretely while preserving provenance boundaries:

```text
1861 WMC Physiological Botany
-> microscope explicitly integrated into adult teaching

1862 Society of Amateur Botanists
-> excursions / specimen interchange / herbarium / museum

1865 Quekett Microscopical Club
-> microscopy-centered mutual-instruction institution

1864–95 BM(NH) acquisition span
-> 332 Cooke-associated "preparations for the microscope" purchased into institutional custody
```

This does not prove that a WMC class preparation entered the British Museum. It shows that the same actor who introduced microscope use into WMC Physiological Botany also generated or supplied a sufficiently substantial microscope-preparation corpus to be recorded quantitatively in the Museum's official collection history.

The next historically meaningful question is therefore not whether Cooke "used slides" in general. It is whether the accession records can identify **what those 332 preparations were, how they were acquired, and whether Cooke was seller, preparer, compiler, or another kind of source**.

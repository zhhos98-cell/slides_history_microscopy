## 3. Institutional science context

The University of Sheffield collection history states that by 1844 the College offered about fifty classes per week to more than 300 students. Subjects included, among others:

- Science;
- Natural History;
- Philosophy;
- Logic and Algebra;
- Geography;
- literature/languages and other general subjects.

Bayley also published textbooks for College classes.

This establishes a strong **institutional science context** before the Journal began.

### Governing boundary

`INSTITUTIONAL_SCIENCE != SERIAL_SCIENCE`

The presence of Science and Natural History in the curriculum does **not** establish that the Journal printed science/natural-history articles, notices, or correspondence.

Until an issue is read, use:

`SCIENCE_IN_JOURNAL = CONTENT_PENDING`

This is an important negative-discipline control for the whole RSVP project: do not transfer an institution's known activities into its periodical merely because the periodical was its organ.

## 4. Why Sheffield is a high-value comparison

The Sheffield title predates London Working Men's College and its 1859 Magazine.

Current comparison:

### Sheffield, 1846–47

`People's College -> College-printed monthly -> popular education`

### London, 1859–61 (leading run model)

`Working Men's College -> Macmillan-published monthly -> institutional orientation / reading guidance / educational debate`

### Halifax, 1866–67

`Literary & Scientific Society / WMC ecology -> manuscript magazine -> twopenny printed Circulator -> serialized local geology/natural history`

The comparison therefore allows institutional serials to differ by:

- printer/publisher location;
- authorship/governance;
- audience;
- scientific content;
- relation to manuscript sociability;
- relation to external commercial publishing;
- duration and financial sustainability.

Do not narrate these as a linear evolutionary sequence without direct transmission evidence.

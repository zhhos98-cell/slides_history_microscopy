## 1. Bibliographic identity

Title: *The Circulator: A Magazine of Literature, Science & Art*  
Corporate attribution: Haley Hill Literary and Scientific Society  
Place: Halifax  
Run: 1866–1867  
Publisher/imprint in bibliographical descriptions: R. Leyland & Son  
Price: contemporary/local historical descriptions give **2d. per number / twopenny monthly**.

Biodiversity Heritage Library currently indexes the title as:

- *The Circulator : a Magazine of Literature, Science & Art*;
- by Haley Hill Literary and Scientific Society;
- volume `1866-1867`;
- holding institution: **Natural History Museum Library, London**.

This makes the title a directly identifiable surviving natural-history/science periodical object rather than a lost local-magazine lead.

## 2. Manuscript -> print prehistory

Local bibliographical history states:

`Haley Hill Working Men's College / scientific society -> handwritten magazine -> printed Circulator in 1866`

The handwritten magazine reportedly circulated until 1866; *The Circulator* then began to be printed and survived for roughly two years.

This relation remains one of the strongest comparative cases in the RSVP corpus because it permits a medium transition to be tested rather than assumed:

`internal manuscript circulation -> cheap printed serial -> potentially wider local/regional scientific readership`

Open questions:

- was the manuscript magazine formally the organ of the same Literary and Scientific Society?
- which contributors/genres survive the transition?
- did print enlarge geographic circulation or exchange?
- did print stabilize authorship, pagination, issue sequence or citation practices?

## 3. Historical witness to the bound volume

A 1915 retrospective in *The Naturalist* reports having seen a volume of *The Circulator*.

The witness records:

- Haley Hill Literary and Scientific Society formed in 1861;
- *The Circulator* published in 1866–67;
- volume seen bore Halifax imprint and date 1867;
- **190 pages**;
- **8vo** format;
- the witness could not determine from that bound state exactly how frequently it appeared or how many pages were issued at a time;
- papers covered local natural history and geology as well as poetry, music and other subjects;
- several contributions were considered among the earliest records of the district;
- up to **p.117** there were **six continued articles on geology by `J. S[pencer]`**;
- the witness inferred that probably about **twelve parts** had been issued in all.

### Material consequence

This is a critical warning against reconstructing original serial form from the bound volume:

`bound 190-page volume != securely reconstructed issue sequence`

The 1915 observer explicitly could not tell issue frequency/page packet size from the bound object. Therefore original wrappers, issue headings, pagination breaks and advertisements become genuine material-history evidence, not decorative bibliography.

## 4. James Spencer geology sequence

A biographical reconstruction of James Spencer identifies a substantial serial geology programme in the twopenny *Circulator*:

1. first two numbers — introduction to **Popular Geology**;
2. two papers on the **strata of Halifax**;
3. article(s) on the **coal pits at Low Moor**;
4. article on the **geology of Ingleborough**.

The same reconstruction then follows Spencer into the Manchester Geological Society and the *Yorkshire Naturalist*.

This creates a testable publication/career chain:

`local working-men/scientific-society serial -> repeated geology instruction/local observation -> regional geological society / later natural-history publication`

Do not call this a proved causal career pipeline; treat it as a serial-to-specialist publication sequence to be page-closed.

## 5. Content range

Jarndyce's periodicals catalogue describes the surviving volume as containing a wide range including:

- `Mollusks found in the neighbourhood of Halifax`;
- geology;
- natural history;
- science;
- weather;
- effects of tobacco;
- poetry;
- local-dialect Christmas story;
- literary material including Macaulay.

This matters because the science is embedded in a mixed literary/scientific magazine rather than isolated in a specialist scientific journal.

## 7. Evidence conflicts / cautions

### Society naming

Reliable metadata and local history identify **Haley Hill Literary and Scientific Society**. Some OCR/catalogue renderings appear as `Halifax Literary and Scientific Society`. Treat `Haley Hill` as the leading controlled corporate attribution but page-check the title page before publication-level transcription.

### Frequency

Local descriptions call it a twopenny monthly, while the 1915 observer says the bound volume itself did not reveal frequency clearly. Preserve both levels:

- `FREQUENCY_BIBLIOGRAPHIC = monthly`;
- `FREQUENCY_VISIBLE_FROM_BOUND_VOLUME = unresolved to 1915 observer`.

### Price

Two independent descriptions support **2d./twopenny**. A modern local webpage renders `2/-`, likely a transcription error; do not use that value without primary price evidence.

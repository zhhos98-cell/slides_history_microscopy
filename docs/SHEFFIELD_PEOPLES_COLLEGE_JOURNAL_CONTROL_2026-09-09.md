## 4. Institutional context relevant to periodical analysis

The People's College archive describes a broad curriculum by the mid-1840s including:

- reading, writing, arithmetic;
- geography;
- moral knowledge;
- English and general history;
- English composition;
- **science**;
- logic and algebra;
- philosophy;
- **natural history**;
- literature;
- Latin and Greek.

By 1844 the College reportedly offered around fifty classes per week to more than 300 students.

This makes science/natural-history content a legitimate extraction target, but curriculum presence must not be converted into Journal content without issue evidence.

## 5. Periodical function currently closed

### `SELF_PUB`

The College itself maintained a monthly periodical directed toward popular education.

### `POPULAR_ED_ARGUMENT`

The title was not merely an administrative bulletin; its stated purpose was to advance the cause of popular education.

### `INSTITUTION_PRINT_LAB`

DNB states the Journal was printed at the College. If primary copies confirm College imprint / printing arrangements, the serial is especially useful for asking how an adult-education institution operated not only as a teaching site but as a site of print production.

## 6. Comparison questions

### Against London WMC Magazine

- Sheffield precedes London by more than a decade;
- Sheffield title is explicitly framed around `popular education`;
- London later combines orientation, reading guidance, governance argument and institutional self-definition;
- London uses Macmillan as metropolitan publisher, whereas Sheffield appears more directly institutionally printed.

### Against Halifax *Circulator*

- Sheffield is explicitly College-led / Bayley-edited;
- Halifax emerges from a Literary and Scientific Society and manuscript-magazine tradition;
- Halifax science/natural history content is already partially reconstructable through later descriptions;
- Sheffield scientific content remains open pending issue inspection.

These differences reinforce the need to study `institutional serial` as a family of forms rather than one genre.

# Davies–Maskelyne object closure — reading-room checklist v1

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Purpose:** convert the current web/catlogue reconnaissance into a bounded archival workflow. This checklist is ordered by expected evidential yield, not by archive hierarchy.

## A. Natural History Museum Archives — formation period, 1858–63

### A1. `DF/MIN/10/1/6` — Thomas Davies, list of meteorites, 1860–62

**Priority:** P0

Why first: this is the clearest contemporaneous document authored by Davies during the period in which Maskelyne was training him in microscopic mineral determination.

Photograph / extract:

- cover/title/dates;
- every page with Davies handwriting;
- accession/specimen numbers;
- locality, donor, purchase, exchange and collector fields;
- corrections, cancellations and later hands;
- any words such as `section`, `slice`, `thin`, `microscope`, `transparent`, `crystal`, `polar`, `mount`, `glass`;
- any cross-reference to another catalogue/register;
- page/order structure.

Goal:

`Davies hand -> named meteorite/specimen -> classification/documentation -> possible thin-section relation`.

### A2. `DF/MIN/4/61` — Mineralogy reports to Trustees, 1861

**Priority:** P0

Catalogue closes this as a 1861 bundle of draft progress reports, memoranda and official letters, including Maskelyne's printed paper on observation/collection of aerolites. The parent `DF/MIN/4` series explicitly covers equipment, staffing, accommodation and specimen acquisition.

Search terms/concepts:

`microscope | optical | apparatus | instrument | goniometer | polarising | section | aerolite | meteorite | fire | blowpipe | laboratory | Davies | assistant | expense | maker`.

Goal: find a contemporaneous administrative trace for the microscope/thin-section programme, rather than relying only on the 1904/1911 retrospective histories.

### A3. `DF/MIN/5/59` — Trustees authorisations, 1861

**Priority:** P0

This is the receiving/authorising side of the administrative relation. The parent series includes purchases, staff matters and accommodation. The child catalogue gives only the year, so **no microscope entry is presumed**.

Crosswalk every relevant memorandum to `DF/MIN/4/61` by date/topic. Preserve:

- Standing Committee date;
- action authorised/refused;
- amount if any;
- named maker/supplier;
- named officer/assistant;
- wording for equipment or accommodation;
- marginal annotations/file numbers.

Ideal closure:

`Maskelyne request/report -> Trustees decision -> apparatus/accommodation action`.

### A4. `DF/MIN/15/1` — Keeper's internal correspondence, 1857–61

**Priority:** P0

The parent series explicitly covers staffing, finance/accounts, receipt of collections, exhibits, security, heating/lighting and departmental procedure, and is related to `DF4/DF5`.

This file is especially valuable because it may document **execution inside the department** after a Trustee decision.

Search:

`Davies | microscope | instrument | section | glass | polar | goniometer | laboratory | fire | light | lamp | maker | bill | invoice | permission | assistant`.

Goal:

```text
DF4 request/report
 -> DF5 authorisation
 -> DF15 internal execution
```

### A5. `DF/MIN/13/2` — accommodation evidence, 1860–62

## B. British Museum Trustees / final-report layer

The NHM `DF4` series consists of drafts/duplicates and notes; its catalogue states that **final copies of early reports are held in the archives of the British Museum**.

A published historical study on the Melbourne meteorite exchange gives exact contemporaneous Standing Committee references:

- Standing Committee Minutes, vol. XXIX, **18 May 1861, p. 9963**;
- **25 May 1861, p. 9970**;
- for 1862 correspondence/decisions: Officers' Reports vol. 69 (Aug–Dec 1862), Maskelyne to Trustees **5 Nov 1862**, Owen to Trustees **6 Nov 1862**;
- Standing Committee Minutes vol. XXIX, pp. **10225–26** and **10332**.

These references are securely tied to the meteorite acquisition/exchange administrative world, but **not yet to the custom microscope**. Use them to reconstruct the expanding object stream against which optical classification developed.

Photograph surrounding entries, not only cited lines, to preserve agenda/order and adjacent departmental business.

## C. Natural History Museum — late object crosswalk, 1875–76

### C1. `DF/MIN/10/3/36` — Clifton Ward thin-section list, 1876

**Priority:** P0 for late-career object closure

Catalogue explicitly identifies thin sections from Lake District and Wales sent to Maskelyne in 1876, with accession-number span `85498–86211`.

Do not equate the numerical span with slide count without inspection.

Capture:

- every section/material entry;
- accession number;

- rock/mineral name;
- number of preparations per accession if stated;
- physical format language (`section`, `slice`, `slide`, etc.);
- sender/preparer wording;
- dates;
- annotations in Davies/Maskelyne/other hands.

### C2. `DF/MIN/20/2/1` — Davies official diary, Oct 1875–Jan 1877

**Priority:** P0 paired with C1

Crosswalk the Clifton Ward intake day-by-day.

Search around every relevant date for:

`Ward | sections | slices | Wales | Lake | Cumberland | microscope | examined | mounted | labelled | registered | returned | cabinet`.

Goal:

`named incoming thin section -> Davies daily operation -> register/cabinet outcome`.

This is the cleanest present opportunity to turn Davies from a retrospective biography into an object-level worker.

## H. The closure test

The case becomes genuinely object-level if at least one of these is achieved:

1. **Formation closure:** 1861 report/authorisation/internal correspondence names the microscope/apparatus or section work and can be cross-linked to Davies's contemporaneous meteorite list.
2. **Career closure:** WMC student record securely identifies BM Davies and dates his French/German education against his museum apprenticeship.
3. **Lecture closure:** WMC minutes/programme identifies a Maskelyne lecture by date/topic.
4. **Late object closure:** a Clifton Ward section can be cross-linked from list/accession to Davies diary and later custody.
5. **Survival closure:** 1951 donation inventory identifies the `slides` sufficiently to connect one or more physical preparations to Maskelyne's mineralogical work.

Until one of these closes, retain the current formulation: **composite education + museum thin-section labour, with strong but still partly uncoupled institutional/object traces**.

## Additional closures

### Cole Volume I surviving material counterpart

The 2025 Toovey's lot substantially closes the surviving-object side of the Cole case. The lot contains 51 Arthur Cole-labelled slides plus one other slide in a numbered/indexed 52-position cabinet. The photographed opening positions reproduce the diagnostic order of *Studies in Microscopical Science* Volume I: Yellow Fibro-Cartilage, Copper Beech, Human Bone, Umbrella Plant, Human Skin, Pikrite, Cat Spinal Cord, Bracken Fern, Human Liver and *Fucus vesiculosus*. Combined with the direct-primary Volume I structure, this gives an A++ / near-S identification of the lot as a surviving material Volume I set.

The remaining questions are afterlife questions rather than publication-architecture gaps: whether the mahogany cabinet is one of the subscriber cabinets promised in the 1882 prospectus, who the original subscriber was, and what the auctioneer's “one other slide” represents. Full 52-label transcription is therefore optional unless the writing turns specifically to provenance or replacement.

### Quekett / Oxford Cole route demoted

The Quekett archive has the right evidentiary infrastructure: more than 20,000 slides, partial label transcription and tray photography. Brian Davidson's later specialist account also explicitly describes Volume I as based on a set of 52 Martin Cole slides paired with the book. Oxford separately preserves RMS manuscript collections and the Society's internal archive, including slide-catalogue genres.

This no longer justifies a broad archive request. Cole's publication architecture and a surviving material set are already closed strongly enough. A Quekett/Oxford search should now happen only if an institutional-afterlife paragraph requires an actual label/database match. Archive capability is established; Cole-specific institutional survival remains open and nonessential.

### Balfour publication-side optical harvest closed

The complete Memorial Edition volume-I plate-caption pass is now treated as closed at designation/publication level. Printed optical vocabulary includes `aa`, `a`, `A`, `B`, `C/c`, `CC`, `D`, `DD`, `E`, `F`, numbered/immersion forms and camera references. Secure designation-level matches to the surviving Whipple objective set are `aa`, `a`, `A`, `B`, `C/c` and `E`. The earlier first-pass negative for `E` is superseded: Plates 24–25 and the posthumous Plate 52 explicitly use Zeiss E.

The remaining question is much narrower and materially harder: which exact surviving lens, if any, produced a given figure. Only exact Zeiss serial/dispatch evidence for 573, 1295, 710, 780 and 542, followed by taxon/stage matching against surviving Balfour sections, can move that claim. Generic designation agreement is already enough to reconstruct the optical repertoire and should not be pushed into physical identity.

### Minot / Harvard Embryological Collection demoted to control case

The HEC public metadata already establishes the methodological point for which it was introduced: historical and modern relations can survive recataloguing because an object can retain an old HEC number, a current MCZ number, within-series position, technique, preparator, publication citation, paper card/drawing/ledger relation and conservation state. This is an S-level control architecture for distinguishing anatomical seriality from Cole-style material publication.

The two sampled public records are early twentieth century, so they should not be turned into nineteenth-century evidence. A broad pre-1900 HEC harvest would now be infrastructure-building by another name. It is parked unless a specific UK-linked Minot argument requires one or two nineteenth-century object-level comparators.

# Bourgogne / NMAH / West Point bridge — 2026-08-10

Machine-readable authority: `data/analysis/bourgogne_nmah_west_point_accession_bridge_v1.json`.

The Bourgogne target produced a stronger result than the earlier surviving-object note. NMAH record `nmah_723375` preserves a **two-box microscope-slide set**, ID `MG.217544.01`, catalog numbers `217544.01` and `M-09791`, accession **217544**. NMAH states that the `BOURGOGNE` inscription appears on each slide and is probably that of Joseph Bourgogne.

## New institutional bridge

Accession **217544** is not an isolated object number. Other NMAH records under the same accession explicitly identify a large scientific-apparatus transfer from the **U.S. Military Academy at West Point**. The Grunow spherometer `MA.315739` states that it belonged to the Department of Physics and Chemistry at West Point and was **transferred to the Smithsonian in 1958**. The Koenig tonometer `PH.315716` independently says that its West Point components were transferred to the Smithsonian in 1958. Numerous other objects in the same accession carry U.S. Military Academy credit lines or explicit West Point use histories.

The Bourgogne slide record itself currently omits a credit line, so this remains an **accession-level provenance closure**, not yet an item-level transfer statement. The evidentiary grade is **A++ / S-candidate** for West Point provenance pending the accession file or original object card.

## M-09791 clue

The Bourgogne set carries historical catalog number **M-09791**. NMAH also preserves a ca. 1890 R. & J. Beck Improved National Monocular Microscope under ID `MG.M-09791`, accession 217544; its record explicitly states that the microscope came from the U.S. Military Academy. This is too exact to ignore as an administrative clue. It may mean that the two Bourgogne boxes were once catalogued as part of, alongside, or under the same historical microscope grouping.

For now this is an **A++ clue**, not an accessory claim. The public database does not state that the Bourgogne slides arrived with the Beck microscope, were stored in its case, were bought at the same time, or were used exclusively with it. The accession file / catalog card is the correct source for that question.

## 1862 catalogue side

The project already had Joseph Bourgogne's 1862 `Prix Courant des Préparations Microscopiques` as an S-priority catalogue source. A public PDF locator is now confirmed on *Histoire du microscope*; its viewer points to a file named `BOURGOGNE-catalogue1862-BRIAN.pdf`. The binary was not ingested in this pass, so row-level claims remain closed off.

Secondary work by Brian Stevenson, drawing on Pierre Girodet's 2001 translation, confirms why the catalogue is worth pairing with the NMAH boxes: the 1862 list distinguished three quality levels, multiple slide formats, a larger format for whole large specimens, and a separate projection-microscope holder format. These are planning clues only until the original catalogue PDF is read directly.

## Revised chain

`Paris commercial preparation / graded catalogue -> Bourgogne-labelled two-box set -> probable West Point teaching-collection address -> Smithsonian transfer cluster, 1958 -> NMAH object record`

This is now a better comparator to Cole than first expected. Cole's publication identity survived through a fixed numbered cabinet. Bourgogne's commodity identity appears to have survived inside a later **institutional apparatus collection**, acquiring new addresses without losing the maker inscription.

# Charles Elcock / Whipple: workshop-to-market upgrade — 2026-08-11

## Result

Upgrade to **S-level workshop/product-system evidence**. The surviving Whipple archive closes an unusually complete chain: foraminiferal raw/collected material → preparation operations → public recognition → standardized commercial Type Slides → named dealer network → retained Type Slide drawers, working tools, stains, mounting materials, raw specimens, books and notes → modern museum accession.

## Surviving archive

Whipple accession **6601 / Wh.6601** is the Charles Elcock archive of microscope slides, preparatory tools and materials, book and archival material, dated 1872–1910. The Museum believes these were the slides and materials remaining in Elcock’s possession at death, later descending in the family before acquisition in 2015.

The decisive cabinet is **6601.1**: 24 custom-fitted drawers and 617 parts, apparently all Elcock-made foraminifera slides. Drawers 9–10 retain printed labels **“TYPE SLIDE, No. 1.”** and drawers 11–12 **“TYPE SLIDE, No. 2.”** Slides use black matrices, numbered grids and a mixture of printed and manuscript labels. **6601.2** preserves a mixed cabinet in which finished slides coexist with stains, tools, mounting supplies, raw/mountable substances and labelled packets of collected specimens. **6601.3** contains twelve trays of six foraminifera slides each, many retaining locality and/or depth information.

Official sources:
- Whipple Museum, “The Foraminifera Slides and Working Tools of Microscope Slide Maker Charles Elcock”
- Whipple Collections Online, object 15311 / accession 6601

## Project corpus crosswalk

The existing UK corpus supplies the market and process sides of the same system.

1. **Belfast Naturalists’ Field Club, April 1880 newspaper reports**: Elcock received prize no. 14 for a set of twenty-five microscopic slides. This is independent contemporaneous evidence for a judged slide set and public reputation.
2. **Journal of Microscopy and Natural Science / Postal Microscopical Society, 1885, record R49267**: advertisement for Elcock’s `TYPE SLIDES`, each containing **FIFTY SPECIES** with the species name visible in the field; `Type Slides No. 1 & No. 2` ready; **PRICE TWENTY SHILLINGS EACH**; further slides in preparation. Named outlets include Beck and Beck, Watson & Sons and Chas. Collins in London, E. Ward in Manchester and Alfred Allen in Bath.
3. **Microscopical News, 1883**: Elcock’s foraminifera practice is described from collection and raw sediment through washing, sieving, settling/floating and mounting. A Sutton Bridge silt slide contained 48 species; the material is explicitly characterized as difficult to manipulate.

The surviving drawer labels therefore match the historical commercial product names at the level of **Type Slide No. 1 / No. 2**. This is much stronger than a generic maker attribution.

## Interpretation

Cole preserves a publication after individual preparations can be replaced. Elcock preserves something different: **the workshop behind the commercial object**. Raw matter, preparative materials, tools, retained exemplars and standardized priced products remain within one accession. The Type Slide address did not merely describe a specimen. It made a complex arrangement reproducible, advertisable, priceable and distributable through a dealer network.

A compact formulation is:

> The commercial slide had a material prehistory. In Elcock’s surviving archive, the Type Slide still sits beside the substances, tools and operations from which such products were made.

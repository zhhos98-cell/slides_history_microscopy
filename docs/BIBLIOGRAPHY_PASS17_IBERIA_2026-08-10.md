# Bibliography pass 17 — Iberia

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: verified bibliography expansion only. The frozen 307 / 155 surviving-object survey remains closed and unchanged.

## Admission rule

Pass 17 keeps the existing **physical-slide first** threshold. Dedicated preparation methods, inventories with substantial microscopic-preparation holdings, bounded slide collections, material teaching systems and object/conservation studies are admitted. Broad microscopy catalogues, general museum inventories, projection systems, current retail listings and histories in which preparations are only incidental remain outside the bibliography.

## Spain

1. **Eduardo García Solá, `Inventario del material científico existente en el Departamento Micrográfico de la Facultad de Medicina de Granada` (1918).** UCM's reconstruction states that the inventory covered the laboratory apparatus and an extensive collection of microscopic preparations. No slide count is inferred from García Solá's earlier claims about collections.

2. **Miguel Pérez Gutiérrez and Álvaro de Miranda, `Preparaciones sistemáticas de diatomáceas...` (1918).** The Consello bibliography gives *Boletín de Pescas* 28: 399–425. The 1918 issue state is retained rather than a later retrospective 1919 dating.

3. **Ernesto Caballero y Bellido, `Técnica de las preparaciones sistemáticas` (1925).** A later, expanded state of the already admitted 1897 technique. The 1925 version, pp. 5–73, was issued through the JAE/Museo de Ciencias Naturales and includes a prologue by Santiago Ramón y Cajal.

4. **MNCN Caballero collection history (2015).** The museum records a donated core of 205 preparations made 1891–1927 and a separate group of 202 preparations found in 2014. They are not collapsed into a synthetic total.

5. **Montero Bastarreche and Diéguez Jiménez (1993).** A conservation/curation study of the Caballero diatom collection, pp. 309–321. Dialnet dates the volume 1993; a later MNCN bibliography gives 1992. The dedicated volume metadata is used and the discrepancy retained.

6. **Legado Cajal.** CSIC states that the histological microscopic preparations include 3,000 originals by Cajal. The more than 28,000 mixed scientific/personal assets in the whole legacy remain a separate namespace.

7. **Dr. Sigmund commercial slide in the Cajal Legacy.** A 26 × 76 mm glass preparation with a Stuttgart / Carl Zeiss commercial label and trilingual specimen description. Simurg says it was *possibly* used by Cajal for histology teaching; the attribution remains conditional.

8. **UCM historical Histoteca.** More than 800 preparations by Emilio Fernández Galiano survive from the former Chair of Micrographic Technique and Plant/Animal Histology; the department explicitly intends to correlate them with his publications and research.

9. **UPM wood-anatomy preparations.** Official pages currently expose more than 1,300, more than 1,400 and 1,500 microscopic-preparation states. These are retained as versioned live counts rather than normalized. The collection is linked to macroscopic wood blocks and international Index Xylariorum exchange.

10. **IES San Isidro.** Its historic Natural History cabinet explicitly retains a collection of nineteenth-century microscopic preparations. No count is published.

11. **MNCN non-insect arthropods.** The current page reports more than 12,000 microscopic preparations in dedicated cabinets. It separately mentions more than 10,000 recently located decapod preparations partly from the late nineteenth century; the relationship between that group and the current total is not defined as disjoint, so the figures are not added.

12. **University of Barcelona CeDocBiV.** A dedicated Catalan-language preparation collection page separately records more than 500 Suárez Cervera preparations, about 600 freshwater-microalgae preparations, pollen preparations and 24 Pando preparations derived from the Losa España herbarium. This adds Catalan as the nineteenth represented publication language.

13. **José Pedro Marín Murcia (2024).** The chapter on microscopy catalogues and modern biology teaching in Spain repeatedly uses physical slide collections as evidence: more than 800 Fernández Galiano preparations, a Sigmund collection of 25 cases with an initial inventory of 249 slides, and roughly one thousand Hensoldt preparations in the former University of Murcia biology laboratory.

## Portugal

14. **MHNC-UP Herbarium PO – Xylarium.** The xylarium has 2,737 wood specimens / 1,280 taxa and about one thousand corresponding microscope-slide specimens. The collection originates in the nineteenth century; slide and wood-specimen counts remain separate.

15. **Carlos Zimmermann, `Microscopia vegetal` (1902–06).** Four technical instalments in *Brotéria* describing instruments and methods for permanent microscopic preparations in experimental natural-history teaching.

16. **Carlos Zimmermann, `Diatomaceas. Como se colhem e preparam` (1910).** A direct Portuguese-language account of collecting and preparing diatoms for permanent microscopic observation.

17. **Romeiras and Leitão (2012).** A historical study of Zimmermann and the teaching of plant microscopy, providing the research context for the permanent-preparation techniques in the two primary Zimmermann entries.

## Screened but excluded / held out

- **García Solá, `Técnica histológica. Preparaciones microscópicas definitivas` (1878)** — a modern biography confirms the title and periodical, but the exact volume/pages were not closed in this pass. Held out rather than creating an incomplete citation.
- **Museo José Loustau, `Inventario 2019` (2021)** — the illustrated museum inventory reaches down to boxes of microscopic preparations, but the book is museum-wide rather than slide-centred. The stronger object evidence is instead captured through Marín Murcia's slide-focused 2024 chapter.
- **University of Valencia broad entomology/teaching collections** — microscopic preparations are present but are one preservation class inside broad zoological holdings and no bounded slide collection was closed.
- **Current Spanish/Portuguese retail catalogues** — physically real teaching slides, but ordinary current product listings were not admitted merely to create commercial continuity.
- **D. Carlos I plankton photomicrography** — the historical activity involved microscopic preparations, but the public record currently closes surviving photographs more securely than surviving slides.
- **Rodrigues de Freitas school museum** — preparations are part of a broad nineteenth-/twentieth-century teaching collection; no dedicated preparation inventory was closed.
- **MHNC-UP arthropods** — the public page only states a small nucleus of microscopic preparations within more than 510,000 mixed arthropod specimens.
- **Geological/petrographic thin sections** remain outside the bibliography pending the explicit medium-scope decision already recorded in pass 16.

## Priority 1 — Thomas Davies + Nevil Story-Maskelyne

### A. Working Men's College archive — `LMA/4535`

Collection summary confirms surviving student register, correspondence, scrapbooks and wider college records.

**Exact questions:**

1. Is **Thomas Davies (1837–1892), British Museum attendant/mineralogy assistant** present in the student register?
2. What are his enrolment and attendance dates?
3. Which French and German classes did he attend, and who taught them?
4. Is his occupation/address given, allowing secure disambiguation from other Thomas Davieses?
5. When did **N. S. Maskelyne** enter the Council, and how regularly did he attend?
6. Do Council minutes assign Maskelyne a science, museum, lecture, examination or advisory function?
7. Search correspondence and scrapbooks for `Maskelyne`, `British Museum`, `mineralogy`, `minerals`, `meteorite`, `microscope`, `section`, `slides`, `Davies`.
8. Does the College Museum receive British Museum or Maskelyne-linked material?

**Source route:** https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/c/F157579

**Closure standard:**

- Davies identity requires occupation/address or other distinguishing evidence.
- Maskelyne Council membership is already proven in 1866 programme; minutes should determine function, not re-prove membership.
- No inference of WMC microscopy teaching without explicit class/lecture evidence.

### B. NHM Archives — Story-Maskelyne / Davies

**Core fonds:** `DF/MIN/20/1/4`, Papers of Mervyn Herbert Nevil Story-Maskelyne.

The current catalogue says the donation included papers, minerals, laboratory equipment and **slides**, with donation letters at legacy ref `DF1/55`.

**Exact questions:**

1. What medium is meant by `slides`?
2. Are they meteorite/rock/mineral thin sections, microscope slides, photographic slides, lantern slides, or mixed?
3. Do surviving object labels/numbers connect them to Maskelyne publications or Davies's work?
4. Is Davies credited as preparer, identifier, cataloguer or custodian?
5. Can any section be tied to the 1861 instrument or 1869 meteorite paper?
6. Does the donation paperwork contain inventories, counts, makers, labels or cabinet information?

**Reference warning:** `DF1/55` is a legacy citation in the current fonds description. Ask NHM Archives for the current reference before ordering. Do not substitute `DF/MIN/1/55` automatically.

**Related exact files:**

- `DF/MIN/20/1/4/12` — `Physical properties of minerals - I. optical`, lecture notes with diagrams;
- `DF/MIN/20/1/4/13` — visitors to Maskelyne's crystallography lecture, 23 Nov 1874;
- `DF/MIN/20/1/4/15` — Viktor von Lang measurements/drawings from Museum specimens;
- meteorite manuscripts and Department registers via `DF/MIN/10` and staff papers.

**Source route:** https://www.nhm.ac.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=DF%2FMIN%2F20%2F1%2F4&src=CalmView.Catalog

## Priority 3 — WMC College Museum -> Harry Govier Seeley -> Cambridge

### London

Search WMC museum records for:

- responsibilities: accession, labelling, cataloguing, display, correspondence;
- fossils/minerals handled during his tenure;
- Samuel P. Woodward or British Museum links;
- whether the post was student-run and how museum officers were chosen.

### Cambridge

Search Woodwardian Museum / Sedgwick records for:

- Seeley's appointment in 1859;
- his cataloguing duties;
- lists/catalogues produced;
- correspondence discussing his prior experience;
- technical skills expected of assistant;
- any microscopic/petrographic work in Cambridge distinct from fossil cataloguing.

**Target closure:** not simply `WMC student became scientist`, but whether WMC museum work is explicitly recognized as preparation for later object labour.

## Priority 6 — Cambridge WMC material-pedagogy test

### A. Josiah Chater diaries

Read 1855–c.1863 systematically for:

`Working College | lecture | class | exhibition | museum | microscope | microscopic | slides | specimens | natural history | chemistry | apparatus | experiment | glass | mineral | botany | physiology`

Record every named fellow student/teacher and occupation.

### B. Cambridge Free Library custodial lead

Trinity `Add. MS c/104/21` (Robert Bowes to Nora Sidgwick, 13 Apr 1904) records that Canon Gray expected to pass material he found relating to Cambridge WMC to the **Cambridge Free Library**.

Trace:

1. accession registers c.1904;
2. local-history manuscripts/scrapbooks;
3. WMC prospectuses, minutes, class lists or correspondence;
4. later transfer to current Cambridge library/archive custody.

### C. University actor papers

Only after actual WMC teaching responsibilities are fixed, search:

- G. D. Liveing;
- James Clerk Maxwell;
- Harvey Goodwin;
- F. Gerald Vesey;
- H. M. Butler;
- Henry Sidgwick.

**Target closure:** a specific WMC class/event with a scientific object, apparatus, specimen or practical operation.

Famous scientific identity of the teacher is insufficient.

## Priority 7 — manual-to-visual competence comparison

George Allen and related Ruskin WMC pupils form a useful control corpus for skill transformation.

Questions:

- what aspects of joinery/manual precision were explicitly valued in drawing/engraving training?
- how were pupils selected as assistant teachers?
- who paid for specialist engraving training?
- how did visual reproduction become paid occupation?
- do microscopical preparers show analogous patronage/training transitions?

This strand matters because slide preparation and engraving both convert manual accuracy into reproducible visual evidence, but the comparison should be demonstrated rather than metaphorical.

## Stop rule for the next phase

Broad web searching should pause for an actor once one of the following is reached:

1. an exact archival reference and closure question exists;
2. further hits only repeat biographies without adding an object relation;
3. a claim depends on reading an un-digitised register/minute book;
4. medium ambiguity (`slide`, `specimen`, `preparation`) can only be resolved by object/archive inspection.

The project should now spend effort on **closing relations**, not increasing the number of names.

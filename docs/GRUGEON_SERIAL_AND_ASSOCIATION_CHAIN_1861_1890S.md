## 1. WMC Magazine botany papers — existence now strong

The Darwin Correspondence Project authority entry for **Alfred Grugeon (1826–1913)** states that he:

- was a woodturner and botanist;
- took botany lessons at Working Men's College;
- after instructor certification, taught botany there;
- **contributed several papers on botany to the college magazine**;
- wrote a botany primer in 1873.

The authority entry cites Ray Desmond and John Ramsbottom's *Journal of Botany* 55 (1917): 193–94.

Evidence state:

`WMC_MAGAZINE_BOTANY_PAPERS_EXISTENCE = STRONG_SECONDARY_AUTHORITY`

`EXACT_ARTICLES = PENDING`

Do not invent titles, dates or pages until the Magazine or attribution literature is directly inspected.

## 2. 1861 WMC science-print environment

N. E. C. Smith cites:

> `Report of General Meeting`, *Working Men's College Magazine* 3 (1861), 173.

for M. C. Cooke's WMC Physiological Botany teaching plan, which included emphasis on microscope use.

Smith further identifies Grugeon among Cooke's WMC pupils/former pupils.

This places Grugeon's WMC learning/teaching and his reported Magazine botany contributions inside a demonstrably science-bearing institutional serial environment.

Boundary:

Do not assume Grugeon's Magazine papers were written under Cooke's class or were about microscopy; exact items remain open.

## 3. Direct *Science-Gossip* primary closure — 1 June 1865, p.143

*Hardwicke's Science-Gossip*, vol.1, **1 June 1865**, printed p.143 contains two separately signed botanical observations by Grugeon.

### `Paris quadrifolia`

Signed `A. Grugeon`.

Grugeon reports collecting specimens with five rather than four leaves, notes a corresponding floral/pistil anomaly, and asks other readers whether they have observed the same aggregate pattern.

### `Valeriana dioica`

Signed `Alfred Grugeon`.

Grugeon compares observations from Nazing Meads and another locality, notes an apparent scarcity of female flowers, and asks whether the sex disparity is general / seasonally variable.

Evidence state:

`CLOSED_PRIMARY_PERIODICAL`

- `FIELD_OBSERVATION_TO_CORRESPONDENCE`

- `AMATEUR_DATA_SHARING`

## 5. Society of Amateur Botanists — direct participation, 2 May 1866

A contemporary report in *The Naturalist* records the Society of Amateur Botanists meeting at the schoolroom, St Martin's in the Fields, London, **2 May 1866**.

The report says **Mr Grugeon read a paper on the morphology of foliar and floral organs** (the printed/OCR wording gives the subject in relation to Fabaceae).

Evidence state:

`GRUGEON_TO_SAB_PAPER_GIVER = CLOSED_CONTEMPORARY_REPORT`

This is stronger than inferring Society participation from Cooke's former-pupil network or *Science-Gossip* page adjacency.

Normalized chain:

`botanical observation / WMC actor -> amateur botanical society -> formal paper -> periodical meeting report`

## 7. 1873 WMC Natural History Society and Field Club

The 1904 WMC institutional self-history retrospectively records Grugeon as:

- committee member of the 1873 Natural History Society and Field Club;
- one of its paper-givers;
- a major contributor to the grass collection displayed at the summer conversazione, formed/named principally by him.

The club later explicitly terminated because it lost its Tuesday room.

Evidence state:

`CLOSED_1904_INSTITUTIONAL_RETROSPECTIVE`

This is institutional club activity, not a periodical article, but it shows the material/associational practices into which Grugeon's print observations fit.

## 9. Later botanical/Field Club recurrence

Modern reconstruction and the 1904 WMC self-history connect Grugeon to the late nineteenth-century revival/reinstitutionalization of WMC field-natural-history activity.

Strong states:

- Grugeon resumed WMC teaching in the 1880s and retired around 1892;
- College Journal(s) recorded botanical walks under his guidance;
- he became president of the newly formed Lubbock Field Club in the 1892/93 formation window;
- the 1904 history explicitly says WMC Journal pages document Lubbock activity.

Founding year remains conflicted; see dedicated genealogy file.

### What this model supports

- Grugeon is a strong continuity-bearing actor in WMC botanical/natural-history practice.
- His scientific activity repeatedly crosses field observation, teaching, association, objects/collections and serial print.
- Several different periodical genres are involved: institutional magazine, participatory popular-science correspondence, society meeting report, specialist geological correspondence, later institutional journal.

### What it does not support

- one continuous institution from 1860s to 1890s;
- a single coherent doctrine/programme preserved unchanged;
- automatic causation from one publication to the next;
- assumption that every Grugeon paper was microscopy-related.

## 1. What changed in this pass

The Davies–Maskelyne case is stronger than the first pass, but its archival chronology must be exact.

Three corrections/upgrades are now necessary:

1. **Thomas Davies's official daily-duty diaries do not cover his initial 1858–62 formation under Maskelyne.** The first surviving volume currently identified, `DF/MIN/20/2/1`, begins in October 1875. The diary series is therefore a late-career source for daily labour, object intake, laboratory/custodial routines and later thin-section work; it cannot be used as contemporaneous evidence for the initial apprenticeship.
2. **Nevil Story-Maskelyne's relation to the Working Men's College was more active than Council membership alone.** The Royal Society obituary states that he frequently lectured at the Working Men's College. The subject(s) and dates of those lectures remain open. The 1866 College programme independently places him on the Council but does not list him among that term's regular Physical Sciences teachers.
3. **`DF/MIN/21` is not an early preparation-invoice route.** The series-level date span begins in 1816 because specimen/financial records survive early, but the surviving files that explicitly combine or isolate the `preparation account` are twentieth-century: `DF/MIN/21/30` begins in 1910, `DF/MIN/21/14` in 1937 and `DF/MIN/21/15` in 1945. It must not be used as a prospective 1861 microscope/sectioning invoice series.

This yields a more precise model: the same scientific actor crossed museum and adult-education institutions, but the technical and pedagogical functions of those institutions must not be collapsed; archive series must also be checked at child-item chronology before being used as historical routes.

## 2. The early formation period, 1858–62

### 1858 — Davies enters the Mineralogy Department

NHM's Thomas Davies authority record gives his appointment as **3rd Class Attendant on 20 February 1858**. A later British Museum departmental history emphasizes that attendant duties were formally non-scientific and that Davies entered with little prior mineralogical education.

This matters because the thin-section skill was acquired while Davies occupied a low-status museum grade whose official job definition did not itself recognize scientific work.

Working relation:

```text
non-scientific attendant grade
 -> repeated collection/labelling/arrangement labour
 -> Maskelyne notices aptitude
 -> technical initiation into microscopic mineral identification
 -> later scientific-grade promotion
```

NHM's authority record gives promotion to Transcriber / Junior Assistant on **25 August 1862**. This agrees with the later British Museum departmental history and the Geological Magazine obituary. Fletcher's 1893 obituary instead gives 1869; retain that as a source discrepancy rather than silently harmonizing it.

### 1860–62 — Davies is already producing meteorite documentation

`DF/MIN/10/1/6` is catalogued as **List of meteorites in the British Museum by Thomas Davies**, dated **1860–1862**.

This is the best presently indexed contemporaneous Davies document for the formation period. It overlaps directly with the construction/use of the special 1861 microscope described in the later departmental history.

Research questions for the file:

- handwriting and corrections;
- specimen/accession numbers;
- locality / donor / exchange fields;
- signs that microscopic examination altered naming or classification;
- cross-references to sections or preparations;
- whether later catalogue hands can be distinguished from Davies's own.

### 1861 — optical method becomes departmental infrastructure

The British Museum meteorite history states that the Mineralogy Department lacked a safe chemical laboratory and even blowpipe work was restricted because of fire risk. Under Maskelyne a microscope was therefore constructed by 1861 with a revolving graduated stage, eyepiece goniometer and polarized-light capability for meteorite thin sections.

This is a workplace-driven material solution, not generic popular microscopy:

```text
restricted chemical infrastructure
 + meteorite/mineral classification problem
 + thin-sectionable material
 -> custom optical apparatus
 -> polarized-light comparison
 -> microscopic mineral determination
```

## 4. Closed false lead: `DF/MIN/21` preparation invoices

The parent catalogue says `DF/MIN/21` covers departmental financial records from 1816–1970 and distinguishes specimen invoices from `preparation invoices` for goods/services apart from specimens and books. That wording is potentially misleading if used without opening the children.

Child-level chronology shows:

- `DF/MIN/21/30` — financial journal covering specimens/books/preparation/other accounts, **1910–1937**;
- `DF/MIN/21/14` — comparable financial journal, **1937–1970**;
- `DF/MIN/21/15` — invoices paid from the preparation account, **1945–1948**;
- later preparation-account files continue after that.

Therefore:

```text
DF/MIN/21 series date = 1816–1970
DOES NOT MEAN
preparation invoices survive from 1816 onward
```

Repository rule: **do not use `DF/MIN/21` as a 1858–63 microscope, glass, sectioning or optical-fitting payment route.** Search Trustee authorisations/reports and early departmental correspondence instead.

## 5. Maskelyne at the Working Men's College

### 1866 — official institutional position

The College's printed programme for its thirteenth year lists **N. S. Maskelyne** among the Council. The same programme describes the College as joining working students with university/professional teachers and says that study should be connected with pupils' daytime engagements so that work and study illuminate one another.

Its Associateship/science structure includes Botany, Physiology, Geology/Physical Geography, Mechanics, Hydrostatics and Optics; the 1866 term's named Physical Sciences teachers include Alfred Grugeon in Botany, A. Goalen in Natural Philosophy and T. Spencer Cobbold in Zoology.

Maskelyne is **not** listed among those regular class teachers in this particular programme.

### Royal Society obituary — active lecturing

Maskelyne's Royal Society obituary states that he **frequently lectured at the Working Men's College**.

This upgrades the relation from:

`Council member only`

to:

`Council member + repeated lecturer`.

But the current evidence does **not** establish:

- the subjects of his WMC lectures;
- the dates of individual lectures;
- whether he taught mineralogy, crystallography, optics or microscopy there;
- whether Thomas Davies attended any Maskelyne lecture at WMC;
- whether Maskelyne directed Davies to the College.

These remain explicit open edges.

See `WMC_MASKELYNE_LECTURE_PROVENANCE_PASS1_2026-09-08.md` for venue controls. In particular, the 23 Nov 1874 crystallography lecture preserved in `DF/MIN/20/1/4/13` belongs to the **Chemical Society** course, not to WMC.

## 6. Davies at WMC: keep the two curricula separate

Fletcher's obituary says Davies attended evening classes at Great Ormond Street and acquired French and German. It separately attributes his microscopic mineralogical training to Maskelyne in the Museum.

The strongest formulation is therefore a **dual or composite curriculum**:

```text
WMC
 -> French / German / general education

British Museum Mineralogy Department
 -> specimen handling + registration/labelling
 -> Maskelyne apprenticeship
 -> meteorite / rock thin-section microscopy

combined over one career
 -> practical petrology + scientific assistantship
```

Maskelyne's WMC lectures make the institutional overlap tighter, but do not erase the distinction between these two educational sites.

## 7. Contemporary institutional argument: actor overlap, functional partition

The case becomes more interesting because Victorian observers explicitly debated whether museum custody and elementary/public teaching should be combined.

In 1866 Ruskin defended Maskelyne and the British Museum mineral collection while arguing that a national museum and a working-men's college performed different functions: the former primarily treasures/custodies exceptional objects, the latter teaches.

The Devonshire Commission later records Maskelyne's own view that Keepers who had aptitude for lecturing could be encouraged to lecture, but systematic formal lectures should not be made part of museum duties; a lecturer might instead bring a class to the Museum for demonstrations of specimens too large or valuable to move.

This gives a historically explicit structure:

```text
same actor can move across institutions
BUT
custody / cataloguing / specialist object work
is not institutionally identical to
adult teaching / elementary instruction
```

Davies sits inside precisely this partition: his general education is documented at WMC; his microscopic apprenticeship is documented in museum labour.

## 9. 1876 Clifton Ward thin-section intake — a closed late object route

`DF/MIN/10/3/36` includes a **list of thin sections sent to Prof. H. N. S. Maskelyne by Clifton Ward in 1876**, from the Lake District and Wales. The catalogue gives an accession-number span `85498–86211`.

Arithmetically that span contains 714 numbers if continuous, but the project must **not** convert this into `714 physical slides` until the original list is inspected. Possible complications include gaps, compound accessions, multiple sections per number or non-section entries.

What is already secure is the relation:

```text
Clifton Ward geological material/thin sections
 -> sent to Maskelyne
 -> Mineralogy Department registration/list
 -> contemporaneous Davies daily-duty diary exists for 1876
```

This is an excellent object↔diary crosswalk target because the diary chronology actually overlaps the event.

## 10. Story-Maskelyne donation: the word `slides` must be resolved materially

NHM `DF/MIN/20/1/4` describes the Story-Maskelyne papers and states that letters relating to the 1951 donation of **papers, minerals, laboratory equipment and slides** were filed under legacy reference `DF1/55`.

Research rule:

- treat `slides` as a hard catalogue word;
- do not decide from metadata alone whether these were petrographic thin sections, ordinary specimen slides, photographic/lantern slides, teaching slides or a mixed body;
- do not mechanically translate legacy `DF1/55` into a modern `DF/MIN/...` shelfmark;

Highest-value fields if an inventory survives:

`slide/object number | material | locality | date | preparer | label wording | dimensions | mounting medium | cabinet/box | donor wording | current custody`.

# Hunterian / RCS: Quekett catalogue-address survival upgrade

Date: 2026-08-11

## Revised grade

**S — 1846 acquisition of the 2,500-preparation corpus.**  
**S — teaching and printed-catalogue architecture.**  
**S — survival of named Quekett slides with historical alphanumeric marks inside current RCSMS addresses.**  
**A++ / S-candidate — five J. W. Bond catalogue-entry ↔ surviving-object matches, pending direct primary/current-object verification.**

The older survey had this case at REVIEW because it contained two strong but insufficiently integrated facts: the Royal College of Surgeons purchased 2,500 Quekett preparations in 1846, and the Hunterian still holds a much larger microscope-slide collection. The primary corpus and present Hunterian records now make the middle of the chain visible.

Quekett made his large preparation collection during his human and comparative anatomy studentship. The project’s nineteenth-century source states that the 2,500 preparations were purchased by the College in 1846 and became part of the museum’s histological department. The same account records that in 1844 the College appointed him to annual demonstrations for the exhibition and connected description of the collection and for explaining the methods and resources of microscopical study. In 1850 he published volume I of the *Descriptive and Illustrated Catalogue of the Histological Series contained in the Museum of the Royal College of Surgeons of England*. This is therefore a bounded conversion of preparations into a teaching collection and then into a printed retrieval/classification system.

The current endpoint is unusually legible. The Hunterian states that its microscope-slide collection now contains more than 15,000 slides and uses `RCSMS` identifiers. Its public collection page shows, among others, `RCSMS/Quekett/Division 2/Bb 89`, “Vertical Section of Paddle of an Ichthyosaurus”; `RCSMS/Quekett/Division 2/Xl 144`, “Tick of Hedgehog”; and `RCSMS/Quekett/Division 2/Db 97`, “Human Stomach.” The older alphanumeric marks remain visible on the physical slides while the modern museum namespace wraps them in a new address. That is enough to promote the case to S-level address survival/translation at collection and selected-object level.

A particularly promising bounded crosswalk comes from James William Bond. A specialist study reproduces five Hunterian slides donated by Bond and states that the handwriting is Quekett’s. It maps them to Quekett’s 1850 catalogue as `Ap24`, `Ap63`, `Ap64`, `At7`, and `Au6`, describing Ceylon sponge spicules, an Echinus shell section, and a male cockchafer antenna. This is very close to a complete historical catalogue-entry → surviving slide relation. I am keeping it at A++ / S-candidate because our project has not yet independently checked both the relevant primary catalogue pages and the current Hunterian object records/images.

## Mechanism

`private/preparatory corpus → 1846 institutional purchase → demonstration/teaching collection → 1850 printed catalogue → alphanumeric marks on objects → later museum cataloguing → current RCSMS address`

This sits beside the QMC case without duplicating it. The Quekett Microscopical Club built a heterogeneous circulation cabinet out of donations and exchanges. The Royal College of Surgeons bought and institutionalised a large bounded corpus, then made it teachable and retrievable through demonstrations and a printed histological catalogue. The present objects retain traces of that older address system inside the modern one.

## Next bounded test

The cheapest decisive step is to read the 1850 catalogue entries for `Ap24`, `Ap63`, `Ap64`, `At7`, and `Au6` directly and compare them with current object records/images. If they close, the Bond subgroup becomes an S-level five-object historical catalogue crosswalk. Separately, when the already-open RCS `MS0027` enquiry progresses, the useful pages are those recording preparation, labelling, accession and demonstration practices; a broad rereading of the whole archive is unnecessary at this stage.

## Guards

- The current 15,000+ total describes the wider Hunterian microscope-slide collection, not Quekett’s 1846 purchase.
- A present `RCSMS/Quekett/...` object should not automatically be assigned to the 1846 purchase without object-level provenance.
- Similar subject wording alone is insufficient for identity; old alphanumeric marks and exact catalogue text are the important crosswalk variables.
- The Bond slides are Bond donations in the secondary account; Quekett’s handwriting/cataloguing does not make them Quekett-made preparations.
- The existing RCS archive enquiry should be followed up narrowly rather than duplicated.

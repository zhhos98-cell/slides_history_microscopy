## 2. Material / volume state

A 1915 *Naturalist* bibliographical discussion reports seeing a volume of approximately **190 pages, octavo**, dated 1867, and explicitly says the surviving bound volume did not make the original publication frequency or per-part pagination obvious. The writer inferred roughly a dozen parts because six continued geology articles run through p.117.

This is a direct warning for the RSVP serial-materiality problem:

`bound volume -> original part structure obscured`

The issue sequence therefore must be reconstructed from internal headers, signatures, wrappers, advertisements, or external notices rather than assumed from later binding.

A bookseller description of a surviving copy likewise describes a hardbound volume of `ii, 190 pp.` and notes plant lists for localities including Shibden Dale. Treat bookseller metadata as supporting material-state evidence only, not as primary bibliographic authority.

## 3. Institutional transition: manuscript -> print

Halifax historical sources consistently state:

- Haley Hill Working Men's College fostered a literary/scientific society;
- the College/Society circulated a **handwritten magazine** before 1866;
- in 1866 this was replaced or succeeded by printed *The Circulator*.

Controlled transition:

`Working Men's College / local literary-scientific sociability -> manuscript serial circulation -> printed periodical`

This is analytically stronger than treating *The Circulator* as an isolated magazine title.

Open questions:

## 5. James Spencer geology serialization

A later biographical account of geologist / local naturalist **James Spencer (1834–1898)** reconstructs the following sequence in *The Circulator*:

1. first two numbers — introduction to `Popular Geology`;
2. following two papers — strata of Halifax;
3. later article(s) — coal pits at Low Moor;
4. later article(s) — geology of Ingleborough.

A 1915 periodical-history note independently states that up to p.117 there were **six continued articles on geology by J. S[pencer]**.

This gives a strong periodical function:

`local field observation -> serial geology sequence -> local scientific readership`

The important feature is not merely that geology appeared in the magazine. A local worker/naturalist's repeated field practice was converted into **continued serial instruction / description**.

## 6. Natural-history content beyond Spencer

Current bibliographic descriptions report:

- local natural history;
- geology;
- plant lists for localities, including Shibden Dale;
- poetry and music alongside science;
- some contributions described later as early records of local natural history.

This mixed genre profile matters. The serial should not be classified as a specialist scientific journal. Its scientific function operated inside a local literary / educational miscellany.

Recommended genre codes:

- `SERIAL_SCIENCE` — continued scientific exposition;
- `LOCAL_RECORD` — locality-specific flora/fauna/geology record;
- `FIELD_TO_PRINT` — excursion / observation transformed into periodical text;
- `MS_TO_PRINT` — manuscript serial becomes print;
- `SELF_PUB` — society / adult-education network publishes its own serial;
- `MIXED_MISCELLANY` — science coexists with poetry/music/literature.

## 7. Later circulation / reputation chain

Later Halifax natural-history publishing explicitly looked back to Spencer's *Circulator* contributions and listed them alongside his later work in Manchester geological and Yorkshire natural-history periodicals.

Candidate chain:

`Haley Hill local serial -> later geological society / regional naturalist print -> retrospective local scientific canon`

This should be tested article by article rather than treated as automatic career progression.

## 8. Comparison with London WMC Magazine

Current contrast:

### London WMC Magazine

- institutionally directed by Council;
- strong orientation / reading-guide / educational self-definition functions;
- metropolitan publisher Macmillan;
- contemporary reader uses annual volume to enter College life.

### Halifax *Circulator*

- conducted by members of a local Literary and Scientific Society;
- emerges from manuscript circulation;
- strong continued local-science / field-observation content;
- cheap local print;
- scientific and literary material deliberately mixed.

This is already enough to reject any assumption that `Working Men's College periodical` names one stable genre.

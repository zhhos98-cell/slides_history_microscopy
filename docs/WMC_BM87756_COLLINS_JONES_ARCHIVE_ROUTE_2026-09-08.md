# BM87756 afterlife control: Collins 1975 and the R. V. Jones Hannay-diamond files

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Branch:** `research/working-mens-microscopy-19c`  
**Status:** bounded archive route; physical-object question remains open.

## Question

Can the 1975 re-examination of the Hannay diamonds explain the change from the **twelve fragments** described on the historical Canada-balsam glass slide in 1943 to the **nine original fragments** examined by A. T. Collins in 1975, or otherwise document removal, remounting, selection, return, or alteration of BM.87756?

At present the answer is **not yet controlled**. The useful advance is narrower: the 1975 analytical episode now has an exact bibliographic identity and a dedicated contemporary archival route.

## 1. Collins 1975: bibliographic control, object handling still unresolved

The paper is:

> A. T. Collins, “The Hannay Diamonds,” *Industrial Diamond Review* 35 (1975), 434–437.

A 1976 notice in *Journal of Gemmology* 15.3 identifies the paper and states that Collins re-examined **nine of the original diamond fragments**. It summarizes the method as cathodoluminescence under a 50 kV electron beam, analysed with a Hilger Monospec spectrometer, and reports that the results were characteristic of natural diamond.

The original Collins article was not recovered in the bounded public-web pass. Therefore the following remain uncontrolled:

- why Collins examined nine rather than twelve fragments;
- whether nine were selected from a still-intact twelve-fragment preparation;
- whether any fragments had previously been removed, lost, destroyed, separated, or transferred;
- whether Collins removed fragments from the historical glass support for testing;
- whether any tested fragments were remounted or returned;
- the physical state of the Davies-labelled slide/card in 1975.

**Evidence level:** `A_BIBLIOGRAPHIC` for the paper identity; `B_SECONDARY_REVIEW` for the nine-fragment count and method until the original article is inspected.

Source: *Journal of Gemmology* 15.3 (1976), p. 138, review notice for Collins, “The Hannay Diamonds,” available through Gem-A: `https://gem-a.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/JoG1976_15_3.pdf`.

## 2. R. V. Jones papers: dedicated Hannay-diamond archive route

The catalogue of the papers of R. V. Jones, `NCUACS 95/8/00`, contains a dedicated group:

- `F.116–F.118` — **Hannay diamonds, 1968–1979**.

The catalogue explains that E. P. Flint reopened the Hannay question in *Chemistry and Industry* in 1968; Jones refereed the paper and published “A note on the Hannay diamonds.” It then states that tests in 1975 suggested that the Hannay diamonds were natural rather than synthetic. The group contains 1968 material, correspondence and papers, and later correspondence concerning the authenticity of Hannay’s diamonds and the Flint/Robertson biographical work, extending to 1979.

The catalogue-level sequence identifies `F.117` as the 1975–1979 correspondence/papers on the authenticity question. This is the smallest archival unit currently capable of connecting the Collins-era test to contemporary discussion without reopening a broad diamond-history search.

**Evidence level:** `A_ARCHIVE_ROUTE` for the existence, date range, subject and catalogue-level content of `F.116–F.118`. This is not evidence that Collins himself corresponded with Jones, nor that these papers contain BM.87756 handling records.

Catalogue: Centre for Scientific Archives, **JONES, Reginald Victor Vol2**, `NCUACS 95/8/00`, history-of-science section, `F.116–F.118 Hannay diamonds 1968–1979`: `https://centreforscientificarchives.co.uk/catalogues/jones-reginald-victor-vol2/`.

Repository/custody note: the catalogue is for the R. V. Jones papers associated with the University of Aberdeen archival holdings; verify current access/request mechanics with the holding repository before ordering material.

## 3. Claim ceiling

The new archive route does **not** close the 1943→1975 physical-object gap.

Current secure sequence remains:

1. **1943:** twelve particles are described on a Canada-balsam glass slide; Bannister and Lonsdale reactivate the historical preparation for optical/X-ray analysis.
2. **1959:** at least one Hannay diamond is re-examined by X-ray methods; recovered text does not specify support/remounting.
3. **1975:** Collins examines nine original fragments by cathodoluminescence; the reason for the nine-fragment analytical set is not stated in the controlled secondary notice.
4. **1968–1979 archival afterlife:** Jones preserves a dedicated Hannay-diamond dossier, including 1975–1979 authenticity correspondence/papers.

Do **not** infer from this sequence that three fragments were lost or destroyed. Selection, prior analytical removal, later custody changes, or another undocumented handling history remain possible.

Do **not** claim that Collins appears in the Jones correspondence unless the file itself establishes this.

Do **not** use the Jones catalogue as evidence for the current physical integrity of the historical glass slide or Davies card.

## 4. Minimal next move and stop rule

The next archival action should be **one object-level request only**:

> Inspect/request `F.117` and ask whether it contains A. T. Collins, *Industrial Diamond Review*, BM.87756, “nine”/“twelve” fragments, or any statement about removal, mounting, remounting, return, selection, or physical condition of the Hannay specimens.

If `F.117` identifies a relevant leaf, inspect only that local sequence and then decide whether `F.118` is needed.

If `F.117` contains no Collins/BM.87756 handling information, **stop this analytical-afterlife route**. Preserve the 12→9 difference as a bounded conservation/selection gap rather than broadening into an open-ended search through the Jones papers or general diamond-synthesis archives.

## 5. Effect on the WMC/Davies case

This does not add a new Working Men’s College claim. Its value is object-historical: the Davies-labelled Hannay preparation already provides a rare surviving endpoint for Davies’s material labour. The Jones files offer a tightly bounded way to test what happened when that nineteenth-century preparation was repeatedly reactivated by twentieth-century analytical techniques. Until the physical handling is documented, the historical object should be treated as a **serially re-examined specimen with an unresolved support/fragment history**, not as an unchanged slide surviving continuously from 1880 to the present.

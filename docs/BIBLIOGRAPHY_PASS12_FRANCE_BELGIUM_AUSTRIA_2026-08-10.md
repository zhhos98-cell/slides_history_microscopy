# Bibliography pass 12 — France, Belgium, Austria

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: complete module pending merge.

## Scope

This pass applies the existing **physical-slide-first** admission rule to a large France/Belgium/Austria module. It targets professional preparers, slide-specific trade catalogues and exhibition records, named physical slide series, exchange/circulation evidence, surviving historical collections, and modern studies in which the slides or their catalogues are the primary evidence.

The pass does not admit a source merely because microscopes or preparations appear in it. Instrument-first optical catalogues, broad anatomical catalogues, general histology manuals, and institutional reports where slides are incidental remain outside the bibliography.

## Added layers

### France: professional preparation trade and serial circulation

The Bourgogne sequence is treated as a changing workshop and market rather than as a single maker biography. The pass adds the Bourgogne Frères London exhibition record (1862), Bourgogne & Alliot at Paris (1867), Eugène Bourgogne's 1868 description of vine/aphid preparations, Joseph Bourgogne's 1874 dedicated catalogue, Eugène Bourgogne's 1884 dedicated preparation catalogue, and a 1895 American advertising state. These records sit alongside the already-listed 1862 Joseph Bourgogne catalogue and the existing modern Bourgogne literature without duplicating them.

Charles Marchand's 1878 Paris exhibition record adds a separate professional preparer whose comparative-anatomy and geology slides survive with maker labels.

Jean Tempère is reconstructed as a circulation-to-publication sequence. Hardwicke's Science-Gossip exchange notices from 1878–1883 document the pre-commercial exchange phase. Three distinct physical publication series are added: Tempère & Petit, *Diatomées de France* (begun 1887; later continued with Peragallo, c. 400 slides); Tempère & Dupray, *Algues de France* (1889–1892); and Tempère & Dutertre, *Champignons de France* (1892–1893, 250 slides in ten sets). The already-listed Tempère–Peragallo *Diatomées du Monde Entier* remains a separate series. Pierre Girodet's 2004 study of Tempère's artistic mounts supplies an object-centred secondary layer.

Axel Garboe's 1961 Danish study of Carl (Charles) Hansen adds a Paris-based preparation maker and dealer whose career joined Danish natural-history networks to the French scientific market. Its Danish publication language raises the bibliography's language count by one.

### Belgium: exsiccata, subscription series and commercial supply

Charles-Henri Delogne's *Diatomées de Belgique* (1880–1881) is admitted as a bounded 100-slide exsiccata in four fascicles. Surviving slides and associated handwritten sample information remain usable for provenance and typification.

Van Heurck & Grunow's *Types du Synopsis des Diatomées de Belgique* is admitted as a physical component of the larger *Synopsis*: series I–XXII, nos. 1–550, twenty-five microscope slides per series, with printed schedules and boxed issue. Harvard dates the bibliographic series 1882–1885; documentary evidence from Iowa places actual distribution of the type-slide boxes in the mid-1880s. The row records that dating distinction rather than forcing a false single event date.

Robert Drosten's 1885 Antwerp exhibition display and the 1886 report of his stock establish a Brussels commercial-preparation node. His surviving labelled slides provide an object counterpart to the trade record.

### Austria: surviving preparations and Grunow's catalogue-object system

The Naturhistorisches Museum Wien preserves microscopic preparations made personally by Ferdinand I, including algal mounts dated ca. 1850. This is admitted as a surviving-object collection record and becomes the earliest object date in the public bibliography.

Two modern NHM Vienna studies reconstruct Albert Grunow's collection and catalogue. Schuster, Williams & Van de Vijver (2022) treats the historical slide/sample collection as a provenance system; Schuster et al. (2023) explains how the handwritten catalogue links samples, exsiccata, preparation types, and coordinates of individual valves on slides. Together they create an Austria-centred bridge connecting Delogne, Van Heurck, Eulenstein, Cleve & Möller and other distributed slide series.

## Screened out under the same rule

- Nachet optical catalogues: retained outside the bibliography because the catalogues are instrument-first even when they list slides, coverslips, preparation tools or prepared objects.
- Karl Fritsch (Vienna) 1882 *Illustriertes Preis-Verzeichniss der Astronomischen und Optischen Instrumente*: instrument-first; excluded.
- broad Deyrolle natural-history/anatomy catalogues: excluded unless a separately verifiable slide-specific catalogue or section becomes the substantive publication unit.
- broad university or pathological-anatomy reports that merely note use of microscopic preparations: kept as routing evidence, not admitted as bibliography rows.

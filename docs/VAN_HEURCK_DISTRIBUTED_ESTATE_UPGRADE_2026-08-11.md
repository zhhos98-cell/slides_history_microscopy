# Van Heurck distributed estate upgrade — 2026-08-11

## Upgrade

Promote `BE-VAN-HEURCK-DISTRIBUTED-ESTATE` from A+ comparator to **S-level collection-history / re-addressing chain**, while keeping item-level historical-to-current identifier crosswalks open.

The decisive source is Edward Frison's 1968 *Geïllustreerde inventaris van de historische microscopen, onderdelen en uitrusting*, now linked directly from the University of Antwerp heritage history. It describes Van Heurck's scientific patrimony as one integrated system rather than a retrospective grouping of separate collections. Besides historical microscopes and parts, Frison records **more than 19,000 microscopic preparations, mainly diatoms**, cleaned and uncleaned diatom material from major nineteenth-century diatomists, a teaching drug collection, the Sieber–von Reichenbach–Van Heurck herbarium, the scientific library, and physical instruments.

## Re-addressing sequence

- 1909: estate acquired by the City of Antwerp after Van Heurck's death.
- 1926: collections except physical instruments transferred to the Botanical Garden Museum.
- 1934–ca.1949: museum display phase, followed by closure, packing and storage in multiple places.
- ca.1958: threatened dispersal, described by Frison as narrowly avoided.
- 1959–60: provisional descriptive catalogue of microscopes and optical apparatus.
- 1963–64: long-term loan to the Royal Zoological Society; microscopes, parts and laboratory equipment reordered, cleaned and where possible restored to usability.
- 1968: illustrated inventory publishes the new museum address system. Frison explicitly says page-bottom letter/number codes refer to the provisional catalogue, photographic negative, enlarged positive and image album.
- 1985: scientific instruments transferred to RUCA for inventory/restoration.
- early twenty-first century: University of Antwerp records the collection as distributed across Belgium: books to Hendrik Conscience Heritage Library, botanical objects to Meise, scientific instruments including microscopes to Ghent.

This gives a rare sequence in which one scientific working estate repeatedly changes **physical address, curatorial category and identifier namespace**.

## Current recipient systems

Hendrik Conscience Heritage Library now treats the Van Heurck library as a named scientific collection. Its collection history says that a limited group of books and offprints needed for managing/consulting the herbarium and diatom collection were loaned to Meise; those were given the catalogue siglum `Fonds Van Heurck` so that the split fund could remain virtually legible.

Meise identifies Van Heurck as a major nineteenth-century collector, states that its herbarium includes microscopic preparations, and places the historical Van Heurck diatom material within its current algae/diatom holdings. Its current algae page gives an aggregate of about 35,000 diatom specimens including the Van Heurck collection. Keep this current aggregate separate from Frison's 1968 `>19,000 microscopic preparations`: they are different dated collection states, not a count to harmonise.

University of Antwerp states that scientific instruments went to Ghent's Museum for the History of Sciences and that Van Heurck microscopes can be seen there. Ghent's academic bibliography also records a 2009 museum exhibition catalogue on microscopy with Van Heurck as a subject keyword.

## Mechanism

The important point is no longer simply that the estate was 'dispersed'. It had already been moved, packed, catalogued and remapped before the recent cross-institutional split. Frison's 1968 inventory captures one intermediate museum information system: **object → provisional catalogue → negative → enlarged positive → album**. The later distribution added new library, herbarium and instrument-museum addresses on top of that.

This makes Van Heurck an inverse-scale comparator to Cole. Cole's publication identity survives through a fixed slot/index system. Van Heurck's scientific identity is progressively decomposed because later institutions separate slides, raw samples, books, herbarium material and instruments by disciplinary/material type.

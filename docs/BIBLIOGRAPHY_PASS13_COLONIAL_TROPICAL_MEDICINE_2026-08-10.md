## Admission rule

The threshold remains **physical-slide first**. Colonial medicine, tropical medicine, parasitology, pathology, public health and microscopy are not sufficient subjects on their own. A record enters only where a physical microscope slide/preparation, its material sampling support, a preparation-centred archival file, an explicit request/transfer of slides, or a surviving slide collection does substantive evidentiary work.

## Included records

### India / India Office

1. **India Office Records, `IOR/F/4/2450/135466a`, “Wax models of diseases of the skin and microscopic preparations” (1851).** The British Library catalogue itself identifies a one-file India Office medical record centred in part on microscopic preparations. Its sparse metadata does not justify assigning a maker, destination or teaching use, so none is asserted.

2. **D. D. Cunningham, _Microscopic Examinations of Air_ (Calcutta, 1873).** Admitted because the microscopic glass support participates in collection: airborne material is deposited onto an adhesive microscopic substrate before examination. This is a slide/cover-glass sampling system, not merely a general microscopy monograph.

### Asian tropical-medicine object survival

3. **Patrick Manson, `GB 0809 Manson/08`, “Scientific artefacts” (1879–1883).** LSHTM describes the surviving two-item series directly as a “set of slides and glass vials.” The catalogue does not identify the contents or locality of individual slides, so no Amoy/Hong Kong attribution is made at row level.

### Ross malaria slides: production → circulation → afterlife

4. **Ronald Ross malarial microscope slides created in India, c.1895–1897.** LSHTM explicitly identifies the surviving originals as slides created in India during the research culminating in the 1897 mosquito-malaria discovery.

5. **Patrick Wilkins O'Gorman to Ronald Ross, 9 April 1903, `GB 0809 Ross/144/36`.** O'Gorman asks Ross to send a dozen Romanowsky-stained malarial blood slides. This is a direct circulation/request record in which stained slides are transferable medical objects.

6. **LSHTM, “Sir Ronald Ross's Slides under the Microscope” (2016).** Modern re-examination of the nineteenth-century box with malaria/parasitology specialists documented still-legible oocysts, gametocytes and historical staining. It is admitted as an object-afterlife and re-observation study.

### Surviving medical collections

7. **Wellcome Historical Medical Museum exhibition record for apparatus and microscope slides belonging to Timothy Lewis (1958).** The record establishes survival and museum display of slides attributed to Lewis; it does not by itself establish the preparation site/date of each slide.

8. **Science Museum Group, J. C. Wagner histological slides (1950–1991).** A surviving cabinet made in Johannesburg and Llandough, including original material associated with the 1960 mesothelioma/asbestos paper. This supplies a direct southern-African pathology collection and a clear slide → published case series → museum custody chain.

## Screened but excluded

The following were deliberately left outside the public bibliography in this pass:

- Timothy Lewis, _A Report on the Microscopic Objects Found in Cholera Evacuations_ (1870): important Indian medical microscopy, but the currently verified bibliographic evidence centres microscopic observation rather than a physical preparation/slide system.
- Lewis and Cunningham's cholera research reports (1872 onward): same problem; disease/microscopy first, physical-slide object role not yet independently closed.
- _Leprosy in India_ and other broad colonial medical reports: rejected where microscopic preparations are incidental.
- Hyderabad Medical School reports adjacent in the India Office series: catalogue adjacency to `IOR/F/4/2450/135466a` does not demonstrate that the microscopic preparations belonged to Hyderabad, so no connection is inferred.
- Ronald Ross photographs/lantern material made from microscopic observations: image derivatives are not microscope slides.
- Dutton and Todd material preserving drawings from microscopic slides of African spirochaetes: useful routing evidence, but the surviving catalogued object is the drawing rather than the physical slide.
- Charles Donovan watercolours made from microscope-slide observations: same exclusion.
- Generic Sierra Leone / sleeping-sickness / malaria expedition literature: excluded unless a direct slide register, preparation set, transfer record or surviving object can be verified.

## Interpretation

This pass intentionally produces fewer entries than the European trade modules. The archival footprint of colonial medical microscopy is large, but much of it describes observations, diseases and laboratories rather than preserving the physical slide as an accountable object. The lower yield is therefore evidence about the source ecology, not a reason to weaken the threshold.

The strongest result is the emergence of a different slide economy from the commercial catalogues of Germany, France and Belgium: **preparation file → sampling substrate → research slide → requested/transported slide → retained diagnostic object → museum/archival re-examination**.

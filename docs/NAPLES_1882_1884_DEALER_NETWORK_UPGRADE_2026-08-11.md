# Naples microscopic preparations: 1882–1884 dealer-network upgrade

Date: 2026-08-11

The Naples case already has one direct product-to-object closure. The 1880/1881 *Preis-Verzeichnis* defined 423 numbered offerings, and St Andrews BPM/1/T8/6 preserves the Naples station identity, *Delphinus phocaena* taxon and exact number 383. That establishes catalogue-offering identity while leaving the history of the individual physical copy open.

The next layer is commercial redistribution. Anton Dohrn's report *Bericht über die Zoologische Station während der Jahre 1882-1884* is located in *Mittheilungen aus der Zoologischen Station zu Neapel*, Bd. 6 (1886), pp. 93–148. A detailed secondary extraction by Brian Stevenson points specifically to pp. 130–131 for a list of microscopic-preparation sales. It reports 225 slides sold to Edmund Wheeler in February 1882 and names R. & J. Beck in London, Edward Ward in Manchester, Gustav Schneider in Basel, and Charles Baker in London among purchasers. Stevenson further reports that Wheeler, Beck, Ward and Schneider were commission sales and quotes/summarizes the report's conclusion that preparation sales failed to cover production costs. Baker's resale role is an interpretation and remains a lower-confidence inference.

The primary report has been located in BHL, but pp. 130–131 have not yet been independently transcribed from the page images in this pass. The dealer network is therefore held at **A++ pending primary-page verification**, rather than silently promoted to S. Once those two pages are checked, the mechanism can potentially be stated at primary-source level as:

**research station production → numbered catalogue commodity → bulk/commission dealer → dispersed retail/institutional object**.

This matters because the Station is operating differently from both Cole and Elcock. Cole distributed a serial publication whose glass preparations occupied fixed issue addresses. Elcock was a professional preparator whose standardized Type Slides entered a retailer network. Naples was a research institution attempting to turn laboratory preparation capacity into a priced commodity system and then using established microscope/optical dealers to extend distribution.

The archival next step is unusually bounded. The Historical Archive of the Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn officially preserves correspondence, general-affairs registers, copybooks, manuscripts, and other administrative/scientific records from the Institute. Those series are plausible locations for orders, invoices, dispatch notes and commission correspondence. The first query should be Edmund Wheeler / February 1882 / 225 preparations, followed by R. & J. Beck, Edward Ward, Gustav Schneider and Charles Baker for 1882–1884.

## Current grade

- **S/A++:** catalogue offering 383 ↔ surviving St Andrews object BPM/1/T8/6.
- **S:** primary 1886 report locator and ASZN archival-series locator.
- **A++:** named 1882–1884 dealer network and Wheeler quantity, pending direct pp. 130–131 verification.
- **Open:** any individual surviving slide ↔ a specific Wheeler/Beck/Ward/Schneider/Baker transaction.

## Guards

1. Catalogue numbers are product/offering addresses, not unique physical serial numbers.
2. A surviving Naples slide cannot be assigned to a dealer transaction without copy-level provenance.
3. Charles Baker should not be called a documented commission reseller until the primary wording is checked.
4. Post-1880 Naples slides should not automatically be attributed to Fritz Meyer, who died in 1880.

## Next exact check

Transcribe pp. 130–131 of the 1886 report first. If the secondary extraction is confirmed, search ASZN copybooks/correspondence/accounts with the five dealer names and the bounded 1882–1884 window. This is a substantially better route than another broad search for surviving Naples slides.

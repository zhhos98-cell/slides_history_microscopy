# Bibliography pass 19 — Wits Van der Horst → Mossman collection bridge

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: verified bibliography expansion only. The frozen 307 / 155 surviving-object survey remains closed and unchanged.

## Why this pass

Pass 18 exposed a much stronger southern-African slide node than the generic pathology-laboratory leads: the University of the Witwatersrand publishes a current, bounded **41,000-slide** embryological histology collection under the Van der Horst name, with catalogue status. A separate research article then follows Van der Horst slides into the Harland W. Mossman Embryological Collection at Wisconsin. This pass closes that object → research → later repository chain without reopening the frozen nineteenth-century census.

## Included records

1. **Wits Life Sciences Museum — Van der Horst slides.** The current zoological-collections table records **41,000** histology slides of embryological material, mainly chordate and mammalian vertebrate. Documentation is explicitly described as 100% card catalogue, about 60% computer database, plus some field notes. The museum history says that under Professors Van der Horst and Balinsky an important research collection of more than 40,000 embryological histology slides was built up and calls it the largest of its kind in the southern hemisphere. The present 41,000 figure is a current collection state; it is not retroactively treated as the exact number originally made by Van der Horst.

2. **A. M. Carter, “Classics revisited: C. J. van der Horst on pregnancy and menstruation in elephant shrews” (2018).** Carter treats Van der Horst’s collection as continuing evidence for embryonic development and placentation in South African mammals. Crucially for this project, the acknowledgement states that Carter accessed **Van der Horst slides curated as part of the Harland W. Mossman Embryological Collection at the University of Wisconsin Zoological Museum**. The article does not state the size or precise transfer mechanism of the Wisconsin Van der Horst subset, so no quantity or original/duplicate status is inferred.

3. **University of Wisconsin Zoological Museum — Harland Mossman Zoological Collection.** UW independently confirms that the museum is repository for a Mossman research collection of microscope slides and preserved anatomical material, especially vertebrate reproductive systems. Its public page does not name Van der Horst or give a slide total. The Wits→Wisconsin relation therefore rests on Carter’s explicit slide statement, while the UW page independently verifies the destination collection’s physical-slide character.

## Evidentiary guards

- `41,000` = current Wits collection size for the named Van der Horst slide collection, not a historical manufacturing total.
- `>40,000` in the Wits history = historical institutional description of the research collection built under Van der Horst and Balinsky; it is not an additional disjoint count.
- Carter establishes that Van der Horst slides exist within the Mossman collection at Wisconsin. The public sources do not establish how many, when they moved, who transferred them, whether they were originals or duplicate preparations, or whether they came directly from Wits.
- The UW Mossman institutional page establishes a microscope-slide research collection but does not itself establish the Van der Horst subset.
- St Andrews correspondence between Van der Horst and D’Arcy Thompson was screened because it discusses zoological specimens and Van der Horst’s career, but no dedicated microscope-slide transfer was closed from the public catalogue description, so it is not added here.

## Research consequence

This is the sort of source relation the bibliography is meant to retain: a very large named physical slide collection is not treated as a static museum count. Wits provides the collection and catalogue state; Carter provides later scientific reuse and a second repository; Wisconsin independently confirms the physical Mossman slide collection. The open problem is now narrow and archival: determine the Van der Horst subset in Wisconsin and its transfer/provenance relation to the Wits 41,000-slide corpus.

# Technikum profile-album distribution / update chain, 1889–1907

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** MAJOR EXTERNAL CLOSURE / EBA 4/13 TERMINAL BOUNDARY CONTROL  
**Authority:** this note supersedes any earlier shorthand that treats technical-school circulation as a one-off 1896 intention or a uniquely Bär-specific marketing practice.

## 0. Current conclusion

Winterthur Technikum annual reports now document a **serial profile-album circulation and update regime** involving at least two Zürich iron merchants:

- **1889/90 — Schinz & Bär:** `1 Partie Albums von Profil-Eisen`;
- **1899/1900 — Julius Schoch & Cie.:** `1 Profil-Eisen-Album`;
- **1903/04 — Bär & Cie.:** `6 neue Profil-Albums`;
- **1907/08 — Bär & Cie.:** `Supplementsblatt für das Profileisenalbum in 12 Exemplaren`.

This changes the documentary model decisively.

The profile album is no longer best treated as a static catalogue periodically published and then merely retained. The evidence supports a stronger test:

> **profile albums functioned as renewable reference carriers that could be distributed in batches, replaced by “new” albums, and subsequently updated by supplement sheets in multiple copies inside technical education.**

This does **not** prove that every recipient inserted supplements, that all albums were identical editions, or that teaching use occurred in specific classes. But it establishes repeated institutional distribution and explicit updating.

## 1. 1889/90 — Schinz & Bär batch distribution

Winterthur Technikum annual-report donations/acquisitions list, signed **15 March 1890**:

- donor: `Von den Herren Schinz & Bär in Zürich`;
- object: **`1 Partie Albums von Profil-Eisen.`**

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=twj-002%3A1889%3A16%3A%3A56

The surrounding list is a technical teaching/reference acquisition environment: drawings, machine details, specimens, engineering books, atlases and technical periodicals.

### Secure conclusion

By the reporting date of 15 March 1890, Schinz & Bär had supplied the Technikum with a **batch / group of iron-profile albums**.

### Open

- exact quantity;
- edition/date of the albums;
- whether they correspond to the 1885 public album;
- whether they survive;
- classroom/library/workshop allocation;
- evidence of use.

## 2. 1899/1900 — competitor network practice: Julius Schoch & Cie.

Winterthur Technikum annual-report donations list for 1899, reported in March 1900:

- donor: `Von den Herren Jul. Schock [OCR; Schoch] & Cie. Zürich`;
- object: **`1 Profil-Eisen-Album.`**

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=twj-002%3A1899%3A26%3A%3A85

Independent bibliographic control identifies a surviving 1899 Julius Schoch & Cie. catalogue:

- **`Muster-Buch über Stab- und Profil-Eisen`**;
- Zürich: Juchli & Beck Buchdruckerei-Lithographie, **1899**;
- ETH-Bibliothek Zürich, **Rar 9483**.

A later Zürich architectural-history publication reproduces its cover and identifies it as `Julius Schoch & Cie, Musterbuch über Stab- und Profil-Eisen, Zürich 1899`.

Modern secondary bibliographic control:
- *Discovering Early Modernism in Switzerland: The Queen Alexandra Sanatorium* identifies the item as ETH-Bibliothek `Rar 9483` and later demonstrates catalogue-number selection/use by architects.

### Historical importance

Julius Schoch / Schoch interests belong to the same Zürich iron-merchant environment in which Schinz & Bär participated in collective commercial governance. Therefore technical-school album circulation cannot be reduced to a unique Bär strategy.

At minimum, by 1899/1900 **multiple Zürich iron merchants were placing profile-album reference carriers into the Technikum environment**.

### Strong comparative opportunity

The surviving Schoch 1899 album is a high-value **competitor morphology control** for Iron Library `EBA 4/13`:

`BINDING / TITLE PAGE / PROFILE NUMBERING / PAGE ORDER / CROSS-SECTIONS / DIMENSIONS / WEIGHTS / NORMALPROFILE / PROPRIETARY FAMILIES / PRICES / APPLICATION CATEGORIES / INDEX / SUPPLEMENTABILITY`.

Do not assume that the Technikum's donated copy is the ETH `Rar 9483` copy; these are separate evidence statements.

## 3. 1903/04 — six new Bär profile albums exactly at the EBA 4/13 terminal boundary

Winterthur Technikum report for 1903, signed March 1904:

- donor: `Von den Herren Bär & Cie., Zürich`;
- object: **`6 neue Profil-Albums.`**

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=twj-002%3A1903%3A30%3A%3A91

### Critical chronological fit

Iron Library:

- `EBA 4/13 Profileisenalbum` — **1886–1903**, **2 vols**.

External distribution:

- Bär & Cie. gives Winterthur Technikum **six new profile albums** in the **1903** reporting year.

This is an exceptionally tight boundary:

`EBA 4/13 ENDS 1903`
→ **`6 NEW PROFIL-ALBUMS DISTRIBUTED TO TECHNIKUM, 1903/04`**.

### What this does NOT prove

Do not assert that:

- `/13` was a master copy for the six albums;
- the six albums were all one edition;
- `/13` contains publication paste-up;
- 1903 is a publication date rather than donation/acquisition year;
- the six albums replace the 1889/90 Schinz & Bär batch.

### Onsite implication

The physical end layers of both `/13` volumes become extremely high priority. Search for:

- terminal additions dated c.1901–03;
- replacement sheets;
- revision numbering;
- `neu` / `neue Ausgabe` wording;
- printer/lithographer information;
- clean-copy vs worked-copy layers;
- removed/stubbed pages;
- loose or pasted supplements;
- multiplication/distribution marks;
- correspondence or annotations indicating quantity/order/distribution.

## 4. 1907/08 — explicit supplement mechanism in 12 copies

Winterthur Technikum annual report for 1907 records:

- donor: `Von Herren Bär & Cie., Zürich`;
- object: **`Supplementsblatt für das Profileisenalbum in 12 Exemplaren.`**

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=twj-002%3A1907%3A34%3A%3A83

### Major consequence

The Bär profile-album system had an explicit **supplement-sheet update mechanism** by 1907.

The quantity — **12 copies of one supplement sheet** — is particularly important. It implies that the recipient institution possessed or was expected to maintain multiple album/reference instances requiring synchronized updating, or that the supplement itself was deliberately distributed across multiple teaching/reference locations/users.

The exact institutional allocation remains OPEN.

### Revised carrier model

Not merely:

`ALBUM -> USE`.

But potentially:

`BASE ALBUM / EDITION`
→ `NEW ALBUM SET`
→ `SUPPLEMENT SHEET`
→ `MULTIPLE LOCAL COPIES UPDATED / DISTRIBUTED`.

This is a version-control / maintenance problem in material reference infrastructure.

## 6. Revised research object: maintenance, versioning and synchronized reference

The strongest new question is:

> **How was a product-reference system kept current when iron profiles, standards, proprietary forms, prices and engineering data changed?**

Operational subquestions:

1. What counted as a **new album** rather than a supplement?
2. How were old and new profile numbers related?
3. Were pages designed for replacement/insertion?
4. Did proprietary and Normalprofile systems coexist across updates?
5. Were weight/dimension/static tables revised independently of profile drawings?
6. How many simultaneous album copies did firms or schools maintain?
7. Did merchants distribute synchronized reference copies to customers, schools or competitors?
8. Did archival `Profileisenalbum` volumes function as **master/reference/update compilations** rather than ordinary customer catalogues?

All remain empirical tests.

## 8. Comparative morphology control: Schoch 1899

The Schoch 1899 `Muster-Buch über Stab- und Profil-Eisen` is especially valuable because a later architectural study demonstrates **actual downstream selection by catalogue number**: architects used Schoch catalogue elements such as profile/article numbers 3507 / 3517 in building details, including repurposing an element from a `Fenstereisen` section for a different architectural function.

This downstream-use evidence is later than the core period but analytically important:

`CATALOGUE NUMBER / DRAWN SHAPE -> DESIGNER SELECTION -> PRODUCT ORDER / PHYSICAL BUILDING DETAIL`.

It shows one way a profile catalogue could become an operational design interface, not merely a sales brochure.

Use it as a **function control**, not as proof that Bär albums were used identically.

## 10. Claim ceiling

Do not assert that:

- the 1889/90, 1903/04 and 1907/08 Bär materials are the same edition lineage;
- the Technikum's profile albums survive today;
- the 12 supplement sheets prove exactly 12 album copies;
- every supplement was inserted;
- `EBA 4/13` is a master publication copy;
- the 1903 terminal date of `/13` was caused by the six-new-album distribution;
- the Schoch 1899 ETH copy is the Technikum donation copy;
- technical-school receipt automatically proves classroom use.

What is secure is **repeated distribution, new-album replacement/addition, and explicit supplement-sheet updating**.

## Resume point

Resume from the **1903/1907 update mechanism** as the strongest new closure:

`EBA 4/13 (1886–1903)`
→ **`6 NEW BÄR PROFILE ALBUMS, TECHNIKUM 1903/04`**
→ **`PROFILE-ALBUM SUPPLEMENT SHEET ×12, TECHNIKUM 1907/08`**.

The next research operation is to determine whether `EBA 4/13` physically preserves the kind of versioning/update infrastructure that these external institutional records prove existed.

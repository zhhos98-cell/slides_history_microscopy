## 0. Current conclusion

The Georg Fischer Corporate Archives public catalogue explicitly records in:

- **`GFA 1/171 :: Georg Fischer III`**

an item dated **3 January 1895**:

> **`Vertrag zwischen Baer & Cie und Georg Fischer III betreffend Produktion und Verkauf von Geländerfittings durch Georg Fischer III`**.

Public catalogue:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/9756

This is the first tightly controlled documentary bridge found in the current pass between:

- the **EBA 4 Baer / Schinz & Bär merchant-profile reference system**, and
- a **Georg Fischer production/sales arrangement in Schaffhausen**.

Chronologically it falls:

`EBA 4/13 PROFILEISENALBUM 1886–1903`
→ **`BAER & CIE – GEORG FISCHER III CONTRACT, GELÄNDERFITTINGS, 3 JAN 1895`**
→ **`BÄR & CO. NEW/CURRENT FAÇONEISEN-ALBUM, 1896`**.

The 1896 public album review independently identifies **profiles for Geländer-Konstruktionen / Handleisten** among Bär & Co.'s notable proprietary/new profile families.

This proximity is highly significant, but the two product descriptions must not yet be collapsed into one object genealogy.

## 2. Provenance / originality gate

`GFA 1/171` is located in the **historical old holdings** under the Georg Fischer III person dossier.

The catalogue's general note explicitly states:

> `Viele der Dokumente sind Abschriften bzw. Kopien.`

The item list itself distinguishes many individual records as `Kopie`, `Kopien` or `Abschrift`. The Baer contract entry is listed simply as `Vertrag`, without an explicit `Kopie` label.

### Safe status

`CONTRACT_TEXT_EXISTENCE / DATE / PARTIES / SUBJECT = CATALOGUE-SECURE`

`PHYSICAL_ORIGINALITY = OPEN`.

Do **not** state from metadata alone that the surviving document is the signed 1895 original.

Onsite determine:

- original / contemporaneous duplicate / office copy / later transcript;
- paper and ink;
- signatures;
- stamps / seals;
- corrections;
- enclosures;
- old file marks;
- provenance / transfer notes.

This source-critical distinction matters because GFA 1 as a whole is a retrospectively assembled historical archive.

## 3. The 1896 Bär album creates an immediate product-domain concordance test

The 9 May 1896 *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* review of Bär & Co.'s `Façoneisen-Album`, Ausgabe 1896, states that the new profiles of particular interest include those for:

- `Gesims-` constructions;
- `Vitrinen-` constructions;
- **`Geländer-Konstruktionen (siehe Handleisten)`**.

It describes these profiles as created by / belonging to Bär & Co. and associated with the firm's reputation abroad.

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1896%3A28%3A%3A214

### Critical distinction

The contract says **`Geländerfittings`**.

The review says **`Geländer-Konstruktionen (siehe Handleisten)` profiles**.

These may be complementary elements in a railing system, but that is **not yet proven**.

Do not assert that:

- Georg Fischer produced the Bär `Handleisten` profiles;
- the 1896 album illustrates the contracted GF fittings;
- Bär designed the fittings;
- GF manufactured every Bär railing component;
- the contract caused the 1896 album content.

The correct operation is a **component-system concordance test**.

## 7. GF-side material/product controls

The GFA public catalogue independently exposes:

- **`GFA 19/50 — Geländerfittings: Lanzenspitzen`**, broadly dated `ca. 1850 – ca. 1950`, marked **Digitalisiert**.

This is a morphology lead, not yet a dated 1895 object.

The broad chronology prevents direct identification with the contract. Onsite or via the digital object establish:

`OBJECT_TYPE / IMAGE_TYPE / MAKER / GF_MARK / MODEL_NUMBER / DIMENSIONS / MATERIAL / DATE_EVIDENCE / OLD_LABEL / CATALOGUE_SOURCE / PROVENANCE`.

A much later 1923 GF advertisement shows complete railings assembled from **pipes + Geländerfittings, Marke +GF+**, for stairs, balconies, fences and machine guards. This demonstrates that `Geländerfittings` later constituted a stable GF product-system vocabulary, but **must not be projected unchanged back to 1895**.

The historical task is to recover the early stage, not assume continuity.

## 8. Relation to GF's broader fitting reference system

GF's own company history states that:

- its first fittings brochure appeared in **1865** with **91 items**;
- the repertoire had expanded to about **750 items by 1890**.

This provides important producer-side context: GF already operated a large differentiated fitting repertoire before the Baer contract.

However, do not assume that the 1895 `Geländerfittings` used the same numbering system as pipe fittings or that they were simply extensions of that earlier brochure. The contract may represent a distinct product family / commercial arrangement.

Potential comparison:

`GF PRODUCER MODEL/ITEM IDENTITY`
↔ `BÄR MERCHANT PROFILE/ORDER IDENTITY`.

The high-value empirical question is whether the contract or albums contain a **crosswalk** between the two.

## 9. Strong possible finding / falsifier

### Strong finding

If the contract, EBA 4 albums and transaction books share identifiable numbers/drawings/product terms, the project can demonstrate a concrete passage:

`MERCHANT REFERENCE IDENTITY`
→ `CONTRACTUAL PRODUCTION SPECIFICATION`
→ `GF MANUFACTURE`
→ `BÄR PUBLIC / ORDER REFERENCE`
→ `SALE / STOCK / UPDATE`.

That would provide an unusually tight cross-fonds study of how industrial product identities became interoperable between producer and merchant.

### Falsifier / complication

If the contract is a later transcript with no technical detail, and EBA 4 contains no GF/product concordance, retain it only as evidence of a commercial relationship. Do not let the contract force a product genealogy.

A negative concordance remains informative: it would indicate that the product identity may have been managed outside the surviving profile-album/reference system.

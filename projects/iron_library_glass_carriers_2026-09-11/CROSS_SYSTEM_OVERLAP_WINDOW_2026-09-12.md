## 2. Two distinct overlap peaks

### Peak A: c.1868–1872 — transition among record regimes

This is the cleanest moment when four different archival/knowledge regimes overlap at once:

1. **Library** — continuous printed/serial technical knowledge;
2. **Haffter** — older trade/accounting archive still alive in its tail;
3. **Küderli/Bär** — active correspondence archive;
4. **native GF** — product/prospectus/price-document regime now visible;
5. **Rauschenbach** — operational/company correspondence and staff/social record systems begin to thicken.

This period is structurally valuable because it captures **change in documentary form**, not simply more records.

### Peak B: c.1877–1885 — product/ordering/industrial-operation convergence

The composition changes:

1. Library remains continuous;
2. EBA 4 shifts from correspondence to **profile albums, price lists, cashbooks and sales journals**;
3. native GF continues product/price/exhibition documentation;
4. Rauschenbach operational records remain strong;
5. Traisen contributes technical plans and continuous content, though copy/original status must be controlled.

This period is structurally less about correspondence networks and more about **material forms, product ordering, technical documentation, and routinized company records**.

## 3. Onsite dimension

### Peak A onsite strength

- Haffter and Küderli are both strong onsite candidates because their major historical series are metadata-visible but not publicly exposed as digital source objects in the controlled catalogue.
- Rauschenbach begins its strong operational series in this window; GFA source consultation is formally onsite.
- native GF product documents are mixed digital/physical.

**Result:** c.1868–72 combines the highest diversity of archival regimes with strong onsite necessity.

### Peak B onsite strength

- EBA 4 remains a strong physical-series candidate through profile albums/price lists and the onset of cashbook/sales-journal sequences.
- Rauschenbach remains strong and onsite.
- native GF / Traisen are mixed-access, cross-carrier corpora.

**Result:** c.1877–85 also has strong onsite value, but the Haffter layer has largely disappeared.

## 4. What this does NOT mean

Do not infer that the final project should automatically be `1868–1872`.

The map establishes only that:

- **1868–72 has the highest cross-system coexistence of old trade, new commercial, native-GF product, and acquired industrial-company records**;
- **1877–85 has the strongest convergence of product/profile/price documentation, company operations and technical plans**;
- the Library remains the continuous backbone across both.

A final historical question may use one peak, both peaks, or a wider 1854–85 arc. The point is to choose the chronology from the holdings morphology rather than impose a date range first.

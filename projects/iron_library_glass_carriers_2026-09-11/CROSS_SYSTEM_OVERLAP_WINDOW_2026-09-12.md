# Cross-system overlap window — 1850–1885

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE CHRONOLOGICAL SYNTHESIS — RECALIBRATED AFTER RAUSCHENBACH SCALE CORRECTION  
**Rule:** this file does not choose a research topic. It asks when the strongest Library / EBA / GFA regimes overlap in time, and separates **material density** from **regime diversity** and **onsite value**.

## 0. Why the earlier `peak` language needed correction

Earlier versions treated c.1868–72 as the `maximum cross-system transition window` because many different systems coexist there. That remains true at the level of **regime diversity**, but it was too easy to read as a claim about **raw archival density**.

Two corrections matter:

1. **Haffter is already in its tail by 1868–72.** Its strongest correspondence block ends in 1860 and most major business-book series terminate in 1867, although some accounting/debtor/execution series continue to c.1870–72.
2. **Rauschenbach must be scale-demoted.** Public ANTON proves a small continuous bound-register core (`GFA 1/806` = 1 employee-register volume from 1871; `/809` = 10 sickness-fund volumes from 1872). The broader correspondence/crisis units are modern one-file dossiers with long date envelopes and unresolved internal pre-1885 scale.

Therefore the chronology must distinguish at least three different maxima:

- **archival density maximum:** strongest amount/seriality of primary archival material;
- **regime-diversity maximum:** largest number of different record-generating systems coexisting;
- **late product/order maximum:** strongest convergence of profile, price, sales, product and technical-plan evidence.

## 3. Three different chronological maxima

### A. c.1858–1867 — strongest proven archival density

This is now the safest **dense-source window**.

The core conjunction is:

1. **Library** — continuous technical/scientific print and serial environment;
2. **Haffter** — a large old iron-trade archive still in its dense phase;
3. **Küderli/Bär** — an active correspondence archive from 1858;
4. by **1865–67**, **native GF product/prospectus communication** becomes visible.

Within this broad decade, two sub-windows matter:

- **1858–60:** maximum direct overlap of Haffter's 555-file correspondence block and Küderli correspondence;
- **1865–67:** Haffter's surviving copybook/accounting/price/freight series overlap with Küderli correspondence and the emerging native-GF product regime.

This is the best present candidate if the eventual project requires a **large, demonstrably dense archival base** rather than the largest number of different collection systems.

### B. c.1868–1872 — strongest regime diversity

This window still matters, but for a different reason.

Coexisting systems include:

- Library technical print/serials;
- Haffter accounting/debtor/execution tail;
- Küderli correspondence;
- native-GF product documents;
- a Rauschenbach labour/company node, with bound registers beginning 1871/72;
- the opening of Traisen's represented chronology.

Its value is **morphological transition**: older trade records, newer commercial correspondence, product communication, and emergent labour/company registration coexist.

Do **not** describe it as the largest proven archival corpus. Haffter is already tapering and Rauschenbach scale remains unresolved.

### C. c.1877–1885 — strongest late product/order/document convergence

This is the cleanest later cluster even if Rauschenbach stays demoted.

Core evidence:

1. **Library** — continuous serial and technical/product print;
2. **Küderli/Bär** — profile albums 1877–85, price lists 1878–80, cashbooks from 1884, sales journals from 1885;
3. **native GF** — product/price/exhibition documentation;
4. **Traisen** — original 1880 technical plans plus represented Blaubuch sequence, with copy/original distinction controlled;
5. **Rauschenbach** — optional company/labour comparator, not a required large-block premise.

This period is less about correspondence networks and more about **profiles, product definition, pricing, ordering, routinised bookkeeping and technical form**.

## 4. Corpus geometry implied by the chronology

The present holdings do not naturally produce one homogeneous `1850–1885 archive`. They produce a **two-cluster spine with a transitional bridge**:

> **Cluster 1 — c.1858–67:** correspondence + copybooks + calculation/accounting + emerging product communication  
> **Bridge — c.1868–72:** residual old-trade books + Küderli correspondence + native-GF product forms + small company/labour registration node  
> **Cluster 2 — c.1877–85:** profile albums + price lists + cash/sales books + product/exhibition documents + technical plans

This geometry is analytically more useful than one single `peak` because it permits a controlled question about **changes in documentary form** without presuming that every block belongs to one continuous institutional genealogy.

Use only as a conditional comparator until an internal file list or retrieval-unit description establishes scale.

### Printed backbone

- **Library:** structurally essential but default onsite value remains MEDIUM because many titles have remote or interlibrary substitutes. Promote individual Library subsets onsite only after a copy-specific/rarity/local-run test.

Without selecting a final topic, the holdings can now test three kinds of proposal architecture:

1. **Dense mid-century archive study:** c.1858–67, anchored by Haffter + Küderli + Library, with native GF entering late in the window.

The final choice must still wait for staff/export confirmation of physical retrieval units and non-substitutability.

## 7. What this does NOT mean

- Do not infer a direct institutional lineage from Haffter to Küderli to GF/Rauschenbach.
- Do not treat `many regimes present` as equivalent to `largest archival corpus`.
- Do not restore Rauschenbach to Tier A without internal descriptive evidence.
- Do not use Library richness by itself as a travel-necessity argument.
- Do not promote microscopy merely because later glass plates are compelling objects; the collection-first chronology must independently support any return to that question.

# Haffter network gate and catalogue conflict

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** CORPUS-GEOMETRY CONTROL / STAFF-QUERY GATE  
**Decision:** Haffter remains a **structural predecessor/comparator** to the Zürich Küderli/Wiser network. A direct Haffter -> Wiser / Schinz & Baer / Vogel / Schoch link is **NOT YET ESTABLISHED**.

## 0. Main correction

Do not use Haffter as if it were simply the first half of one continuous Zürich merchant network.

Current evidence supports a stronger and safer contrast:

### Haffter

A mature earlier-nineteenth-century transregional iron-trade infrastructure built around:

- a wide supplier/customer geography;
- deposited warehouse stock;
- forwarding and commission houses;
- landed-cost calculation including purchase price, insurance, transport and customs;
- extensive incoming correspondence;
- multiple accounting books linking goods, debtors, carriers and commission work.

### Küderli / Wiser Zürich network

A later urban merchant network in which we can independently document:

- collective conventions;
- explicit `Kartellabsprache` correspondence;
- changing supply geography;
- firm-specific profile albums and price lists;
- direct inter-firm correspondence;
- later cash/sales journals.

The historical value lies partly in the **difference between these commercial information regimes**, not in forcing them into one chain.

## 1. What the Haffter archive securely proves

Official Iron Library / EBA 3 description:

- **15 linear metres**;
- 1953 donation of **185 volumes + 42 brochures** of business books plus **7,900 business letters**;
- over **7,000 original incoming letters**, arranged by company and accessible through a person/place register;
- ten `Calculationen` volumes with purchase prices plus insurance, transport and customs;
- books on deposited warehouses, commission/forwarding houses, foundries, debtors and carriers;
- wide business geography: Styria, Tyrol, southern Germany through Regensburg/Frankfurt/Mainz/Saarbrücken, with frequent French and English connections;
- close relationships with Johann Conrad Fischer and especially Neher at Laufen.

Source:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&index=3&parent=2&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position

The official description explicitly characterizes this material as especially rich for economic life **before 1848**.

## 2. Published historical morphology — peak before the Zürich core

A published short communication summarizing the Haffter archive/history reports:

- the iron business reached its **high point in 1842** and then consolidated until dissolution in **1867**;
- major suppliers included Basel connections, Johannes Reinhart & Söhne in Winterthur, Johann Daniel Korn in Strasbourg, Wegelin in Lindau, Baden ironworks/foundries and later multiple Fürstenberg/Württemberg/Mainz works;
- at its mature phase the firm had about **200 regular customers + 250 occasional customers**;
- it used roughly **twenty forwarding and commission houses**;
- `Lager-Scontro 1819–1855` documents a chain of depots around Lake Constance, allowing goods to reach customers without passing through Weinfelden;
- from **1850** the business lost importance amid stronger domestic competition;
- the account attributes eventual closure in 1867 to tariff reduction, railway construction and Weinfelden's geography, alongside succession/family factors.

Source:
- Clemens Moser, short communication in *Gesnerus* 46 (1989), pp. 265–270; searchable PDF result: https://brill.com/view/journals/ges/46/3-4/article-p265_9.pdf

### Interpretation

This is **not** a trivial precursor to Zürich 1870s product documentation. It is a different commercial topology:

`TRANSREGIONAL SUPPLIER NETWORK -> DEPOTS / FORWARDERS -> LANDED-COST CALCULATION -> CUSTOMER DISTRIBUTION`.

The later Zürich archive currently exposes more visible collective market-rule and profile-documentation layers.

## 3. Direct Haffter -> Zürich-merchant-network connection is OPEN

The published finding aid is:

Monique Kriescher-Fauchs, `Das Haffter-Archiv in der Eisenbibliothek im Klostergut Paradies. Register der Geschäftskorrespondenz 1835–1842 der Haffter’schen Eisenhandlung in Weinfelden`, *Thurgauische Beiträge zur vaterländischen Geschichte* 121 (1984), pp. 119–166; Iron Library `EBA 3/39`.

E-Periodica confirms that volume 121 (1984) is digitised.

Current public web/index searches have **not produced a verified hit** for:

- Joh. David Wiser;
- Schinz / Schinz & Baer;
- Bär/Baer as the Zürich iron merchant;
- Gebrüder Vogel;
- Fäh & Schoch / Julius Schoch.

This is **not evidence of absence** because:

1. the searchable web layer does not currently expose the full 1835–42 register reliably;
2. `EBA 3/24 Korrespondenz` extends to **1860**, later than the published 1835–42 register;
3. the Zürich network of direct interest is best documented from the 1850s–80s.

### Rule

Until the register/export is actually searched, code:

`HAFFTER_DIRECT_ZURICH_NETWORK_LINK = OPEN / NOT ESTABLISHED`.

Do not write `Haffter -> Wiser -> Schinz/Baer` as a network genealogy.

## 4. Catalogue conflict — EBA 3/1 must not be used at face value

The **same official EBA 3 page** contains contradictory chronology for the 57 `Copier-Bücher`:

### Fonds prose

> copies of outgoing correspondence cover **1779–1825**, 57 volumes.

### Series row

`EBA 3/1 | Buchhaltung: Copier-Bücher | 1779–1867 | 57 Bände`.

This is a major corpus-geometry ambiguity.

Possible explanations include:

- the series-level envelope was expanded during later cataloguing;
- only some of the 57 volumes are letter-copy books;
- later volumes contain a different copying/accounting function;
- the prose description inherited older scholarship and is incomplete;
- the row date is simply erroneous.

### Rule

Do **not** claim a continuous 1779–1867 outgoing-letter series.

Staff/onsite confirmation required:

- volume-by-volume dates for all 57 `EBA 3/1` volumes;
- exact book function/title for post-1825 volumes;
- whether each volume has an index;
- whether late 1850s/1860s volumes contain outgoing correspondence;
- whether this series is the source behind the prose statement or a broader `Copier-Bücher` category.

## 5. Haffter late-tail density gate

Current public series relevant to a late comparison include:

- `EBA 3/10 Calculationen` — 1824–1867, 10 vols;
- `/11 Ertheilte Commissionen` — 1833–1867, 5 vols;
- `/13 Soll` — 1827–1870, 6 vols;
- `/16 Ertheilte Preis-Courants` — 1857–67, 1 vol;
- `/19 Frachten-Rechnungen` — 1865–67, 1 vol;
- `/23 Diverse Preis-Courants` — 1842–63, 1 vol;
- `/24 Korrespondenz` — 1835–60, 555 files;
- `/25 Diverse Debitoren` — 1783–1876, 18 vols.

Envelope dates do **not** prove late-period density.

### Minimal onsite gate

Before allocating significant residency time to Haffter 1858–67, inspect:

1. `/10` — page/entry density 1855–67;
2. `/16` — whether 1857–67 is continuous and what a `Preis-Courant` entry contains;
3. `/19` — all 1865–67 if compact;
4. `/24` — determine correspondence density in 1855–60 and whether Zürich iron firms occur;
5. `/25` — identify whether named Zürich counterparties/accounts are present.

Capture:

`SERIES / VOLUME / TOTAL_PAGES / LATE_PERIOD_PAGE_RANGE / ENTRIES_PER_YEAR / COUNTERPARTY / PLACE / PRODUCT / UNIT / PRICE / FREIGHT / CUSTOMS / COMMISSION / DEPOT / CROSS_REFERENCE`.

If late material is sparse, stop. Haffter remains a background/control corpus.

## 6. Staff query — exact high-value asks

Rather than a generic `what is digitised?` enquiry, ask Iron Library/GFA staff for:

1. **EBA 3/24 export/list** of the 555 correspondence-file titles or correspondent names, with dates if available;
2. person/place index for the incoming correspondence, especially whether `Zürich`, `Wiser`, `Schinz`, `Bär/Baer`, `Vogel`, `Schoch`, `Fäh` occur;
3. clarification of the **EBA 3/1 1779–1825 vs 1779–1867** contradiction;
4. volume-level dates for `/10 Calculationen` and whether 1855–67 is represented densely;
5. whether `/16 Ertheilte Preis-Courants` and `/19 Frachten-Rechnungen` can be briefly pre-screened or photographed.

These five answers would settle whether Haffter belongs in the core or should remain a comparator.

## 7. Current role in the application

### Use Haffter for

- demonstrating an earlier mature infrastructure for transforming remote supply into calculable landed goods;
- a limited comparison with Zürich around the late 1850s if density permits;
- testing what changes when trade is reorganised around rail-connected Zürich/Basel, inter-merchant conventions and profile-based stock documentation.

### Do not use Haffter for

- claiming a direct institutional lineage into Zürich conventions;
- padding the residency corpus with 15 lm of undifferentiated archival mass;
- claiming 1858–67 is as rich as 1830s–40s;
- claiming the 57 outgoing correspondence volumes run continuously to 1867.

# Wedding 1903 memoir cross-control against Mss 25

Date: 2026-09-11

Status: SOURCE CROSS-CONTROL. This note uses the OCR of Hermann Wedding, `Jugenderinnerungen aus Oberschlesien` (1903) as a retrospective witness against the contemporary Mss 25 travel notebook. The memoir is not allowed to silently rewrite the notebook chronology. It is used to identify episodes and to separate journeys that the notebook OCR can otherwise collapse.

## 1. The pre-1861 long England journey is securely distinct

The memoir says that after Wedding completed university study in Berlin/Freiberg, received the doctorate on **7 April 1859**, and completed military service, there followed a **long journey via Belgium to England**, where he remained **almost three quarters of a year** in order to study the iron industry as a whole. It singles out **South Wales**, where he stayed at **Abercarn** in the house of a friend of the Oberberghauptmann (OCR: `von Dehen`).

After this journey he could present himself for the Bergreferendar examination. One assigned task was a **comparison of South-Welsh and Upper-Silesian blast-furnace practice**; he passed the examination in **March 1861**.

This is strong retrospective control for the 1860 layer now being extracted from Mss 25:

- the extensive South-Wales/Abercarn run in Mss 25 belongs to a major comparative study journey, not an incidental tour;
- the notebook's repeated furnace/process/quantity pages can be read against an explicitly remembered task of cross-regional blast-furnace comparison;
- `comparison` was not merely our analytical category: Wedding retrospectively says that the trip fed a formal comparative examination exercise.

Claim ceiling: this retrospective account does not by itself identify every 1860 notebook stop or exact date.

## 2. A second England sequence occurs after April 1861 and around the 1862 Exhibition

The memoir then moves forward chronologically. Wedding says he was sworn in on **10 April 1861**, served first in the Siegerland and then trained further at the Bonn Oberbergamt. From Bonn he was sent to London to organise/install the mining and metallurgical section of the **1862 International Exhibition**, where he met **Dr John Percy**, beginning a lifelong friendship.

He then describes an especially influential **journey through England with Krug von Nidda**, then head of the mining administration. The next printed page describes the trip as exceptionally strenuous and explicitly recalls travelling to **Glasgow** before daybreak, with Wedding acting as technical guide/interpreter and having to study the next day's route and monuments at night.

This creates a crucial chronological warning for Mss 25:

> **There are at least two historically distinct British travel layers relevant to the notebook period: the long 1860 study journey before the March 1861 examination, and a later England journey associated with the 1862 Exhibition / Krug von Nidda sequence.**

Therefore a Glasgow, Sheffield or London reference must not be assigned to the 1860 trip merely because the same place occurs in the 1860 itinerary. The current OCR index must preserve date/document mode before merging repeated places.

## 3. Direct retrospective evidence of Wedding–Bessemer contact in Sheffield

On printed page **752** of the memoir, in a later discussion of Wedding's ministerial work and the Bessemer installation at Königshütte, Wedding states that he supplied drawings which he had brought back from England and that in Sheffield he had been instructed by **the inventor Bessemer himself** about the arrangement and execution of the process/plant.

The same passage says:

- Wedding's England-derived drawings were used for the first Bessemer plant in Silesia at Königshütte;
- those drawings also served as the basis for the first German Bessemer plant to enter operation at **Hörde**, apart from Krupp's earlier but still secret installation;
- Wedding had undertaken a **journey to Sheffield with the leaders of the Hörde works, `dem alten Daelen und von Hoff`**;
- the Silesian plant initially worked satisfactorily while **Cumberland pig iron** was used at Wedding's instigation; switching to Upper-Silesian pig iron produced cold-short products, retrospectively connected to phosphorus and the acid Bessemer process.

Evidence level: **A for the existence of Wedding's retrospective claim of personal Bessemer instruction in Sheffield and a Sheffield journey with Daelen/von Hoff.**

Chronological ceiling: **the memoir passage does not, in the text presently extracted, date the Bessemer/Daelen/von Hoff Sheffield journey.** Its narrative position is later than the 1862/Krug discussion and inside Wedding's post-1863 ministerial retrospective. Do not yet equate this Bessemer Sheffield episode with the Mss 25 `22 July` probable Sheffield/Turton page or any single 1860 stop.

## 4. Consequence for the Mss 25 search strategy

The old working claim ceiling `do not claim Wedding met Bessemer` can now be refined:

- **secure from the 1903 retrospective witness:** Wedding says he was personally instructed by Bessemer in Sheffield;
- **not yet secure:** the exact year/date, the precise Mss 25 page corresponding to that meeting, whether the Daelen/von Hoff Sheffield journey is identical with the Bessemer instruction episode, and whether either belongs to the 1860 or later 1862/63 layer.

The immediate manuscript task is therefore no longer a blind name search. It is to locate the notebook episode that can anchor this retrospective claim.

Priority visual/OCR-control targets:

1. Mss 25 probable Sheffield pages around the July 1860 sequence (`22 July`, Turton-like heading, later `Steffeld Brown`/Sheffield references);
2. any Mss 25 pages containing Hörde/Hörder, Daelen, von Hoff, converter/Bessemer vocabulary, drawings/sketches, or references to plant arrangement;
3. later notebook layers that may belong to 1862 and the Krug von Nidda journey, especially repeated London/Glasgow/Sheffield nodes;
4. front-matter contact lists for Daelen/von Hoff/Bessemer/Hörde-related names.

## 5. New analytical bridge

The retrospective account adds a concrete transformation chain that can now be tested against the contemporary notebook:

**English site access / personal technical instruction → drawings carried across borders → plant design at Königshütte and Hörde → raw-material transfer from Cumberland → operating result/failure → chemical explanation.**

This is unusually close to the project's governing problem of how comparison and technical evidence move between site, portable inscription, material sample, test and institution. It is stronger than a generic diffusion story because the carriers are named: **personal instruction, drawings and specified pig iron**.

Keep the sequence source-controlled. The 1903 memoir provides the retrospective claim; Mss 25 must supply contemporary dating, page-level observation and the actual form of the drawings/notes before the chain is promoted into the main argument.

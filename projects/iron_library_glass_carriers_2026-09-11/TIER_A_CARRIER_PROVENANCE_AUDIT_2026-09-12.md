# Tier A carrier / provenance audit — Haffter / Küderli / Rauschenbach

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE COLLECTION-LEVEL AUDIT  
**Rule:** Tier A does not mean that the three archival blocks have the same archival morphology. Separate surviving carrier seriality, provenance coherence, accession/arrangement history, and original filing order.

## 0. Why this audit matters

The collection-first heat map currently promotes three archival blocks as the strongest c.1850–1885 onsite candidates:

1. **EBA 3 — Hafftersche Eisenhandlung**;
2. **EBA 4 — Küderli & Co. / Bär & Co.**;
3. **GFA — Rauschenbach operational/company records**.

They are all strong, but for different structural reasons. Treating them as three interchangeable `business archives` would erase important differences in how the surviving evidence was generated, transferred, arranged and preserved.

## 1. EBA 3 Haffter — business archive dominated by bound serial records + correspondence

### Public catalogue structure

- fonds envelope: 1722–1983;
- physical extent: **15 lm**;
- extent description: **2 lm special formats + 13 lm books**;
- c.60 description units;
- very large surviving business-accounting/book series;
- `EBA 3/24 Korrespondenz`, 1835–1860 = **555 Akten**.

Controlled bound-series examples:

- `EBA 3/10 Calculationen`, 1824–1867 — 10 vols;
- `/11 Ertheilte Commissionen`, 1833–1867 — 5 vols;
- `/12 Schuldbuch`, 1825–1867 — 1 vol;
- `/13 Soll`, 1827–1870 — 6 vols;
- `/14 Executionsbücher`, 1837–1871 — 2 vols;
- `/16 Ertheilte Preis-Courants`, 1857–1867 — 1 vol;
- `/17 Auszüge aus dem Hauptbuch`, 1824–1867 — 3 vols;
- `/19 Frachten-Rechnungen`, 1865–1867 — 1 vol;
- `/20 Conti der Fuhrleute`, 1850–1857 — 1 vol;
- `/23 Diverse Preis-Courants`, 1842–1863 — 1 vol;
- `/25 Diverse Debitoren`, 1783–1876 — 18 vols.

The fonds description also states that 57 copybooks of outgoing correspondence survive from 1779 onward and that the business correspondence/accounting is unusually rich.

### Arrangement warning

The official fonds description says:

> `Der Bestand war ursprünglich ungeordnet.`

It also says that during the 2014–2016 archive reorganisation the fonds was checked/reworked, the existing order was retained, and missing descriptive information was added to meet ISAD(G).

Therefore distinguish:

- **carrier seriality:** VERY HIGH;
- **business-provenance coherence:** VERY HIGH;
- **survival of contemporary business records:** HIGH;
- **original nineteenth-century archival order:** **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

Do not write as though the current ANTON series hierarchy reproduces Haffter's own original filing system.

### Current carrier judgement

`ORIGINAL_CONTEMPORARY` is strongly plausible for many bound business books and letters, but exact item-level originality still requires physical/catalogue control. Arrangement history is later.

**Structural type:** `OLD BUSINESS ARCHIVE / LATER ARRANGED; STRONG ORIGINAL CARRIER SERIALITY`.

## 2. EBA 4 Küderli/Bär — historical business collection assembled through multiple accessions

### Public catalogue structure

- fonds envelope: 1854–1971;
- 4 lm;
- c.10 files + c.150 books / c.20 description units;
- object type at fonds level: `Sammlung`.

Pre-/around-1885 carrier units:

- `EBA 4/5 Convention der Zürcher Eisenhändler`, 1854 — 1 file;
- `/7 Korrespondenz`, 1858–1872 — 1 file;
- `/14 Profileisenalbum`, 1877–1885 — **3 vols**;
- `/6 Preislisten`, 1878–1880 — **2 files**;
- `/4 Kassabuch`, 1884–1889 — **2 vols**;
- `/15 Verkaufsjournal`, 1885–1897 — **11 vols**.

Later continuous carrier runs include 43 inventory volumes (1888–1927), further profile albums and inventory series.

### Accession-history warning

The official fonds history states:

- **1980:** Davum Stahl AG transferred the historical archive of Schinz & Baer / Baer & Co. / Küderli & Co. to the Iron Library;
- **1986:** additional material arrived in connection with transfers relating to the Schweizerische Stahlhandelszentrale (EBA 6);
- **2006:** a final transfer came from former Küderli director Ernst Hardtmeyer.

Therefore the current fonds is provenance-related but **accessionally composite**.

Distinguish:

- **carrier seriality:** HIGH / VERY HIGH in bound books;
- **corporate lineage/provenance coherence:** HIGH;
- **single-transfer / frozen original archive:** NO;
- **current fonds completeness relative to historical company archive:** OPEN;
- **original arrangement:** OPEN.

### Current carrier judgement

The pre-1885 profile albums, cashbooks and sales journals are the strongest serial physical carriers. Correspondence and price-list dossiers are useful but catalogue metadata alone does not establish their original filing structure.

**Structural type:** `MULTI-ACCESSION HISTORICAL COMPANY COLLECTION; STRONG BOUND BUSINESS SERIES`.

## 3. Rauschenbach — acquired-company evidence dispersed across retrospective GFA systems

### Corporate/provenance history

- company founded 1842;
- converted into a joint-stock company in 1892;
- GF acquired the majority shareholding in 1921;
- company integrated into GF in 1929.

This immediately creates a different archival morphology from Haffter/Küderli: the surviving pre-1890 material is now distributed across later GF archival systems rather than preserved as one intact pre-acquisition fonds.

### Strongest pre-1885 operational units

Current public catalogue anchors:

- `GFA 1/780 Rauschenbach: Korrespondenz, Verträge`, 1868–1905 — **1 file**;
- `/781`, 1869–1921 — **1 file**;
- `/806 Mitarbeiterverzeichnisse`, 1871–1930 — **1 bound volume**;
- `/809 Krankenkasse`, 1872–1930 — **10 bound volumes**;
- `/2288 Korrespondenzen J. Rauschenbach 1876–1879, Vereinbarungen Bucher-Guyern 1928–1941` — **1 file**;
- `/786 Krise, Umzug auf Ebnat`, 1878–1934 — **1 file**;
- contemporary photographs survive separately in GFA photo systems.

### Carrier split

The catalogue now permits a stronger distinction than the earlier generic `Rauschenbach HIGH seriality` label.

#### A. Bound-register layer

`GFA 1/806` and especially `/809` are the clearest physical serial-carrier evidence:

- one employee-register volume beginning 1871;
- ten sickness-fund volumes beginning 1872.

These are structurally stronger candidates for continuous operational recording than a later thematic dossier.

#### B. Dossier layer

`/780`, `/781`, `/2288`, `/786` are each catalogued as **one file/dossier** despite long or internally discontinuous date ranges.

Most telling example:

- `/2288` combines **1876–1879 correspondence** with **1928–1941 agreements** in one present dossier.

This proves that present dossier unity is not automatically equivalent to a nineteenth-century original filing unit.

Therefore:

- **operational evidence density:** HIGH;
- **company provenance relevance:** HIGH;
- **bound-register carrier seriality:** HIGH;
- **dossier-level original filing order:** OPEN / sometimes demonstrably mixed;
- **retrospective GF archival mediation:** HIGH.

### GFA 51 correction

The separated `GFA 51 Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach AG` has an envelope beginning 1865, but formal AG governance records begin only with the conversion to an AG in **1892**. Do not use `GFA 51 = 1865–1949` to imply a continuous formal governance fonds from the 1860s.

**Structural type:** `ACQUIRED-COMPANY ARCHIVAL REMAINDER DISTRIBUTED ACROSS RETROSPECTIVE GFA; STRONG OPERATIONAL REGISTERS + MIXED DOSSIERS`.

## 5. What this changes in the collection map

### Secure

- all three Tier-A archival blocks remain strong;
- **their strength comes from different carrier/provenance structures**;
- Haffter's strongest evidence is dense original business-book/correspondence survival, not preservation of an original filing hierarchy;
- Küderli's strongest evidence is a multi-accession historical company collection with excellent bound serials;
- Rauschenbach's strongest pre-1885 evidence includes real bound operational registers, while several important correspondence/crisis units are present-day mixed/long-span dossiers inside retrospective GFA 1.

### Open

- item-level original/copy status of individual letters and loose records;
- degree to which EBA 3/EBA 4 present series hierarchy reflects historical arrangement;
- internal composition and chronological continuity of Rauschenbach `/780`, `/781`, `/786`;
- whether internal scans or more granular file-level descriptions exist.

## 6. Research-design implication — without choosing a topic

Do not describe the Tier-A landscape as `three company archives`.

A more accurate collection morphology is:

> **Haffter: dense old iron-trade business archive**  
> **Küderli: multi-accession historical iron-trade collection with strong serial books**  
> **Rauschenbach: acquired industrial-company evidence dispersed through retrospective GF archival systems**

This difference may itself become historically useful later, but at this stage it is primarily a source-critical constraint.

## 7. Access/staff questions sharpened by this audit

For any future staff enquiry, ask specifically:

1. whether the bound books/registers are normally ordered by the catalogue unit shown in ANTON;
2. whether correspondence dossiers have internal file lists or more granular unpublished descriptions;
3. whether `/780`, `/781`, `/2288`, `/786` contain clearly separable contemporary subfiles;
4. whether any existing internal scans preserve full volumes/files;
5. whether staff can clarify original-order / later-arrangement information for EBA 3 and EBA 4 beyond the public fonds descriptions.

Do not ask staff to interpret the history; ask for existing descriptive/access infrastructure.

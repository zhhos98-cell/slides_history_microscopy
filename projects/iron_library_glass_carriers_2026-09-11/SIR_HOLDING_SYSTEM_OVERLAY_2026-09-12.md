# Prior Scholar in Residence overlay — time × holding system

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** STRUCTURAL PROGRAMME OVERLAY  
**Rule:** this is not a ranking of scholars or projects. It asks which parts of the Iron Library / EBA / GFA landscape are visibly represented in the public Scholar in Residence descriptions.

## 0. Evidence rule

Holding-system labels use three levels:

- **OFFICIAL / EXPLICIT** — the public project description explicitly identifies the Iron Library / printed holdings, GF Corporate Archives, or a named documentary base.
- **INFERRED** — source system is inferred conservatively from the official project description, period and stated source type.
- **NOT STATED** — the public description does not identify the primary holding system clearly enough.

Absence from a public project description is **not** evidence that a scholar did not consult a collection. In particular, this file does not claim that EBA was never used; it records whether an EBA fonds is publicly foregrounded as a primary corpus.

### Artemis Yagou (2021) — Johann Conrad Fischer and watchmaking

Public description says she expected to use Fischer's diaries and related documents; the programme page later states that her research at the **GF Corporate Archives and Iron Library** produced the prize-winning article on Fischer's materials expertise and networks.

**Holding-system label: OFFICIAL / EXPLICIT MIXED Library + GFA.**

Historical zone: Johann Conrad Fischer, 1773–1854.

Structural significance:

- this is the clearest prior example of a scholar crossing the institution's Library/Corporate-Archive boundary around an early industrial actor;
- it strongly occupies the **pre-1854 Fischer/private-network/travel-diary** zone;
- it does not occupy the later 1854–85 distributed regime now visible across Library / EBA / acquired industrial-company archives.

### Other mixed/unclear cases

Projects such as Sandra Becker (plastics + GF site), Andrea Keller / Melanie Mock (Nagelfabrik Winterthur, founding 1895), and some broad heritage/Wikipedia projects use Iron Library resources without a publicly stated single primary corpus. Keep them **MIXED / NOT STATED** rather than forcing a Library/GFA classification.

## 4. EBA visibility in the public SiR descriptions

### Controlled finding

In the official Scholar in Residence project descriptions reviewed in this pass, **no project explicitly names an EBA fonds such as Haffter, Küderli/Bär, Archiv des Schweizerischen Eisenhandels, or Gonzen as its primary corpus**.

This finding has a strict claim ceiling:

> **PUBLIC DESCRIPTION GAP:** no EBA-led project is currently explicit in the programme descriptions controlled here.

It does **not** mean:

- no scholar ever consulted EBA material;
- EBA has never supported a SiR project;
- the older institutional terminology always distinguished EBA from the Library in the same way it does today.

Because the Library/EBA catalogue boundary itself has a history, an older scholar may have described material generically as “Iron Library” even where the current catalogue would place it in EBA.

### Why the gap matters

The present holdings census shows that EBA contains some of the cleanest nineteenth-century archival blocks:

- EBA 3 Haffter — especially c.1835–67;
- EBA 4 Küderli/Bär — exceptionally good c.1854–85 fit;
- EBA 1 engineering/map cluster;
- EBA 6 later association archive;
- EBA 2 Gonzen, especially later scientific/material archive.

Therefore the EBA layer is structurally more important than the public SiR programme page alone would suggest.

## 5. Time-zone overlay

### Early modern / 17th century

**Heavily represented, chiefly Library-led.**

Avramov, Neumann, Battell, Seilern and rare-book/copy projects demonstrate repeated exploitation of early-modern mining, alchemy and science holdings.

### c.1770–1854

**Represented.**

Yagou strongly covers Johann Conrad Fischer/private networks; early industrial/engineering projects and long-19c studies also reach this zone.

### c.1850–1885

**Partly represented thematically, but not exhausted structurally.**

Overlaps include:

- Haumann, 1850–1930 materials/raw-material history;
- Boothby, 1875–95 engineering literature;
- Carnemolla, 18th–19th c. maritime/iron literature;
- Piotrowska, c.1750–1910 machine/instrument culture;
- Lütolf, metal-surface treatment;
- Pannese, nineteenth-century metallurgy/aesthetics.

But the public descriptions do not show a project organized around the **simultaneous crossing of the Library's technical/scientific print system, EBA's nineteenth-century trade/product archives, and a pre-AG industrial-company record system such as Rauschenbach**.

### 1890s onward

The archival landscape and SiR record both thicken:

- 1895 Nagelfabrik projects;
- corporate financial history;
- 20th-century GF business/social history;
- industrial/corporate photography.

### 1918–1970s

**Very strongly represented on the GFA/media side.**

Priem, Staubesand, Fracheboud and Stremmel align with the densest corporate/photo/social holdings.

## 6. The structurally underrepresented zone

The most defensible programme-positioning statement is **not**:

> “Nobody has researched the nineteenth century.”

That would be false.

The stronger statement is:

> **Previous SiR projects visibly use either the specialist Library, Fischer/private-network material, or the later thick corporate/media archives. The public programme record does not yet show a project centered on the mid-nineteenth-century transition among different record regimes: classified technical print, non-GF iron-trade archives (EBA), native-GF product/market documents, and an acquired continuous industrial-company archive such as Rauschenbach.**

That is a collection-structural opening rather than a topic-novelty claim.

---

## 7. Consequence for current project design

Do not optimize the proposal around a keyword that no previous scholar used.

Instead test candidate questions against a distinct **research operation**:

1. Library — reconstruct published/serial/scientific knowledge environment;
2. EBA — reconstruct trade, product-profile, accounting/correspondence or other non-GF archival regimes;
3. GFA — reconstruct native-GF product/document regimes and/or acquired industrial-company operational records;
4. compare how the same material/technical problem becomes recordable in these different systems.

If early materials microscopy eventually survives this test, its strength will come from occupying this cross-system transition, not simply from being an unstudied topic.

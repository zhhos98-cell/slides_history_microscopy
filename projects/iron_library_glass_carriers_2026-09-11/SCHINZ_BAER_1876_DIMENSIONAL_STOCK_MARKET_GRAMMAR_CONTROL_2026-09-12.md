# Schinz & Bär 1876 dimensional-stock language — market-grammar control

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** SOURCE-CRITICAL CORRECTION / SUPPLIER-GRAMMAR HYPOTHESIS GATE  
**Supersedes:** any reading of `SCHINZ_BAER_PRE_ALBUM_PUBLIC_STOCK_REFERENCE_1876_2026-09-12.md` that treats `Profile 80–400 mm Höhe` as a Schinz & Bär-specific classificatory innovation.

## 0. Governing correction

A Schinz & Bär advertisement in *Die Eisenbahn* (22 Dec. 1876) offers rolled girders:

> `vorräthig in Profilen von 80 bis 400 Millimeter Höhe`.

On the **same advertising page**, another iron-materials advertisement, for **Hoppenstedt & Thomas, General-Agenten**, uses strikingly parallel stock language:

> `Tragbalken von 80–406 Mm. Höhe. Winkel-, U-, T-Eisen etc.`

The Hoppenstedt & Thomas wording recurs again in January 1877.

Primary controls:
- *Die Eisenbahn*, 22 Dec. 1876, advertisement page containing both Schinz & Bär and Hoppenstedt & Thomas material.
- *Die Eisenbahn*, 19 Jan. 1877, repeated Hoppenstedt & Thomas advertisement.
- E-Periodica digitisation.

## 1. Correct historical inference

The Schinz & Bär `80–400 mm` formulation must **not** be treated as evidence that the firm invented a distinctive dimensional classification system.

The same-page comparison shows that late-1870s iron merchants/agents could advertise rolled structural iron through a shared public grammar combining:

`PRODUCT FAMILY + SECTION/PROFILE FAMILY + HEIGHT RANGE + STOCK AVAILABILITY`.

At minimum, this grammar was not unique to Schinz & Bär.

What remains open is where that grammar originated in each case:

- producer catalogues;
- rolling-mill price/profile lists;
- agents' stock systems;
- trade conventions;
- engineering Normalprofile practice;
- merchant reformatting;
- or mixtures of these.

Do not resolve that genealogy without documentary evidence.

## 2. Revised value of the 1876 baseline

The 1876 advertisement remains valuable, but as a **market-language baseline**, not as a firm innovation claim.

The controlled chronology becomes:

`SHARED DIMENSIONAL / PROFILE STOCK GRAMMAR IN PUBLIC IRON TRADE, 1876`
→ `EBA 4/14 PROFILEISENALBUM begins 1877`
→ `1881 firm-specific protected Schinz & Bär roof-profile system`
→ `1885 public Schinz & Bär Profil-Album`
→ `1896 revised Bär edition with profile-local quantitative data`
→ `1903/1907 new-album / supplement maintenance`.

The archive can therefore test **how a merchant transformed shared supplier/market categories into a firm-maintained reference system**.

## 3. New high-value hypothesis: merchant as compilation / translation node

Official Iron Library fonds history states that from around **1870** Schinz & Baer shifted its sourcing emphasis from France toward Germany and that Basel became increasingly important as an import gateway.

This creates a testable, but not yet proven, possibility:

> the archival `Profileisenalbum` volumes may preserve the merchant work needed to reconcile heterogeneous external producer/supplier profile systems with local stock, price, order and public-reference systems.

Possible operations include:

`RECEIVE PRODUCER/SUPPLIER SHEET`
→ `SELECT`
→ `PASTE / COMPILE`
→ `TRANSLATE / RELABEL`
→ `ADD LOCAL PRICE OR STOCK MARK`
→ `RENUMBER / CROSS-REFERENCE`
→ `REISSUE IN PUBLIC ALBUM`
→ `UPDATE / SUPPLEMENT`.

This is a **physical-morphology hypothesis**, not a claim about what `/14` or `/13` already contain.

## 4. Explicit non-link: Hoppenstedt & Thomas

Do **not** infer that Hoppenstedt & Thomas supplied Schinz & Bär.

Their advertisement is useful only because it proves that similar dimensional/profile stock language was circulating publicly in the same technical press at the same time.

A supplier relationship would require independent evidence such as:

- `EBA 4/7` correspondence;
- supplier names/imprints inside `/14` or `/13`;
- invoices / price lists;
- cash/sales/inventory cross-references;
- shipping/import documentation;
- external producer records.

Until then:

`HOPPENSTEDT & THOMAS = MARKET-GRAMMAR CONTROL / NOT A PROVEN SCHINZ & BÄR SUPPLIER`.

## 5. Revised onsite provenance fields

Add to the EBA 4 album capture schema:

`SUPPLIER_IMPRINT / PRODUCER_IMPRINT / PLACE / COUNTRY / LANGUAGE / ORIGINAL_CATALOGUE_OR_SHEET_TITLE / PRINTING_FIRM / SHEET_FORMAT / ORIGINAL_EDGES / PASTED_STATUS / MERCHANT_OVERSTAMP / HANDWRITTEN_TRANSLATION / LOCAL_PRICE_OVERWRITE / LOCAL_STOCK_MARK / LOCAL_PROFILE_NUMBER / ORIGINAL_PROFILE_NUMBER / CROSS_REFERENCE / IMPORT_ROUTE_MARK / DATE_STAMP`.

These fields allow the physical volumes to distinguish among:

1. **received producer reference material**;
2. **merchant-made compilation**;
3. **locally re-authored / re-numbered reference system**;
4. **public catalogue/work copy**;
5. **mixed cumulative object**.

## 6. Strong falsifiers

The compilation/translation model should be reduced if:

- `/14` and `/13` are clean bound public catalogues with no pasted or heterogeneous supplier layers;
- supplier/producer imprints are absent and page architecture is uniform throughout;
- there are no local overwrites, stock/price marks, renumbering or cross-references;
- album morphology does not track the documented sourcing transition in any recoverable way.

Conversely, heterogeneous imprints, languages, sheet sizes, local annotations, pasted leaves, over-stamps, price marks or renumbering would make merchant mediation directly visible.

## 7. Revised research formulation

A stronger non-teleological formulation is:

> **How did an iron merchant turn heterogeneous producer and market descriptions into locally stable product identities that could be stocked, priced, ordered, published and later updated?**

This does not assume that merchants created formal standards. It asks what documentary work was required to make externally produced forms usable inside a merchant's own commercial/reference infrastructure.

## Resume point

Treat **1876 dimensional stock language as shared market grammar**. Resume from the provenance question:

`EXTERNAL PRODUCER/SUPPLIER GRAMMAR`
→ **`EBA 4 ALBUM COMPILATION / TRANSLATION / RELABELLING?`**
→ `LOCAL STOCK / PRICE / ORDER IDENTITY`
→ `PUBLIC PROFILE ALBUM`
→ `EDITION / SUPPLEMENT MAINTENANCE`.

Do not convert parallel trade-advertising language into an unproven supplier relationship.

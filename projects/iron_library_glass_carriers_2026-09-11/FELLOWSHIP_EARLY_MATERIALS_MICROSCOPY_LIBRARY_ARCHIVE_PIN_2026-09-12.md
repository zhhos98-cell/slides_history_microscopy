# PIN — Early materials microscopy / Library–Archive split

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** PINNED APPLICATION-FRAMING NOTE  
**Scope:** Iron Library Scholar in Residence preparation; Sheffield–Berlin / early materials microscopy line.

## 1. Non-negotiable structural rule

Treat the **Eisenbibliothek / Iron Library** and the **GF Corporate Archives** as two distinct evidence systems. Do not collapse them into a generic claim that “the archive/library holds X.”

### Library / Eisenbibliothek

Primary function for this project:

- printed technical and scientific literature;
- serial runs and journal environments;
- handbooks, manuals and bibliographies;
- printed plates and image sequences;
- specialist holdings in metallurgy, mineralogy, materials testing, microscopy, optics and related fields;
- later-entering technical books/objects that can preserve traces of earlier scientific practice.

Historical question answered by the library layer:

> **How did methods, terms, images, comparison objects and preparation protocols become readable, citable, reproducible, teachable and portable across institutions and cities?**

### Archive / GF Corporate Archives and historical/family fonds

Primary function for this project:

- provenance and working context;
- travel notebooks and correspondence;
- family / firm papers;
- production and testing files;
- object histories and later corporate records;
- photographic and media fonds;
- records of institutional reorganisation and archival formation.

Historical question answered by the archive layer:

> **How were particular materials, observations, journeys, objects and technical practices produced, moved, preserved and later reorganised in concrete local settings?**

## 2. Current project position in time

The strongest current chronological cut is **c. 1850–1885**, with a core confrontation in **1863–1885**.

This period must be read as an interface between two different archival regimes:

1. **1802–1854:** Fischer-family / travel / works-observation world; relatively strong family and historical documentation.
2. **c. 1850–1885:** emerging microscopic/material evidential infrastructure; stronger in **library, serial, specimen, image and collection evidence** than in continuous corporate administration.
3. **1896 onward:** GF Aktiengesellschaft and modern corporate bureaucracy; the corporate archive becomes structurally much thicker.

Therefore apparent archival thinness in 1850–1885 is not itself an evidential absence. It may reflect a shift in the carrier of technical knowledge from family/company papers toward:

`periodical → handbook → prepared specimen → numbered collection → drawing/plate → photograph → reference system`.

## 3. Preferred framing

Do not broaden into a general history of microscopy.

Preferred umbrella:

> **early materials microscopy**

or, where useful:

> **microscopy before metallography**

Working title:

> **Making Materials Microscopic: Preparation and Evidence between Sheffield and Berlin, c. 1850–1885**

Admission rule: include microscopy only where it changes **how material becomes evidence**.

Priority material classes:

- mineral / rock;
- metal / alloy;
- slag;
- crystal;
- other technically prepared inorganic material where directly relevant.

Priority operations:

`CUTTING / GRINDING / POLISHING / ETCHING / MOUNTING / GLASS / ORIENTATION / ILLUMINATION / DRAWING / PHOTOGRAPHY / NUMBERING / REFERENCE COLLECTION`.

General zoological, medical or cell-theoretical microscopy belongs outside the core unless it directly supplies a preparation or representational technique demonstrably transferred into materials work.

## 4. Sheffield–Berlin comparative axis

### Sheffield — c. 1863–1865

Sorby regime:

`provenanced industrial sample → marked series → glass-supported prepared section → grinding/polishing → etching → controlled illumination → microscopic comparison → image / collection`.

Emphasis:

- local industrial provenance;
- marked samples and orientation;
- laborious preparation;
- glass support;
- illumination geometry;
- early photomicrography;
- expert observational control.

### Berlin — c. 1878–1885

Martens / Wedding regime:

`selected material → prepared Schliff → looking-glass support / polishing / etching / treatment → numbered 120-section collection → microscopic field → drawing / photographic option → Wedding investigation / publication`.

Emphasis:

- institutional preparation;
- systematic numbered reference collection;
- specimen addressability;
- representation through drawing, print and photography;
- relation between preparation protocol and public claim.

### 1885 as confrontation

The Wedding–Sorby episode is the strongest comparative event, not the whole project.

Current controlled chain:

`Wedding request → Martens preparation → 120-section collection → Wedding microscopic argument → Sorby criticism of preparation + illumination → invitation to inspect Berlin specimens`.

Use this to ask:

> **When Sheffield and Berlin microscopists disagreed about the structure of iron, were they looking at the same kind of microscopic object?**

## 5. Library-led, archive-supported

Current default classification for the fellowship project:

> **LIBRARY-LED / ARCHIVE-SUPPORTED**

Reason:

- the core 1850–1885 problem is likely to be reconstructed most densely through serials, technical literature, printed images, catalogues and reference systems;
- the archive supplies provenance, prehistory, local context, object trajectories and later institutional controls;
- post-1896 corporate archive density should not drag the project into a later GF-company-history frame.

This classification is provisional and should be tested against a holdings audit.

### B. Archive matrix

`TIME × ARCHIVE FONDS / SERIES × RECORD TYPE × ACCESS / DIGITISATION × PREVIOUS SiR USE × PROJECT VALUE`

Do not count later thick corporate series as evidence for the 1850–1885 core unless they preserve a genuine retrospective or provenance link.

Priority archival uses:

- Fischer / travel / works / material-judgement context;
- Wedding notebooks as digitised prework rather than onsite justification;
- object provenance for surviving glass plates and later donated Berlin material;
- evidence of how older technical objects or records entered later collections;
- archival formation itself where it affects what survives.

## 7. Application consequence

Avoid the weak formulation:

> “The Iron Library/archive contains many relevant materials.”

Prefer:

> **The library reconstructs the printed and comparative infrastructure by which early materials microscopy became portable and referenceable; the archive reconstructs the local provenance and material trajectories against which those published practices can be tested. The project requires the intersection of the two without treating them as the same evidential system.**

The residency-necessity argument should ultimately be built from:

1. a library corpus thick enough to require concentrated comparison;
2. material / copy-specific / object-level features not exhausted by remote surrogates;
3. archive-supported provenance or object-history questions;
4. precise cross-carrier matching among specimen, glass, image, plate, caption, catalogue and publication.

## 8. Guardrails

Do not:

- call the GF Corporate Archives simply “the Iron Library archive” when provenance matters;
- infer archival absence from a thin 1850–1885 corporate record layer;
- let 1896+ corporate files redefine the core chronology;
- broaden into general microscopy;
- treat shared use of glass as proof of genealogy;
- assume `early microscopy` means biological microscopy;
- treat digitised Wedding manuscripts as the onsite rationale.

## 9. Resume point

Next research pass should begin with a **split Library / Archive holdings audit**, not with another general web survey.

Highest-value test:

> Does the Iron Library preserve a sufficiently dense **1850–1885 early materials microscopy shelf / serial corpus** that the Sheffield–Berlin comparison can be reconstructed as a history of preparation, evidence and reference systems?

If yes, retain **library-led / archive-supported** as the governing application architecture.

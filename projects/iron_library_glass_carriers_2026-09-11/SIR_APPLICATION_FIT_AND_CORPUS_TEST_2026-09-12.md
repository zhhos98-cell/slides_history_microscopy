# Scholar in Residence application fit / corpus test

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE APPLICATION-CONSTRAINT MAP  
**Rule:** use current official SiR requirements as a design constraint. The application should demonstrate a bounded, collection-intensive 2–3 week operation, not merely an interesting historical topic.

## 0. Current official SiR requirements controlled

Current Iron Library Scholar in Residence call states:

- applicants may be established researchers, younger scholars, or advanced students from Switzerland or abroad;
- preference is given to projects in the history of technology/materials, metallurgy/mining, with history of chemistry, history of science, industrial history/culture also possible;
- innovative projects making intensive use of the historical holdings are especially welcome;
- projects using the Georg Fischer Corporate Archives are also especially welcome;
- residence can last **2–3 weeks**;
- the scholar is expected to work intensively with Library holdings and reside near the Library;
- results must feed into a publication and/or qualifying thesis;
- application requires:
  - **abstract, max. 150 words**;
  - **full project description, max. 1000 words, explicitly showing which Iron Library holdings will be consulted**;
  - CV;
- applications are accepted **on a rolling basis**.

Official source:
- https://www.eisenbibliothek.ch/de/research/sir.html

## 1. Immediate implication

The application does not reward a generic statement that the Iron Library is relevant. The 1000-word description has to show:

1. which holdings will be used;
2. why those holdings answer the question;
3. why the work is feasible in 2–3 weeks;
4. why onsite work adds something remote sources cannot supply;
5. what bounded output the residence contributes to.

Therefore the current collection-first work is not preparatory overhead. It is the structural core of the application.

## 3. Current best application geometry

The cleanest proposal architecture currently has **four core physical research units**:

- **H1** — Haffter correspondence sample, especially 1858–60;
- **H2** — Haffter calculation/commission/price/freight books, especially 1865–67;
- **K1** — Küderli correspondence samples at 1858–60 / 1865–67 / 1870–72;
- **K2** — Küderli profile/price/cash/sales transition, 1877–85.

These are bounded enough to name explicitly in the 1000-word application and flexible enough to survive staff-level changes in retrieval logistics.

The broader architecture is:

> **Cluster A c.1858–67 — dense communication/calculation archive**  
> **Bridge c.1868–72 — multiple record regimes coexist, but proven density falls**  
> **Cluster B c.1877–85 — product/profile/price/order/accounting convergence**

## 4. Why this geometry fits the call better than the earlier microscopy-first design

The official call explicitly asks for intensive use of the historical holdings and a project description specifying those holdings.

A project centred mainly on fully digitised Wedding manuscripts would therefore have a weaker residence-specific logic.

The Haffter/Küderli architecture is stronger because:

- it depends on two large/coherent archival blocks;
- the material is currently not replaceable by a controlled public facsimile layer;
- the physical series can be named in advance;
- the operation can be sampled within 2–3 weeks;
- Library/GF material can be added as comparison rather than used to inflate the core.

The Berlin glass plates remain useful because they provide an especially clear physical-object necessity claim, but their scale is too small to carry the whole residence by themselves.

## 6. Application-level novelty that can be claimed cautiously

Do not claim `first study` without literature control.

Safe application language later can emphasize that the project:

- joins two underused business-archive morphologies with the Library's specialist printed environment;
- compares record-generating practices rather than narrating one firm's corporate history;
- follows how material/product information is formatted across letters, calculations, price lists, profile albums, sales books and printed catalogues;
- focuses on a pre-1890 documentary infrastructure that is less dominated by formal corporate governance series.

This is a **collection-configuration novelty claim**, not yet a historiographic priority claim.

## 7. 1000-word application allocation model

Do not draft final prose yet. A useful word-budget architecture is:

- **120–150 words:** historical problem and intervention;
- **180–220 words:** why Iron Library holdings uniquely permit the study;
- **280–330 words:** exact corpus / H1-H2-K1-K2 + supporting Library/GF material;
- **150–180 words:** onsite method, sampling, physical/documentary features recorded;
- **100–130 words:** 2–3 week feasibility and expected output;
- remainder: relation to existing project/publication and claim ceiling.

The holdings section should be the largest block because the call explicitly requires it.

## 8. 150-word abstract admission test

The abstract should eventually contain four things only:

1. historical transformation/problem;
2. date range;
3. named core holdings;
4. output/argument direction.

If the abstract requires a paragraph of genealogy to explain why Wedding/Martens/Sorby are present, the question is probably not yet collection-driven enough.

## 9. Current project hypotheses that fit the call

### A. Documentary comparability of iron products

How were iron/material products made comparable across letters, calculations, price lists, profile albums, sales books and printed catalogues?

**Holdings fit:** excellent.

### B. From correspondence to standardized commercial forms

How did commercial/technical knowledge move from person-to-person correspondence and copybooks toward profile/price/order/sales formats between the late 1850s and mid-1880s?

**Holdings fit:** excellent and chronologically maps directly onto Haffter → Küderli carrier change without requiring a direct institutional genealogy.

### C. Pre-corporate information infrastructure

How did iron traders and manufacturers coordinate product, price and material information before the thick formal corporate-governance archive of the 1890s?

**Holdings fit:** strong, but must avoid implying a teleology toward modern corporation.

### D. Late metallographic image/reference carriers

How did material evidence become transferable as prepared specimens and photographic reference objects?

**Holdings fit:** conceptually strong but currently too dependent on a small glass-plate cluster plus remotely available Berlin print/manuscript sources to outrank A–C as the main residency architecture.

## 10. Current selection rule

Do not choose among A–C on intellectual elegance alone.

Select after staff/export answers establish:

- physical retrieval units for Haffter/Küderli;
- whether internal scans already exist;
- practical order/reproduction limits;
- whether Küderli correspondence is physically large enough for meaningful sampling;
- whether Library export reveals a rare/non-substitutable product/serial subset;
- whether Rauschenbach can contribute substantial separable pre-1885 files.

Until then, preserve **A/B/C as hypothesis family**, not three separate projects.

## Current catalogue architecture — non-negotiable correction

Do not describe the institution as only `Library / EBA / GFA` when mapping the total holdings.

Use:

1. **LIBRARY — Eisenbibliothek / Iron Library**: classified printed holdings, serials, rara and a manuscript/special-collection layer whose full current mapping is still being resolved.
2. **EBA — Eisenbibliothek Archiv**: non-GF archival fonds and special collections; main c.1850–1885 strengths include Haffter and Küderli/Bär.
3. **GFA — Georg Fischer Archiv**: family/company/subsidiary/corporate archival provenance, media and objects; Rauschenbach is the strongest pre-1890 operational-company block currently controlled.
4. **GFKS — Georg Fischer Kunstsammlung**: physical art, prints, maps, views and objects. Real nineteenth-century originals exist, including Schaffhausen industrial views/maps, but many are already digitised.
5. **GFD — Georg Fischer Dokumentation**: modern digital documentation/reference-image system. `GFD 3` is explicitly a purely digital collection formed in 2022, largely for the Johann Conrad Fischer digital edition and keyword illustration. Historical dates represented in GFD images are **not archival date density**.

The four ANTON systems have current top-level extents of approximately GFA 1070 lm, EBA 90 lm, GFKS 48 lm and GFD 1 lm.

### Analytical hierarchy inside that architecture

For the **c.1850–1885 archival-series question**, EBA and GFA remain the principal archival systems. GFKS is a visual/original-object supplement; GFD is a reference/discovery layer. The earlier three-way split was therefore useful for the core series problem but incomplete as a total institution map.

### Current-boundary / historical-boundary rule

Use today's Library / EBA / GFA / GFKS / GFD distinctions for present retrieval and provenance. Do **not** assume older institutional statistics used these same boundaries.

This is now demonstrated, not merely suspected:

- the Swiss ISIL record reports institution-level `66 manuscripts / 3 estates / c.300 graphical sheets / 80 Schaffhausen maps`;
- current ANTON separately holds EBA graphical/special collections and GFKS maps/views;
- EBA 8 records that objects were transferred from GF Kunstsammlung into EBA 8 in 2023;
- individual EBA 8 objects can state that they were formerly registered in GF Kunstsammlung while marked as property of Eisenbibliothek.

Therefore special-collection boundaries have changed over time. Do not add legacy institutional counts naively on top of present ANTON fonds.

### Holdings originality rule

A source dated to an early historical period is not necessarily a contemporary surviving record. Classify surviving material where possible as:

- `ORIGINAL_CONTEMPORARY`
- `CONTEMPORARY_BUT_RETROSPECTIVELY_FILED`
- `LATER_COPY_OF_EARLIER_RECORD`
- `LATER_REGISTER_WITH_EARLIER_DATA`
- `RETROSPECTIVE_HISTORICAL_DOSSIER`
- `ORIGINALITY_OPEN`

Do not rank blocks by envelope dates alone.

### Onsite-value rule

For every candidate corpus, keep separate:

- `METADATA_REMOTE`
- `SOURCE_IMAGES_REMOTE`
- `REMOTE_STAFF_SUPPORT`
- `REMOTE_SUBSTITUTABILITY`
- `ONSITE_INCREMENT`
- `RULE_STATUS`

Do not use `held at the Iron Library` as a synonym for `requires a residency`.

Wedding Mss 23–25 are fully available in high-quality e-codices facsimile/IIIF and therefore belong primarily in the pre-visit corpus. EBA 3, EBA 4 and Rauschenbach operational series currently expose rich metadata without corresponding public digital source objects and are stronger onsite candidates. GFKS contains physical originals but many visible objects are digitised, so its onsite value is usually material/copy-specific rather than basic image access. GFD is digital and contributes essentially no source-access necessity.

For GFA, current regulations require source consultation on the premises and prohibit sending archival materials out for consultation; archive staff nevertheless handle remote research enquiries. For EBA, confirm the exact regulatory scope with staff before quoting the GFA rule as universal.

### Cross-system overlap rule

Do not choose a date range first and then hunt sources. Current collection morphology shows two natural test windows:

- **c.1868–72:** maximum coexistence of Haffter's tail, active Küderli correspondence, emerging native-GF product documents, and newly strong Rauschenbach operational records, against the continuous Library background.
- **c.1877–85:** EBA 4 shifts to profile albums/price lists/cashbook/sales journals while native-GF product documentation, Rauschenbach operations and Traisen technical records coexist.

GFKS supplies a parallel visual Schaffhausen layer across parts of both windows but does not currently change the archival-series peak calculation because its logic is art/map collection rather than operational serial records.

### GFA pre-1896 rule

Do not treat `GFA 1` or any early-starting GFA class/fonds as a continuous nineteenth-century corporate archive without series-level proof. Current census result:

- native GF before 1896 = selective genuine regimes plus many mixed/retrospective historical classes;
- Rauschenbach = strongest acquired nineteenth-century operational-company provenance;
- Rauschenbach formal AG governance begins 1892;
- Singen thickens from the mid-1890s;
- native-GF board, general-meeting and annual-report systems all begin in **1896**.

### Library serial rule

Keep three things separate:

- `PUBLICATION_RUN`
- `IRON_LIBRARY_HOLDING_RUN`
- `RUN_COMPLETENESS`

A journal's publication dates are not evidence that the Library owns every volume for those dates. Later donations can also backfill or thicken historical runs.

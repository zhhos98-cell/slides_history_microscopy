# Holdings time map — density/originality addendum

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE STRUCTURAL ADDENDUM  
**Parent:** `HOLDINGS_TIME_MAP_2026-09-12.md`

This addendum refines the current holdings map by adding a fifth structural test: **contemporaneity/originality**. A fonds/class date range can contain later copies, retrospectively assembled dossiers, or later registers carrying earlier data. Therefore date overlap alone does not establish a strong nineteenth-century archival block.

## 1. Five-axis test for a genuinely strong block

Evaluate every candidate corpus by:

1. **Scale** — physical extent / volumes / description units.
2. **Internal seriality** — recurring books, correspondence runs, journals, registers, plans, etc.
3. **Date density** — how much of the actual material falls inside the historical window, rather than merely the fonds envelope.
4. **Provenance coherence** — whether the material accrued through one organizational/personnel activity or was retrospectively assembled.
5. **Contemporaneity/originality** — whether the surviving records are contemporary originals, later copies, later compilations containing earlier data, or retrospective historical dossiers.

Do not promote a corpus to Tier A solely because it has an early start date.

## 2. Rauschenbach correction: strong operational archive, but governance starts later

Rauschenbach remains the strongest pre-1890 industrial-company block currently identified in the GFA environment, but it must be split into two archival layers.

### 2.1 Pre-1892 operational/company layer

Catalogued records include:

- property / history / contracts from 1855;
- correspondence and contracts from 1868–1905 and 1869–1921;
- employee registers from 1871;
- sickness-fund books from 1872 (10 vols);
- Johannes Rauschenbach correspondence 1876–1879;
- crisis / relocation material from 1878;
- workshop images from 1869 and 1880–1885;
- technical classes whose envelopes reach into the period: general mechanical engineering from 1852, foundry machinery from 1827, woodworking machinery from 1842, management from 1871.

These are the strongest evidence that a real pre-1890 company record environment survives.

### 2.2 Formal corporate-governance layer

`GFA 51 Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach AG` is 1 lm / c.240 description units and has a top-level date of 1865–1949, but its principal formal governance classes are later:

- Generalversammlung: 1892–1929;
- Verwaltungsrat: 1892–1929;
- Geschäftsberichte: 1917–1927;
- Verwaltungsratsausschuss: 1920–1926.

The first constituting general meeting and board minute are both from 25 November 1892, matching conversion into a joint-stock company.

**Correction:** do not describe GFA 51 itself as a continuous corporate-governance series from 1865. For c.1868–1885, the strength lies mainly in operational/company records distributed through GFA 1 and related records; formal AG governance begins in 1892.

## 3. Native GF before 1885: stronger product/document cluster, still not a regular corporate registry

The pre-1885 native-GF material is richer than a few isolated pieces, but its morphology remains mixed.

A visible cluster around Georg Fischer II includes:

- outgoing book of Georg Fischer I, 1855–1860;
- plan of hardening/annealing furnaces, 1856;
- early prospectuses, price lists and posters, 1865–1879;
- two product-catalogue volumes for keys, tools and cast-steel parts, c.1868–1876;
- poster of malleable-cast products, 1868–1876;
- fittings/utensils for steam, gas and water pipes, 1870–1879;
- price courant for cast-steel files and rasps, 1872;
- factory regulation, 1873;
- cast-steel price list, 1878;
- recommendation letter for steel casting, 1881;
- Zurich exhibition list, 1883.

This is a meaningful **product / market / works-document cluster** rather than random singletons. However, it is still not a year-by-year management or production registry comparable to later corporate records.

Official actor/catalogue page:

## 4. Personnel warning: early data can sit inside later registers

`GFA 1/1502 Arbeiterverzeichnisse` contains 50 folio volumes. Its catalogue demonstrates why contemporaneity must be separated from date coverage:

- several volumes compiled in 1897–1932, 1907–16 or 1914–32 contain entries reaching back to 1855;
- foundry registers compiled from 1897 onward contain entries back to 1872;
- a Rauschenbach mechanical-workshop register dated 1879–1987 contains entries back to 1846.

Thus the class `Personelles, Soziales` really does preserve nineteenth-century personnel data, but not all of it survives in records created contemporaneously with the earliest entries.

**Map status:** valuable serial data; classify separately as `LATER REGISTRY WITH EARLIER DATA` where appropriate.

Official catalogue:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/11537

## 5. Traisen: a newly visible pre-1890 cluster, with mixed originality

The Fischer/Traisen materials deserve a distinct node in the time map.

### 5.1 Blaubuch sequence

The archive lists four volumes of the `Blaubuch von Dr. Sigmund Schudel, Traisen`, covering 1872–1892:

- GFA 1/104.1, Band 1;
- GFA 1/104.2, Band 2;
- GFA 1/105.1, Band 3;
- GFA 1/105.2, Band 4.

However, the parent descriptions explicitly mention copies and correspondence concerning production of copies in 1960. Therefore the sequence is historically continuous in content but must not automatically be treated as four surviving contemporary original volumes.

### 5.2 Plans

`GFA 1/106` is stronger on originality:

- copies of various plans, 1878;
- copies of tempering-furnace plans, 1879/80;
- **original plans of tempering furnaces, 1880**, physically stored in the plan cabinet in the Iron Library magazine;
- later 1950–54 correspondence concerning the Traisen estate.

**Interpretation:** Traisen is a real 1870s–80s industrial-technical node, but it must be split into `continuous-content later-copy material` and `contemporary original plans`.

## 6. Revised time-map reading for c.1850–1885

The strongest conjunction is now better described as follows:

### 1850s–mid 1860s
- **Library:** very dense and continuous.
- **EBA 3 Haffter:** very dense contemporary trade/accounting/correspondence.
- **native GF:** outgoing book, furnace plan and emerging product/technical documents; still an island structure.
- **Rauschenbach:** technical/industrial classes beginning to appear, but exact contemporaneous item density still needs class-by-class quantification.

### late 1860s–mid 1870s
- **Library:** very dense.
- **EBA 3:** tailing off.
- **EBA 4:** active correspondence.
- **native GF:** a coherent product/prospectus/price-list cluster becomes visible.
- **Rauschenbach:** correspondence/contracts + employee/social records make it the strongest operational-company archive.
- **Traisen:** Blaubuch content begins in 1872, but current catalogue indicates later copies must be distinguished from originals.

### late 1870s–1885
- **Library:** very dense.
- **EBA 4:** strongest fit (profile albums, price lists, cashbook/sales journal onset).
- **native GF:** product and exhibition records continue, still not a full management registry.
- **Rauschenbach:** correspondence, crisis material, personnel/social books, images and company operation form the strongest pre-AG company cluster.
- **Traisen:** continuous Blaubuch content + original 1880 tempering-furnace plans.

## 7. Consequence for the residency map

The c.1850–1885 zone is not merely a gap between family papers and the 1896 AG archive. It contains **several different record-making regimes operating at once**:

- classified technical/scientific print (Library);
- trade correspondence/accounts and material profiles (EBA 3 / EBA 4);
- product catalogues, price lists, works rules and technical plans (native GF / Traisen);
- operational company correspondence, contracts, personnel and social-insurance books (Rauschenbach);
- later registries and copies that preserve earlier data but must be distinguished from contemporary originals.

This strengthens the collection-first rationale while also imposing a stricter archival method: the project must track **how a record was produced and when the surviving carrier was created**, not merely the historical date encoded in its contents.

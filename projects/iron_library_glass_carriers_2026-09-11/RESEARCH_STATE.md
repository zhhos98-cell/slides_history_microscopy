# Research state — Iron Library / glass carriers

Updated: 2026-09-11

## 1. Why this belongs beside the slide project

The parent “Using Up Old Slides” paper is strongest when glass is treated as a worked support whose usefulness changes with condition and task. Its British cases already distinguish several fates: an old slip can be cleaned and reused; scratched glass can be reassigned to opaque objects; a preparation can be remounted while the old support is discarded; postal movement can turn the glass itself into the component that must be protected differently.

The Iron Library material extends the question by changing **what the glass carries**. The three Droste plates preserve microscopic images from a material-testing context. The immediate historical sequence to reconstruct is therefore not presumed to be `old biological slide → metallographic plate`, but a different chain:

`material specimen → microscopic observation → microphotographic image on glass → printed plate / publication → later collection object`.

Each arrow is a research problem and must be controlled separately.

## 2. Working comparison, not genealogy

Three carriers should remain distinct until evidence connects them:

1. **specimen support** — the microscope slide carries a mounted object/preparation into repeated observation;
2. **microphotographic support** — a photographic glass plate fixes an image made through or from microscopic observation;
3. **industrial photographic support** — a glass negative records laboratories, products, buildings or production without necessarily being microscopic.

The value of the comparison is that the same broad material class, glass, can enter different practical systems of fragility, reuse, indexing, reproduction and replacement. Shared material does not establish shared dimensions, glass composition, handling practice or historical descent.

## 3. Strongest emerging problem

The parent slide project asks what part of a preparation remains worth saving when a slide deteriorates: glass, mounted object, label, address, or preparation. The Iron Library case permits a neighboring question: **when microscopic knowledge moves onto a photographic glass carrier, what becomes the durable object of reference?**

For the Martens/Heyn route, test whether the relevant continuity lies in:

- the material specimen;
- the microscopic field selected by an observer;
- the glass photographic plate;
- a printed reproduction of that plate;
- captions, plate numbers or publication addresses that allow the image to be re-identified later.

Do not choose among these before linking individual plates to individual publications or records.

## 4. Why the GF archive matters even when it is later

`GFA 25` should be used first as a control rather than pushed backward into the 1862–1883 story. Its fonds structure preserves a later industrial glass-photography system, including a laboratory class, while `GFA 24` preserves prints made from the glass plates and their later digital representations. This creates a tractable carrier sequence:

`glass plate → print → archival classification/address → digitised surrogate`.

That sequence can sharpen questions asked of nineteenth-century microscopy: which properties belong to the physical glass, which to the image/specimen it carries, and which to the catalogue or publication address that lets later users recover it?

## 5. Bounded research queue

### Priority A — close the Droste three-plate object identity

1. Locate the current item/object record for the three original microphotographic glass plates.
2. Establish whether they are accessioned in the Iron Library, Corporate Archives, or another local object register.
3. Record dimensions, physical format, inscriptions/labels, photographic process if stated, condition and image subject for each plate.
4. Identify the publication plate referred to in Droste's figure caption and test whether it is a direct reproduction of one of the surviving plates.
5. Resolve dates to the narrowest supported interval.

**Stop rule:** if public catalogues do not expose item records, convert this into one precise enquiry to Iron Library staff rather than broad-searching indefinitely.

### Priority B — reconstruct the printed metallography chain

1. Resolve IRONCAT holdings for Adolf Martens, Emil Heyn, Oswald Bauer and the *Mittheilungen aus den königlichen technischen Versuchsanstalten zu Berlin*.
2. Inspect the named years 1889, 1892, 1894, 1895, 1896 and 1897 for microphotographic plates and captions.
3. Control the Cu/Cu2O image in Heyn/Bauer, *Metallographie*.
4. Separate photographic production, microscopic observation, drawing/diagram and printed reproduction when the publications distinguish them.
5. Record any explicit language about plate preparation, reflected-light apparatus, magnification, exposure, reproduction or preservation.

### Priority C — inspect the Singen laboratory glass plates

1. Traverse `GFA 25/LAB` item records rather than treating the class title as evidence.
2. Flag only records that explicitly identify microscopic images, metallography/material testing, sample sections, microstructure, laboratory apparatus, or related practices.
3. Where a `GFA 25` glass plate has a corresponding `GFA 24/53–55` print, record the cross-carrier address.
4. Use the resulting later cases as controls for image identity and archival re-addressing; do not import them into the nineteenth-century chronology automatically.

### Priority D — resolve `GFA 1/141.37`

Request or inspect the twelve objects described as `Siegelabdrücke und Dias`. Record object type, carrier, dimensions, images/labels and original context. Until then this stays quarantine.

## 6. Possible article/project growth

The Iron Library branch should not enlarge the current Orléans paper merely because the material is attractive. Its most plausible uses are:

- a short comparative section or coda showing that glass could cease to carry the specimen and instead carry the microscopic image;
- a separate article on microscopy, microphotography and material testing, especially if IL-01 can be linked plate-by-plate to printed metallographic images;
- a future Iron Library Scholar in Residence project if the archive/library corpus proves thick enough to require onsite work.

A residency/grant application becomes sensible only after the holdings-specific chain is controlled. The archive should generate the project; the fellowship should not generate an artificial archive problem.

## 7. Immediate next decision

Do not broaden to generic histories of microscopy or photography. The next useful gain is one of three closures:

1. an item-level locator for a Droste glass plate;
2. a plate-to-publication match;
3. a `GFA 25/LAB` item that explicitly documents metallographic/microscopic work.

Any of these would turn the current comparison from a promising material adjacency into an object-level historical chain.

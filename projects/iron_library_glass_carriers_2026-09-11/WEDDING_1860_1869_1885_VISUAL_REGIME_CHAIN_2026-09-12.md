# Wedding 1860 → 1869 → 1885 — visual regime / material-inscription chain

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: GOVERNING ANALYTICAL CONTROL. This file assembles a same-person longitudinal chain from Wedding's 1860 Sheffield notebook, through his Bessemer-flame spectroscopy work, to his 1885 microscopic-structure paper. ## 1. 1860 — situated process judgement in the Sheffield notebook

Mss 25 image control places Wedding at Bessemer's Sheffield works inside the late-July 1860 sequence. The notebook preserves:

- converter/process drawings;
- Cumberland pig-iron information;
- timed whole-process observation: **`Der ganze Prozeß dauert cca 19–24 Minuten.`**;
- process/flame-stage observations whose exact wording still awaits microtranscription.

This is a works-based, portable inscription regime. Wedding records the process as **geometry + material + time + visible process behaviour**.

## 2. 1868/69 — Wedding turns Bessemer flame into a spectroscopic object

A later literature trail securely identifies the same Hermann Wedding as author of:

**H. Wedding, `Das Spektrum der Bessemerflamme`, Zeitschrift für das Berg-, Hütten- und Salinenwesen im preussischen Staate, vol. XVII, 2. Lieferung, p.117 (1869).**

Contemporary / near-contemporary cross-control:

- an 1870 Austrian mining/metallurgical journal explicitly cites the paper as by **Dr. H. Wedding in Berlin**;
- later English-language metallurgical literature places Wedding among the early investigators of the Bessemer flame spectrum;
- Drown (1871) links Wedding and von Lichtenfels to experimental identification of manganese-spectrum features in the Bessemer flame;
- Silliman's 1870 discussion engages Wedding's explanation of the violet manganese line and its disappearance near the end of the blow.

Current claim ceiling:

- authorship, title, venue, year and the manganese-spectrum interpretive connection are secure;
- exact apparatus, experimental site and full argument must still be controlled against Wedding's original pp.117ff and plate before diplomatic quotation.

### Analytical change from 1860

The process flame is no longer only a visible sign read in situ. It becomes a **decomposable optical signal** whose lines can be compared against elemental spectra and linked to changing material state during the blow.

Working transformation:

**experienced visual process sign → spectrally decomposed process signal.**

This is not a simple replacement of eye by instrument. Contemporary debates preserve skilled visual judgement alongside coloured-glass and spectroscopic methods.

## 3. 1870–71 — parallel visual regimes, not linear displacement

Contemporary discussion around Rowan, Sorby, Silliman, Watts and Parker shows at least three coexisting regimes:

1. **experienced naked-eye judgement** — skilled observers use general flame colour and behaviour to judge process state;
2. **coloured-glass filtering** — selective filtering makes visual change easier/safer to judge, particularly for inexperienced observers;
3. **spectroscopic decomposition** — spectral lines provide a different evidential basis, potentially linking flame changes to chemical species/process stages.

Parker's 1871 work at **Cyclops Steel and Iron Works, Charles Cammell & Co.** is especially important because it sits in the same Sheffield/Cyclops industrial environment that Wedding had visited in 1860. Parker explicitly argues that coloured glasses do not inherently surpass expert eyesight in accuracy; their value is transmissibility and protection, while the spectroscope rests on a different basis.

Thus the historical problem is **redistribution of observational authority**, not `instrument replaces skill`.

## 4. 1885 — Wedding moves from process flame to microscopic internal structure

Wedding's paper is securely catalogued as:

**H. Wedding, `The Properties of Malleable Iron, deduced from its Microscopic Structure`, Journal of the Iron and Steel Institute, 1885, no.1, pp.187–204.**

Contemporary English-language re-publication / reporting survives in:

*Engineering and Mining Journal*, 15 August 1885, vol.40, no.7, under the title **`Properties of Malleable Iron Deduced from its Microscopic Structure`**.

The report gives several governing claims:

- chemical composition does not allow reliable deduction of physical/mechanical properties in malleable iron/steel;
- rails made by the same metallurgical process from the same material and of the same chemical composition may nevertheless show different properties;
- grain/crystalline structure therefore becomes an independent evidential layer;
- grain size changes with cooling conditions and composition;
- forging and thermal history alter the visible composite structure.

A later metallography history independently cites Wedding 1885 as an early major contribution in the international development of microscopic metallography.

### Central shift

By 1885 Wedding's question has moved from:

> What visible/time/material signs allow one to follow a metallurgical process while it happens?

into:

> What internal structural differences remain in the finished material even when chemistry or nominal process identity is insufficient to explain behaviour?

Working transformation:

**process-time inscription → optical process signal → post-process microstructure.**

## 5. Contemporary reception in 1885

The 22 May 1885 report of the Iron and Steel Institute meeting is revealingly critical: it says Wedding's paper described microscopic appearances but was difficult to follow because no illustrations were shown. The discussion was limited, and Sir Henry Bessemer commented on crystallisation and the influence of crystal size/form.

This is analytically valuable. Even when the microscope supplies new evidence, **the carrier problem remains**: microscopic structure is hard to communicate without images/specimens. Wedding's argument therefore exposes the same issue that runs through the whole project: observation is only as portable as its inscription medium.

This links directly to the later rise of microphotography, prepared specimens, numbered collections and standardized image/reference systems.

## 6. Same-person longitudinal chain

The strongest current same-person sequence is:

**1860 — works observation**
- converter sketch;
- raw material;
- process timing;
- flame/stage judgement.

**1869 — spectral analysis of the Bessemer flame**
- flame decomposed into spectral evidence;
- manganese-related interpretation;
- process stage becomes linked to a chemically interpreted optical signal.

**1885 — microscopic structure of malleable iron**
- finished material becomes an internal visual field;
- composition/process label is insufficient;
- structure records cooling, forging and metallurgical history.

This is not a biography of increasing sophistication. It is a change in **where evidence is located**:

**in the running process → in emitted light → in the finished material's internal structure.**

And a change in **how evidence travels**:

**notebook sketch/timing → spectrum/plate/text → microscopic description/image/specimen/reference system.**

## 7. Relation to Sorby

Do not return to a one-way `Sorby → Wedding` influence story without direct contact evidence.

The stronger question is comparative/convergent:

- Sorby had already made metal structure a microscopic field;
- Wedding independently arrives at a process-to-spectrum-to-microstructure trajectory through metallurgical practice, Bessemer process work and later microscopy;
- the two trajectories may intersect institutionally and conceptually, but direct transmission/co-presence remains open.

This turns Sorby from `origin` into one actor in a wider reorganisation of metallurgical evidence.

A much stronger thesis is now available:

> **Wedding's metallurgical practice can be followed as a redistribution of evidence across scales and carriers: from skilled observation of a live industrial process, to instrumentally decomposed optical signals, to microscopic structures in the finished material.

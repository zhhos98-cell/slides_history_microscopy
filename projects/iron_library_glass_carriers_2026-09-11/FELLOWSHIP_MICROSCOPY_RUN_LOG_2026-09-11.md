## 2026-09-11 — BAM provenance / Martens working materials

### BAM Teilnachlass
A 2025 official research-state report commissioned/published through the German federal economic ministry records that the BAM library holds a **Teilnachlass of Adolf Martens**. The report states that there is **no inventory**, that library staff do not know where all portions not placed in display cases are located, and that the original/current extent of the partial estate is not known.

Research consequence: do **not** describe the 1884 `120 Schliffe` collection as lost. Current defensible status is **unresolved provenance/fate**. BAM is now the first external institution to query about surviving Martens notebooks, specimen/collection documentation, old shelf marks, exhibition-case objects, and any trace of the Royal Mining Academy 120-section collection.

Source: Helmut Maier, 2025 research-state report on BAM history / archival situation (federal ministry PDF).

### Martens notebook at BAM
P. D. Portella's BAM presentation, `Adolf Martens and his contributions to materials engineering` (ESOMAT 2006), explicitly captions two slides **`Notebook of Adolf Martens, BAM, library`**. The same presentation reproduces 1878 Martens metallographic drawings and identifies them as part of his early microscopy work.

Research consequence: the Berlin side potentially has surviving working records, not only printed publications. The notebook needs a staff query: physical description, date range, shelfmark/inventory status, whether it belongs to the same unlisted Teilnachlass, and whether it records specimen preparation, section numbers, microscope use, drawing, photography, or the 120-section collection.

### Martens 1880: microscope + photographic glass plates — source relation still to resolve
Portella's 2006 BAM presentation explicitly attributes to Martens's **1880 ZVDI paper** a new microscope suitable for high-magnification metallic sections with oblique illumination. It states that **photographic plates could be directly exposed**, that Martens gave practical advice for depositing suitable emulsions on **glass plates**, and that he nevertheless considered drawing the observed microstructure epistemically more powerful because it forced more intensive knowledge of the object.

Portella's reference [4] is Martens, `Ueber das mikroskopische Gefüge und die Krystallisationen des Roheisens, speciell des grauen Eisens`, *ZVDI* 24 (1880), 397–406. Separately, bibliography confirms a distinct Martens paper, `Mikroskop für die Untersuchung der Metalle`, *Stahl und Eisen* 2 (1882), 423–425.

Research consequence: **do not simply shift the photographic-plate discussion from 1880 to 1882**. The current source situation suggests either (a) the 1880 paper already described the instrument/photographic arrangement and the 1882 article was a separate instrument note, or (b) later retrospective wording has compressed the relationship. Original 1880 and 1882 texts must be compared.

### Open route to ZVDI 24 (1880)
The full 1880 ZVDI volume is publicly digitised through Google Books/Google Play and German digital-library routes; MDZ object identifier located: **bsb11524092**. BSB/SLUB also expose the volume bibliographically and offer reproduction access.

Research consequence: the next primary-source pass should recover pp. 397–406 directly and test the exact wording around microscope, photographic plates, glass emulsions, drawing, illumination and specimen preparation.

### BAM surviving image corpus
BAM's current image gallery publicly reproduces Martens drawings from 1878 and 1882, including etched polished sections of Spiegeleisen and steel. These demonstrate institutional survival of at least part of Martens's early visual microscopy corpus along the BAM predecessor-institution lineage.

Research consequence: query BAM for original-object status and provenance of these drawings, relation to the library notebook/Teilnachlass, and any accompanying specimen numbers or publication references.

## 2026-09-11 — Bergakademie / TU Berlin provenance route

### 1916 institutional transfer
TU Berlin's official institutional history confirms that the Königliche Bergakademie was incorporated into the Königliche Technische Hochschule Berlin in **1916**. A historical description of the TH library states that the 1916 merger brought a **large part of the Bergakademie library holdings** into the Technische Hochschule library.

Research consequence: the search for Martens's 1884 `Erläuterungen ... 120 Schliffen ...` should include the present TU Berlin library / predecessor-holdings route even if no current web-catalogue hit is indexed. The 1884 booklet may have travelled through the library merger independently of the physical specimen collection.

### 1943 destruction as a provenance break, not proof of loss
TU Berlin's archive history states that the destruction of the TH main building in November 1943 almost completely destroyed the university archive and much of the faculty/institute research and teaching documentation for 1879–1945.

Research consequence: this supplies a documented reason for later provenance gaps. It does **not** establish that Martens's 120 physical sections, his 1884 booklet, or all associated records were destroyed. Keep `war loss` as one possible break in the chain, not an asserted fate.

### Collection histories diverge
TU Berlin's official history notes that some older Bergakademie collections followed different institutional paths, while new collections were built after the 1916 incorporation and survive despite war losses. Therefore `Bergakademie collection` must not be treated as a single undifferentiated transfer unit.

Research consequence: pursue three separate object classes: **booklet/library item**, **120 prepared metallographic sections**, and **working/administrative documentation**.

## 2026-09-11 — Sorby 1885 preprint title / reprint audit

### Full reprint in Cyril Stanley Smith
Later scholarship states that Sorby's 1885 Iron and Steel Institute preprint was reprinted in full in Cyril Stanley Smith, *A History of Metallography* (1960), pp. 256–260. The 1988 MIT Press edition table of contents confirms Appendix B on p. 256.

However, there is a title discrepancy:
- *Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society* (1886) footnote: **`On the Microscopical Structure of Iron and Steel`**, 8vo, Iron and Steel Institute, 1885, 8 pp.
- Smith's Appendix B: **`On the Microscopical Study of Iron and Steel` (1885)**.
- Later Sorby bibliographies often normalize the 1885 item as `Structure` while also stating that Smith reprinted it in full.

Research consequence: do not silently normalize the 1885 preprint title. Verify against an original preprint or Smith's complete Appendix B title page/editorial note. This may be a simple editorial retitling, but it matters because the 1887 JISI paper definitely uses `Structure` and should not be conflated with the 1885 preliminary paper.

### 1885 versus 1887 representation states
The 1885 item is bibliographically attested as an 8-page preprint. The full 1887 JISI paper is a much larger publication with six plates and 17 photographic/heliographic images plus text illustrations. This strengthens the bounded question of how microscopic observations became publicly reproducible evidence over the 1885–87 interval.

### Colliery Guardian original page status
The *Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society* (1886) preserves a substantial summary of Sorby's critique and gives the precise citation **`Colliery Guardian`, vol. XLIX (1885), p. 908**. The exact original page image has not yet been obtained. Pagination across surviving indexed issues strongly suggests an early-June 1885 issue, probably around 5 June, but the date remains **inference only** until p. 908 itself is inspected.

The Common Room's digitisation project states that individual page JPGs can be supplied on request, making p. 908 a tractable exact-source request rather than a dead end.

## 2026-09-11 — Wedding 1885 contemporaneous US serial reprint

### Three-part Engineering and Mining Journal reprint
A contemporaneous American reprint of Wedding's 1885 paper has now been located in *Engineering and Mining Journal*, vol. 40, across three weekly instalments:

1. **15 August 1885**, no. 7, starting p. 112;
2. **22 August 1885**, no. 8, starting p. 130, explicitly marked `Continued from page 112`;
3. **29 August 1885**, no. 9, concluding instalment, explicitly marked `Concluded from page 130`.

The Internet Archive full-text layer exposes substantial continuous prose, not merely an abstract. Treat it as a contemporaneous reprint/variant witness, not automatically as textually identical to JISI.

### Exact primary wording now recovered for the preparation regime
The 15 August instalment explicitly states that Martens, as manager of the Royal Prussian Mechanical Testing Institute, had developed etching/tempering techniques, and that **at Wedding's request** Martens prepared for the Royal Mining Academy a collection of **120 sections**; Wedding says the investigations reported in the paper were based on that collection.

The same passage gives the preparation sequence in primary wording: small test pieces separated from a larger iron mass; coarse and progressively finer grinding; fixation with resin/wax cement to a thick piece of **looking-glass**; water to prevent heating and loosening of the cement; fine hand polishing; strongly diluted chemical reagents; controlled heating for differential coloration; and, in some cases, faint gilding by Martens.

Research consequence: the core Berlin `preparation regime` is now directly grounded in an 1885 contemporaneous text, not reconstructed from later technical history.

### Physical collection offered as proof
The final instalment ends with Wedding saying that, although he has given only a fragmentary account of the sections, he hopes they have provided explanations/suggestions, and that it would give him pleasure to show the members of the Iron and Steel Institute **in Berlin** the **proofs of the statements made in his paper**.

Research consequence: this is a crucial primary-source anchor for the proposal. The 120-section collection was not merely background material or a teaching aid; Wedding presented the Berlin physical collection as a material basis for **verification** of his published claims. The central 1885 sequence can therefore be stated more sharply:

**prepared/numbered Berlin sections → London textual/oral presentation → limits of public visualization → subsequent critique of preparation/illumination → Berlin collection offered as inspectable proof.**

This makes travel/transnational movement a mechanism of evidentiary verification rather than a separate general topic.

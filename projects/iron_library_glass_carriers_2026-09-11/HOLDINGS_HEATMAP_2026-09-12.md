# Holdings heat map — nineteenth-century collection structure

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE STRUCTURAL MAP — SIX-AXIS / FIVE-SYSTEM VERSION  
**Rule:** collection first; question second; onsite necessity third. Rank collection blocks by archival structure before selecting a research problem.

## 0. Current retrieval architecture

Use the current catalogue/retrieval architecture rather than the earlier three-system shorthand:

1. **LIBRARY / IRONCAT** — classified printed holdings, serials, rara, plus a separately-listed manuscript/special-collection layer whose current internal mapping is still partly OPEN.
2. **EBA / ANTON** — Eisenbibliothek Archiv, non-GF archival fonds and special collections.
3. **GFA / ANTON** — Georg Fischer Archiv, family/company/subsidiary/administrative/media/object provenance.
4. **GFKS / ANTON** — Georg Fischer Kunstsammlung, physical art/maps/prints/views/objects.
5. **GFD / ANTON** — Georg Fischer Dokumentation, modern digital documentation/reference-image layer.

The institution's 2024 reporting model (`Library / Archive / Art collections`) is a broader management view; do not force a one-to-one correspondence with current ANTON systems. Historical collection-management boundaries also differ from today's catalogue boundaries.

## 1. Six axes

Each block is assessed independently on:

1. **Scale** — physical extent / volumes / description units / title count.
2. **Seriality** — whether recurring internally ordered series survive.
3. **Date density** — how much material actually falls inside c.1850–1885 rather than merely sharing an envelope date.
4. **Provenance coherence** — whether the block accrued through one identifiable activity/institution rather than retrospective assembly.
5. **Contemporaneity / originality** — whether surviving carriers are contemporary originals rather than later copies, retrospective dossiers, or later registers containing earlier data.
6. **Onsite value / remote substitutability** — whether physical consultation materially adds evidence not already available through public facsimiles, digital objects, remote staff support, or reference images.

Ratings: `VERY HIGH / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / VERY LOW / OPEN`.

Carrier status vocabulary:

- `ORIGINAL_CONTEMPORARY`
- `CONTEMPORARY_BUT_RETROSPECTIVELY_FILED`
- `LATER_COPY_OF_EARLIER_RECORD`
- `LATER_REGISTER_WITH_EARLIER_DATA`
- `RETROSPECTIVE_HISTORICAL_DOSSIER`
- `ORIGINALITY_OPEN`

Access fields remain analytically separate:

- `METADATA_REMOTE`
- `SOURCE_IMAGES_REMOTE`
- `REMOTE_STAFF_SUPPORT`
- `REMOTE_SUBSTITUTABILITY`
- `ONSITE_INCREMENT`
- `RULE_STATUS`

## 3. Chronological cross-section

### c.1850–1867

- Library = continuous VERY HIGH backbone.
- EBA 3 Haffter = VERY HIGH contemporary correspondence/accounting/trade series.
- EBA 4 begins in 1854.
- Native GF = outgoing book, original 1856 furnace plan and, by 1865–67, the first fittings prospectus: a visible product-document regime, not a regular corporate registry.
- Rauschenbach technical activity is present; the clearly controlled operational run strengthens from 1868.
- Gonzen contributes genuine early documents but not a dense series.
- GFKS supplies contemporary Schaffhausen visual originals, but not a comparable operational series.
- GFD has no historical-source-density role.

### c.1868–1872 — first collection-driven overlap peak

- Library remains continuous.
- Haffter tail remains active.
- Küderli correspondence is active.
- Native-GF product/prospectus/price-document regime becomes clear.
- Rauschenbach gains correspondence/contracts, employee register and sickness-fund series.
- GFKS can supplement with contemporary visual material but does not drive the peak calculation.

### c.1877–1885 — second collection-driven overlap peak

- Library remains continuous.
- EBA 4 shifts to profile albums, price lists, cashbook and sales-journal onset.
- Native GF product/exhibition regime continues.
- Rauschenbach is the strongest operational-company archive.
- Traisen contributes copied continuous Blaubuch content plus original 1880 technical plans.
- EBA 1 is securely a late-nineteenth-century engineering-map corpus, although many item dates remain broad.
- GFKS contributes contemporary maps/views/industrial imagery; onsite value depends on physical image materiality rather than basic content access.

### 1889–1896 transition

- EBA 6 association/convention infrastructure becomes visible around 1889/90.
- Rauschenbach formal AG governance begins in 1892.
- Native-GF systematic board/general-meeting/annual-report regimes begin in 1896.

## 5. Methodological and stop rules

Never equate the **historical date represented in a record** with the **creation date of the surviving carrier**.

Also:

- **EBA 1/35–59:** public catalogue currently supplies the same broad `Gotthardbahn / ca.1850–1900` envelope without finer creator/date/type data for the 25-record run. Freeze exact dating/provenance/originality unless staff/item-level metadata adds information.
- **GFA 1/162 scale:** do not infer child count from suffixes `.110/.116`; the exposed records establish a regime, not a total catalogue count.
- **EBA 6:** stop treating the 1852 envelope as evidence of mid-century density unless genuinely early series emerge.
- **GFA 8:** do not treat 30 lm as 30 lm of pre-1885 material.
- **GFKS:** do not claim the collection is fully digitised; the professional digitisation project remained ongoing in 2024–25.
- **GFD:** historical subject dates are not dates of original GF-owned archival carriers.
- **Library serials:** `PUBLICATION_RUN != IRON_LIBRARY_HOLDING_RUN != RUN_COMPLETENESS`.
- **Library special collections:** do not add ISIL `66 / 3 / 300 / 80` naively on top of current EBA/GFKS counts; historical collection boundaries moved.

### Personnel chronology warning

`GFA 1/1502` includes registers created 1897+ or 1900+ that explicitly carry entry dates back to 1855/1872.

### EBA 1 Gotthardbahn anchors

EBA 1/35–59 are 25 consecutive Gotthardbahn-labelled records with broad `ca.1850–1900` envelopes; EBA 1/55 and /81 are confirmed single plans; EBA 1/1 is Martin Wanner's digitised `Übersichtskarte der Gotthardbahn` (1880); EBA 1/92 is an 1882 dossier. The Gotthardbahn-Gesellschaft was founded in 1871, so the broad catalogue envelope is not evidence of 1850s production.

- `GFKS 2/861`, Schaffhausen map, 1879–85, digitised;
- `GFKS 2/862`, Karte des Kantons Schaffhausen, 1883, digitised.

### GFD control

`GFD 3` is a purely digital collection created in 2022; examples reproduce/reference external holdings. Use for discovery/iconography, not as a nineteenth-century physical-source corpus.

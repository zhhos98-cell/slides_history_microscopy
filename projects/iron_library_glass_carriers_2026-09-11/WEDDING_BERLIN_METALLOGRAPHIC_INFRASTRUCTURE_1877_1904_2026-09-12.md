# Wedding / Berlin metallographic infrastructure, 1877–1904

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: INSTITUTIONAL-INFRASTRUCTURE CONTROL. This file isolates the institutional path by which metallurgical observation, specimen preparation and metallography became durable Berlin testing infrastructure. It materially strengthens the project beyond a publication-only history.

## 1. Wedding enters as an institution-builder, not only an author

A 1904 institutional history of the Königliches Materialprüfungsamt, published in *Stahl und Eisen* and based on the official memorial volume prepared under A. Martens, states that in **1877 Professor Dr. Wedding sought the establishment of a `Versuchsanstalt für das preußische Eisenhüttenwesen`** in connection with the Berlin Bergakademie.

The proposal was opposed by Spangenberg and Reuleaux. The resulting institutional settlement did not create Wedding's proposed institute in exactly that form, but the Bergakademie did establish the **Chemisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt** under Finkener, while the Mechanisch-Technische testing line remained associated with the Gewerbeakademie / later Technische Hochschule.

Primary routing/control:
- *Stahl und Eisen*, 15 July 1904, `Das Königliche Materialprüfungsamt der Technischen Hochschule Berlin`, esp. pp.807–811; institutional-history lines corresponding to printed pp.807–808.

Analytical consequence:

> Wedding's later microscopy sits inside an institutional project he had already tried to build in 1877: a state testing infrastructure specifically for iron metallurgy.

Claim ceiling: the 1877 proposal does not by itself prove that Wedding foresaw metallography as the later institutional form. It does establish his direct role in attempting to create a Prussian iron-testing institution.

## 2. Martens creates a systematic microscopic branch inside mechanical testing

The same 1904 history states that **A. Martens was the first in Germany, from 1878, to pursue microscopic examination of metals systematically, particularly on iron polished sections (`Eisenschliffe`)**.

In **1884**, when the Mechanisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt moved to Charlottenburg / the new Technische Hochschule building, Martens became its director.

This independently fits the already controlled material object:

**A. Martens, `Erläuterungen ... Sammlung von 120 Schliffen ...`, Berlin, 1884.**

The collection now has at least one primary-controlled durable item identity:

**`Gießereiroheisen (Nr. 36)`**, re-cited in *Stahl und Eisen* in 1900 together with the treatment/colour response recorded for that specimen.

Working institutional sequence:

**mechanical/material testing → systematic Eisenschliff microscopy → numbered comparative specimen collection.**

## 3. 1886: specimen preparation becomes an institutional subunit

The strongest new control is the 1904 history's description of the Chemisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt and its later branches.

It states that a section/branch **`zur Herstellung von Schliffen für mikroskopische Untersuchungen`** was added in **1886**.

This is a major transition.

Before this point, `Schliff preparation` can appear as a researcher's technical method. By 1886 it has become a named institutional function inside a Prussian technical testing establishment.

Therefore use:

**`INSTITUTIONAL_SCHLIFF_PREPARATION_BRANCH = 1886, PRIMARY-CONTROLLED VIA 1904 OFFICIAL HISTORY`.**

This closely follows:
- Martens' systematic microscopic work from 1878;
- the 1884 120-section reference collection;
- Wedding's 1885 JISI microscopic-structure intervention;
- Sorby's 1885/86 methodological criticism of Wedding's specimen preparation.

Do not infer a direct causal chain from Sorby's criticism to the branch's creation without additional evidence. The chronology nevertheless shows that sample preparation itself was becoming institutionally separable and administratively visible at exactly this moment.

## 4. 1895: Metallographie becomes a formal department

The 1904 history states that in **1895**, when the two Prussian technical testing institutions were brought together as the Königliches Materialprüfungsamt, formal departments for:

- **Metallographie**;
- **allgemeine Chemie**

were added.

The Metallographie / chemistry structure developed from the connection between:

- the metallographic branch of metal testing;
- the Chemisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt;
- its later attached branch for Schliff preparation (1886), among other units.

This yields a controlled institutional sequence:

**microscopic method → dedicated specimen-preparation function → formal metallography department.**

By 1904 Abteilung 4 für Metallographie was under **Professor Heyn** and explicitly carried out metallurgical, microscopic, chemical and physical investigations of iron and other metals.

## 5. Metallography is coupled to standards, contracts and public/private testing

The same history shows that the testing institutions were not isolated academic laboratories.

From the 1880s onward they worked on large programmes funded by ministries, professional associations and private industry, including railway material, heated iron, corrosion, nickel steels, wire, gun-barrel steel and many other materials.

The institute's 1904 mandate included:

- improving testing methods, machines and instruments;
- testing materials for public/scientific purposes or for paying public/private clients;
- issuing official certificates and expert reports;
- acting as arbitrator in disputes over technical materials;
- teaching students and practitioners;
- allowing outside researchers to use facilities where possible.

This is crucial for the Iron Library problem. The institutional endpoint is not simply `scientists study microstructure` but:

**prepared object + standard procedure + institutional comparison + official judgement + reusable report/reference.**

## 6. The 1904 metallography laboratory materialises the full carrier chain

The 1904 facility description is unusually concrete.

The Metallographie department included a **Schleifraum** with:

- cold saw;
- shaping machine;
- lathe;
- auxiliary apparatus for cutting and preparing specimens;
- grinding/polishing currently done on the lathe, with a small vertical-spindle grinding machine planned.

The microscopy room contained:

- a **Martens-type microphotographic apparatus**;
- several microscopes for photographic recording at lower magnifications;
- equipment for producing stereoscopic structure images;
- mineralogical/petrographic microscopes;
- a darkroom kept physically separate from the microscope room to avoid moisture.

This is the mature institutional architecture of the carrier chain already visible in Wedding/Martens work:

**sample → cutting → preparation → polishing → microscope → photographic apparatus → darkroom → image/reference.**

## 7. Revised relation to the Wedding 1885–1893 method chain

The publication/method chain now sits inside this institutional chronology:

**1877 Wedding proposes Prussian iron-testing institute**
→ **1878 Martens systematic metal microscopy / Eisenschliffe**
→ **1884 Martens 120-section collection**
→ **1885 Wedding microscopic-structure paper based on Berlin prepared specimens**
→ **1886 institutional branch for production of microscopic sections**
→ **1888 Wedding projected micro-images**
→ **1889 Wedding explicitly writes on light imaging + production of sections**
→ **1893 Wedding handbooks microscopy / Schliff production / interpretation**
→ **1895 formal Metallographie department**
→ **1899 third parties follow Wedding preparation instructions; Heyn codifies mature preparation/illumination methods**
→ **1904 full Materialprüfungsamt metallography infrastructure with cutting, polishing, microscopy and microphotography rooms.**

This is a much stronger institutional genealogy than a narrative of instrument adoption alone.

## 9. Claim ceilings

Secure:

- Wedding proposed a Prussian iron-metallurgy testing institute in 1877;
- the Bergakademie subsequently established the Chemisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt, though not simply as Wedding's proposal realised intact;
- Martens systematically pursued microscopic examination of metals / Eisenschliffe from 1878;
- Martens headed the Mechanisch-Technische Versuchsanstalt from 1884;
- a branch for **production of sections for microscopic investigation** existed from 1886 in the institutional genealogy reported in 1904;
- formal Metallographie and general-chemistry departments emerged with the 1895 consolidation;
- by 1904 Metallographie was an explicit department under Heyn with integrated specimen preparation, microscopy and microphotography facilities.

Strong inference:

- specimen preparation became a distinct institutional function rather than remaining an invisible craft stage;
- Berlin metallography emerged through the coupling of testing, comparative specimens, preparation, image production, teaching and official judgement;
- the Wedding/Martens method publications should be read as part of this institutionalisation process.

Open:

- exact administrative file for Wedding's 1877 proposal;
- personnel and workflow of the 1886 Schliff-preparation branch;
- direct organisational connection between Martens' 120 numbered sections and the 1886 branch;
- whether Wedding directly supervised or used that branch;
- when specific numbering/image-reference conventions became formal institutional standards.

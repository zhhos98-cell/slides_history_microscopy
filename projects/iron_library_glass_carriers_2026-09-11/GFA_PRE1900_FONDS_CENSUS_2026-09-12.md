# GFA pre-1900 fonds census

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE STRUCTURAL CENSUS  
**Rule:** identify which current top-level GFA fonds actually provide coherent nineteenth-century provenance. Do not equate a fonds envelope start date with a continuous contemporary archive.

## 1. Main GFAG record group: current top-level fonds

The current `GFA_GFAG :: Georg Fischer AG` record group contains 13 top-level fonds. The pre-1900-starting entries are unusually few:

| Fonds | Envelope | Extent | Pre-1900 structural reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **GFA 5 — Personalabteilung** | 1862–2019 | 18 lm / c.320 units | Early envelope, but department identity and controlled heads are much later; early personnel data often survive in later-created registers. Not a demonstrated continuous 1862 personnel-office archive. |
| **GFA 8 — Karten und Pläne** | 1830–2001 | 30 lm / c.140 units | Assembled plan collection, formerly unordered and reorganized 2014–16. Genuine early plans exist, but pre-1885 density is concentrated in a few nodes; not a broad continuous nineteenth-century provenance series. |
| **GFA 2 — Verwaltungsrat und Konzernleitung** | 1896–2023 | 41/42 lm / c.4,000 units | First genuinely systematic native-GF corporate governance block. Board class begins 1896. |
| **GFA 34 — Generalversammlung** | 1896–2023 | 3 lm / c.150 units | Original shareholder/general-meeting records since company formation in 1896. |

All other current GFAG top-level fonds begin in the twentieth century or later: GFA 4 communications 1947+, GFA 6 finance 1906+, GFA 10 social department 1922+, GFA 28 works school 1915+, GFA 39 pensions 1916+, GFA 45 pensioners association 1989+, GFA 53 investment committee 1997+, GFA 54 sport club 1965+, GFA 58 employee representation 1918+.

### Structural result

The present GFAG fonds architecture itself confirms a sharp break:

> **Before 1896 there is no currently identified large, regular, native-GF governance fonds comparable to the post-1896 corporate series.**

The two earlier envelope fonds, GFA 5 and GFA 8, are not evidence for a thick nineteenth-century bureaucratic registry because their early content/carrier structure is mixed.

## 2. Publication record group reinforces 1896

The current publication fonds begin as follows:

- `GFA 33 — Geschäftsberichte Georg Fischer AG`: **1896–2025**, c.4 lm / c.400 description units;
- `GFA 30 — Publikationen GF und Familie Fischer`: 1903+;
- `GFA 31 — Wissenschaftliche Publikationen ...`: 1931+;
- `GFA 9 — Mitarbeiter-/Kunden-/Firmenzeitschriften`: 1944+;
- `GFA 43 — Nachhaltigkeitsberichte`: 1999+.

`GFA 33/1` is explicitly the **first annual report, 1896**. The serial continues year by year.

Together with GFA 2 and GFA 34, this provides three independent systematic record families beginning at the same institutional transition:

- board / executive governance — 1896;
- general meetings — 1896;
- annual reports — 1896.

### Verdict

**1896 is not merely a convenient narrative boundary; it is a catalogue-visible archival caesura across multiple independent corporate record systems.**

## 3. Singen: second major corporate thickening at 1893/95+

`GFA 22 — Historisches Archiv Werk Singen` is a large coherent site archive:

- envelope: 1893–2017;
- extent: c.71 lm;
- c.1,400 description units;
- Singen works established/built in the mid-1890s and incorporated into the expanding GF company system.

### Verdict

Singen is a major late-nineteenth-/twentieth-century block, but it is **not** evidence for c.1850–1885. It marks the same broader 1890s institutional thickening visible in GF governance records.

## 4. Rauschenbach: the major nineteenth-century acquired provenance

Rauschenbach is structurally different from native GF.

Actor history:

- founded 1842;
- converted to an Aktiengesellschaft in 1892;
- GF acquired majority control in 1921;
- integrated into GF in 1929.

### 4.1 Operational records in GFA 1

The strongest pre-1890 material currently sits in GFA 1 rather than in the later separated formal-governance fonds:

- `GFA 1/780` correspondence/contracts, 1868–1905;
- `GFA 1/781` correspondence/contracts, 1869–1921;
- `GFA 1/806` employee register, 1871–1930;
- `GFA 1/809` sickness fund, 1872–1930, 10 vols;
- `GFA 1/2288` includes correspondence 1876–79;
- `GFA 1/786` crisis/relocation material from 1878;
- technical machine classes and contemporary workshop images extend the operational record.

### 4.2 GFA 51 formal governance

`GFA 51 — Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach AG` has the broad envelope 1865–1949, c.1 lm and c.240 units, but its formal AG governance starts in **1892**:

- first constitutive general meeting: 25 Nov 1892;
- first board minutes: 25 Nov 1892;
- Generalversammlung class: 1892+;
- Verwaltungsrat class: 1892+.

The archive's earlier material belongs to `Diverses` / retrospective history rather than an AG governance sequence.

### Verdict

**Rauschenbach is the strongest coherent nineteenth-century industrial-company provenance in the GFA environment, but its pre-1892 strength is operational/company material preserved largely in GFA 1, not formal AG governance in GFA 51. Keep its provenance distinct from native GF.**

## 5. Other separated subsidiary fonds do not create another c.1850–1885 core

### GFA 50 — GF Paris

- envelope: 1892–1962;
- c.1 lm / c.230 description units;
- GF's own Paris sales company was founded in **1904**;
- earlier French representation was handled by the Wanner firm.

The 1892 fonds start therefore does not establish an 1892 GF Paris corporate series. The fonds mainly contains later general-meeting/board/sales-organization records.

**Verdict:** not a pre-1885 block; even its pre-1904 envelope requires interpretation.

### GFA 52 — Elektrostahlwerke Giubiasco und Schaffhausen

- envelope: **1906–1956**;
- c.1 lm / c.200 description units;
- Geissberg electrical steel foundry built by Georg Fischer III after leaving GF in 1902; production from 1907;
- Giubiasco St. Gotthard plant established 1915; GF acquired the works in 1917.

**Verdict:** twentieth-century technology/company block, not relevant to pre-1885 density.

### GFA 41 / photo-linked Traisen material

Public actor indexing exposes records such as `GFA 41/74.2 Werk Traisen, 1880`, but these are **photo/media records**, not evidence that `GFA 41` is a large contemporaneous Traisen corporate fonds. Do not promote the signature group from isolated actor hits.

## 6. Current pre-1900 GFA morphology

The GFA landscape before 1900 now resolves into four different layers:

### A. Native GF before 1896 — selective regimes, not thick bureaucracy

Strongest currently controlled blocks:

- product / market-document regime c.1865–83;
- selected original technical plans;
- selected exhibition/certification records;
- Traisen original technical plans;
- family/outgoing correspondence islands.

Broad GFA 1 classes such as production history, exhibitions, documentation, buildings and communication remain mixed/retrospective and require carrier-level filtering.

### B. Acquired nineteenth-century company provenance

- **Rauschenbach** — the major case, operationally strong from late 1860s onward.

### C. 1890s institutional thickening

- Rauschenbach formal AG governance — 1892+;
- Singen site/archive — 1893/95+;
- GF AG formation — 1895/96;
- GF board/executive records — 1896+;
- GF general meetings — 1896+;
- GF annual reports — 1896+.

### D. Twentieth-century separated subsidiary/system fonds

- GF Paris — effectively 1904+ corporate activity despite 1892 envelope;
- Elektrostahlwerke — 1906+;
- later functional, technical, media and social fonds.

## 7. Consequence for the c.1850–1885 archive map

The census strengthens rather than weakens the earlier conclusion:

> **The c.1850–1885 weakness is specifically a weakness of native-GF bureaucratic/formal corporate record series. It is not a weakness of the total Iron Library / EBA / GFA research environment.**

For that interval, the structurally strongest evidence systems remain:

1. **Library** — subject-classified nineteenth-century printed holdings and serials;
2. **EBA 3 Haffter** — c.1835–67 trade/accounting/correspondence archive;
3. **EBA 4 Küderli/Bär** — c.1854–85 commercial/product/profile archive;
4. **Rauschenbach operational records** — late-1860s–1880s industrial-company archive;
5. **native GF product/market-document regime** — c.1865–83, narrower but genuine;
6. selected original technical/visual carriers where contemporaneity can be established.

The major corporate-fonds environment arrives only in the 1890s.

## 8. Stop rule

Do not keep searching random numbered GFA fonds in the hope of finding another hidden c.1850–1885 corporate archive unless:

- the top-level fonds envelope predates 1900 **and**
- the producing institution existed in the target period **and**
- series-level records demonstrate recurring contemporary carriers.

Current controlled separated-fonds evidence does not reveal another block comparable to Rauschenbach for 1850–1885.

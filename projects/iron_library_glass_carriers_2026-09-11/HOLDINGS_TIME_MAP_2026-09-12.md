# Holdings time map — Library / EBA / GFA / prior SiR

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE STRUCTURAL MAP  
**Rule:** collection first; question second. This map records where large or internally coherent holdings actually thicken over time. It does not select a final research problem.

## 0. Reading key

- **LIBRARY** = Eisenbibliothek classified printed holdings and serials.
- **EBA** = Eisenbibliothek Archiv, non-GF archival fonds held by the Iron Library.
- **GFA** = Georg Fischer Corporate Archives.
- **SiR overlay** = publicly described Scholar in Residence projects, used only to see what historical zones/holding types have already been researched.

Density labels are qualitative and based on currently controlled catalogue structure, not raw page counts:

- `VERY HIGH` = large, internally systematic/continuous corpus.
- `HIGH` = substantial coherent corpus over the period.
- `MEDIUM` = useful but partial or retrospective/assembled.
- `LOW` = only isolated/late/retrospective material currently visible.
- `OPEN` = top-level dates are promising but internal date density remains unresolved.

## 1. The archive is not one continuous time series

The central structural result is that the holdings form **overlapping temporal islands rather than a single uniformly thick archive**.

### 1.1 Library time profile

The Library is the only system that stays broadly dense across the entire nineteenth century. The 2011 historical-holdings census counted **2,603 pre-1900 titles from the nineteenth century** and described the holdings as organized by an internal subject classification. It also counted c. **15,550 periodical volumes / c.750 periodical and series titles** at that time.

For the nineteenth century, the old holdings include:

- mining: 375 titles;
- geology/geognosy: more than 100 titles;
- mineralogy: 110 titles;
- iron/steel production: roughly 300 nineteenth-century titles within a 370-title class;
- processing/forming of iron and steel: c.150 titles overall;
- metallurgy: 272 titles overall, defined broadly around physical, chemical and technical properties of metals/materials;
- research-travel reports: 123 titles, including nineteenth-century mining/geoscientific travel literature;
- world-exhibition publications: 95 nineteenth-century titles;
- periodicals: 700 titles in the historical census, **123 nineteenth-century titles**, including long runs such as *Dinglers Polytechnisches Journal* (1820–1931) and *Annales des Mines* (from 1816).

**Structural implication:** c.1850–1885 should be treated first as a **classified printed knowledge environment**, not as a few named books.

Official sources:
- https://www.eisenbibliothek.ch/de/ressources/library.html
- https://www.eisenbibliothek.ch/content/dam/ironlibrary/documents/benutzungsordnung/hb_schweiz_band_02_001_524.pdf (Eisenbibliothek entry, pp. 514–517)

## 3. EBA temporal anatomy

### 3.1 EBA 3 Haffter is an early/mid-nineteenth-century island, not a uniform 1722–1983 block

Top-level: 15 lm, c.60 description units; donation preserved as a closed `Haffter-Archiv`.

The series-level dates show the actual density:

- Copier-Bücher: 1779–1867, 57 vols;
- Journals: 1825–1866, 11 vols;
- Cassa-Bücher: 1825–1905, 5 vols;
- Wechsel-Copier-Bücher: 1825–1869;
- Soll & Haben: 1779–1872;
- Calculationen: 1824–1867, 10 vols;
- Ertheilte Commissionen: 1833–1867;
- Preis-Courants: 1857–1867;
- Frachten-Rechnungen: 1865–1867;
- Conti der Fuhrleute: 1850–1857;
- Diverse Preis-Courants: 1842–1863;
- Korrespondenz: 1835–1860, **555 files**.

**Interpretation:** EBA 3 is exceptionally dense for roughly **1835–1867**, with a long accounting tail. It is not an equally strong 1870s–80s archive.

Official catalogue: https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&index=3&parent=2&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position

### 3.2 EBA 4 Küderli/Bär is a compact 1854–1885 bridge archive

Top-level: 4 lm, c.150 books, c.20 description units.

Pre-/around-1885 series:

- Convention der Zürcher Eisenhändler: 1854;
- correspondence: 1858–1872;
- price lists: 1878–1880;
- Profileisenalbum: 1877–1885, 3 vols;
- cash book: 1884–1889;
- sales journal: 1885–1897, 11 vols;
- later profile albums and inventories continue the sequence.

**Interpretation:** smaller than Haffter but unusually well aligned to the **1854–1885** window. It preserves market forms, product profiles, prices and commercial ordering across exactly the period where GFA's own formal administrative archive is thin.

Official catalogue: https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/EBA%204

### 3.3 EBA 6: the 1852 start date is misleading for our central period

Top-level: 39 lm, 1852–2011.

The first publicly visible institutional layer shows:

- VOE statutes from 1889;
- agreement between Zurich dealers and VOE from 1890 (preserved partly through later copies/notebooks);
- central-Swiss dealer association statutes from 1898;
- Basel association from 1908;
- much of the organized convention/market-regulation material is twentieth century.

**Interpretation:** EBA 6 is a **large association archive whose coherent institutional life becomes visible around 1889/90**, not currently evidence for a thick 1850s–70s block. Keep the top-level 1852 date open, but do not use it as proof of early density.

Controlled example: https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&index=1&parent=15&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position

### 3.4 EBA 7 should be demoted for 1850–1885

EBA 7 is only 3 lm and is explicitly a heterogeneous container. Its visible classes are mainly later: Graubünden mining 1906–41, ore-deposit study society 1922–57, Granger 1897–1956, Schütt 1968–89, etc. The only clearly early named mini-fonds is Viktor Heberlein 1848–50, plus a heterogeneous `Diverses` class beginning 1869.

**Interpretation:** do not let the top-level date `1848–1989` make EBA 7 look like a major mid-century corpus.

## 4. GFA temporal anatomy

### 4.1 GFA 1 is a retrospective historical container, not a continuous nineteenth-century registry

GFA 1 is enormous (280 lm, c.3,900 description units), but its archival formation matters:

- historical collecting began in the 1920s;
- the historical company archive was formally established in 1943;
- a 1945 filing plan shaped later arrangement;
- material was lost during later spatial relocations;
- present classes/subclasses were formed/reworked in the 2014–16 reorganization.

Current top-level nineteenth-century-starting classes include:

- Ausstellungen, Messen — 1804–1971;
- Dokumentation — 1860–2025;
- Familie Fischer — 1525–2023;
- Firmenübernahmen / subsidiaries / holdings — 1772–1998;
- Giessereien, Produktion, Produkte — 1817–2006;
- Grundstücke und Gebäude — 1799–1990;
- Kommunikation — 1880–2017;
- Personelles, Soziales — 1846–2008.

But these dates are **class envelopes**, not proof of continuous nineteenth-century series.

Official catalogue: https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?fields%5B%5D=object.identifier&operators%5B%5D=%3D&values%5B%5D=GFA+1

### 4.2 Native GF material before 1885 currently looks like islands

Examples visible in the Fischer-family/history classes include:

- Georg Fischer I outgoing book: 1855–1860;
- GF product catalogue: 1860–1870 (catalogue record marked vacat);
- price courant for cast-steel files/rasps: 1872;
- cast-steel product price list: 1878;
- recommendation letter for steel casting: 1881;
- pressure-test price list / cast-steel material in the broader 1855–87 envelope;
- individual family/technical notes and retrospective histories.

**Interpretation:** this is valuable primary material, but at present it behaves as a **constellation of surviving objects/dossiers**, not like a dense annual corporate registry.

### 4.3 Rauschenbach is the strongest continuous nineteenth-century company block inside GFA's world

Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach (founded 1842; acquired by GF in the twentieth century) contributes a genuinely earlier industrial-company archive.

Its class structure includes:

- general mechanical engineering: 1852–1981;
- management: 1871–1962;
- foundry machinery: 1827–1968;
- woodworking machinery: 1842–1954;
- agricultural machinery from 1885.

Actor-linked records show additional nineteenth-century continuity:

- property/contracts/history from 1855;
- correspondence/contracts 1868–1905 and 1869–1921;
- staff registers from 1871;
- sickness-fund books from 1872;
- correspondence 1876–79;
- crisis/relocation material from 1878;
- workshop photographs 1869 and 1880–85;
- separate Rauschenbach corporate fonds GFA 51 begins 1865, although its formal AG governance records begin in 1892.

**Interpretation:** if the goal is to find a **large pre-1890 industrial-company body**, Rauschenbach is structurally stronger than assuming the native GF nineteenth-century records form one continuous series.

Official catalogue/actor pages:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&page=1&parent=9351&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/actors/49

### 4.4 1896 is still the clean corporate caesura

`GFA 2 Verwaltungsrat und Konzernleitung` begins in **1896**, with 42 lm and c.4,100 description units. This matches the conversion to a joint-stock company and marks a sharp increase in systematic corporate governance records.

Official catalogue: https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?page=1&parent=4

## 5. Carrier/media chronology

The photo/media fonds become structurally coherent mainly after our central window:

- GFA 16 glass-plate archive includes some nineteenth-century images, but its major systematic classes are largely twentieth-century;
- GFA 25 Singen glass plates: 1900–1960, c.1,300 description units, with subject classes including laboratories, products, works, exhibitions, etc.;
- GFA 11/12/16/17 reflect a later reorganization in which a previous image identity system was split by carrier into cards/prints/glass plates/negatives.

There are isolated early carrier survivals — e.g. an 1888 Schaffhausen works glass-plate image — but the **large coherent carrier archive is a post-1900 phenomenon**.

**Interpretation:** later photo/glass systems are excellent controls for how technical evidence becomes a carrier-organized archive, but they should not be allowed to stretch a c.1850–85 project by themselves.

## 6. Prior Scholar in Residence overlay

The public SiR record shows that earlier projects cluster in recognizable zones:

### Early / first-half nineteenth century

- **Artemis Yagou (2021)** — Johann Conrad Fischer (1773–1854), watchmaking, diaries/documents, transnational know-how. Strongly occupies the Fischer/private-network side of the pre-1854 zone.

### Broad mid-/late nineteenth-century material and engineering history

- **Sebastian Haumann (2015)** — limestone as critical raw material, 1850–1930. Broad materiality/raw-material history; not a microscopy/preparation or archive-formation project.
- **Thomas Boothby (2016, 2018)** — iron bridge design, 1875–1895, using engineering literature. Strong Library-led late-century engineering project.
- **Julia Lütolf (2019)** — methods of treating metal surfaces; exhibition/material-method research, not a narrow historical microscopy corpus.
- **Alessia Pannese (2026)** — nineteenth-century circulation between metallurgy and aesthetics around Labrouste's iron libraries; explicitly specialist-Library-led and cross-national.

### Later corporate/media zone

- **Ralf Stremmel (2026)** — industrial photography 1918–39, comparative Ruhr/GF, naturally aligned with the thick photo/media archive.
- **Karin Priem (2022–23)** — corporate photography/social-educational reform, 1940s–70s.
- **Duncan Alford (2022)** — GF financial structure from the mid-/late nineteenth century forward, moving into the modern corporate archive.

**Current gap visible from the overlay:** there is no public SiR project presently identified that treats the **1850–1885 transition among printed technical knowledge, non-GF iron/trade archives, pre-AG industrial-company records, and emerging material/visual reference practices as a single historical problem**. That remains a structural opening, but the final topic should still be allowed to emerge from holdings rather than being imposed now.

Official scholars page: https://www.eisenbibliothek.ch/en/research/sir/scholars.html

## 7. Where our current exploratory line sits — without yet freezing the question

If we simply place the previously discussed c.1850–1885 exploratory zone onto the map, it falls across **four different archival regimes**:

1. **Library — continuous and very dense:** nineteenth-century scientific/technical publishing, serials, manuals, atlases, travel and exhibition literature.
2. **EBA 3 — older trade/accounting/correspondence regime:** strongest c.1835–67.
3. **EBA 4 — compact commercial/material-form regime:** strongest c.1854–85.
4. **GFA/Rauschenbach + GF historical islands — industrial-company regime:** Rauschenbach is much more continuous than the native GF pre-AG record; native GF GFA 1 is important but retrospective/fragmentary.

This means the likely value of a residency is not simply “many sources on one person.” The institution preserves **several different ways nineteenth-century iron/material knowledge became recordable**: classified publication, trade correspondence/accounting, product/profile books, industrial-company records, and later carrier-specific visual archives.

That structural fact should precede any decision about microscopy, metallography, trade, testing, travel or another final problem.

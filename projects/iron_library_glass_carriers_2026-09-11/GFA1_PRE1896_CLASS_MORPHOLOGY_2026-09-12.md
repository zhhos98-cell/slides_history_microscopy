# GFA 1 pre-1896 class morphology audit

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE STRUCTURAL AUDIT  
**Rule:** do not infer archival density, continuity, or contemporaneity from GFA 1 top-level class envelope dates. This file asks which apparently early classes actually contain coherent pre-1896 record regimes.

## 0. Governing archival-history caveat

`GFA 1 :: Historisches Firmenarchiv (Altbestand)` is a **retrospectively assembled historical-company collection**, not a continuous registry created in 1802.

Controlled formation history:

- historical collecting began in the 1920s around company-history work;
- the Historisches Firmenarchiv was formally established in 1943;
- a filing plan was published in 1945;
- later relocations caused losses;
- the current classes/subclasses were formed/reworked during the 2014–16 archive reorganization while retaining the existing broad order;
- GFA 1 also contains acquired/subsidiary records, especially Rauschenbach material dating back to that firm's nineteenth-century history.

Therefore every pre-1896 GFA 1 class needs at least three separate questions:

1. Is the historical subject/date actually dense in the target period?
2. Are the surviving carriers contemporary originals or later copies/retrospective files?
3. Does the material form a recurring series, or merely a retrospectively assembled dossier spanning many decades?

## 1. `Giessereien, Produktion, Produkte` — early envelope, mixed documentary morphology

Top-level envelope: **1817–2006**.

This class does contain important early technical/product material, but the envelope cannot be treated as a continuous production archive.

### Controlled mixed example

`GFA 1/580 :: Geschichte und Entwicklung Temperguss und Fittings`, dated **1865–1950**, is one dossier. Its contents mix:

- historical exposés on the origin/development of fittings production;
- technical reports and comparisons between Schaffhausen and Singen;
- a chronology/statistical list extending 1865–1950;
- a translated 1930 report;
- 1935 international commercial-cooperation material;
- a 1952 retrospective memoir by Emil Ganz.

The 1865 start date therefore marks historical content inside a later mixed dossier, not a continuously surviving technical register from 1865.

Other broad dossiers show the same danger, e.g. `GFA 1/1647 Geschichte des Stahlgusses` (1817–1952) and `GFA 1/524` (1860–1960).

### Verdict

**Broad class = MIXED / RETROSPECTIVE.**

For c.1850–1885 use only carrier-level nodes whose contemporaneity can be controlled, especially:

- the 1856 Härt-/Glühöfen plan;
- the native-GF product/market-document regime c.1865–83;
- Traisen plans where originality is explicit.

Do not promote the whole `Giessereien, Produktion, Produkte` class to a pre-1885 core merely because its envelope begins in 1817.

## 2. Native GF product / market-document regime — genuine pre-1885 cluster

This is currently the strongest **native-GF** contemporaneous documentary regime before the AG era, though it is not a management registry.

Controlled carriers include:

- `GFA 1/162.1` — first fittings prospectus, 1865–67;
- `GFA 1/162.2` and `.3` — product-catalogue volumes, c.1868–76;
- `GFA 1/162.110` — Temperguss poster, 1868–76;
- `GFA 1/162.116` — pipe/gas/water fittings and utensils, 1870–79;
- `GFA 1/74` — goods catalogue/poster, c.1870–90;
- `GFA 1/145.55` — price courant for cast-steel files/rasps, 1872;
- `GFA 1/160.101` — factory regulation, 1873;
- `GFA 1/79.1` — exhibition certificate for shoemaking tools, 1876;
- `GFA 1/145.117` — cast-steel product price list, 1878;
- `GFA 1/145.112` — recommendation letter for steel casting, 1881;
- `GFA 1/141.114` — Zurich national-exhibition list, 1883;
- `GFA 32/217` — group of 19 cast-steel/malleable-cast three-dimensional products, broadly dated 1865–95.

### Verdict

**REAL REGIME, Tier B.**

It represents product definition, market presentation, pricing, rules, certification/exhibition and surviving objects. It is structurally stronger than a few isolated curiosities, but it does **not** substitute for missing continuous management/production accounts.

**Scale stop rule:** do not infer the number of child records under `GFA 1/162` from suffixes such as `.110` and `.116`. Publicly controlled scale remains OPEN.

## 3. `Ausstellungen, Messen` — represented nineteenth-century events, mixed surviving carriers

Top-level envelope: **1804–1971**.

A substantial warning is now controlled: many apparently early exhibition objects in `GFA 1/149` are explicitly catalogued as **Faksimile**.

Examples:

- `GFA 1/149.3` and `.4` — London 1851, facsimiles;
- `.5` — Bern 1857, facsimile;
- `.10/.11/.12` — Paris 1878, facsimiles;
- `.13` — Paris 1889, facsimile;
- `.8` — Salzburg exhibition, facsimile.

The actor record for Kaltbrunner AG Top Coatings records a **1959 conservation event** for these facsimile objects.

At the same time, genuinely contemporaneous-looking exhibition records survive elsewhere, such as:

- `GFA 1/79.1` — 1876 exhibition certificate;
- `GFA 1/141.114` — 1883 Zurich exhibition list.

### Verdict

**MIXED SECONDARY LAYER.**

The class is useful for the history of exhibition memory, corporate self-presentation and later heritage preservation, but its early event dates must not be read as original-carrier dates. For a pre-1885 project, inspect individual carriers and label facsimiles/later reproductions explicitly.

## 4. `Dokumentation` — early date envelope, largely retrospective documentary function

Top-level envelope: **1860–2025**.

Current public evidence has not exposed a coherent contemporaneous 1860s–80s documentary series. Instead, the early envelope is represented by retrospective/hybrid structures such as:

- `GFA 1/756 Berichte und Erinnerungsschriften`, 1860–1982, one dossier, marked `vacat`;
- `GFA 1/6521 Dokumentation Transparente ab diversen Mikrofilmen`, 1855–1937;
- later `Dokumentation / Historische Bilder` photo classes whose surviving photographic/card systems are chiefly twentieth century.

### Verdict

**RETROSPECTIVE / MIXED; not a pre-1885 core.**

Use as evidence for later historical documentation, copying and archive-making, not as proof that GF possessed a continuous `Dokumentation` department/series from 1860.

## 5. `Grundstücke und Gebäude` — broad dossiers plus scattered original nodes, not a continuous nineteenth-century building series

Top-level envelope: **1799–1990**.

The early layer currently resolves into broad multi-decade dossiers plus individual technical/visual carriers:

- `GFA 1/1116 Grundstücke, Gebäude Schaffhausen, Mühlental`, 1817–1966, one dossier;
- `GFA 1/1117 Grundstücke, Gebäude Schaffhausen`, 1802–1953, one dossier;
- `GFA 1/1544` estate/revision/property reports, 1856–1974, one dossier;
- `GFA 8/89` Mühlental situation plan, 1843;
- `GFA 1/73.3` plan of hardening/annealing furnaces, 1856;
- visual/photographic nodes for the works from 1868 and especially 1880s onward.

Important carrier warning:

- `GFA 17/15300 Situationsplan der Werke Mühlental, 1854–1887` is catalogued inside the **negative/photo archive** as a photographic carrier. Its represented plan chronology is not automatically the creation date of an original 1854 plan.

### Verdict

**MIXED SECONDARY LAYER.**

There are genuine original technical-plan nodes, but the public catalogue does not presently demonstrate one continuous pre-1885 building/property series. Do not promote the top-level 1799 start date to a claim of high nineteenth-century serial density.

## 6. `Kommunikation` — 1880 envelope does not currently reveal an 1880s communication registry

Top-level envelope: **1880–2017**.

The public catalogue currently exposes an early-looking dossier:

- `GFA 1/2952 Presseberichte und Broschüren Konzern allgemein`, 1880–1958, one dossier.

Its very broad range is more consistent with retrospective accumulation of press/brochure material than with a regular communication office series beginning in 1880. The strongly structured photo/communication systems visible elsewhere are chiefly twentieth century.

### Verdict

**EARLY ENVELOPE OPEN / likely retrospective; not promoted.**

Until a recurring contemporaneous 1880s sequence is demonstrated, do not treat `Kommunikation 1880–2017` as a large pre-1896 corpus.

## 7. `Aktiengesellschaft` — the 1885 class start is not the corporate caesura

Top-level GFA 1 class envelope: **1885–2019**.

The controlled formation chain instead remains firmly centered on **1895/96**:

- `GFA 1/223 Werkgeschichte: Gründung der AG`, 1895–1902;
- `GFA 1/183` shares, 1896–1999; `GFA 1/183.1` share certificate, 1896;
- `GFA 2/1` board minutes begin 1896;
- `GFA 34/1` original general-meeting minutes begin 7 April 1896;
- `GFA 33/1` first annual report = 1896;
- the separately formed annual-report fonds `GFA 33` begins 1896;
- official corporate history states the joint-stock company was founded in 1896.

No currently controlled source demonstrates a coherent AG record series beginning in 1885.

### Verdict

**1885 = unexplained class envelope; 1896 remains the secure corporate caesura.**

Do not weaken the 1896 boundary simply because the GFA 1 class label begins in 1885.

## 8. `Personelles, Soziales` — high data seriality, but early data can live on later-created carriers

The worker-register corpus is large (e.g. `GFA 1/1502`, 50 folio volumes), but the catalogue shows that some later-created registers contain earlier employment-entry dates:

- registers made from 1897 onward can contain entries back to 1855;
- foundry registers can contain entries back to 1872.

### Verdict

**VALUABLE DATA SERIES, MIXED CONTEMPORANEITY.**

Label these cases `LATER_REGISTER_WITH_EARLIER_DATA`. They are excellent for historical reconstruction but are not automatically contemporary 1850s/70s documentary carriers.

## 9. Rauschenbach — the major provenance exception inside the GFA environment

Rauschenbach remains the strongest genuinely continuous pre-1890 industrial-company block currently identified in the GFA environment, but it is an **acquired company provenance**, not native GF administration.

Controlled operational chain:

- correspondence/contracts from 1868/69;
- employee register from 1871;
- sickness-fund series from 1872 (10 vols);
- correspondence 1876–79;
- crisis/relocation dossier from 1878;
- contemporary workshop images in 1869 and 1880–85;
- technical classes such as general mechanical engineering (from 1852), foundry machinery, woodworking machinery.

Formal AG governance still begins only in 1892.

### Verdict

**Tier A operational/company archive, but keep provenance distinct from native GF.**

## 10. Updated pre-1896 GFA morphology

The pre-1896 GFA landscape is best described as a set of **different documentary regimes**, not one thick corporate archive:

### Strong / structurally coherent

- Rauschenbach operational/company records, late 1860s–1880s — acquired provenance;
- native GF product/market-document regime, c.1865–83 — native GF, but functionally narrow;
- selected original technical plans / visual carriers where carrier dates are controlled;
- Traisen original technical plans, with separate copy-history control for the Blaubuch sequence.

### Useful but mixed / retrospective

- exhibitions and fairs;
- documentation / historical memory;
- broad production/product-history dossiers;
- land/building dossiers;
- worker registers containing earlier data on later carriers;
- communication/press dossiers with long retrospective envelopes.

### Institutional thickening

- 1892: Rauschenbach formal AG governance becomes visible;
- 1895: GF AG-foundation dossier begins;
- **1896: GF annual reports, general-meeting minutes and board records begin as systematic corporate series.**

This strongly supports the existing archive map: **the 1850–1885 zone is not empty; it is structurally plural. What is thin is the native-GF bureaucratic registry, not the institution's total relevant holdings.**

## 11. Stop rules

- Do not search more GFA 1 top-level classes merely because their envelope begins before 1885 unless there is evidence of recurring series or original carriers.
- Do not treat one dossier with a 100-year range as 100 years of continuous records.
- Do not treat represented image/plan dates as the surviving carrier's creation dates.
- Do not merge Rauschenbach provenance into native GF.
- Preserve 1896 as the corporate caesura unless contemporaneous governance records demonstrably predate it.

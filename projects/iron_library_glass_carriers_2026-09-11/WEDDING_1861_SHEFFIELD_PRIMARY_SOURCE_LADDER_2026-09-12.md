# Wedding / Sheffield 1861 — primary-source ladder for Bessemer demonstration control

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: SOURCE-HIERARCHY CONTROL. This note separates the exact-date / primary-publication evidence from later syntheses for the 1861 Institution of Mechanical Engineers Sheffield meeting. It is designed to stop the project from treating Bishop's 1959 summary as the terminal citation when the underlying 1861 publications are identifiable and, in several cases, publicly readable.

## 1. Meeting date: 31 July 1861 is the governing date

Henry Bessemer's own autobiography explicitly recalls Sir William Armstrong's visit to Sheffield as President of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers and states that the Institution held its summer meeting there on:

**31 July 1861.**

Bessemer adds that his printed Institution paper proves the date.

Source:
- Henry Bessemer, *An Autobiography*, chapter 15, web transcription: https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21374/2.html

Chronological consequence:

- use **31 July 1861** as the meeting anchor;
- Bishop's phrase `In August 1861` is a broad retrospective framing of the meeting/reporting period and must not replace the exact date.

## 3. John Brown paper: primary bibliographic control

The contemporary Proceedings article is directly catalogued as:

**John Brown, `On the Manufacture of Steel Rails and Armour Plates`, Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, vol. 12, issue 1 (1861), pp. 121–132.**

The publisher record lists John Brown's affiliation as Sheffield.

Source:
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1243/PIME_PROC_1861_012_015_02?download=true

A modern engineering-history paper independently cites the Brown paper to the Sheffield meeting on **31 July 1861**, with p.124 used for the rolling/armour discussion.

Routing source:
- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17581206.2022.2057887

Claim ceiling: the current publisher metadata is enough to control title/pages/authorship, but not to reconstruct the full excursion itinerary.

## 4. Bessemer paper / meeting context

Bessemer states that because the Institution was visiting Sheffield, where his steel works were located, he read a paper to the meeting and deliberately brought physical specimens / proofs to demonstrate the properties of his mild steel.

Source:
- Henry Bessemer, *An Autobiography*, chapter 16: https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21375/6.html
- continuation with his description of placing actual specimens before the chair: https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21375/7.html

This is relevant because it independently supports a technical culture of **demonstration through objects and works access**, not merely oral lecture or printed text.

## 6. Bishop's role: high-value routing, not terminal authority

Philip W. Bishop, *The Beginnings of Cheap Steel* (Smithsonian Institution, Bulletin 218, 1959), states that the Institution meeting in Sheffield heard papers by Bessemer and John Brown; after the meeting the engineers visited Bessemer's works; and at John Brown & Co. the Bessemer process was repeated on a larger scale and a heavy armour plate rolled before about 250 visitors.

Bishop explicitly cites:

- *The Engineer*, 1861, vol. 12, p. 10;

## 7. Vickers is already inside the IMechE network — but do not invent a 1861 paper

The Vickers connection becomes more concrete at institutional level.

Later IMechE membership lists preserved in Google Books record:

- **Thomas Edward Vickers, Messrs. Vickers Sons and Co., Sheffield — member from 1856**;
- **Albert Vickers — member from 1861**.

The firm designation in the later list reflects the later company name/address, but the membership years are the relevant point.

Source:
- Google Books, later *Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers*, membership list, entry `Vickers, Albert ... 1861` and `Vickers, Thomas Edward ... 1856`.

This matters because the Gruner–Lan Sheffield statistics are routed through `Mr Vickers of the house of Naylor, Vickers & Co.`. The manufacturer-information channel therefore intersects an established professional-engineering network: at least Thomas Edward Vickers was already an Institution member several years before the 1861 Sheffield meeting.

### Authorship ceiling

A bookseller catalogue for an original 1861 PIME volume lists `BESSEMER, Henry. VICKERS, T.E. BROWN, John. et.al.` among contributors. This is useful as a routing clue only. It does **not** presently prove that T. E. Vickers read a separate paper at the 31 July 1861 Sheffield meeting.

Until the 1861 table of contents / meeting minutes show a titled Vickers contribution, use:

`VICKERS = IMechE_MEMBER / NETWORK_PARTICIPANT`

not

`VICKERS = 1861_SHEFFIELD_PAPER_AUTHOR`.

The membership fact is enough to sharpen the institutional picture without overclaiming authorship.

## 9. Why this matters for Wedding 1860

This primary-source ladder strengthens, but also disciplines, the comparison to Wedding's 25 July 1860 visit.

The 1861 evidence should be used to establish that Sheffield's Bessemer transfer environment could include:

**formal technical papers + physical specimens + works visits + live process repetition + expert collective witnessing.**

The Vickers membership control adds another layer:

**manufacturer testimony / firm knowledge ↔ professional engineering institution ↔ works demonstration.**

It should not be used to assert that Wedding's own 1860 visit followed the same group itinerary.

The comparison is institutional and epistemic, not a one-to-one reconstruction:

**Wedding 1860: individual expert notebook inscription in a still-forming adoption window**

versus

**IMechE 1861: organized expert witnessing and repeated works demonstration after local adoption had advanced.**

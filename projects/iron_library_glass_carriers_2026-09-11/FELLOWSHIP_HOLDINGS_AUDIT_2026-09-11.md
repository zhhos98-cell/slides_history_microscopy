# Fellowship holdings audit — travel, transnational information flow, Sheffield / Berlin / Schaffhausen

Updated: 2026-09-11

Purpose: separate the fellowship/application line from the active Wedding PDF transcription line. This file is an application-facing holdings audit, not a replacement for `CURRENT_PROGRESS.md`.

## 1. Working project form

Do **not** frame the project as a static city-to-city industrial comparison. Stronger formulation:

> How did metallurgical knowledge change form as it travelled between works, travellers, laboratories and testing institutions?

Potential core axis:

**Sheffield works access and travelling technical inscription → Berlin material testing / metallography / microphotography → Schaffhausen as an industrial uptake / comparison case.**

The historical unit is therefore not simply the city but the **node** at which information is selected, reformatted, tested, stored or circulated. Relevant node types include works, personal contacts, laboratories, testing rooms, notebooks, specimens, drawings, photographic plates, publications and corporate decision structures.

No mapping output is currently proposed; the node/route language is an analytical device only.

## 2. Wedding core: already digitised pre-work, not the onsite justification

### Eisenbibliothek Mss 23
- 1856–1857, 208 pp.
- Freiberg smelting notes; combines Wedding's own observations with copied scientific/technical material.
- Useful as a baseline for how he decomposed processes and mixed observation with textual authority.

### Eisenbibliothek Mss 24
- 1858, 98 pp.
- Study tour through Thuringia, Bavaria, Saarbrücken, Lorraine, Rhine, Westphalia.
- Daily works/mining visits; operating facilities, production processes and geological context.

### Eisenbibliothek Mss 25
- 1860–1862, 328 + 48 pp.
- Germany, Belgium, England; Wedding travelled as a referendary for the Prussian mining administration.
- Public catalogue explicitly notes South Wales, early Bessemer-process works and contacts with contemporary specialists.
- Current project work has image-controlled the late-July 1860 Sheffield sequence and located `Bessemer's Stahlwerk` between 25 July Sheffield and 26 July Leeds.

Public source: e-codices, Schlatt, Eisenbibliothek, Mss 23–25.

**Application use:** these manuscripts justify the question and allow very advanced pre-selection before residence. Because they are digitised, they should not carry the onsite-necessity argument by themselves.

## 3. Strong comparative travel corpus in the GF archive

Johann Conrad Fischer provides an unusually strong pre-Wedding control for travel as a technology of knowledge production. His repeated British journeys make Sheffield a recurring node rather than a one-off destination.

### GFA 1/151.5
`Notizen auf der Reise über Paris nach London, Leeds, Low, Moore, Sheffield, und zurück im Sommer 1845`

### GFA 1/151.6
`Notizen auf meiner Reise über Carlsruhe, Mannheim, Cüln und Ostende nach London, Derby, Wingfield, Alfredton und Butterly Ironworks nach Sheffield, Liverpool` (1846)

### GFA 1/151.7
1851 journey to the Great Exhibition and English industrial towns.

The digital Fischer edition shows repeated contacts with Sheffield firms and people, including Thomas Turton & Sons, Francis Huntsman, Charles Cammell and Sanderson; the 1846 sequence includes a visit to Cammell and Sanderson's Sheffield works. Fischer's British travel repeatedly depended on recommendations and personal introductions.

### GFA 1/145.26
`Notizen zur Gusstahlproduktion`, 1802–1854, 2 files. Not marked digitised in the public catalogue.

### GFA 1/145.41
Small note by Johann Conrad Fischer on casting steel (`Abgiessen von Stahlformguss`), 10 Aug 1846. Not marked digitised.

### GFA 1/148.8
Loose sheets concerning crucible steel and steel-casting production, 1805–1835, 7 files. Not marked digitised.

### GFA 1/144.57
`Ausgangsbuch von Georg Fischer I`, 1855–1860, one volume. Public catalogue does not mark it digitised. Chronologically valuable because it reaches right up to Wedding's 1860 trip and may expose outward correspondence / commercial-technological contacts.

**Comparative payoff:** Fischer and Wedding both pass through Sheffield, but at different moments and with different documentary practices. This permits a controlled comparison of how access, conversation, works visits and portable notes became usable metallurgical knowledge.

## 4. Berlin material-testing / metallography cluster held by the Iron Library

Bernhard Droste's 2026 holdings article confirms that the Iron Library received a material-testing and metallography collection including:

- volumes of `Mittheilungen aus den königlichen technischen Versuchsanstalten zu Berlin` for 1889, 1892, 1894, 1895, 1896 and 1897;
- works by Emil Heyn;
- **three original glass plates from early microphotographic work** associated with the Martens/Heyn metallography line.

The article identifies Berlin materials testing as developing from Wöhler's fatigue work and the Königliche mechanisch-technische Versuchsanstalt; Adolf Martens led the institution from 1885 to 1914. Droste explicitly connects the surviving glass plates to early microphotographic metallography.

Relevant public identifiers already in the project:
- `G 745,1` — image credit associated with the three microphotographic glass plates in Droste's article; catalogue function still requires staff clarification.
- `Per765` — identifier appearing with Martens's 1891 microscopy article; catalogue function likewise to be clarified.

**Onsite question:** inspect the plates as physical information carriers: size, labels, edge/verso marks, numbering, packaging, condition, relation to printed plates and relation to the Iron Library's Martens/Heyn publications. This is much stronger than merely re-reading published Berlin literature.

## 5. Schaffhausen / GF comparison: material testing becomes an industrial system

### GFA 1/732.3
Rudolf Gnade, `Aus der Geschichte der Materialprüfung in den Schaffhauser Fischer-Werken` (1951), coverage 1890–1951. **Digitised.**

Use this remotely as a routing document. The archive has digitised the retrospective account and many of its associated images; it can be mined for names, instruments, institutional references and dates, then used to request primary files onsite.

### GFA 16/68, GFA 16/69
Amsler tensile-testing machines, ca. 1900. Digitised.

### GFA 16/220
`Labor für Festigkeitsprüfung im ersten Verwaltungsgebäude`, ca. 1900. Digitised.

### GFA 16/221
50-ton tensile-testing machine and press, ca. 1900. Digitised.

These establish a material-testing infrastructure in Schaffhausen by ca. 1900; they are controls, not the main onsite target.

## 6. High-value onsite dossiers for industrial uptake / process change

### GFA 1/550 — PRIORITY A
`Berichte und Unterlagen Schmelzofen`, 1893–1951. Not marked digitised.

The public description explicitly includes:
- drawing of a **Bessemer converter, 1896**;
- converter-plant plans, 1897–1925;
- GF engineers' reports on new furnaces, 1911–1927;
- furnace tests and comparisons;
- correspondence and plans;
- materials and process investigations.

This is currently the strongest onsite bridge between the 1860 Wedding/Bessemer observations and later corporate process systems. It must be treated as a later comparison/uptake case, not as proof of a direct line from Wedding.

### GFA 1/724 — PRIORITY A/B
`Werkgeschichte: Elektro-Stahlwerk Geissberg Bauakten`, 1887–1911. Not marked digitised.

Potential use: reconstruct how new steelmaking plant was specified, planned and materially realised; test where external technical references, suppliers or comparison sites enter the building process.

### GFA 1/725 — PRIORITY A/B
`Elektrostahlwerk Geissberg`, 1896–1950. Not marked digitised.

Potential use: broader technical and institutional history of the electro-steel works. Exact relevance must be determined onsite / through staff-level file description.

### GFA 2/1 and GFA 2/2 ff. — PRIORITY B
Board minutes, 1896–1904; the public catalogue exposes the full sequence of individual meetings but no digital images.

Potential use: identify how capital investment, plant changes, testing arrangements, external suppliers and expert authority entered corporate decision-making. This should be sampled rather than read indiscriminately.

### GFA 13/3 and GFA 13/4 — PRIORITY B
Photo albums, 1889–1903, respectively `Ausstellungen und Produkte` and `Kataloge, Ausstellungen, Menschen + Produkt.` Not marked digitised.

Potential use: visual regimes of products, exhibitions and works; secondary to the core travel/testing question unless they contain testing/process images or externally sourced comparison material.

### GFA 1/554 — PRIORITY B
`Glühöfen, Härteöfen, Trockenöfen, Tiegelöfen`, 1856–1951. Not marked digitised.

Potentially valuable because it starts in 1856 and spans the same period in which Wedding's notebook practice develops. Need staff/file-level triage before treating as central.

## 8. Best comparative architecture at present

### Core historical comparison
**Sheffield as a recurrent field site**
- Fischer: repeated visits, recommendations, firms, social access, publication of travel experience.
- Wedding: state-backed technical travel, heterogeneous notebook, converter drawings, process timing, raw-material specification, comparative reassembly.

### Transformation node
**Berlin**
- mechanisch-technische Versuchsanstalt / Materialprüfungsanstalt;
- Martens / Heyn;
- specimen → microscopic field → photographic glass plate → publication / numbered reference.

### Industrial control
**Schaffhausen**
- material testing infrastructure by ca. 1900;
- Amsler machines and test laboratory;
- Bessemer converter drawing and plant plans in GFA 1/550;
- Geissberg steelworks files and board decisions.

This produces a project about **transnational information flow without assuming frictionless circulation**. The object is to identify where information changes format, evidentiary status and institutional ownership.

## 10. Application logic

The Scholar in Residence programme currently asks for:
- abstract max. 150 words;
- project description max. 1000 words explicitly identifying holdings to be consulted;
- CV;
- rolling applications.

The programme is explicitly intended for intensive use of Iron Library / Corporate Archives holdings, and recent scholars have pursued cross-regional questions about circulation of metallurgical/technical knowledge rather than projects confined to GF company history.

For the proposal, onsite necessity should rest on **undigitised dossiers + physical carrier inspection + cross-collection reconstruction**, while the digitised Wedding and Fischer material should be presented as pre-residence preparation that makes the onsite work unusually targeted.

## 2. Revised historical object

Do **not** describe Schinz & Bär simply as merchants adopting standardized iron sections.

By 1881 the firm is demonstrably participating in a more complicated regime in which iron cross-sections can be simultaneously:

- stocked and sold;
- drawn and publicly described as technical solutions;
- made firm-specific / proprietary;
- legally protected in foreign jurisdictions;
- compared with other profile families;
- inserted into albums, price systems and later engineering reference carriers;
- redistributed into technical education.

The stronger object is therefore:

> **how iron cross-sections were made simultaneously identifiable, protectable, comparable, orderable and teachable through merchant documentary and reference infrastructures.**

This does not replace the Zürich merchant-network / convention problem. It specifies the product-form layer operating inside that collective commercial environment.

## 5. Patent / trademark distinction

### Secure

The 1881 article says the roof profile had been patented in several countries.

### Institutional context

Swiss federal patent law did not begin until 1888, so the 1881 protections were necessarily foreign-jurisdiction protections.

### Open

Exact 1881 patent jurisdictions and numbers remain unrecovered.

A secondary extraction of the Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce trademark corpus reports:

- Schinz & Bär, Zürich;
- trademark no. 1289;
- goods: `Metallurgische Produkte`;

This is **not yet admitted as primary-secure evidence**. Do not merge it with the 1881 foreign patents or the 1885 phrase `geschützte Specialitäten` until the original SHAB trademark page is recovered.

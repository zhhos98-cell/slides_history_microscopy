# Iron Library subject structure — historical control and current claim ceiling

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE LIBRARY-LEVEL STRUCTURAL CONTROL  
**Rule:** use subject structure to map the collection before selecting a topic. Do not infer the current 2024 classification hierarchy from an older scheme without checking.

## 1. Current official collecting profile

The current Iron Library page describes c.50,000 media units, c.1,000 periodicals (c.70 current), and c.6,000 pre-1900 rara titles. Current declared thematic priorities include metallurgy, mining, history of technology, history of science, GF history, economic/industrial history, industrial heritage, product catalogues/company publications and encyclopaedias.

The current page also exposes two official 2024 classification PDFs:

- `Systematik des Bibliotheksbestands`
- `Systematik des Rarabestands`

Their URLs are controlled, but the PDF bodies were not recoverable through the present web cache during this pass. Therefore the **exact 2024 hierarchy remains OPEN** and must not be reconstructed from expectation.

## 2. 1996 full-library Sachgruppen distribution — exact historical control

An Iron Library staff publication from 1996 states that books were shelved by subject groups and gives a complete percentage distribution. The percentages sum to 100% and provide a rare quantitative picture of the collection's internal morphology at that time.

| Historical subject group (1996 wording, normalized) | Share of collection |
|---|---:|
| Encyclopaedias, dictionaries and other reference works | 11.4% |
| Mining and smelting; pig-iron and steel production | 10.4% |
| History of iron and general history of technology | 10.0% |
| Foundry technology (forming and processing), metallography/materials science (`Metallkunde`) and materials testing (`Werkstoffprüfung`) | **7.5%** |
| Industrial uses of iron: mechanical engineering, transport technology, architecture | 7.1% |
| Iron and metal industry; trade | 6.6% |
| Geology and ore-deposit science; geography | 6.0% |
| Chemistry, mineralogy, petrography | **4.3%** |
| Art/craft; militaria | 4.0% |
| Astronomy, mathematics, physics, mechanics | 2.4% |
| Economic and social history | 2.0% |
| Varia | 3.5% |
| Company publications and anniversary publications | 14.3% |
| Archives and company estates | 8.0% |
| Art sheets, graphics, views, maps and plans | 2.1% |
| Manuscripts, handwritten materials and files | 0.4% |

Source: Annette Bouheiry, `Die Eisenbibliothek, Stiftung der Georg Fischer AG, Schaffhausen`, *Berichte der Geologischen Bundesanstalt* 35 (1996), esp. the collection-structure table on p. 52.

## 3. What this changes in the collection-first map

### 3.1 Metallkunde / Werkstoffprüfung was already a major shelf block

The combined `Gießereitechnik ... sowie Metallkunde und Werkstoffprüfung` group represented **7.5%** of the collection in 1996. This is not a marginal keyword cluster assembled for the present project; it was one of the largest explicit knowledge blocks in the Library's own historical subject system.

### 3.2 Mineralogy/petrography is a separate substantial neighboring block

`Chemie, Mineralogie, Petrographie` represented **4.3%**, while `Geologie und Lagerstättenkunde, Geographie` represented another **6.0%**. The collection therefore historically maintained a strong materials/mineral/geoscience environment adjacent to metallurgical/material-testing literature.

### 3.3 Industrial application and market/document literature are also structurally large

- industrial uses of iron: 7.1%;
- iron/metal industry and trade: 6.6%;
- company and anniversary publications: 14.3%.

This reinforces the present collection-first finding that the Library is not only a metallurgical science collection; it also preserves very large industrial-application and corporate-publication environments.

### 3.4 Do not map 1996 shelf categories directly onto today's Library / EBA / GFA split

The 1996 table includes `Archive und Firmennachlässe` (8.0%), maps/plans and manuscripts/files within the institutional collection description. Current research practice must still keep **Library / EBA / GFA** separate because provenance and present cataloguing systems differ. The 1996 percentages are a historical collection-structure control, not a replacement for current fonds architecture.

## 4. Relationship to the 2011 historical-book census

The later historical-holdings profile independently shows substantial pre-1900 depth in mining, geology/geognosy, mineralogy, iron/steel production, metallurgy and applications of iron/steel. The two sources are complementary:

- **1996 percentages** show the morphology of the whole subject-classified collection at that date;
- **historical-book counts** show the scale and nineteenth-century density of specific pre-1900 classes.

Together they justify treating the nineteenth-century Library as a **systematic knowledge environment** rather than an author-by-author bibliography.

## 5. Implication for later topic testing — without selecting a topic now

If a later research question involves microscopy, metallography, specimen preparation or visual/material testing, do **not** assume a dedicated historical `microscopy` shelf is required. The collection structure suggests that such practices may naturally cross at least:

- Metallkunde / Werkstoffprüfung / foundry technology;
- chemistry / mineralogy / petrography;
- geology / ore-deposit science;
- physics/mechanics;
- periodical/serial environments.

That is a hypothesis to test **after** the current classification and serial runs are controlled, not a reason to keyword-select the holdings now.

## 6. Claim ceiling

Secure:

- the 1996 subject percentages above;
- subject-based shelving was explicit at that time;
- Metallkunde/Werkstoffprüfung and mineralogy/petrography were substantial existing collection blocks;
- current official site still identifies metallurgy/mining/history of science/history of technology as major collecting priorities;
- official 2024 classification PDFs exist.

Open:

- exact 2024 class hierarchy and codes;
- how closely current classes preserve the 1996 arrangement;
- exact nineteenth-century title counts inside the 7.5% and 4.3% historical groups;
- whether microscopy/optics was separately classified or distributed across neighboring classes;
- how many current holdings in these blocks entered through later donations/backfilling.

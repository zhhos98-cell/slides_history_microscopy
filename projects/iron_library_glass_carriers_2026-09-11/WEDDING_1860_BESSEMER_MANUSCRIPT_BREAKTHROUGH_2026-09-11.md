# Wedding Mss 25 — 1860 Bessemer manuscript breakthrough

Date: 2026-09-11

Status: IMAGE-CONTROLLED MANUSCRIPT BREAKTHROUGH + CONTEMPORARY RETROSPECTIVE CROSS-CONTROL.

This note supersedes the previous assumption that the Bessemer episode still had to be sought primarily in the 1862 block. A first-person 1896 retrospective by Wedding fixes the Bessemer encounter to **1860**, and renewed image control of the July 1860 Sheffield leaves in Mss 25 identifies a dedicated **`Bessemer's Stahlwerk`** section with converter drawings and operational notes.

## 1. 1896 first-person retrospective fixes the encounter to 1860

In *Stahl und Eisen*, 15 May 1896, reporting the Iron and Steel Institute meeting in London on 7–8 May, Wedding replied to the award of the Bessemer Gold Medal. The report explicitly identifies the speaker as `Geh. Bergrath Dr. Wedding`.

Wedding says that long stays on English ironworks in **1860 and 1862**, together with his Britain journey with Krug von Nidda, gave him extensive opportunities for technical study. Crucially, he then states that **already in 1860**, through the extraordinary kindness of Sir Henry Bessemer, he came to know Bessemer's then-new process **in Sheffield**.

The same speech immediately links this early encounter to later German installation work: Wedding says that on his urgent initiative the Bessemer works at **Hörde** was built and that the Bessemer works at **Königshütte** was erected according to his designs (`nach meinen Entwürfen`).

Source: *Stahl und Eisen*, 16. Jg., Nr. 10, 15 May 1896, report `Iron and Steel Institute (Versammlung in London am 7. und 8. Mai)`, printed p. 398.

Controlled consequence:

> The year of Wedding's personal encounter with Bessemer in Sheffield is no longer open: Wedding himself publicly dated it to **1860** in 1896.

This does not by itself date the exact notebook leaf to a particular day, but it radically changes the manuscript search geometry.

## 3. Date inference: Bessemer is almost certainly in the 25 July Sheffield day, but preserve the manuscript ceiling

The folio structure is now:

**24 July Leeds/Sheffield → 25 July Sheffield White Lead Works → continuation → `Bessemer's Stahlwerk` → continuation → Cammell/Cyclops Works → 26 July Leeds.**

The Bessemer heading itself does **not** repeat `25 Juli`. Therefore the strict archival claim should be:

- **secure:** Bessemer's Stahlwerk section lies between the explicit 25 July Sheffield work-entry and the explicit 26 July Leeds entry;
- **strong inference:** it belongs to Wedding's **25 July 1860 Sheffield** industrial-visiting sequence;
- **do not silently convert inference into literal manuscript dating** unless another contemporary source gives the exact day.

The 1896 speech independently secures **Sheffield, 1860**, which makes this identification exceptionally strong.

## 6. External cross-control on the works

Bessemer's own later autobiography states that he established his own steel works in Sheffield so that the trade could inspect the process; his firm permitted the works to be inspected and the process explained to intending licensees. It also states that the first converter mounted on axes at the Sheffield works was put into operation in **1858**.

Independent industrial-history sources likewise place Henry Bessemer's Sheffield steel works in operation by 1858–59.

These sources support the institutional plausibility of Wedding's 1860 inspection but do not replace the manuscript evidence.

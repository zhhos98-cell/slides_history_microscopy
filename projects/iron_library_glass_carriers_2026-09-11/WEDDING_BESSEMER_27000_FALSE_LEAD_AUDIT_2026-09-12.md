# Bessemer £27,000 licensing claim — false-lead audit

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: CONTROLLED NEGATIVE / CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION.

## Problem

A modern popular history/search result states that in 1858 Bessemer licensed his process to John Brown and `George Cammell` for £27,000. This conflicts with the independently controlled Sheffield chronology used in this project (Brown 1860; Charles Cammell 1861) and even misidentifies Charles Cammell as George.

## Primary control from Bessemer's autobiography

Bessemer's own account makes clear what the **£27,000** actually refers to. Immediately after the 1856 Cheltenham announcement he sold early licences to make malleable iron by his process:

- Dowlais — £10,000;
- Smith Dixon / Govan Iron Works — £10,000;
- Butterley Iron Company — licence for 10,000 tons annually on the same low royalty terms;
- a Welsh tin-plate manufacturer — £2,000 payment for a 4,000-ton annual licence.

Bessemer explicitly totals these sales as **£27,000 in less than one month** after the announcement.

Source: Henry Bessemer, *An Autobiography*, chapter 12, especially part 9:
https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21371/9.html

He also states that these early licences turned out to have no commercial value in their original form and were later repurchased. Chapter 12 part 11 explicitly refers to `these licenses ... for which £27,000 had been paid`.

Source:
https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21371/11.html

## Crucial Sheffield statement

In the same primary passage Bessemer says that at this early licence stage the Sheffield steel trade stood aside, confident in its existing method and dismissive of the idea of making several tons of cast steel rapidly from pig iron.

Therefore the £27,000 first-licence episode cannot simultaneously be a Brown/Cammell Sheffield licence package.

## Controlled conclusion

Reject:

`1858 Brown + Cammell licence = £27,000`

as a conflation of two distinct stages:

**1856 — first failed/early malleable-iron licences totalling £27,000 (Dowlais, Govan, Butterley, Welsh tin-plate concern)**

versus

**1859 — Bessemer process stabilised at Sheffield works**

**1860 — John Brown adopts/takes licence**

**1861 — Charles Cammell follows**

The Brown 1860 / Cammell 1861 sequence remains independently supported by Barraclough and Hey.

## Claim ceiling

This audit establishes what the £27,000 does **not** mean. It does not yet supply the exact financial terms or surviving contract reference for Brown's 1860 or Cammell's 1861 licences. Those remain separate archival targets.

## Research consequence

Do not use popular summaries that attach the £27,000 figure to Brown/Cammell. Any future claim about the price/royalty/contract terms of the Sheffield licences requires a separate primary source: Bessemer account, company deed/minute, patent licence, royalty book, or equivalent contemporary record.

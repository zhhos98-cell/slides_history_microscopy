# Fischer 1845–1846 — iterative testing and return-journey circuit

Status: active evidence note, 2026-09-11.

Source basis: supplied machine-readable Fischer 1845 and 1846 travel texts. OCR/research texts only; not manually verified diplomatic transcriptions.

## Core finding

The 1845 and 1846 journeys should not be treated as two independent travel accounts. The evidence supports an **iterative material/information circuit** in which the first journey produces samples, orders, contacts and claims; material is then tested across distance; written test results and continued supply generate a second journey in which the earlier results are carried back into British industrial discussion.

The useful unit is therefore not simply `journey`, but:

`journey -> sample/order -> shipment -> external test -> written judgement -> repeat supply -> return journey -> reuse of test authority in new encounters`.

## 1. 1845: Fischer arrives carrying material claims

Fischer travels with cast-wrought-iron and other manufactured samples. He explicitly says he wants to see what people in England will say about them.

The 1845 journey therefore begins not merely as observation of British industry but as a distributed judgement circuit for Fischer's own material.

Samples are shown and examined in Basel, Paris and London before and during the northern industrial journey.

## 2. July 1845: Brande converts inspection into an order

At the Royal Mint Brande has examined Fischer's die/sample material and gives him an order for steel. Brande contrasts Fischer's material with steel previously obtained from Huntsman with uneven results.

This converts a travelling demonstration object into a commercial/institutional relation:

`sample shown -> institutional examination -> order`.

Brande also asks Fischer to return after the northern journey, so the London encounter is already temporally open-ended rather than a one-off visit.

## 3. Late 1845: material continues travelling after Fischer returns

Brande's January 1846 letter states that the Royal Mint has made trials with a steel bar Fischer sent from Schaffhausen in late 1845.

The material therefore travels without the traveller:

`Schaffhausen -> steel bar -> London Royal Mint`.

This is important because the comparative/testing circuit is no longer coterminous with Fischer's physical presence.

## 4. January 1846: test result becomes a future-production instruction

The Royal Mint reports satisfaction with the tested bar and asks for more steel of specified dimensions. Future preference is made conditional on Fischer maintaining the same quality as the sample bar.

The test therefore generates both:

- a written judgement;
- a normative instruction for future production.

Working sequence:

`sample bar -> test -> favourable judgement -> sample quality becomes reference -> requested repeat production`.

## 5. By October 1846: repeated supply has stabilised the relation

On returning to London in October 1846 Fischer states that he has supplied cast steel / cast wrought iron to the Royal Mint for the **third time within a year**.

On 23 October he receives another order for coin-die material, which he describes as the fourth delivery/order and as evidence of satisfaction. Brande also demonstrates dies made from Fischer's steel under the Mint press in Fischer's presence.

Thus the relation is no longer a single sample test. It has become an iterative supply/testing/use relation.

## 6. The return journey carries earlier test authority back into Sheffield

On 30 October 1846 Fischer visits Cammell and explicitly shows/discusses Brande's letter while arguing that the firm's cast-steel arrangements could be improved.

A document generated by Royal Mint testing is therefore redeployed as technical/credibility capital in a Sheffield factory encounter.

Working movement:

`Schaffhausen steel -> Royal Mint test -> Brande letter -> Fischer carries letter -> Sheffield discussion`.

This is a key example of an institutional test result circulating back into an industrial knowledge-exchange network.

## 7. The circuit carries multiple media at once

By the second journey Fischer is not carrying only material samples. The returning circuit includes:

- additional steel/cast-wrought-iron objects;
- Brande's written institutional judgement;
- copied recommendation letters and covering instructions;
- Beck's coloured drawings of Fischer's own works;
- his own accumulated comparative experience from the previous journey.

In London the works drawing is additionally transformed into a photographic negative and positive copies.

The return journey therefore bundles different evidential carriers:

`material + letter + recommendation + drawing + embodied expertise`.

## 8. Why this matters for Wedding

The Wedding notebooks should not be read as a single outward observational trip whose knowledge is simply brought home afterward.

The Fischer control case suggests testing for **iterative loops**:

- Did Wedding arrive with prior samples, drawings, letters or technical problems?
- Did British contacts test or modify anything that later generated correspondence?
- Did information/material continue to move after he left Britain?
- Did later letters, publications or specimens trigger a return exchange?
- Did Wedding reuse British judgements or contacts inside Berlin institutions?
- Did Berlin-produced specimens/results later travel back to Britain?

This matters especially for the 1885 Wedding–Sorby exchange: it may be more productive to reconstruct repeated circuits of object/preparation/judgement than to search only for a single origin point.

## 9. Relevance to the later metallographic problem

The Fischer circuit provides a mid-century baseline for **distributed testing across distance**:

`producer -> portable sample -> external institutional test -> written judgement -> repeat production -> return circulation of judgement`.

The later metallographic system may add different means of stabilising such circuits:

`producer/material -> prepared specimen -> microscopic observation -> microphotograph -> labelled/numbered image/specimen -> distributed comparison`.

The later sequence remains a hypothesis until controlled in Wedding/Martens evidence.

## Claim ceiling

The current evidence supports an iterative transnational material/testing/document circuit between Fischer, the Royal Mint and Sheffield contacts. It does **not** establish:

- that every 1846 journey objective was caused by Royal Mint testing;
- a direct institutional genealogy from Royal Mint testing to Berlin material-testing laboratories;
- that later metallography was designed to replace this travel circuit;
- that all participants understood the circuit in the same analytical terms used here.

# Collection-first holdings census — Iron Library / EBA / GFA

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE COLLECTION-LEVEL CENSUS  
**Rule:** Do not build the holdings map around Wedding, Martens, Sorby, microscopy, or any other already-selected problem. First identify large, internally coherent, chronologically usable blocks. Item-level extraction comes later.

## 0. Governing correction: three evidence systems, not two

Keep the following analytically separate:

1. **LIBRARY — Eisenbibliothek / Iron Library**: classified printed holdings, serial runs, handbooks, technical literature, product/company literature, rara, manuscripts treated as library holdings.
2. **EBA — Eisenbibliothek Archiv**: archival fonds acquired/maintained by the Iron Library but not belonging to GF corporate provenance.
3. **GFA — Georg Fischer Archiv / GF Corporate Archives**: Fischer-family, company, subsidiary, media, object and administrative fonds.

Do not write generic claims such as “the Iron Library archive holds …” when the evidence actually belongs to one of these three systems.

## 1. LIBRARY — large systematic blocks first

### 1.1 Overall scale and collecting structure

Current official library description:

- c. **50,000 media units**;
- c. **1,000 periodicals**, c. 70 current;
- c. **6,000 pre-1900 rara titles**;
- declared collecting priorities include metallurgy, mining, history of technology, history of science, economic/industrial history, product catalogues/company publications and encyclopaedias.

Important structural rule: donated books are generally **not preserved as closed donor libraries**. They are integrated by subject into the library’s systematic classes. Therefore donor provenance is usually a poor first unit for a collection-first census; **subject blocks + serial runs** are the stronger units.

Official source: Iron Library, `Bibliothek / Library`; `Schenkungen / Donations`.

### 1.2 Historical pre-1900 subject blocks

The published *Handbuch der historischen Buchbestände in der Schweiz* profile gives a useful systematic snapshot of the old holdings. The figures are historical catalogue counts, not current totals, but they establish real collection density:

- **Mining / Bergbau:** 579 works, including c. 375 nineteenth-century titles.
- **Geology / Geognosy:** 172 works, over 100 nineteenth-century titles.
- **Mineralogy:** 182 works, 110 nineteenth-century titles.
- **Smelting / iron and steel production:** 370 works, with the nineteenth century dominant.
- **Metallurgy** (physical, chemical and technical properties of metals, alloys and materials): 272 works.
- **Applications / uses of iron, steel and other metals:** 458 works, almost all nineteenth century.
- Historical reference/encyclopaedic literature is also extensive; the Krünitz encyclopaedia (242 vols, 1773–1858) is complete.

For the present census these are **large subject shelves**, not a list of potentially relevant books.

Official source: *Handbuch der historischen Buchbestände in der Schweiz*, Eisenbibliothek entry, pp. 515–517.

### 1.3 Serial / periodical block — potentially the strongest nineteenth-century continuous corpus

The historical profile records c. **15,550 periodical volumes**, around 750 individual titles/series at the time of compilation; the current official site states c. 1,000 periodicals. Nineteenth-century technical publishing is therefore a collection block in its own right rather than a set of isolated journals.

Long/important historical runs explicitly documented in descriptions of the Iron Library include:

- *Annales des Mines*;
- *Dinglers Polytechnisches Journal* (1820–1931);
- *Journal of the Iron and Steel Institute* (from 1871 in the Iron Library holdings descriptions);
- *Stahl und Eisen* (from 1881 in holdings descriptions).

**Census priority:** reconstruct the complete 1800–1900 serial-title list and date completeness before selecting any author/topic. This may be the single largest coherent source environment for c.1850–1885.

### 1.4 Later donations that thicken existing systematic shelves

Because donations are integrated into subject classes, these should be treated as later accretions to existing blocks rather than independent fonds. Examples:

- Bernhard Droste: material testing / BAM history (2020, 2023; 228 titles described in *Ferrum* 93);
- Maschinenfabrik Rieter AG: journals in materials engineering, metallurgy, foundry, materials testing (2006);
- Rudolf Burkolter: steel/iron, iron research, metallurgy, foundry journals (2006);
- Urs Werner: metallurgy, aluminium, foundry, welding, copper, measurement/testing technology (2006);
- Axel Hamm: foundry and metal science (2007);
- Vreni Nadig: *Stahl und Eisen* (2007).

These are useful indicators of **subject depth**, not preserved research archives.

### 1.5 Library census judgement so far

For c.1850–1885 the strongest “big block” hypothesis is currently:

> **nineteenth-century subject shelves (mineralogy / geology / metallurgy / iron-steel production) + continuous technical/scientific serial runs.**

This must be tested through IRONCAT/classification-level harvesting, not author searches.

## 2. EBA — Eisenbibliothek Archiv

EBA must be separated from both the Library and GF Corporate Archives. Current ANTON root contains nine fonds.

### 2.1 EBA fonds map

- **EBA 1 — Karten und Pläne**, 1737–2000, 2 lm.
- **EBA 2 — Eisenbergwerk Gonzen**, 1438–2019, 8 lm.
- **EBA 3 — Hafftersche Eisenhandlung, Weinfelden**, 1722–1983, 15 lm.
- **EBA 4 — Küderli & Co. (vormals Bär & Co.), Zürich**, 1854–1971, 4 lm.
- **EBA 5 — Studiengesellschaft für die Nutzbarmachung schweizerischer Lagerstätten mineralischer Rohstoffe**, 1916–2025, 3 lm.
- **EBA 6 — Archiv des Schweizerischen Eisenhandels**, 1852–2011, 39 lm.
- **EBA 7 — Verschiedene Archive und Nachlässe**, 1848–1989, 3 lm.
- **EBA 8 — Graphische Sammlung und Kunstsammlung**, 1496–1995, 3 lm.
- **EBA 9 — Stiftung Eisenbibliothek**, 1946–2025, 13 lm.

Official source: ANTON, EBA root.

### 2.2 EBA 3 — Haffter Archive: genuine closed coherent block

**High collection-coherence. High nineteenth-century continuity.**

- 15 lm;
- transfer documentation states that the heirs donated **185 volumes and 42 brochures of business books plus c.7,900 business letters**, on condition that the entire archive be maintained as the closed `Haffter-Archiv`;
- business correspondence/accounting is extensive and internally serial;
- outgoing correspondence copybooks: 57 volumes, 1779–1825;
- correspondence survives strongly into the nineteenth century; an indexed correspondence block covers 1835–1860;
- accounting/journals/calculation books continue through the 1860s and beyond.

This is exactly the sort of corpus the census is looking for: an internally coherent archive, not an extraction. It is primarily iron trade/business/technical-commercial knowledge, and should be retained even if it never becomes a microscopy case.

### 2.3 EBA 4 — Küderli/Bär iron-trade archive

**Coherent but smaller; unusually good overlap with c.1850–1885.**

- 4 lm;
- c.150 books, c.20 description units;
- fonds begins 1854;
- correspondence 1858–1872;
- price lists 1878–1880;
- profile-iron albums 1877–1885;
- cash book begins 1884; sales journal begins 1885;
- later inventories and price-list runs continue the archive.

Content classes: business books, sales journals, profile albums, price lists, product programmes.

### 2.4 EBA 6 — Swiss iron-trade archive

**Very large block: 39 lm, 1852–2011.**

The top-level date begins inside the present nineteenth-century window, but current public item visibility suggests much of the organized association material is later nineteenth/twentieth century. Do not infer a dense 1850s block from the fonds date alone.

**Next census action:** identify sub-fonds / predecessor organizations and quantify what actually predates 1885. Keep EBA 6 high on the structural list because of its scale, but chronology remains OPEN.

### 2.5 EBA 2 — Gonzen iron mine

**Large scientific/material archive, but main scientific density is twentieth century.**

- 8 lm, c.800 description units, c.120 digitised photographs;
- built partly around the Neher family papers and mineralogist/physicist Willfried Epprecht’s papers;
- contains a highly coherent `Geologie / Petrographie` class with rock/ore series, thin sections (`Dünnschliffe`), polished sections (`Anschliffe`), chemical analyses, optical/refraction material, X-ray films and field/mine investigations;
- however the petrographic/microscopic class is chiefly 1940s–1960s;
- older classes/publications reach into the nineteenth century, including `Rohstoffe: Gewinnung und Verarbeitung` from 1868.

For the present census: **important as proof of a coherent materials-observation archive at Paradies, but weak chronological fit for c.1850–1885.** Do not mine isolated early items from it yet.

### 2.6 EBA 7 — assorted archives and estates

- 3 lm, c.100 description units;
- includes materials on Graubünden mining, Swiss ore-deposit studies, Granger archive, Viktor Heberlein, Hans-Werner Schütt, Schaffhausen bog-iron mines, Edelgussverband.

This is a container of separate mini-fonds, not one coherent nineteenth-century block. Keep for later sub-fonds census; do not treat its 1848 start date as evidence of density.

### 2.7 EBA 8 — graphic/art collection

- 3 lm; c.170 sheets plus tin-figure boxes; c.180 description units; c.200 digitised objects;
- metallurgy, mining/smelting and metalworking prints/maps/sketches/photos/drawings;
- chronologically broad and assembled rather than provenance-coherent.

Useful as a **representation collection**, but not a continuous c.1850–1885 evidential archive. Retain as a secondary collection block.

## 3. GFA — GF Corporate Archives

### 3.1 GFA 1 Historisches Firmenarchiv (Altbestand)

**Largest historically relevant container, but archivally composite.**

- 280 lm;
- c.2,600 A4/folio boxes + special formats/books;
- c.3,900 description units + c.1,300 media;
- includes company records since the nineteenth century, Fischer family papers and acquired/subsidiary material;
- crucial caveat: the historical archive was created retrospectively (collecting began in the 1920s; HFA established 1943), and the present thematic classes were formed/reworked in later archival reorganisations. It is therefore not a single continuously accrued nineteenth-century registry.

Top-level classes overlapping c.1850–1885 include:

- `Familie Fischer` (1525–2023);
- `Ausstellungen, Messen` (1804–1971);
- `Giessereien, Produktion, Produkte` (1817–2006);
- `Personelles, Soziales` (1846–2008);
- `Dokumentation` (1860–2025);
- `Kommunikation` (1880–2017);
- `Firmenübernahmen ...`, including substantial inherited Rauschenbach material from the nineteenth century.

**Census rule:** GFA 1 may contain thick nineteenth-century clusters, but each class must be checked for actual series continuity; do not equate the 280-lm fonds with 280 lm of usable 1850–1885 material.

### 3.2 Rauschenbach cluster inside GFA 1

Potentially one of the more genuinely continuous nineteenth-century technical blocks embedded in the Altbestand. The Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach classes include:

- general mechanical engineering from 1852;
- foundry machines with records reaching back to 1827;
- woodworking machines from 1842;
- management material from 1871;
- later specialized machinery/documentation.

This deserves collection-level quantification before any item selection.

### 3.3 GFA 5 Personnel Department

- 18 lm, 1862–2019, c.320 description units.

Although the fonds date starts in 1862, its administrative identity is a later corporate personnel-department structure. **Do not classify it as a strong 1860s corpus solely from the start date.** Needs date-distribution audit before promotion.

### 3.4 GFA 8 Maps and Plans

- 30 lm, 1830–2001, c.140 description units;
- primarily plans of various GF works plus technical drawings/other plan material.

Large physical block and chronologically early at top level, but current description indicates strong later plant emphasis. Needs a pre-1890 date-distribution census before relevance can be judged.

### 3.5 GFA 32 Object Collection

- 48 lm; c.1,000 objects; c.430 description units;
- includes products, work tools, family objects, models, fittings, machine products etc.; earliest material reaches the founding period;
- current object classes were formed only in 2021 from materials that often lacked context.

This is a large **object collection**, but not provenance-coherent. It may become useful for material culture, but should not be mistaken for a continuous nineteenth-century technical series.

### 3.6 Later coherent media systems (chronologically secondary for 1850–1885)

Examples include:

- **GFA 25 Werk Singen, Glasplatten** — 1900–1960, 3 lm, c.1,300 description units; coherent glass-plate photo corpus divided by subject (labs, products, works, exhibitions etc.);
- GFA 16 general glass-plate photo archive and related GFA 12/GFA 17 carrier-separated photo systems;
- GFA 13 photo albums.

These are excellent large media blocks, but mainly postdate the current nineteenth-century window. Retain as later controls, not as reasons to stretch the chronology.

## 4. First-pass ranking by COLLECTION COHERENCE, not by current research question

### Tier A — large / coherent / nineteenth-century

1. **LIBRARY nineteenth-century subject system + serial runs** — likely the largest and most directly usable environment.
2. **EBA 3 Haffter Archive** — 15 lm closed archive with large internally serial correspondence/accounting structure.
3. **GFA 1 selected nineteenth-century classes** — huge but composite; requires class-level density audit before use.

6. **GFA 8 Maps and Plans** — 30 lm, starts 1830; actual pre-1890 density open.

### Tier C — coherent but mainly later, useful as controls

8. **EBA 2 Gonzen**, especially Geology/Petrography — scientifically/materially coherent, mainly 1940s+.
9. **GFA photo/glass-plate systems (GFA 16/25 etc.)** — strong carrier/infrastructure corpora, mainly 20th century.

### Tier D — assembled / heterogeneous

10. **EBA 7 miscellaneous archives/estates** — requires decomposition into mini-fonds.
11. **EBA 8 graphic/art collection** — relevant representation material but collected/heterogeneous.
12. **GFA 32 object collection** — large, materially rich, but assembled and often decontextualized.

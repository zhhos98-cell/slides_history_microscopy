# Supply geography, metrology and profile commensurability — hypothesis stress test

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE HYPOTHESIS / EXTERNAL-CONTEXT CONTROL  
**Rule:** this note does not assert a transition from `letters` to `standards`. It identifies a historically specific conjunction that the Haffter/Küderli corpus can test: changing supply geography, changing measurement regimes, and the emergence of profile-based/normal-profile ordering.

## 0. Why this is stronger than generic `documentary standardization`

The collection-driven corpus now contains a striking chronological alignment:

- **Haffter** preserves a dense transregional iron-trade archive whose business books explicitly record purchase prices plus ancillary costs such as insurance, transport and customs;
- **Küderli/Bär correspondence runs 1858–1872**;
- the Küderli fonds history states that **from 1870 the firm shifted its centre of supply from France to Germany, increasing Basel's importance as an `Eingangstor der Importe`**;
- **Küderli Profileisenalbum runs 1877–1885 (3 vols)** and price lists 1878–80;
- Switzerland's mid-century national measurement regime already embedded decimal/metre-kilogram equivalences while retaining traditional names, and the fully metric legal regime was introduced in **1877**;
- German engineering associations established **Normalprofile für Walzeisen in 1879–80**, followed by the first `Deutsches Normalprofilbuch` in 1881;
- already in 1869, German technical print described French Zores rolled sections through cross-section type, millimetre dimensions and kilograms per running metre.

This produces a precise empirical problem:

> **how did iron merchants make products comparable across changing suppliers, measurement systems and profile series, and what role did correspondence, calculations, profile albums, price lists and sales books play in that work?**

The point is not that standardization inevitably replaced correspondence. The point is to test where equivalence was produced, by whom, and in which carrier.

## 1. Haffter as the earlier transregional comparison regime

### Controlled fonds-level evidence

EBA 3 is 15 lm. The current official fonds description states that the Haffter firm's trading area included:

- Styria;
- Tyrol;
- southern Germany as far as Regensburg, Frankfurt a.M., Mainz and Saarbrücken;
- frequent business relations with France and England;
- contact with Johann Conrad Fischer;
- especially close relations with Neher in Laufen.

The same official description states:

- **over 7,000 original incoming letters** survive, arranged by firms and accessible via person/place indexes;
- the 1953 donation included **7,900 business letters**, 185 volumes and 42 brochures;
- the ten `Calculationen` volumes record **purchase prices together with ancillary expenses such as insurance, transport and customs**;
- bookkeeping for stocks deposited with forwarding/commission houses and iron foundries, debtor problems and carriers gives unusually rich evidence for commercial coordination.

Official ANTON fonds description:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&index=3&parent=2&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position

### Consequence

Haffter is not merely `earlier correspondence`. It is a transregional **conversion/calculation infrastructure** in which heterogeneous supplier locations, freight, insurance, tariffs, prices and product terms had to be made actionable in one merchant system.

This sharpens **H2**. The `Calculationen` volumes should be inspected not only for prices but for:

`SUPPLIER_LOCATION / PRODUCT_TERM / UNIT / PURCHASE_PRICE / CURRENCY / INSURANCE / FREIGHT / CUSTOMS / COMMISSION / CONVERSION / LANDED_COST / PRICE_QUOTATION / CROSS_REFERENCE`.

### Important source-critical correction

`EBA 3/24 = 555 Akten` must not be paraphrased as `555 individual letters`. The fonds-level history documents >7,000 original incoming letters / 7,900 business letters in the donated archive. The 555 catalogue units are therefore filing/descriptive units at a different scale.

This makes retrieval-unit clarification even more important.

## 2. Küderli contains an explicit supply-geography hinge in 1870

The official EBA 4 fonds history states:

> founded in Zürich in 1852 as `Schinz & Baer`; **from 1870 the company shifted its centre of supply from France to Germany, making Basel more important as an entrance gate for imports**.

Official ANTON fonds description:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/EBA%204

The relevant surviving carrier sequence is:

- `EBA 4/5` — Convention der Zürcher Eisenhändler, 1854 — 1 file;
- `EBA 4/7` — correspondence, 1858–72 — 1 present catalogue file;
- `EBA 4/14` — Profileisenalbum, 1877–85 — 3 vols;
- `EBA 4/6` — price lists, 1878–80 — 2 files;
- `EBA 4/4` — cashbooks, 1884–89 — 2 vols;
- `EBA 4/15` — sales journals, 1885–97 — 11 vols.

### Consequence for K1

The previous three-slice sampling strategy becomes historically stronger:

1. **1858–60** — early French-centred supply phase;
2. **1865–67** — mature pre-shift phase, comparable with Haffter's last dense business-book years;
3. **1870–72** — the exact documented France → Germany supply shift.

For K1, add fields:

`SUPPLIER / SUPPLIER_PLACE / COUNTRY_REGION / ROUTE_GATEWAY / BASEL_REFERENCE / PRODUCT_TERM / PROFILE_OR_DIMENSION / UNIT / CURRENCY / PRICE / FREIGHT / CUSTOMS / DELIVERY_TIME / SUBSTITUTE_EQUIVALENT / COMPARISON_LANGUAGE`.

### Claim ceiling

Secure:
- fonds biography reports the shift from France to Germany from 1870 and Basel's greater importance.

Open:
- causal mechanism;
- whether the 1870–71 Franco-Prussian War caused, accelerated, or merely coincided with the shift;
- which suppliers/products moved first;
- whether Basel's importance is visible directly in the surviving correspondence rather than only in later historical reconstruction.

Do not convert `since 1870` into a causal war thesis without manuscript evidence.

## 3. Transport-system context: useful hinge, not yet causal explanation

Swiss railway history gives a plausible structural context:

- by **1860** a continuous rail connection ran from Geneva through the Swiss plateau to Lake Constance;
- by **1870** the other principal lines were largely built;
- international connections included Basel to the Baden railway and Alsatian railway, Schaffhausen to the Baden State Railway, and Les Verrières toward Paris.

HLS railway overview:
- https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/007961/2015-02-11/

The HLS account of the Franco-Prussian War adds:

- in 1870 Switzerland's rail connections abroad were with France and countries of the German Customs Union;
- war disrupted some access routes and imports.

HLS war overview:
- https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008925/2010-03-17/

### Research use

This context tells us what to look for in K1:

- route names;
- forwarding agents;
- station/gateway changes;
- delays or route substitutions;
- freight-price changes;
- Basel versus western gateways;
- explicit war/disruption language.

It does **not** prove that railway integration or the war explains the corporate supply shift.

## 4. Switzerland's measurement regime is a two-stage problem, not a simple `metrication in 1877` story

### Mid-century federal standardisation

The federal measure/weight law adopted in 1851 and implemented across the Confederation by the end of 1856 established a nationally uniform system based on the earlier concordat. Crucially, it retained traditional names while fixing them against decimal metric values:

- Swiss foot = exactly **0.3 metre**;
- decimal subdivision of foot → Zoll → Linie → Strich;
- Swiss pound = **500 grams / 0.5 kg**;
- Zentner = **50 kg**.

Primary law / conversion table:
- Staatsarchiv Zürich, `Bundesgesetz betreffend die Maß- und Gewichtsordnung`, 1851/1852, published in the cantonal law collection in 1856;
- digitised federal-law conversion table on e-rara.

Sources:
- https://www.zentraleserien.zh.ch/api/html/data/OS/OS_10__S__245-252__t.xml
- https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/30512436.pdf

### Full metric regime

A federal law of 3 July 1875 and implementing regulations brought the metric regime into force in **1877**. Swiss archival/HLS summaries consistently mark 1877 as the legal replacement of earlier named units by metre/kilogram-based units.

Useful controls:
- https://books.google.com/books?id=QPFZMOm2EloC
- https://dls.staatsarchiv.bs.ch/records/629870
- HLS unit histories, e.g. Zentner/Zoll.

### Consequence

The question is **not** `when did merchants discover metric measurement?`

By the 1850s Swiss commercial units were already deliberately commensurable with metric values. Instead test:

- which unit vocabulary merchants actually used in correspondence and books;
- whether imported French/German product specifications carried different unit conventions;
- whether traditional Swiss names persisted despite metric equivalence;
- whether 1877 changes notation, tabulation or product-description practice;
- whether profile albums use millimetres/kg-per-metre even before/independently of formal German Normalprofile.

## 5. Profile iron was already a dimensional commodity before German Normalprofile

A 1869 *Dinglers Polytechnisches Journal* notice on French `Zores-Eisen` describes multiple rolled profile types by:

- cross-sectional form;
- a sequence of dimensions in **millimetres**;
- weight in **kilograms per running metre**;
- intended construction use.

Source:
- https://dingler.bbaw.de/articles/ar192070.html

Example structural point:

`profile shape → dimensional series → linear weight → expected use`.

This is already a commodity-description technology that makes multiple rolled forms comparable, even before a national German Normalprofil system.

Therefore avoid the false binary:

> bespoke/local profiles → suddenly standardised profiles in 1881.

The more plausible historical sequence to test is:

> **maker-/merchant-specific profile series and dimensional tables → proliferating cross-firm comparison → association-level Normalprofile.**

## 6. German Normalprofile arrive inside the Küderli Profileisenalbum window

Historical engineering reference literature states that:

- a commission of the Verband deutscher Architekten- und Ingenieurvereine and Verein Deutscher Ingenieure established **Normalprofile für Walzeisen in 1879–80**;
- Heinzerling and Intze issued the first **`Deutsches Normalprofilbuch` in 1881**;
- the aim was to reconcile theoretical, construction and rolling-mill requirements;
- later reference literature treats these profiles as widely adopted while noting that non-normal maker profiles continued to exist.

Useful controls:
- https://technik.de-academic.com/16508/Normalprofile
- https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/MKL1888%3AWalzeisen

### Chronological fit with EBA 4

`EBA 4/14 Profileisenalbum = 1877–1885`.

Thus the three surviving merchant albums **bracket the 1879–81 German Normalprofil moment**.

This is currently one of the strongest item-level reasons to inspect K2 onsite.

### Exact onsite test for each profile-album volume/page

Record:

`DATE / SUPPLIER_OR_MILL / COUNTRY_REGION / PROFILE_FAMILY / DRAWN_OR_PRINTED / DIMENSION_LABELS / UNIT / LINEAR_WEIGHT / PRODUCT_NUMBER / PRICE_LINK / NORMAL_OR_SPECIAL_LANGUAGE / STANDARD_REFERENCE / PASTED_INSERT / OVERWRITE / DELETION / ADDITION / PAGE_SEQUENCE / WEAR / LATER_REVISION`.

Then ask:

1. Do pre-1879 pages already have stable dimensional series?
2. Does terminology or ordering change around 1879–81?
3. Is `Normalprofil` language actually present?
4. Are German mill series substituted for French series after the 1870 supply shift?
5. Do price lists use the same identifiers as the profile albums?
6. Can 1884–85 cash/sales entries be routed back to profile identifiers?
7. Do non-normal/bespoke profiles persist alongside normalized ones?

## 7. Strongest current combined operation

The corpus can now support a much more precise cross-carrier operation:

### Haffter / c.1858–67

`SUPPLIER / PLACE → PRODUCT WORDING → UNIT → PURCHASE PRICE → INSURANCE / FREIGHT / CUSTOMS → LANDED COST → QUOTATION / COMMISSION`

### Küderli correspondence / 1858–72

`SUPPLIER / COUNTRY / ROUTE → PRODUCT WORDING → DIMENSION / UNIT → PRICE / FREIGHT → SUBSTITUTE / COMPARISON → ORDER`

### Küderli profiles / prices / sales / 1877–85

`PROFILE DRAWING / CODE / DIMENSIONS / LINEAR WEIGHT → PRICE-LIST ENTRY → CASH / SALES ENTRY → CUSTOMER / QUANTITY`

### Printed technical comparator

`published profile family → dimensional/weight table → normal-profile language / engineering use`.

This makes **commensurability** an empirical verb rather than a theoretical label:

> products become comparable only if archival carriers reveal operations that align names, shapes, dimensions, units, weights, prices, origins and routes.

## 8. Historiographic stress test

Useful conceptual literature exists on `commensuration` as comparison through a common metric and on commercial calculation as a technology of market participation. These are useful analytic controls, not ready-made historical explanations.

- Wendy Espeland & Mitchell Stevens, `Commensuration as a Social Process` (1998): comparison of different entities according to a common metric.
- Caitlin Rosenthal, `Capitalism by Algorithm` (2023): commercial arithmetic as practical technology for calculation/conversion in expanding markets.

Controls:
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.soc.24.1.313
- https://academic.oup.com/book/45699/chapter/398105548

### Historiographic caution

Do not let a general theory of commensuration swallow the archival problem. The contribution would lie in reconstructing **which specific merchant documents performed which conversions**, and where comparison failed or remained local.

The strongest novelty hypothesis is not `standardization happened` but:

> **a merchant archive may preserve the practical intermediate layer between heterogeneous regional supply and later association-level normal profiles.**

This remains a hypothesis until EBA 4/14 is physically inspected.

## 10. Claim ceiling

### Secure

- Haffter's archive is transregional and preserves thousands of business letters plus accounting/calculation series.
- its `Calculationen` explicitly include purchase price plus insurance, transport and customs costs.
- Küderli's official fonds history records a supply-centre shift from France to Germany from 1870, increasing Basel's role.
- Küderli correspondence survives through 1872.
- three `Profileisenalbum` volumes survive for 1877–85, with price lists 1878–80, cashbooks from 1884 and sales journals from 1885.
- Swiss measurement unification at mid-century already used exact metric equivalences under traditional unit names; full metric legal adoption follows in 1877.
- profile iron was described dimensionally in mm and kg/running metre in German technical print by 1869.
- German Normalprofile were formulated 1879–80 and the first Normalprofilbuch appeared in 1881.

### Strong hypothesis

- EBA 4/14 may preserve a merchant-level response to or precursor of profile normalization.
- K1 + K2 may allow supply-geography change to be linked to changes in product-description/order carriers.
- Haffter can supply an earlier model of transregional conversion/cost calculation against which Küderli can be compared.

### Open / do not assert

- that the Franco-Prussian War caused Küderli's supply shift;
- that metrication caused the profile-album form;
- that the 1877–85 albums use German Normalprofile;
- that profile albums replace correspondence rather than coexist with other communication;
- that German suppliers replaced French suppliers uniformly across all product types;
- that merchant standardisation preceded/followed engineering standardisation in one direction;
- that surviving album order is original and contemporaneous without physical inspection.

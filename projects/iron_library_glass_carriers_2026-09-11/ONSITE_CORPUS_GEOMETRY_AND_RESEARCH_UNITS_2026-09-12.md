# Onsite corpus geometry and research units — Iron Library residency

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE FEASIBILITY / CORPUS-DESIGN MAP  
**Rule:** convert the collection-first census into physically consultable research units without pretending that every promising catalogue block can be exhaustively read in a 2–3 week residence.

## 0. Current conclusion

The strongest c.1850–1885 holdings do **not** form one homogeneous archive. They form two dense archival-commercial clusters separated by a transition zone:

> **Cluster A — c.1858–67:** Haffter + Küderli correspondence/business books + Library technical/serial environment, with native-GF product communication entering c.1865–67.  
> **Bridge — c.1868–72:** Haffter accounting tail + Küderli correspondence + native-GF product documents + small/provisional Rauschenbach labour/company node.  
> **Cluster B — c.1877–85:** Küderli profile/price/cash/sales records + Library product/serial environment + native-GF product/exhibition documents + selected Traisen technical plans.

This is currently a stronger corpus geometry than either:

- a broad `1850–1885 iron history` survey; or
- a project built around Rauschenbach as though a large continuous pre-1885 operational archive were already proven.

The key methodological consequence is that the residence should be built from **research units** with explicit stopping rules.

---

## 1. Research-unit definition

A viable onsite research unit should have:

1. a stable catalogue/fonds anchor;
2. a bounded chronological or carrier slice;
3. a legible record-generating activity;
4. a realistic physical retrieval unit;
5. a reason why onsite comparison adds evidence beyond remote substitutes;
6. a pre-defined capture/logging schema so the visit is not consumed by undirected reading.

Use three levels:

- **CORE UNIT** — must be consultable for the project to work;
- **COMPARATIVE UNIT** — strengthens comparison but can be dropped if retrieval proves inefficient;
- **OBJECT CODA / CONTROL** — high-density object evidence, but should not determine the entire corpus unless the question later requires it.

## 2. Core Unit H1 — Haffter correspondence overlap, 1858–1860

### Catalogue anchor

- **EBA 3/24 Korrespondenz**, 1835–1860 — **555 files**.

### Why this slice

1858–60 is the cleanest direct overlap between:

- Haffter's still-active dense correspondence archive;
- Küderli correspondence beginning in 1858;
- the continuous Library printed/serial environment.

This is the best current window for comparing two iron-trade/commercial communication systems without relying on later retrospective corporate files.

### Onsite operation

Do **not** plan to read all 555 files.

Required first-stage operation:

1. inspect arrangement / box / file numbering physically or through any internal list;
2. determine whether 1858–60 can be isolated cleanly;
3. construct a systematic sample by year and correspondent/document type;
4. photograph/index complete sampled files where rules permit;
5. record annotations, enclosures, letterhead/paper/form, calculation marks, copied/forwarded material and filing traces.

### Capture schema

`DATE / SENDER / RECIPIENT / PLACE / MATERIAL_OR_PRODUCT / QUANTITY / PRICE / QUALITY_TERM / TRANSPORT / CREDIT_PAYMENT / ENCLOSURE / REFERENCE_TO_PRICE_LIST_OR_CATALOGUE / HANDWRITING_OR_FORM / FILE_ORDER / PHYSICAL_MARKS / RELATED_BOOK_SERIES`.

### Claim ceiling

- strong correspondence density = secure;
- original nineteenth-century filing order = **not established**;
- present series hierarchy = later arrangement, so any historical filing claim must come from physical evidence, not the ANTON hierarchy itself.

**Status:** CORE / O1 candidate pending staff access confirmation.

## 3. Core Unit H2 — Haffter business-book / price / freight transition, 1865–1867

### Catalogue anchors

- `EBA 3/10 Calculationen`, 1824–1867 — 10 vols;
- `EBA 3/11 Ertheilte Commissionen`, 1833–1867 — 5 vols;
- `EBA 3/16 Ertheilte Preis-Courants`, 1857–1867 — 1 vol;
- `EBA 3/19 Frachten-Rechnungen`, 1865–1867 — 1 vol;
- `EBA 3/25 Diverse Debitoren`, 1783–1876 — 18 vols;
- fonds-level outgoing-copybook sequence continues to 1867.

### Why this slice

This isolates the **last dense Haffter years** and overlaps with:

- active Küderli correspondence;
- emerging native-GF product/prospectus documentation;
- Library serial/technical print.

It is therefore stronger than 1868–72 if the historical operation concerns how trade information is converted into calculation, commission, price and freight records.

### Onsite operation

The target is not total transcription. It is a cross-book concordance around selected transactions/products/dates:

`incoming/outgoing communication → calculation → commission/order → price quotation → freight/accounting trace`.

Where possible, follow the same named firm/product/date across more than one surviving book type.

### Capture schema

`DATE / BOOK_SERIES / ENTRY_ID_OR_PAGE / COUNTERPARTY / PRODUCT_PROFILE / QUANTITY / PRICE / COMMISSION / FREIGHT / ORIGIN_DESTINATION / CROSS_REFERENCE / CORRECTION / TABLE_OR_FORM / UNIT_OF_MEASURE / CURRENCY`.

**Status:** CORE / O1 candidate.

---

## 4. Core Unit K1 — Küderli correspondence, 1858–1872

### Catalogue anchor

- **EBA 4/7 Korrespondenz**, 1858–1872 — catalogued as **1 file**.

### Source-critical issue

`1 file` is not a usable estimate of research scale. It may be one folder, packet, box-level unit or a current aggregate description. Staff/internal finding-aid clarification is therefore decisive.

### Sampling logic if physically large

Use three chronological samples rather than reading linearly:

1. **1858–60** — direct Haffter correspondence overlap;
2. **1865–67** — overlap with Haffter business-book end phase + native-GF product regime onset;
3. **1870–72** — end of Küderli correspondence phase + beginning of the later labour/company-registration environment.

This gives three controlled slices through one correspondence corpus and allows documentary change to be tested without assuming continuity of topic.

### Capture schema

Use the same schema as H1 so that comparison is actually possible.

**Status:** CORE / O1 candidate, but practical scale OPEN until retrieval-unit confirmation.

## 5. Core Unit K2 — Küderli product/order/accounting shift, 1877–1885

### Catalogue anchors

- `EBA 4/14 Profileisenalbum`, 1877–1885 — **3 vols**;
- `EBA 4/6 Preislisten`, 1878–1880 — **2 files**;
- `EBA 4/4 Kassabuch`, 1884–1889 — **2 vols**;
- `EBA 4/15 Verkaufsjournal`, 1885–1897 — **11 vols**.

### Why this is exceptionally clean

The carrier types themselves change:

> correspondence → profile albums → price lists → cashbooks → sales journals.

This creates a naturally bounded late cluster around **product form, comparability, pricing and ordering**.

The unit is strong even if Rauschenbach remains demoted.

### Onsite operation

Primary cross-carrier operation:

`PROFILE / DIMENSION / PRODUCT NAME → PRICE-LIST ENTRY → CASH/SALES ENTRY → CUSTOMER/ORDER TRACE`.

The aim is to determine whether product identity and comparability are stabilized differently across visual profile, price, and transaction records.

### Capture schema

`DATE / CARRIER_TYPE / PROFILE_SHAPE / DIMENSION / MATERIAL_TERM / PRODUCT_CODE_OR_NAME / PRICE / UNIT / CUSTOMER / QUANTITY / ORDER_OR_SALE_REFERENCE / ANNOTATION / INSERTION / REVISION / PHYSICAL_WEAR`.

### Feasibility

The core pre-/at-1885 carrier count is compact enough to be a plausible residency unit, but only the relevant chronological portions of the 1884–89 cashbooks and 1885–97 sales journals belong to the central window.

**Status:** CORE / strongest compact O1 candidate.

## 6. Comparative Unit L1 — Library serial snapshots

### Structural anchor

- historical census: **123 nineteenth-century periodical titles**;
- large continuous technical/scientific serial environment;
- controlled examples include *Dinglers Polytechnisches Journal*, *Annales des Mines*, *Journal of the Iron and Steel Institute*, and *Stahl und Eisen*.

### Role

Do not read the Library as a general background bibliography. Use it as a **contemporaneous printed reference environment** around the archival slices.

Potential snapshot years:

- 1858–60;
- 1865–67;
- 1877–80;
- 1884–85.

### Onsite question

What printed classifications, product descriptions, market/technical vocabularies and visual/tabular conventions were available in the same years as the archival records?

### Access ceiling

Default onsite value = MEDIUM because remote and interlibrary substitutes often exist. Promote only:

- rare local runs/gaps;
- copy-specific annotations/provenance;
- difficult-to-source grey literature;
- or a large local comparative shelf operation that cannot be replicated efficiently elsewhere.

**Status:** COMPARATIVE / structural backbone, not travel justification by itself.

## 7. Comparative Unit L2 — Library company/product grey literature

### Structural anchor

The Library explicitly collects product catalogues/company publications/price literature; historical morphology shows this is a large collection programme rather than a handful of selected items.

### Role

This is the natural printed comparison to K2 and native-GF product documents.

Potential operation:

`company publication / catalogue / price book → product naming → profile/dimension system → price/unit → visual/tabular ordering`.

### Admission rule

Only include titles after remote-substitutability screening. A rare title that already has a complete facsimile is not automatically an onsite source.

**Status:** COMPARATIVE; item list requires current Library export or focused catalogue control.

---

## 8. Comparative Unit G1 — native GF product/market regime, c.1865–1883

### Structural role

Publicly controlled evidence shows a real regime of:

- prospectuses;
- product catalogues;
- price lists;
- posters;
- works rules;
- exhibition documents;
- selected product objects.

This is not a regular management registry. It is valuable because it offers a **producer-side product/market communication regime** adjacent to Haffter/Küderli trade evidence.

### Operation

Compare vocabulary/formats with H2 and K2:

`product definition / dimensions / grades / price / audience / visual form / ordering logic / exhibition display`.

### Constraint

Mixed digitisation and retrospective GFA arrangement mean it should be selected item-by-item after remote control.

**Status:** COMPARATIVE / Tier B.

## 9. Conditional Unit R1 — Rauschenbach bound-register core

### Publicly proven carriers

- `GFA 1/806 Mitarbeiterverzeichnisse`, 1871–1930 — **1 volume**;
- `GFA 1/809 Krankenkasse`, 1872–1930 — **10 volumes**.

### Role

These provide a real labour/operational registration system, but they do not by themselves prove a large company archive.

Potential question:

How does a company begin to render workers/social-insurance relations serially recordable at the same moment that commercial/product records elsewhere become more standardized?

This is a **different evidence regime** from Haffter/Küderli and should not be forced into a product-document story unless the entries actually support comparison.

**Status:** CONDITIONAL COMPARATIVE UNIT.

## 12. Object coda B1 — three original Berlin metallographic glass plates

### Controlled evidence

The Iron Library holds three original early microphotographic glass plates donated by Bernhard Droste, with related Berlin testing-institute printed volumes.

### Current position in the collection-first design

These are **extremely evidence-dense objects** but not a c.1858–85 large block. Keep them as a separate object coda/control unless the final question genuinely extends into the late nineteenth-century transformation of product/material evidence into photographic reference systems.

### Unique onsite operation

`physical negative / plate → publication Tafel → later reproduction / archival survival`.

**Status:** OBJECT CODA / high material onsite necessity, low corpus scale.

## 13. Feasible 2–3 week architecture — planning model only

This is a **throughput model**, not a promised schedule. Actual daily orders depend on staff rules and physical retrieval units.

### Phase 1 — orientation and source-structure control

- confirm box/volume retrieval units;
- photograph finding aids / indexes where permitted;
- establish H1/K1 sampling frames;
- verify whether any requested series already have internal scans.

### Phase 2 — Cluster A

- H1 sampled correspondence, 1858–60;
- H2 selected business-book concordances, especially 1865–67;
- K1 matched correspondence slices, 1858–60 and 1865–67;
- Library print snapshots only where remote control leaves real local value.

### Phase 3 — Cluster B

- K2 complete/near-complete physical examination of the compact profile/price carriers where feasible;
- targeted 1884–85 cash/sales entries;
- native-GF product documents selected by carrier/date;
- Traisen only if technically relevant.

### Phase 4 — conditional controls

- Rauschenbach only after scale test;
- glass plates only as a bounded object comparison, not as a detour into a second project.

### Practical stopping rule

If a unit begins to consume time without producing cross-carrier comparability, stop after structural description and move to the next unit. The residency should maximize **comparative evidence density**, not percentage of fonds read.

## 14. Best current corpus architecture for an application

Without yet choosing a final thesis, the most stable architecture is:

> **Archival spine:** Haffter + Küderli/Bär  
> **Printed/reference environment:** Library serials + selected company/product grey literature  
> **Producer-side comparison:** native GF product/market documents  
> **Conditional industrial-company comparator:** Rauschenbach, only after internal scale confirmation  
> **Narrow technical comparator:** Traisen where useful  
> **Material-object coda:** original Berlin metallographic glass plates, only if the final question extends to visual/reference carriers

This architecture survives even if:

- Rauschenbach remains small;
- the Library proves highly remotely substitutable;
- microscopy is dropped entirely.

That robustness is a major advantage.

## 15. Historical-question hypotheses admitted by the corpus — not yet selected

The holdings now support testing, rather than asserting, several question forms:

### Hypothesis A — documentary comparability

How were iron/material products made comparable across **letters, calculations, price lists, profile albums, sales books, catalogues and serial print**?

### Hypothesis B — from correspondence to standardized commercial forms

What changes when commercial/technical knowledge moves from **person-to-person correspondence and copybooks** toward **profile, price, order and sales formats** between the late 1850s and mid-1880s?

### Hypothesis C — pre-corporate information infrastructure

How did trade firms and manufacturers coordinate product, price and material information **before the thick formal corporate-governance archives of the 1890s**?

These questions are attractive because they arise directly from the surviving carrier regimes rather than from one named actor. None should be promoted to final proposal language until access/retrieval answers confirm the physical corpus.

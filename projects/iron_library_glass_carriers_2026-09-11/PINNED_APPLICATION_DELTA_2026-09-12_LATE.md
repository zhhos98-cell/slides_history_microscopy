## 4. Secure 1885 publication hinge

A contemporary *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* notice, Bd. V, Nr. 12 (1885), titled `Profil-Album von Schinz & Bär in Zürich und Basel`, says the iron merchant had **recently issued** the album.

It describes:

- a broad building-profile repertoire;
- Winkel- and T-Eisen;
- Treppenbeschlag- and Fenstereisen;
- a rich range of Gesimseisen;
- the latter speciality groups as **newly created and protected by Schinz & Bär**, absent from other price books;
- Gesimseisen as enabling a more decorative treatment of iron construction.

It also reports a review in **The Ironmonger, 28 February 1885**, praising the execution of the drawings and the publication as an example of continental skill/productive capability. The original *Ironmonger* issue has **not yet been independently inspected**.

## 5. 1896 public carrier — quantified / standard-integrated reference

The contemporary 1896 *Bauzeitung* review describes Bär & Co's `Façoneisen-Album` as combining:

- commonly used **Normalprofile**;
- U / T / angle / Zorès / Quadrant and related profiles;
- **weight, dimension and moment-of-inertia tables**;
- load-bearing data up to 12 m;
- Bär proprietary profiles for cornice, showcase and railing constructions.

The firm offered the album to interested users and to Polytechnikum / Technikum in desired quantities so students could become accustomed to Normalprofile.

Source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1896%3A28%3A%3A214

A direct commercial counterpart's archive preserves this 1896 album/catalogue with the *Bauzeitung* extract.

### Strong longitudinal comparison

**1885 public regime:**
`profile repertoire + proprietary/protected specialities + drawing quality + building/design applications`

**1896 public regime:**
`Normalprofile + dimensional/weight/inertia/load data + proprietary profiles + technical-school distribution`.

The historical question is what was added, retained, reordered, quantified or made portable between these two regimes.

## 6. Live hypotheses for onsite work

### `/14`, 1877–85

1. **supplier substitution** — France -> Germany supply geography drives change;
2. **merchant commensurability** — merchant-built equivalence/comparison across heterogeneous products;
3. **Normalprofil uptake** — c.1879–81 break in terminology/numbering/dimensions/order;
4. **mixed coexistence** — proprietary and Normalprofile systems coexist.

### `/14 -> 1885 public album -> /13 -> 1896 public album`

5. **carrier migration** — profile identities/layouts move from internal albums into public references;
6. **reformatting break** — public catalogues reorganize internal heterogeneity into new commercial/engineering reference architectures;
7. **no direct genealogy** — archival albums are received/reference compilations unrelated to publication production.

All remain tests until physical comparison.

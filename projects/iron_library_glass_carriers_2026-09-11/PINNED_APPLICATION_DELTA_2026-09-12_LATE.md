### 1877–85 — profile / price / inter-firm comparison peak

- three profile albums, 1877–85;
- price lists, 1878–80;
- cashbook from 1884;
- sales journals from 1885.

External control:

- Wiser profile-stock document, 1879;

- direct business correspondence with Schinz & Baer and other Zürich iron merchants, 1879–85.

Formal German Normalprofile, c.1879–81, enters **inside** this already-existing commercial/inter-firm comparison environment.

### c.1886–1896 — internal-to-public carrier transformation

A contemporary *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* review (9 May 1896) says Bär & Co had issued a large profile album roughly ten years earlier; the exact edition is not yet independently located. It reports that *The Ironmonger* praised the earlier album; that review also remains independently OPEN.

The secure 1896 public `Façoneisen-Album` combined:

- commonly used **Normalprofile**;
- U / T / angle / Zorès / Quadrant and related sections;
- **weight, dimension and moment-of-inertia tables** for individual profiles;
- load-bearing data up to lengths of 12 m;
- **Bär's own proprietary profiles** for cornice, showcase and railing constructions.

The firm offered the album to interested users and to Polytechnikum / Technikum in desired quantities so that students could become accustomed to Normalprofile.

External source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1896%3A28%3A%3A214

This makes `EBA 4/13 (1886–1903)` a direct onsite test of how internal/reference profile carriers relate to the public 1896 object.

## 5. Normalprofile — causal warning

Reject:

`Swiss metrication -> German Normalprofile -> merchant standardization`.

Literature control indicates German Normalprofile were only weakly explained by metrication. Late-1870s overcapacity, engineering/railway user requirements, calculation needs and proprietary profile proliferation mattered. The Normalprofile commission itself sought sales/demand statistics from German rolling works.

Use instead:

`PRODUCER PROFILE VARIETY -> MARKET / SALES OBSERVATION -> TECHNICAL SELECTION -> NORMAL SERIES -> PRODUCER / MERCHANT / USER UPTAKE -> REFERENCE REPACKAGING / DISTRIBUTION`.

Metric notation is an empirical field, not the master cause.

## 6. Live hypotheses

### `EBA 4/14`, 1877–85

1. **Supplier substitution** — change chiefly reflects France -> Germany supply geography.
2. **Merchant commensurability** — internally constructed equivalence/comparison system across heterogeneous maker products.
3. **Normalprofil uptake** — c.1879–81 break in terminology, numbering, dimensions or ordering.
4. **Mixed coexistence** — proprietary and formal normal series coexist and still require merchant equivalence work.

### `/14 -> /13 -> 1896`

5. **Carrier migration** — profile identities / numbers / layouts move from internal albums into public reference publication.
6. **Reformatting break** — public album substantially reorganizes heterogeneous internal material into a new engineering-calculation architecture.
7. **No direct genealogy** — archival `Profileisenalbum` volumes are collected/reference material unrelated to publication production; strong internal-to-public chain must then be reduced.

No hypothesis is admitted before physical comparison.

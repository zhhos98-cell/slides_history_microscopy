# External source expansion from Fischer 1845–1846 — 2026-09-11

Status: active acquisition / verification queue.

This note records the source expansion generated directly from the three Fischer OCR files supplied in chat. Two files are duplicate/OCR witnesses of the 1845 journey; the cleaner `...Low-Moor, Sheffield, und zurück im Sommer 1845.txt` remains the canonical working text. The third is the 1846 journey.

The purpose is not to reopen generic metallurgy searching. It is to follow concrete nodes already present in the Fischer texts into downloadable primary sources and controlled comparison corpora.

## 1. Negative-control result: Fischer 1845–1846 is not yet a microscopy corpus

Exact searches across the three supplied OCR texts for `Mikro-`, `Mikroskop-`, `micros-` and related forms produced no positive hit.

Broader searches for `Lupe`, magnification, lenses, polarisation, mineral/crystal sections, transparent preparations and similar terms also produced no positive microscopic episode.

1845 contains industrial grinding/polishing, especially needle manufacture, but not microscopic specimen preparation.

1846 contains a strong optical-photographic cluster — optician Willat, photographic paper, Camera Obscura, daguerreotype, dissolving views / magic-lantern illumination, negative-positive reproduction — plus industrial grinding/polishing of Fischer's Sheffield razor-blade test objects. None should be promoted to microscopy without independent evidence.

Controlled conclusion:

> There is currently no positive textual basis for making Fischer 1845–1846 a microscopy story. Its value is as a pre-metallographic baseline for comparison, access, sampling, preparation/finishing, testing, numbering, provenance, visual representation and circulation.

## 2. Faraday / Royal Institution node

Fischer records visiting Michael Faraday at the Royal Institution on 4 August 1845. Faraday inspects Fischer's cast wrought-iron objects, worn and newly cast horseshoes, percussion-lock parts and a platinum specimen; Fischer leaves an experimental crucible for Faraday's use.

This makes the Royal Institution encounter a material-inspection node, not merely a social visit.

External lead to acquire / verify:

- Faraday & James Stodart, `On the Alloys of Steel` (1822) — public-domain full text/PDF available online.
- earlier Fischer–Faraday correspondence/recommendation history, including the 1825 de la Rive recommendation letter to Faraday (`RI MS F3 B, 139–42`, Faraday Correspondence Letter 257) — archive-level control; obtain digital surrogate if possible.
- Fischer's earlier travel journals (especially 1825–1827) for meteor steel / Faraday context.

Working question:

**How did experimental alloy work, portable steel specimens, recommendation letters and expert inspection interact before formalised metallography?**

## 3. Low Moor: traceability and staged material series

Fischer's 1845 text states that Low Moor takes a sample from every tapping and numbers the output so iron of a specified quality can be supplied to consumers.

The same visit produces a deliberately assembled portable series of ore, limestone, coal, coke and iron through successive production stages.

Keep separate:

- `TRACE_SAMPLE / BATCH_IDENTITY` — sample + numbered production event;
- `STAGED_SERIES` — material assembled across successive production stages.

Do not rename these as modern certified reference materials.

External context to acquire:

- contemporary Low Moor / Yorkshire industrial and commission material, including the 1841–42 Children's Employment Commission evidence where relevant to institutional/workforce context;
- surviving Low Moor company/industrial records only if they bear on sampling, numbering, testing or quality control.

## 4. Royal Polytechnic Institution: a different numbering regime

Fischer's 1846 account describes the Royal Polytechnic Institution as a collection of more than 2,000 objects corresponding to catalogue numbers, with staff explaining both scientific and practical aspects, lectures, experiments and working models.

This is analytically distinct from Low Moor numbering:

- Low Moor number = production/batch traceability;
- Polytechnic number = collection ordering / addressability / retrieval.

Primary source to download:

- `The Royal Polytechnic Institution ... Catalogue` (1843) — public-domain digital copy/PDF available online.

Use this as a control for the later question of numbered specimen/image collections, not as proof of continuity.

## 5. Croucher / Willats: photographic reproducibility as a technical node

Fischer's 1846 text names optician Willat/Willats in Cheapside and photographer J. H. Croucher. He discusses photographic paper including chromotype, chrysotype, cyanotype and energiatype, values negative-to-positive multiplication, and gives Beck's large coloured drawing of his steel foundry/casting operation to Croucher for photographic reproduction.

Primary source to download:

- J. H. Croucher, `Plain Directions for Obtaining Photographic Pictures ...` (1845), published by T. & R. Willats, 98 Cheapside — public-domain full PDF available online.

This provides a rare direct bridge from a travel narrative to a contemporary technical manual circulating through the same named commercial/optical network.

Working sequence:

**industrial operation → drawing → photographic negative → reproducible positive image**.

Do not call this photomicrography.

## 6. Fischer 1851 should be the next travel-journal acquisition

The strongest immediate continuation after 1845–46 is Fischer's 1851 London / industrial journey because the Great Exhibition adds a formal exhibition/catalogue regime to the existing travel/sample/test circuit.

Primary source:

- Fischer, `Tagebuch einer Reise zu der Ausstellung in London und nach einigen Fabrikstädten in England im Sommer 1851` — e-rara full digital copy; DOI `10.3931/e-rara-61269`.

Pair with:

- `Official Descriptive and Illustrated Catalogue of the Great Exhibition of the Works of Industry of All Nations, 1851`, especially the foreign-states/materials volumes where useful.

Question to test:

**Does Fischer's earlier travelling-sample / personal-judgement regime become reorganised through exhibition classification, catalogue addressability and public display in 1851?**

## 7. Sorby and Maskelyne: parallel material-to-microscopic-object regimes

Current working distinction:

### Sorby / Sheffield

Role to test and document:

**industrial material → skilled preparation → polished/etched surface → reflected/direct illumination → microscopic field → photomicrograph / printed image**.

High-value open sources:

- Sorby, `On the Microscopical Structure of Meteorites` (1863/64), public-domain scan/PDF;
- Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society, 1886 volume, for Sorby's response to Wedding and the specimen-preparation / illumination issue;
- modern catalogue/history of Sorby's surviving metallurgical specimens and photomicrographic work (Edyvean & Hammond), open PDF.

Do not reduce Sorby's intervention to `he used a microscope`. The evidential issue is how preparation and illumination make a valid microscopic object.

### Story-Maskelyne / British Museum mineralogy

Role to test and document:

**museum specimen / meteorite aggregate → cut/thin prepared section → glass carrier → transmitted/polarised-light comparison → collection/classification**.

High-value sources:

- Maskelyne, work on mineral constituents of meteorites (1870 and related papers);
- Royal Society `RR/12/397` (28 Dec 1894), Maskelyne referee report on A. E. H. Tutton's instrument for cutting, grinding and polishing section-plates/prisms — digitised original, downloadable PDF;
- British Museum/mineralogical preparation history only where it can be tied to primary documentation.

Do not assume a direct Sorby ↔ Maskelyne transmission chain without evidence. Current value is parallel comparison.

## 8. Wedding / Martens downloadable bridge corpus

Priority sources to acquire / verify locally:

- Wedding, `The Properties of Malleable Iron, deduced from its Microscopic Structure` (1885) — contemporaneous English publication/reprint available in digitised periodical form;
- Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society 1886 — Sorby/Wedding discussion;
- Adolf Martens, `Die mikroskopische Ausrüstung der königlichen mechanisch-technischen Versuchsanstalt` (1891), pp. 278–293, Tafeln IV–VI;
- Martens 1893 material in `Mitteilungen`, Band 11, including Chicago exhibition material and `Das mikroskopische Gefüge von Flusseisen in gegossenen Blöcken`, pp. 273–292, Tafeln VII–XIII;
- Martens & Guth, `Das Königliche Materialprüfungsamt` (1904), especially the metallography section and the separate specimen / microphotograph collection systems.

These are the immediate post-Fischer comparison corpus.

# Julius Schoch numbered profile catalogue as design / order interface, 1899–1912

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** HIGH-VALUE FUNCTION CONTROL / COMPETITOR MORPHOLOGY  
**Purpose:** establish what a Zürich iron merchant's profile catalogue could actually do downstream, so that profile numbers and page morphology in Iron Library `EBA 4/14` and especially `EBA 4/13` are not treated as merely descriptive catalogue features.

## 0. Current conclusion

The Julius Schoch comparison has progressed beyond the fact that a competitor donated a `Profil-Eisen-Album` to Winterthur Technikum in 1899/1900.

A surviving 1899 Schoch catalogue is independently identified as:

> **Julius Schoch & Cie., `Muster-Buch über Stab- und Profil-Eisen`, Zürich: Juchli & Beck Buchdruckerei-Lithographie, 1899; ETH-Bibliothek Zürich, `Rar 9483`.**

A modern architectural-history study using building/project archives shows that architects later selected specific industrial elements **by Schoch catalogue number and shape**, including deliberate reuse outside the product's original category.

Contemporary Schoch advertisements additionally prove that the same merchant simultaneously used **formal Normalprofile numbers** for standardized stock while also circulating proprietary/producer profile families and, in the catalogue, higher merchant/article numbers such as #3507 / #3517.

This gives a concrete downstream function chain:

`DRAWN PROFILE / CATALOGUE NUMBER`
→ `DESIGNER SEARCH / SELECTION`
→ `NUMBERED PRODUCT REFERENCE`
→ `ARCHITECTURAL DETAIL`
→ `PHYSICAL BUILDING COMPONENT`.

It also gives a crucial classification warning:

> **`STANDARD PROFILE NUMBER` ≠ `MERCHANT / ARTICLE NUMBER` ≠ `PROPRIETARY OR PRODUCER PROFILE IDENTITY`.**

The result is a major analytical control for Bär:

> **a profile number can be an operational address connecting representation, ordering/design choice and material realization, not merely an internal classificatory label.**

Do not infer that Bär's numbering worked identically. That becomes an empirical test.

## 3. Dual numbering / reference regimes inside one Zürich merchant

Contemporary *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* advertisements for Julius Schoch & Co. provide an unusually clean control for the meaning of `Nr.`.

### 1893 advertisement

The advertisement says the firm holds:

- a depot of the decorative rolled-iron profiles of **L. Mannstädt & Co.**;
- all the work's profiles for decorative cornices, bases, frames, claddings, gate ornament and related applications;
- illustrated price lists;
- and, separately, a large stock of **German Normalprofile** explicitly enumerated as `Nr. 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 30, 32`, in lengths up to 12 m.

Primary source:
- *Schweizerische Bauzeitung*, April / June 1893 advertisements; e-periodica records including https://selfranga-prod11.ethz.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1893%3A21%3A%3A178 and https://selfranga-prod11.ethz.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1893%3A21%3A%3A263

### 1900 advertisement

A 21 July 1900 advertisement again presents Schoch as holding German Normalprofile and explicitly enumerates stock numbers including `8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 40`, in lengths up to 14 m.

Primary source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1900%3A35%3A%3A385

### Analytical consequence

The same merchant environment therefore simultaneously contains at least three identification regimes:

1. **`NORMALPROFILE NR.`** — formal standardized section designation tied to standard stock sizes;
2. **producer/proprietary profile families** — e.g. Mannstädt decorative rolled profiles, whose identity can be tied to the producing works / proprietary repertoire;
3. **merchant catalogue / article numbers** — e.g. Schoch #3507 / #3517 in the 1899 catalogue and later architectural use.

These are not interchangeable even when all appear as drawings + numbers.

A merchant's album could mix or cross-reference them. That possibility is now a high-priority onsite test for `EBA 4/14` and `/13`.

### Important methodological rule

When a numbered profile appears onsite, never code `Nr.` directly as `standardized`.

First determine:

`NUMBER SYNTAX -> NUMBER AUTHORITY -> PROFILE FAMILY -> PRODUCER / MERCHANT / STANDARD ORIGIN -> DIMENSIONAL LOGIC -> CROSS-CARRIER STABILITY`.

Only then decide whether the number denotes:

- a formal Normalprofil;
- a producer model/profile;
- a merchant stock/article number;
- a public catalogue reference;
- or an unresolved mixed code.

## 4. Later downstream use — catalogue number as design address

The Queen Alexandra Sanatorium study reconstructs Pfleghard & Haefeli's use of Schoch catalogue elements from architectural drawings/project records.

### `Schoch 3517`

The study reproduces an excerpt from the Schoch catalogue and a door detail with the selected part installed.

It states that:

- part **#3517** was found in the Schoch `Fenstereisen` section;
- it was a small streamlined industrial iron element;
- the architects selected it from the catalogue **on the basis of its shape**;
- they deliberately ignored / exceeded its original intended function;
- it was used in a modern threshold / door-detail solution at the sanatorium.

The analytical significance is not the later building itself. It is the documented operation:

`CATEGORY = FENSTEREISEN`
→ `NUMBER = 3517`
→ `SHAPE-BASED SEARCH`
→ `FUNCTIONAL REASSIGNMENT`
→ `BUILT DETAIL`.

This proves that a late nineteenth-century Zürich merchant catalogue could function as a **searchable repertoire of materially orderable shapes**, in which catalogue number stabilized an element sufficiently for architects to refer to and specify it.

### Other numbered selections

The same study identifies additional Schoch-number selections in the architects' work, including profile/article **#3507** in an earlier design context. Treat each exact use case separately when reconstructing chronology; the key secure general point is repeated catalogue-number-based selection.

## 5. Why this matters for EBA 4/14 and EBA 4/13

The current Iron Library morphology protocol already records `PROFILE NUMBER`. This comparison raises its evidentiary status.

For every numbered Bär/Schinz profile encountered onsite, ask:

1. Is the number stable across pages / volumes / editions?
2. Is it a merchant-specific number, producer number, Normalprofile designation, or mixed code?
3. Does a number survive when dimensions/weights/prices change?
4. Can the same number be found in public catalogues, advertisements, technical literature, competitor-retained copies or order/sales records?
5. Do handwritten annotations cite profile numbers without redrawing the form?
6. Are replacement/supplement sheets keyed by number?
7. Are profile numbers reorganized at the 1885, 1896, 1903 or later update boundaries?
8. Is number continuity used to preserve product identity through physical version changes?
9. Does the album explicitly crosswalk merchant/article numbers to Normalprofile numbers or producer identities?

A stable number across changing carriers would be strong evidence of a durable **address system**.

A renumbering event could be equally significant, especially if it aligns with Normalprofile uptake, proprietary-family reorganization or new album editions.

## 7. Stronger conceptual distinction

Do not collapse:

- **shape standardization**;
- **formal Normalprofile numbering**;
- **producer/proprietary profile identity**;
- **merchant/article numbering**;
- **catalogue classification**;
- **ordering address**;
- **engineering reference**;
- **design appropriation**.

A merchant can assign or maintain a stable number without the shape being a formal Normalprofil. Conversely a formal standard profile may circulate under multiple commercial numbering systems.

Schoch now gives direct contemporary proof that both can coexist in one merchant's public reference environment.

This distinction is central to avoiding a teleological standardization narrative.

## 9. Claim ceiling

Do not assert that:

- Bär used profile numbers in the same way as Schoch;
- the Schoch 1899 Technikum donation is ETH `Rar 9483`;
- every Schoch catalogue number directly encoded an order number;
- #3517 was originally designed for the later architectural use;
- Bär profile numbering necessarily maps to sales-journal entries;
- stable numbering equals formal standardization;
- every `Nr.` in a profile album denotes a Normalprofil;
- Mannstädt profile identity and Schoch merchant numbering are the same system;
- catalogue retention equals use.

The secure control is narrower and stronger:

> a Zürich merchant publicly stocked numbered German Normalprofile while also circulating producer/proprietary profile families; a surviving catalogue from that merchant demonstrably supported later shape-based selection by high catalogue/article number and materially realized architectural reuse.

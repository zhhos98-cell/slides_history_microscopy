# Bär–Georg Fischer distributed product identity, 1891–1896

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** STRONG CROSS-FONDS CONTROL / MORPHOLOGY CONCORDANCE STILL OPEN  
**Authority:** this note advances `BAER_GF_GELAENDERFITTINGS_CONTRACT_CROSS_FONDS_BRIDGE_1895_2026-09-12.md` but does **not** replace `PINNED_APPLICATION_RESUME.md`.

## 0. Current conclusion

The 3 January 1895 Bär–Georg Fischer III contract is no longer an isolated commercial bridge. Independent contemporary evidence now places it between two already-developed but different product-reference regimes:

1. **Bär / Schinz & Bär merchant-profile identity** — protected/new profiles, profile albums, public engineering reference, later quantified and versioned reference copies;
2. **Georg Fischer fittings/model identity** — a very large pre-existing fitting repertoire, serial/model-rich production, and by 1896 an explicitly advertised/displayed class of `Geländer-Verbindungsstücke`.

The strongest new research problem is therefore not simply `merchant catalogue -> manufacturer`, but **distributed product identity across organisational boundaries**:

> When an iron building component crossed from Bär's merchant/reference system into Georg Fischer's manufacturing regime, which features made it remain the same product — drawing, cross-section, number, dimensions, model, proprietary status, contract language, price/order key, maker's mark, or sales category?

This strengthens the existing application triad `IDENTITY -> REFORMAT -> MAINTENANCE` by adding a falsifiable cross-organisational test. It does **not** yet justify repinning the whole application because the decisive morphology/number concordance remains open.

## 1. Secure chronological chain

### 1885 — Bär/Schinz & Bär already creates protected special profiles

The contemporary *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* review of the Schinz & Bär `Profil-Album` describes stair-edging, window and cornice irons and calls these products newly created by the firm and protected specialities absent from other price books.

Source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1885%3A5%3A%3A125

**Claim ceiling:** this proves merchant-originated/protected profile forms by 1885. It does not identify the later 1895 railing fittings.

### 1891 — Georg Fischer already operates a large fittings/model system

A contemporary Georg Fischer advertisement for `Verbindungstheile und Utensilien für Gas-, Wasser- und Dampfleitungen` advertises:

- large stocks and production of fittings/flanges;
- controlled pressure-resistant goods;
- `Neue Formen und Combinationen in ca. 2000 Gattungen`;
- fittings supplied by model/drawing logic and differentiated forms.

Source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=ihz-001%3A1891%3A7%3A%3A1199

**Interpretive consequence:** GF did not need Bär to introduce it to fittings as a manufacturing technology. Any 1895 Bär arrangement must be tested as a specific product/market/design/rights interface inside an already mature GF fittings regime.

### 1893/94 — Bär is already positioned in the fittings-adjacent Zürich market

Hans Schwarz's Zürich industry/trade address book lists `Baer & Cie., Wagg. 6 Z.` under:

- `Gas- u. Wasserleitungsartikel. (Handlungen.)`

The same commercial directory ecosystem also places Bär in building-material/building-article trade classifications.

Source:
- https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/25448952.pdf

**Claim ceiling:** category-level market position only. Do not infer from the address book that Bär already stocked GF products or that its specific goods were railing fittings.

### 3 January 1895 — contract bridge

`GFA 1/171 :: Georg Fischer III` records:

`Vertrag zwischen Baer & Cie und Georg Fischer III betreffend Produktion und Verkauf von Geländerfittings durch Georg Fischer III`, dated 3 January 1895.

Source:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/9756

The same dossier places this beside:

- 11 January 1895 Werk III operating instructions;
- 14 February 1895 transcript of the Singen Fittingsfabrik commercial-register entry;
- June 1895 steel-casting supply agreement;
- 1896 photographs of the GF stand at the Swiss National Exhibition.

**Physical originality remains OPEN.** `GFA 1/171` is a retrospectively assembled historical dossier and its general note warns that many documents are copies/transcripts. The contract entry itself is not explicitly labelled `Kopie`, but only onsite examination can establish original / office duplicate / later copy.

### 1896 — Bär publicly claims originator status for new railing-construction profiles

The *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* review of Bär & Co.'s `Façoneisen-Album`, issue 1896, says:

- tables give weight, dimensions and moments of inertia for individual profiles;
- unlike the earlier edition, the current edition places these quantitative data at the relevant profiles;
- load data extend to lengths of 12 m;
- notable new profiles include those for `Gesims-, Vitrinen- und Geländer-Konstruktionen (siehe Handleisten)`;
- Bär & Co. is described as the `Urheberin` of these profiles.

Source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1896%3A28%3A%3A210

**Critical distinction:** `Geländer-Konstruktionen / Handleisten` profiles are not automatically identical with the contract's `Geländerfittings`.

### December 1896 — GF publicly exhibits railing connectors as a new fitting article

The official illustrated journal of the 1896 Swiss National Exhibition describes GF fittings as a repertoire of more than 2,000 types and then identifies several new articles:

- `Verbindungsstücke mit Abschliessung`;
- `Verbindungsstücke mit Verschraubung`;
- **`Geländer-Verbindungsstücke`**.

It states that the latter were used on the railing of the Fischer exhibition group. The next sentence states that one year earlier GF opened a new factory at Singen am Hohentwiel which dealt exclusively with the manufacture of `dieses Artikels`, employing about 200 workers, while nearly as many workers in Schaffhausen worked in `diese Branche`.

Source:
- issue 48, *Journal officiel illustré de l'Exposition nationale suisse / Offizielle illustrierte Zeitung der Schweizerischen Landesausstellung*, 1896, article on Georg Fischer:
- https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/26157734.pdf

### Source-critical reading of `dieses Artikels`

The immediate grammar strongly points back to the last-named `Geländer-Verbindungsstücke` (`welch letztere ... Verwendung gefunden haben` -> `dieses Artikels`). This makes the Singen statement important evidence for rapid industrial expansion of the railing-connector branch around 1895.

However, preserve a narrow claim ceiling in proposal prose until the page can be visually inspected at full resolution: quote/describe the sequence rather than paraphrasing it as a proved legal or administrative factory specialization. The searchable text itself is secure enough to establish the contemporaneous association among:

`NEW GELÄNDER-VERBINDUNGSSTÜCKE -> ACTUAL EXHIBITION USE -> 1895 SINGEN FACTORY EXPANSION -> LARGE LABOUR FORCE`.

## 2. Why the two systems must remain distinct

### Bär system

Current evidence is strongest for:

`PROFILE SHAPE -> PROPRIETARY/PROTECTED IDENTITY -> PROFILE ALBUM -> NUMBER/GEOMETRY/QUANTITATIVE DATA -> PRICE/ORDER/TEACHING/UPDATE SYSTEM`.

### GF system

Current evidence is strongest for:

`FITTING FORM/MODEL -> LARGE REPERTOIRE -> MODEL/DRAWING-BASED PRODUCTION -> MASS/SERIAL MANUFACTURE -> MAKER/PRODUCT CLASS -> DISTRIBUTION`.

The 1895 contract may be a translation interface between these two systems.

Do **not** assume a single common numbering ontology. In fact, a central empirical question is whether the contract or surviving albums create a crosswalk between merchant identity and producer identity.

## 3. Non-equivalence rules

Do not write any of the following unless new primary evidence closes them:

- `Geländer-Konstruktionen (siehe Handleisten)` profile = `Geländerfitting`;
- Bär designed GF's 1895 railing fittings;
- GF manufactured the Bär handrail profiles;
- the 1895 contract caused the 1896 album;
- the 1896 Bär album illustrated the contracted GF fittings;
- GF's ordinary pipe-fitting model numbers were reused for railing fittings;
- Bär was GF's general Zürich agent;
- the Singen factory's entire output was contractually controlled by Bär.

The correct operation is **component-system concordance**, not genealogy by adjacency.

## 6. Falsifiers remain valuable

### Falsifier 1

Contract contains only territory/commission/sales terms and no technical identifier.

**Meaning:** relation is commercial but does not prove technical identity transfer.

### Falsifier 2

`EBA 4/13` contains no GF, railing-fitting or related concordance.

**Meaning:** the contracted product family may have been maintained outside the surviving profile-album system.

### Falsifier 3

Bär railing/handrail profiles and GF railing connectors are morphologically and numerically independent.

**Meaning:** the interesting historical object becomes a **complementary component system**, not a shared product genealogy.

### Falsifier 4

The surviving contract is a later abstract/transcript.

**Meaning:** keep the relationship as catalogue-secure but do not use physical marks/enclosures to infer 1895 office practice.

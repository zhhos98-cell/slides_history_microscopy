# Schinz & Bär public-carrier / distribution chain, 1872–1890

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** NEW EXTERNAL CLOSURE / ACTIVE CONTROL  
**Purpose:** extend the secure 1885 `Profil-Album` hinge outward into warehouse, advertising, prospectus, transaction-network and technical-school circulation without assuming that any two surviving carriers are textually identical.

## 0. Current conclusion

The public/reference layer around the Iron Library `EBA 4/14 -> EBA 4/13` profile-album sequence is no longer represented only by the securely documented **1885** and **1896** public albums.

Independent external evidence now gives a materially denser carrier ecology:

`1872–74 TRANSACTION ACCOUNT / EISENHALLE IDENTITY`
→ `1885 PUBLIC PROFIL-ALBUM`
→ `1887 LETTERHEAD: OFFICE + MAGAZINE + MAIN DEPOT`
→ `1889 TECHNICAL-PRESS ADVERTISEMENT`
→ `1889 ILLUSTRATED FOLDING PROSPECTUS FOR BAUARTIKEL-LAGER`
→ `1889/90 BATCH OF PROFIL-EISEN ALBUMS TO WINTERTHUR TECHNIKUM`
→ `1896 QUANTIFIED FAÇONEISEN-ALBUM + EXPLICIT TECHNICAL-SCHOOL DISTRIBUTION`.

This changes the strongest question. The profile album should not be treated as a free-floating technical representation. It belonged to a commercial information infrastructure coupling **stock, warehouse, transaction, product display, advertising, reference compilation and educational circulation**.

The onsite test remains the same at its core: what kind of objects are `EBA 4/14` and `EBA 4/13`, and how—if at all—do their page/profile structures relate to these demonstrable external carrier regimes?

## 1. 1872–74 — Schinz & Bär already appears as `Eisenhalle in Zürich` in an external commercial ledger

Wirtschaftsarchiv Vorarlberg, Josef Pircher business books:

- `AT-WAV-01-041` — **Josef Pircher, Geschäftsbücher, Hauptbuch Nr. 13, 1872–1874**.
- The account index includes:
  - page/account **177** — `Schinz & Bär` — place/identifier: **`Eisenhalle in Zürich`**.
- The archive's description states that the Hauptbuch contains customer and supplier accounts and that the underlying accounts carry detailed dates, prices and goods.

Primary digital source:
- https://wirtschaftsarchiv-v.at/images/Pircher/01-041-Pircher_Hauptbuch13.pdf
- derivative index page exposing the relevant row: https://wirtschaftsarchiv-v.at/images/Pircher/01-041-01-02-G655.pdf

### Consequence

By 1872–74 the `Eisenhalle` designation was already legible outside Zürich inside a transregional commercial accounting network. This is **not** proof that the later profile albums were used in that transaction, but it anchors the firm-specific information system in actual supplier/customer bookkeeping rather than only in later advertising.

### Next control

The index proves the account exists. If page 177 / the underlying account can be obtained, capture:

`DATES / GOODS / QUANTITIES / PRICES / UNITS / PAYMENT / ROUTE / PRODUCT TERMS / ANY CATALOGUE OR PROFILE REFERENCE`.

Until that account is seen, do not infer which products Pircher bought from or sold to Schinz & Bär.

## 2. 1885 — secure public `Profil-Album`

Contemporary *Schweizerische Bauzeitung*, Bd. V, Nr. 12 (1885):

- title: `Profil-Album von Schinz & Bär in Zürich und Basel`;
- describes the album as **recently issued**;
- ordinary building profiles plus Winkel- and T-Eisen;
- Treppenbeschlag- and Fenstereisen;
- rich Gesimseisen assortment;
- latter groups described as **newly created and protected specialities** of Schinz & Bär, absent from other price books;
- reports a favourable *The Ironmonger* review dated **28 February 1885**.

Primary digital source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1885%3A5%3A%3A125

### Claim ceiling

The original *Ironmonger* review remains not independently located. `geschützte Specialitäten` must not yet be translated into a specific legal protection mechanism without independent patent/trademark/design evidence.

## 3. 1887 — letterhead binds firm identity to office, magazine and depot

Stadtarchiv Zürich:

- `V.L.226.:259` — **Schinz & Baer Eisenhandlung, Zürich**, 20 Aug 1887.
- catalogue note: `Büro und Magazin Eisenhalle, Hauptdepot Lagergasse`.

Primary catalogue record:
- https://amsquery.stadt-zuerich.ch/detail.aspx?ID=208893

### Consequence

This is a direct material-documentary bridge between a business carrier (letterhead) and the spatial/logistical system it represents:

`OFFICE / MAGAZINE EISENHALLE -> MAIN DEPOT LAGERGASSE`.

The profile/reference system can therefore be tested against a physical stock-and-distribution topology rather than treated only as an abstract classificatory system.

## 4. 1889 — technical-press advertising makes `Eisenhalle` a public ordering identity

*Schweizerische Bauzeitung*, 25 May 1889 advertisement:

- `SCHINZ & BAER`;
- Zürich / Waaggasse / `z. Eisenhalle`;
- telegram address: **Eisenhalle**;
- telephone no. 969;
- offers `Eiserne Tragbalken & Säulen` and `alle Bauartikel in Eisen und Guss`.

Primary digital source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=sbz-002%3A1889%3A13%3A%3A220

### Consequence

The same commercial identity appears simultaneously as:

`PHYSICAL STOCK LOCATION / TELEGRAPHIC ADDRESS / ADVERTISING BRAND / ORDERING INTERFACE`.

This strengthens the interpretation of catalogue/profile carriers as part of an ordering and stock-reference infrastructure, while still leaving their precise internal functions open.

## 5. 1889 — surviving illustrated folding prospectus for the `Bauartikel-Lager`

Staatsarchiv Thurgau:

- `Slg. 15, 8'4/105` — **Schinz und Bär, Eisenhandlung, Zürich und Basel: Faltprospekt zu ihrem Bauartikel-Lager mit Illustrationen ...**, dated **1889**.

Catalogue context:
- https://query-staatsarchiv.tg.ch/archivplansuche.aspx?ID=344444

### Importance

This is potentially the most useful surviving intermediate public carrier between the 1885 and 1896 albums because it is:

- firm-issued;
- illustrated;
- explicitly tied to the stocked `Bauartikel-Lager`;
- chronologically only four years after the 1885 `Profil-Album`.

### Required comparison fields

If reproduced or consulted:

`FORMAT / FOLD STRUCTURE / IMPRINT / PRODUCT FAMILIES / PROFILE DRAWINGS / PROFILE NUMBERS / DIMENSIONS / WEIGHTS / PRICES / PROPRIETARY LABELS / NORMALPROFILE TERMS / ORDERING INSTRUCTIONS / DEPOT-OR-WAREHOUSE REFERENCES`.

### Claim ceiling

Do **not** infer that this 1889 folding prospectus is an excerpt, derivative or successor of the 1885 album until page/profile concordance proves it.

## 6. 1889/90 — direct technical-school circulation is already occurring

An e-periodica digitisation of the Zürich Technikum / Winterthur annual-report material for 1889, in a donations/acquisitions list signed at Winterthur **15 March 1890** by the director/reporter, records:

- donor: `Von den Herren Schinz & Bär in Zürich`;
- object: **`1 Partie Albums von Profil-Eisen.`**

The same list contains teaching/reference objects and technical materials from Gebrüder Sulzer, Schweizerische Lokomotivfabrik, teachers at the Technikum and other technical donors.

Primary digital source:
- https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=twj-002%3A1889%3A16%3A%3A56

### Major consequence

This is direct evidence that **batch circulation of profile-iron albums from Schinz & Bär into a technical educational institution had occurred by 1889/90**.

The 1896 *Bauzeitung* statement that Bär & Co would supply albums to Polytechnikum / Technikum in desired quantities is therefore not merely a new 1896 marketing intention. There is a demonstrable earlier practice of supplying multiple profile albums to the Winterthur Technikum.

### Claim ceiling

Do **not** yet assert that:

- the donated albums were copies of the 1885 `Profil-Album`;
- they were identical to any `EBA 4/14` or `EBA 4/13` volume;
- students demonstrably used them in classes;
- the batch quantity is known;
- the exact annual-report bibliographic title has been fully closed beyond the e-periodica record and internal Technikum context.

The strongest safe statement is:

> By the reporting date of 15 March 1890, the Winterthur Technikum had received from Schinz & Bär a batch (`1 Partie`) of albums of iron profiles.

## 7. Revised public-carrier ecology

The external sequence now separates at least six operations:

1. **TRANSACTIONAL PRESENCE** — external customer/supplier ledger (`1872–74`);
2. **PUBLIC PROFILE REFERENCE** — 1885 `Profil-Album`;
3. **SPATIAL / LOGISTICAL ADDRESSING** — office, magazine and main depot (`1887`);
4. **PUBLIC ADVERTISING / ORDER INTERFACE** — Bauzeitung advertisement (`1889`);
5. **ILLUSTRATED STOCK PROSPECTUS** — Bauartikel-Lager folding prospectus (`1889`);
6. **BATCH EDUCATIONAL / REFERENCE DISTRIBUTION** — Winterthur Technikum profile albums (`1889/90`).

Then by 1896:

7. **QUANTIFIED ENGINEERING REFERENCE** — Normalprofile + weight/dimension/inertia/load tables + proprietary profiles;
8. **EXPLICIT PEDAGOGICAL DISTRIBUTION POLICY** — supply to Polytechnikum / Technikum in desired quantities.

These operations overlap but are not interchangeable.

## 8. Impact on Iron Library onsite research

This external closure **raises**, rather than lowers, the value of `EBA 4/14` and `EBA 4/13`.

Remote evidence can already establish that Schinz & Bär operated a dense ecology of commercial/reference carriers. What remote evidence cannot establish is the physical identity and function of the Iron Library's five archival `Profileisenalbum` volumes.

Onsite morphology must therefore ask whether the archival volumes contain or preserve:

- internal compilation;
- pasted producer sheets;
- public catalogue copies;
- annotated reference copies;
- stock/order working marks;
- revision layers;
- profile-number concordances;
- proprietary vs Normalprofile distinctions;
- traces of extraction/reformatting into public carriers;
- or no direct relationship at all.

### Highest-value test

`EBA 4/14 (1877–1885)`  
↕ physical/page morphology  
`PUBLIC PROFIL-ALBUM 1885`  
↕  
`1889 FOLDING BAUARTIKEL PROSPECTUS`  
↕  
`1889/90 TECHNIKUM ALBUM BATCH`  
↕  
`EBA 4/13 (1886–1903)`  
↕  
`PUBLIC FAÇONEISEN-ALBUM 1896`.

A direct genealogy is **not assumed**. The research value lies precisely in testing transformations, coexistence and discontinuities among these carriers.

## 9. Application-level revision

A stronger formulation than generic `standardization` is now:

> **How did Zürich iron merchants connect stock, price, product form and competition through a changing ecology of correspondence, warehouse addresses, profile albums, advertisements, prospectuses and transaction books—and how were selected product-reference systems redistributed into engineering and educational settings?**

The novel layer is not merely the adoption of formal standards. It is the **merchant-built documentary and carrier infrastructure between physical stock, market coordination, engineering comparability and reference circulation**.

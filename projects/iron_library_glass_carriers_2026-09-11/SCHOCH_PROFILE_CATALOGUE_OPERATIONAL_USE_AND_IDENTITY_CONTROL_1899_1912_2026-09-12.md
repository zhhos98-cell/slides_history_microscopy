# Schoch profile catalogue operational use and profile-identity control, 1899–1912

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** COMPARATIVE FUNCTION / MORPHOLOGY CONTROL — NOT A SCHINZ & BÄR GENEALOGY  
**Purpose:** sharpen the Iron Library `EBA 4/14` and `EBA 4/13` onsite test by showing, in a directly comparable Zürich iron-merchant catalogue, how a numbered drawn profile could function as an operational selection object downstream of the merchant catalogue.

## 0. Current conclusion

A later architectural case gives unusually concrete evidence for what a Zürich `Profil-Eisen` catalogue could do in practice.

Pfleghard & Haefeli used the 1899 Julius Schoch & Cie. catalogue not merely as advertising or a passive price book. In documented building design work they searched it for **ready-made iron sections by geometry and dimensions**, selected numbered profiles, and reused profiles outside the product function under which the catalogue had classified them.

The secure operational chain is:

`CATALOGUE CATEGORY / PROFILE NUMBER / DRAWN SHAPE / DIMENSIONS`
→ `DESIGN SEARCH / SELECTION`
→ `CROSS-CATEGORY APPROPRIATION`
→ `SPECIFIED BUILT DETAIL`.

This does **not** prove that Schinz & Bär / Bär & Co. profile albums functioned identically. It supplies a high-value **competitor function control** for the exact Zürich merchant environment in which Schinz & Bär participated.

The application-level consequence is important: the EBA 4 onsite problem should now track not only `STANDARD / PROPRIETARY` status but the material mechanisms by which a profile retained or changed **identity** across albums, editions, supplements, price/order records and downstream use.

## 2. 1909 — profile no. 3507 selected by dimensions, not original catalogue function

For the original Queen Alexandra Sanatorium building, Pfleghard & Haefeli needed a small iron element at the junction of stair tread/riser, plaster and linoleum.

The study reports that they **searched the Julius Schoch catalogue** for a ready-made whose dimensions would make the junction work.

They selected:

- profile / element **`3507`**;
- dimensions **20 mm high × 12 mm deep**;
- catalogue location: **`Fenstereisen`** section.

The element had been catalogued for a different application, but the architects selected it because its **shape and dimensions** solved the stair-detail problem.

### Secure functional inference

For this user, the catalogue supported retrieval by at least:

`NUMBER + DRAWN/LEGIBLE SHAPE + DIMENSIONS`,

and the catalogue's product category did not prevent the same profile from being reassigned to another function.

Do not generalise this to every Schoch customer or every profile catalogue.

---

## 3. 1912 — profile no. 3517 reused as a threshold/detail interface

During the extension, Pfleghard & Haefeli again used a Schoch catalogue profile:

- profile **`3517`**;
- described as an **`Anschlagseisen`**;
- again located in the catalogue's **`Fenstereisen`** section.

They used the ready-made profile in a patient-room / corridor threshold-detail problem. The study explicitly notes that the designers selected the industrial component **on the basis of its shape while ignoring its original intended function**; its thickness matched the linoleum layer and enabled a precise junction.

### Repeated operation

The 1909 and 1912 cases together show that this was not a one-off accidental reference. The catalogue could function as a **shape-indexed inventory of purchasable industrial forms** from which designers selected numbered objects for new contexts.

Again, this is a Schoch case. It does not prove identical Bär practice.

## 4. Why this matters for EBA 4

Iron Library preserves:

- `EBA 4/14 Profileisenalbum`, **1877–1885**, **3 vols**;
- `EBA 4/13 Profileisenalbum`, **1886–1903**, **2 vols**;
- adjacent price, cash, sales and inventory series.

Official catalogue:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/EBA%204

The current external Bär/Schinz chain already proves:

- public firm-specific engineered profile system by **1881**;
- public `Profil-Album` by **1885**;
- repeated profile-album distribution to Winterthur Technikum by **1889/90**;
- a quantified `Façoneisen-Album` in **1896**;
- six **new** Bär profile albums distributed in **1903/04**;
- `Supplementsblatt für das Profileisenalbum` distributed in **12 copies** in **1907/08**.

The Schoch use case changes what should be looked for physically in the five EBA 4 album volumes.

The central empirical object is now:

> **PROFILE IDENTITY ACROSS CARRIER AND VERSION.**

A profile may remain “the same” through one or more of:

- profile number;
- cross-sectional geometry;
- dimensions;
- weight;
- catalogue category;
- proprietary / Normalprofil status;
- price/order code;
- page/sheet position;
- company imprint;
- edition/supplement continuity.

These identity dimensions need not change together.

## 5. Revised onsite identity/versioning protocol

For every sampled profile in `EBA 4/14` and `EBA 4/13`, capture:

`VOLUME / PAGE_OR_SHEET / INTERNAL_DATE / PROFILE_NUMBER / PROFILE_CATEGORY / DRAWN_SHAPE / KEY_DIMENSIONS / WEIGHT / STATIC_OR_LOAD_DATA / INTENDED_USE_LABEL / PROPRIETARY_MARK / NORMALPROFIL_MARK / PRICE_CODE / ORDER_REFERENCE / PRODUCER_OR_SUPPLIER / COMPANY_IMPRINT / PRINTED_PASTED_MANUSCRIPT / REVISION_MARK / REPLACEMENT_SHEET / SUPPLEMENT_STATUS / CROSS_REFERENCE`.

Then explicitly test:

1. **SAME NUMBER / SAME SHAPE / SAME DIMENSIONS** across different album layers;
2. **SAME NUMBER / CHANGED SHAPE OR DIMENSIONS** — number semantics revised;
3. **SAME SHAPE / NEW NUMBER** — renumbering/version transition;
4. **SAME PROFILE / NEW CATEGORY** — classificatory reassignment;
5. **SAME PROFILE / NEW INTENDED USE** — functional reassignment;
6. **PROPRIETARY → NORMALPROFILE or coexistence** — status change without assuming technical causation;
7. **BASE ALBUM → SUPPLEMENT** — whether supplements preserve, append, replace or cancel profile identities;
8. **ALBUM NUMBER → PRICE / CASH / SALES / INVENTORY RECORD** — whether catalogue identity becomes transaction identity.

This last operation is especially important because EBA 4 uniquely places profile albums beside price lists, cash books, sales journals and inventory books.

## 6. Strong hypotheses and falsifiers

### H1 — number as portable identity

If the same profile number recurs across album, price/order and transaction carriers, the number may have acted as a portable commercial reference key.

**Falsifier:** numbers prove local to pages/editions and do not recur outside the album.

### H2 — geometry outruns category

If identical sections migrate between categories or carry annotations for new uses, product categories may be retrieval aids rather than fixed functional ontologies.

**Falsifier:** category and profile identity remain rigidly locked in all surviving material.

### H3 — supplement as identity maintenance

The documented 1907 supplement mechanism suggests that profile identity required maintenance as the reference system changed.

**Test:** determine whether `/13` physically contains replacement, insertion, cancellation or cumulative-update structures.

**Falsifier:** `/13` consists of static bound received catalogues with no update layer.

### H4 — synchronized copies require stable references

Repeated batches to the Technikum and twelve supplement copies make stable numbering/version cues plausible requirements for multi-copy maintenance.

**Claim ceiling:** this remains a functional hypothesis until the Bär album/supplement morphology is inspected.

## 7. Comparative value of Schoch

Schoch is not an arbitrary outside example.

The Zürich Pestalozzi/Wiser archive places **Schinz & Baer** and **Fäh & Schoch / Julius Schoch & Co.** inside the same iron-merchant convention, cartel and business-correspondence environment. Winterthur Technikum reports also show both Schinz/Bär and Schoch circulating profile albums into the same technical-education environment at different dates.

Therefore Schoch provides two distinct controls:

1. **genre/morphology control** — what a Zürich profile catalogue could look like and encode;
2. **function control** — a documented case in which catalogue number, geometry and dimensions became operational design-selection tools.

It must not be used as evidence for direct textual derivation or identical Bär practice.

---

## 8. Application-level consequence

Avoid a generic story in which `standardization` simply reduces variety.

A stronger problem is:

> **How did Zürich iron merchants make industrial forms persist as identifiable reference objects while the forms moved among stock, private albums, public catalogues, prices, orders, technical education and design use—and what happened when editions, proprietary systems and Normalprofile forced those identities to be revised?**

A still tighter empirical formulation:

> **What made a profile the “same profile” across five archival album volumes, changing catalogue editions, price/order systems and supplement sheets: its number, geometry, dimensions, category, price code, proprietary status, or some combination of these?**

This is directly answerable by physical comparison of `EBA 4/14` and `/13` plus adjacent EBA 4 transaction series; it cannot be settled from public reviews alone.

---

## 9. Claim ceiling

Secure:

- Julius Schoch & Cie. had a surviving 1899 `Muster-Buch über Stab- und Profil-Eisen` (`ETH Rar 9483`);
- the later architectural study documents use of Schoch profile **3507**, 20 × 12 mm, from the `Fenstereisen` section for a stair detail;
- it documents later use of profile **3517 / Anschlagseisen**, also from the `Fenstereisen` section, for a different threshold/detail function;
- the published analysis explicitly treats these as selection of industrial ready-mades by shape rather than original intended function.

Strong comparative inference:

- number + geometry + dimensions could make a profile catalogue operational as a design/search interface;
- catalogue category was not necessarily the terminal determinant of use;
- profile identity/versioning is therefore a high-value variable to test in the Schinz/Bär archival albums.

Open:

- whether Schinz/Bär used comparable numbering semantics;
- whether `/14` or `/13` are customer catalogues, internal compilations, master/update copies, received supplier material or mixtures;
- whether EBA 4 profile numbers occur in price lists, cash books, sales journals or inventories;
- whether supplements preserve or alter profile numbers;
- whether downstream Bär users can be documented selecting profiles by number in the same way as the Schoch case.

## Resume point

Resume from **PROFILE IDENTITY / VERSIONING**, not from generic `standardization`:

`NUMBER ↔ SHAPE ↔ DIMENSION ↔ CATEGORY ↔ PRICE/ORDER KEY ↔ PROPRIETARY/NORMAL STATUS ↔ EDITION ↔ SUPPLEMENT`.

The next high-value external search is for **direct Schinz/Bär or Bär & Co. downstream references to profile numbers / catalogue numbers**. If none can be closed remotely, preserve that as an onsite question rather than filling it by analogy.

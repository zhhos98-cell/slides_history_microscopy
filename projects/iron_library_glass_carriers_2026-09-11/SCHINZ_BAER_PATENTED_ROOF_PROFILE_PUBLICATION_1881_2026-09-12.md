# Schinz & Bär patented roof-profile public system — 1881

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** MAJOR PRIMARY-SOURCE CLOSURE / ACTIVE MORPHOLOGY CONTROL  
**Purpose:** insert a secure 1881 public technical profile system inside the Iron Library `EBA 4/14 (1877–1885)` window and test the relationship among patented/proprietary product form, engineering publication, archival profile albums and the 1885 public `Profil-Album`.

## 0. Current conclusion

A contemporaneous primary source now proves that **Schinz & Bär were publicly circulating a firm-specific engineered iron profile system by 29 October 1881**, four years before the securely documented 1885 `Profil-Album`.

The source does more than name the firm. It:

- prints a cross-section figure;
- explains the technical problem and solution;
- identifies Schinz & Bär in Zürich as supplier;
- states that the system had been **patented in several countries**;
- specifies intended architectural/technical applications.

The revised carrier chronology is therefore no longer:

`EBA 4/14 1877–1885 -> PUBLIC PROFIL-ALBUM 1885`.

It is at minimum:

`EBA 4/14 1877–1885 [FORM/FUNCTION OPEN]`
↕
**`PUBLIC ENGINEERING PROFILE SYSTEM 1881 + CROSS-SECTION FIGURE + FOREIGN PATENT PROTECTION`**
↕
`PUBLIC SCHINZ & BÄR PROFIL-ALBUM 1885`
↕
`EBA 4/13 1886–1903 [FORM/FUNCTION OPEN]`.

This materially strengthens the residency logic: the Iron Library albums sit across a period in which Schinz & Bär were not merely merchants receiving standardized forms; they were also publicly identifying and legally protecting their own engineered profile forms.

## 2. Technical problem and designed solution

The article frames the design problem for iron-supported glass roofing as two simultaneous requirements:

1. provide the glass with a clean, straight bearing surface;
2. prevent accumulation or passage of water at sealing points.

It stresses that exposed putty deteriorates under weathering and therefore welcomes a construction that combines stable support and water-tightness.

The illustrated Schinz & Bär profile works through a **two-channel / two-stage sealing logic**:

- a small outer groove is completely filled with putty;
- that putty creates a straight bearing surface for the glass and is hermetically enclosed from weather exposure;
- if the upper putty seal between glass and vertical main rib later becomes defective, penetrating water is caught in the **large inner groove**;
- the water can then collect and drain away rather than causing damage.

This is not merely a profile shape. It is a designed relation among:

`IRON CROSS-SECTION / GLASS SUPPORT / PUTTY SEAL / FAILURE MODE / SECONDARY DRAINAGE`.

That makes it an unusually useful morphology control for any profile drawing encountered in `EBA 4/14` or later public catalogues.

---

## 3. Supplier and patent statement

The article states that the illustrated roofing profile:

- was supplied by **Schinz & Bär in Zürich**;
- had been **patented for them / by them in several countries** (`in verschiedenen Ländern patentirt`).

### Secure claim

By 29 October 1881 Schinz & Bär publicly associated their firm with a technical profile system that had foreign patent protection in multiple jurisdictions.

### Open patent task

The individual patent numbers/countries have **not yet been recovered**.

Searches so far find a later unrelated German patent assigned to Schinz & Bär (`D.R.P. 34679`, 1885, adjustable impact valve for hydraulic rams), which must **not** be conflated with the 1881 roofing-profile system.

The roof-profile patents remain OPEN.

## 4. Swiss patent-law context

The Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property dates the first Swiss federal Patents Act to **29 June 1888** and the creation of the federal industrial-property office to **15 November 1888**.

Official history:
- https://www.ige.ch/en/about-us/the-history-of-the-ipi

The 1881 article's wording `in verschiedenen Ländern patentirt` therefore has a very specific institutional implication:

> the firm was publicly commercializing a technical product system from Zürich before a Swiss federal patent regime existed, while relying on protection obtained in foreign jurisdictions.

This should not be inflated into a general claim about Swiss innovation strategy without further evidence. But it makes the relation among **public disclosure, foreign legal protection, firm-specific product identity and catalogue/profile circulation** a directly testable historical problem.

The same 1881 *Eisenbahn* volume also contains an appendix concerning signatures to a petition to the Federal Council for the introduction of invention protection in Switzerland. This is contextual coincidence at the journal/volume level, not evidence that Schinz & Bär signed or participated unless their names are independently verified in the petition.

## 5. Applications named in 1881

The article predicts broad use for the profile in:

- `Lichthöfe` (light courts);
- `Aufnahmezimmer für Photographien` (photographic studios / photographic rooms);
- similar glazed roofs.

This is important for the carrier project because the profile is represented as a **portable cross-sectional solution for a recurrent building problem**, not only as an item in a merchant's stock list.

Do not yet infer actual installations from predicted applications.

## 6. Later technical codification

A later building-technology treatise/literature survey preserves the citation:

- `Neue Bedachungsprofile. System SCHINZ & BÄR. Eisenb., Bd. 15, S. 107.`

Digital source:
- https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/download/pdf/7411504.pdf

A separate later construction manual also describes and illustrates **`Bedachungsprofile der Firma Schinz [OCR: Sebing] Baer in Zürich`**, with multiple figures (`229a–d` to `230a–d`) and again explains their rain-water sealing function.

Digital OCR source surfaced at:
- https://repozytorium.biblos.pk.edu.pl/redo/resources/39810/file/scans/DEFAULT/OCR_rezultaty/100000297440_A_v1_200dpi_q60.pdf

This suggests the Schinz & Bär profile system entered later technical reference literature beyond the original 1881 notice. The exact bibliographic identity/date of this second manual still needs closure before using it as a chronological claim.

## 7. Relation to the 1885 public `Profil-Album`

The 1885 *Schweizerische Bauzeitung* review describes the recently issued Schinz & Bär `Profil-Album` as including ordinary profiles plus newly created and protected firm specialities, notably Treppenbeschlag-, Fenstereisen- and Gesimseisen forms.

The 1881 roofing profile gives a concrete earlier instance of the same broader commercial-technical regime:

`FIRM-SPECIFIC PROFILE FORM -> TECHNICAL PROBLEM SOLUTION -> DRAWN CROSS-SECTION -> LEGAL PROTECTION ABROAD -> PUBLIC ENGINEERING DESCRIPTION -> COMMERCIAL SUPPLY`.

### What is NOT yet proven

Do **not** assert that:

- the 1881 roof profile appears in the 1885 `Profil-Album`;
- the 1885 review's `geschützte Specialitäten` specifically includes this roofing system;
- the 1881 profile occurs in `EBA 4/14`;
- the archival album generated the patent/publication or vice versa;
- the patent countries or numbers are known.

All of those become morphology/concordance tests.

## 9. Application-level consequence

The strongest project is increasingly not `standardization` in the abstract.

It is a study of how a merchant firm made iron forms **identifiable, protectable, comparable, orderable and redistributable** across different documentary and institutional surfaces.

A compact formulation:

> **How did Schinz & Bär turn iron cross-sections into controlled commercial and technical reference objects—across warehouse stock, profile albums, patented product systems, engineering publication, price and transaction records, public catalogues and technical-school circulation?**

The larger Zürich-network question remains important because conventions, cartel correspondence and competitor archives show that this firm-specific product infrastructure operated inside collective commercial governance rather than outside it.

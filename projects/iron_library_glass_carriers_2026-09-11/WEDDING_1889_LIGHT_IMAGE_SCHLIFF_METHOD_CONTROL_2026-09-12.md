# Wedding 1889 — Lichtabbildung / Schliff method control

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: ARTICLE-LEVEL ROUTING + NEAR-CONTEMPORARY METHOD CONTROL. The full 1889 article text has not yet been directly transcribed, but its article-level location and at least one specific imaging claim are now independently constrained.

## 1. Article identity

**H. Wedding, `Ueber Fortschritte in der Lichtabbildung des Kleingefüges von Eisen und die Herstellung von Schliffen`, Stahl und Eisen, vol. 9 (1889), April.**

Francis Scott Rice's 1897 bibliography independently fixes the paper to `Stahl und Eisen`, vol. IX, April 1889.

A later metallography bibliography gives the Wedding 1889 citation as:

**`Stahl und Eisen 9 (1889), p. 263`.**

An independent retrospective article in *Stahl und Eisen* (1897), `Mikroskopie und Betrieb`, lists the relevant earlier metallographic pages and explicitly includes:

**`1889 S. 263 und 393`**

within a sequence that separately contains Wedding's and Martens' known works. Rice independently gives Martens' May 1889 paper at p.393. Together these controls make **p.263 the current best article-start pointer for Wedding's April 1889 paper**.

Evidence status:

`ARTICLE_START = p.263 (strong secondary/retrospective cross-control; direct 1889 page still pending)`.

Do not quote Wedding's wording from p.263 until the 1889 original page is directly opened.

### Correction: p.268 does not establish Wedding's article end

A 1900 *Stahl und Eisen* discussion contains a footnote `Stahl und Eisen 1889 S.268`, but direct context shows that this footnote is attached to a quotation from **A. Martens** beginning `Meine Aetzungen habe ich mit sehr vielen Reagentien vorgenommen ...`.

Therefore:

- **p.268 is not evidence for Wedding's article text**;
- it must not be used to infer automatically that Wedding's article ended on p.267;
- the exact ending page of Wedding's April 1889 article remains open until the original 1889 issue/table of contents is directly controlled.

This correction is important because the numerical proximity `Wedding p.263 / Martens citation p.268` can otherwise generate a false contiguous-page inference.

## 2. Wedding's 1889 imaging geometry is independently recoverable

The 1889 *Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society*, reporting Schmidt & Haensch's apparatus for photographing tarnish colours of iron surfaces, explicitly states that **Wedding found the details of iron microstructure were brought out much more strongly when the iron surface was inclined obliquely to the microscope axis**.

The report then says the same principle should apply to photomicrography and describes an apparatus whose stage can be fixed obliquely relative to the optical axis.

A contemporaneous 1889 `Jahrbuch für Photographie und Reproduktionstechnik` independently discusses the Schmidt & Haensch setup for photographing iron-surface tarnish colours `nach Professor Wedding in Berlin` and repeats Wedding's observation about oblique orientation increasing the visibility of microstructural detail.

Therefore one component of Wedding's 1889 `Lichtabbildung` programme is now secure at method level:

**specimen orientation relative to optical axis is an image-forming variable.**

## 3. Why this matters to the Sorby–Wedding dispute

Sorby's 1885/86 criticism had already made two variables epistemically consequential:

- preparation, especially soft-stone grinding before final polishing;
- illumination geometry, especially direct/vertical reflected light.

Wedding's 1889 programme explicitly joins `Lichtabbildung` and `Herstellung von Schliffen` in one paper, and the contemporaneous apparatus literature attributes to Wedding a deliberate manipulation of **surface inclination relative to the microscope axis** to intensify visible structural detail.

This establishes a concrete methodological development without yet proving causation from Sorby's criticism.

Use:

**`Wedding 1889 = active optimisation of specimen/image geometry`**

not:

`Wedding adopted Sorby's exact vertical-light protocol`.

Indeed, Wedding's oblique-surface observation points toward a different solution space: changing specimen orientation to alter the reflected-light image.

## 4. Methodological consequence

The metallographic object must now be decomposed into at least four independently variable geometries/processes:

1. **surface construction** — grinding / polishing / relief / etching;
2. **surface orientation** — specimen normal relative to microscope optical axis;
3. **illumination path** — oblique diffuse, coaxial reflected, prism/plate illumination, etc.;
4. **recording geometry** — direct observation versus projection/photomicrography.

The image is therefore not a transparent copy of pre-existing structure. It is generated by a controlled relation among prepared surface, angle, light path and recording apparatus.

## 5. Relationship to the mature German method

E. Heyn's 1899 *Stahl und Eisen* article later gives a mature workflow and explicitly credits:

**Reliefpolieren: introduced by Sorby; substantially perfected by Osmond, Martens and Wedding.**

Heyn separately describes multiple illumination systems, including:

- an obliquely inclined specimen under diffuse daylight;
- a specimen perpendicular to the optical axis illuminated through a 45° plane-parallel plate;
- a specimen perpendicular to the axis illuminated through a prism/through-objective reflected-light arrangement.

This mature method strongly confirms the historical importance of the variables Wedding was already foregrounding in 1889.

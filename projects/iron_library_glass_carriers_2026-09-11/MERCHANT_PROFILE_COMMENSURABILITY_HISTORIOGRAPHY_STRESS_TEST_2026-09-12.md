# Merchant profile commensurability — historiography and corpus stress test

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** ACTIVE HYPOTHESIS CONTROL / CORPUS-GEOMETRY CORRECTION  
**Rule:** do not narrate a smooth sequence `metrication -> Normalprofile -> merchant standardization`. Separate measurement law, producer profile series, merchant comparison practices, user-driven Normalprofile, and later ordering/accounting uptake.

## 0. Main result

The strongest current application hypothesis is no longer generic `documentary standardization`.

The sharper problem is:

> **How did iron merchants make heterogeneous products commercially comparable across changing supply geographies, maker-specific profile series, measurements, weights, prices and routes — and what changed when association-level Normalprofile entered that already-existing comparison system?**

Küderli/Bär is unusually well placed to test this because its surviving carrier sequence crosses three historically distinct moments:

1. **1858–72 correspondence** — supplier/routing/comparison work;
2. **documented supply-centre shift from France to Germany from 1870**;
3. **1877–85 Profileisenalbum + 1878–80 price lists + 1884 cashbooks + 1885 sales journals**, exactly bracketing the German Normalprofil moment of 1879–81.

The archive should therefore be read as a **merchant mediation layer**, not merely as evidence that standardization happened.

---

## 1. Historiographic correction: Normalprofile were not simply an effect of metrication

Peter Kramper, *The Battle of the Standards: Messen, Zählen und Wiegen in Westeuropa 1660–1914* (2019), chapter 9, gives the most important external control found so far.

Kramper argues that:

- the German architects'/engineers' associations and VDI formed a joint commission in the late 1870s;
- the 1881 `Normalprofilbuch` fixed exact dimensions for frequently used rolled-iron sections;
- the relationship between this development and metrication was **weak**: Normalprofile used metric units, but metric reform was not the main causal driver;
- standard profiles made structural calculation easier;
- more importantly, the late-1870s post-Gründerboom steel market had substantial overcapacity, strengthening users' ability to demand standardized products;
- engineers and railway companies benefited because they could calculate more reliably and become less dependent on the proprietary profile systems of individual producers;
- manufacturers bore conversion costs in rolling equipment;
- the system spread quickly in the 1880s; Prussian state operations were instructed to use standardized iron;
- later British standardization likewise reduced the number of section types, exchangeability problems and merchants' inventory costs.

Controlled open text:
- https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110581959/html
- searchable open text mirror: https://dokumen.pub/the-battle-of-the-standards-messen-zhlen-und-wiegen-in-westeuropa-1660-1914-9783110581959-9783110579239.html

Critical passage in searchable text: chapter 9, pp. 508–510, especially the discussion of Walzeisen profiles and market overcapacity.

### Consequence

Reject this simple causal chain:

> Swiss metric reform (1877) -> German Normalprofile (1879–81) -> standardized merchant documentation.

Use instead:

> **existing dimensional/product comparison practices + changing market structure + changing supply geography + association-level user-driven profile reduction -> merchant adoption/translation/resistance visible in local documentary carriers.**

Metric units remain an empirical field, not the master explanation.

## 2. Primary/near-contemporary control: what Normalprofile actually changed

Contemporary and later technical reference literature confirms that `Walzeisen` already existed in many named cross-sectional forms — round, square, flat, L, T, I, U, C, S, Z, Zores, rails, sleepers, etc. — and that Normalprofile restricted/regularized the set of cross-sections.

Useful controls:

- Meyers, `Walzeisen` (1885–90): https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/MKL1888%3AWalzeisen
- Brockhaus entry: https://www.retrobibliothek.de/retrobib/seite.html?id=136313
- historical technical summary of Normalprofile: https://technik.de-academic.com/16508/Normalprofile

The Brockhaus formulation is especially useful because it states that the associated tables of load-bearing capacity and weight made the calculation of iron construction substantially easier.

A later biographical reference on Heinzerling reports that the `Normalprofilbuch` reduced an earlier field of **273** profile-iron forms. Treat the exact 273 count as secondary until checked against the cited historical source, but retain the qualitative point that profile proliferation preceded normalization.

Control:
- https://www.retrobibliothek.de/retrobib/seite.html?id=107863

### Consequence for the archive

The key object is not `a standardized profile` in isolation. The key historical operation is reduction/alignment across:

`PROFILE SHAPE / DIMENSIONS / LINEAR WEIGHT / PRODUCER SERIES / USER REQUIREMENT / PRODUCT NUMBER / PRICE / STOCK / ORDER`.

Küderli's albums can reveal whether a merchant had already built practical equivalence systems before formal Normalprofile and how those systems changed afterwards.

## 3. Küderli/Bär becomes the sharpest core

Official ANTON evidence:

- `EBA 4/7 Korrespondenz`, 1858–72;
- `EBA 4/14 Profileisenalbum`, 1877–85, 3 vols;
- `EBA 4/6 Preislisten`, 1878–80, 2 files;
- `EBA 4/4 Kassabuch`, 1884–89, 2 vols;
- `EBA 4/15 Verkaufsjournal`, 1885–97, 11 vols.

Official fonds history explicitly states that from **1870** the firm shifted its supply centre from **France to Germany**, with Basel gaining importance as an import gateway.

Source:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/EBA%204
- actor/cross-reference page: https://archives.georgfischer.com/actors/show?collection=0&fonds=0&index=109&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=id

### Why this is stronger than a generic standardization story

The sequence contains a documented external change **before** the profile albums:

`French-centred supply -> German-centred supply (from 1870) -> Profileisenalbum 1877–85 -> Normalprofile 1879–81 inside album window -> price lists -> cash/sales books`.

This allows at least four competing explanations to be tested rather than assumed:

### K-H1 — supplier substitution

Profile albums primarily record replacement of French product series by German maker series after the supply shift.

### K-H2 — merchant commensurability

Albums are internally constructed merchant tools for aligning otherwise heterogeneous maker products, dimensions, weights and prices.

### K-H3 — Normalprofil adoption

Albums show a visible documentary break around 1879–81: terminology, numbering, dimensional sequence or organization begins following association-level Normalprofile.

### K-H4 — coexistence / resistance

Normalprofile enter without replacing proprietary/non-normal profiles; the merchant continues to maintain equivalences among multiple systems.

K-H4 is especially plausible given later evidence that non-normal profiles continued even where Normalprofile were accepted.

## 4. Exact K2 physical test — revise capture schema

For **every sampled album page / pasted insert / profile table**, capture:

`VOLUME / PAGE / DATE_OR_LAYER / SUPPLIER_OR_MILL / SUPPLIER_PLACE / COUNTRY / PROFILE_FAMILY / PROFILE_SHAPE / PRODUCT_CODE / DIMENSIONS / UNIT / LINEAR_WEIGHT / MATERIAL_GRADE / DRAWN_OR_PRINTED / NORMALPROFIL_LANGUAGE / EXPLICIT_STANDARD_REFERENCE / PRICE_LINK / CURRENCY / ROUTE_OR_GATEWAY / PASTED_INSERT / OVERWRITE / DELETION / ADDITION / HAND / PAGE_SEQUENCE / WEAR / REVISION / CROSS_REFERENCE_TO_PRICE_LIST / CROSS_REFERENCE_TO_SALE`.

Add a **standardization-status field**:

- `PROPRIETARY_SERIES`
- `MERCHANT_EQUIVALENCE`
- `ASSOCIATION_NORMALPROFILE`
- `AMBIGUOUS`
- `MIXED_COEXISTENCE`

### Required page-level comparison

Split K2 into four strata:

1. **1877–78** — pre-Normalprofil baseline;
2. **1879–81** — commission / first Normalprofil transition;
3. **1882–83** — immediate post-publication uptake;
4. **1884–85** — point at which cash/sales systems become linkable.

Do not merely compare first/last pages. The historical test is whether the information architecture changes *within* the three volumes.

## 5. K1 correspondence test must now be asymmetric around 1870

The 1858–72 correspondence should not use three equal generic samples only. The documented supply shift means the most diagnostic window is **1868–72**.

Recommended sampling:

- **1858–60** — early reference slice; direct Haffter overlap;
- **1865–67** — mature pre-shift slice;
- **1868–69** — immediate pre-hinge;
- **1870–72** — supply-centre transition slice.

## 6. Haffter correction — large archive, but late-tail density must be proved

Official EBA 3 description:

- 15 lm;
- 1953 donation: 185 volumes + 42 brochures + 7,900 business letters;
- over 7,000 original incoming letters arranged by firm and indexed by person/place;
- ten `Calculationen` volumes recording purchase price plus insurance, transport and customs;
- strong evidence for deposited stock, forwarding/commission houses, foundries, debtors and carriers;
- original archive was unordered; current arrangement is later.

Source:
- https://archives.georgfischer.com/objects/show?collection=0&context=tectonical&fonds=0&index=3&parent=2&perPage=50&recordgroup=0&sortAsc=1&sortField=position

Public series endpoints confirm:

- `EBA 3/10 Calculationen` 1824–1867, 10 vols;
- `/11 Ertheilte Commissionen` 1833–1867, 5 vols;
- `/16 Ertheilte Preis-Courants` 1857–67, 1 vol;
- `/19 Frachten-Rechnungen` 1865–67, 1 vol;
- `/24 Korrespondenz` 1835–60, 555 files;
- `/25 Diverse Debitoren` 1783–1876, 18 vols.

### Important correction

The official fonds narrative repeatedly stresses the archive's exceptional value for commercial/economic life **before 1848**. Therefore the mere fact that several books end in 1867 does **not** prove that 1858–67 has the same entry density as earlier decades.

Reclassify Haffter for the application as:

> **proven large transregional conversion/calculation infrastructure; late 1850s–1867 tail promising but density/linkability must be physically validated.**

### Haffter onsite gate

Before H2 is admitted as a major 1865–67 unit, establish for each relevant volume:

`TOTAL_PAGES / LATE_PERIOD_PAGE_RANGE / ENTRIES_PER_YEAR / BLANK_OR_SPARSE_PAGES / INDEX_PRESENT / CROSS_REFERENCE_STYLE / REPEATED_COUNTERPARTIES / PRODUCT_DETAIL / UNIT_DETAIL / FREIGHT_CUSTOMS_DETAIL`.

If late pages are sparse, H2 becomes a targeted comparator rather than one of the two equal cores.

## 7. Revised corpus geometry

Previous shorthand:

> Cluster A c.1858–67 -> Bridge c.1868–72 -> Cluster B c.1877–85.

Current corrected geometry:

### A0 — Haffter deep background / conversion infrastructure

Predominantly earlier nineteenth century, with unusually strong transregional correspondence, calculation and landed-cost architecture. The archive explains what a mature merchant conversion system can look like.

### A1 — 1858–60 direct merchant overlap

Haffter correspondence tail + early Küderli correspondence. Useful for controlled comparison, but Haffter late-density still to validate.

### A2 — 1865–72 Küderli supply-routing hinge

Küderli becomes analytically primary. Haffter price/freight/calculation end series act as a comparator/control rather than automatically equal mass.

### B — 1877–85 Küderli profile-price-order core

This is now the **cleanest compact application core**:

`Profileisenalbum (3 vols) + price lists (2 files) + cashbooks (from 1884) + sales journals (from 1885)`

with the 1879–81 German Normalprofil moment occurring inside the album sequence.

### Comparative layers

- selected Library technical/serial/grey literature;
- native GF product/market documents;
- optional Rauschenbach only after scale confirmation;
- three original Berlin metallographic glass plates remain a separate later material-object coda unless the proposal deliberately extends from commercial comparability into photographic/material reference systems.

## 8. Novelty control

Existing scholarship already covers:

- metrication and national/international measurement reform;
- association-level engineering standardization;
- German Normalprofile as an 1870s–80s engineering/market phenomenon;
- general sociological `commensuration`;
- commercial arithmetic as a technology of market participation.

Useful conceptual controls:

- Peter Kramper, *The Battle of the Standards* (2019), especially ch. 9.
- Caitlin Rosenthal, `Capitalism by Algorithm: Numbers for the Innumerate in the Eighteenth- and Nineteenth-Century Atlantic World` (2023): https://academic.oup.com/book/45699/chapter/398105548
- Wendy Espeland & Mitchell Stevens, `Commensuration as a Social Process` (1998).

### What may still be distinctive

Do not claim `the history of standardization` as the novelty.

The promising contribution is narrower:

> **reconstructing the merchant documentary layer between heterogeneous producer catalogues/supply routes and user/association-level engineering standards, using a rare sequence in which correspondence, profile albums, price lists and transaction books survive together.**

The central historical actor is therefore not `the standard` but the **merchant operation that makes unlike goods actionable as comparable stock/orders**.

## 9. Proposal-compatible question family after this pass

### Q1 — strongest

**How did Swiss iron merchants make rolled-iron products comparable across changing suppliers, routes, profile series, measurements, weights and prices between the late 1850s and mid-1880s?**

### Q2 — Normalprofil hinge

**What changed in merchant product documentation when German Normalprofile emerged in 1879–81, and what continued to require local equivalence work despite formal standardization?**

### Q3 — carriers

**How did correspondence, calculation books, profile albums, price lists and sales journals perform different stages of commercial comparison and ordering?**

### Claim ceiling

Until EBA 4/14 is physically inspected, do **not** assert:

- that it contains German Normalprofile;
- that the 1879–81 standard caused a documentary change;
- that merchant comparison became more standardized over time;
- that French profiles were replaced by German profiles page-for-page;
- that metric notation displaced older units inside the albums.

Those are the onsite tests.

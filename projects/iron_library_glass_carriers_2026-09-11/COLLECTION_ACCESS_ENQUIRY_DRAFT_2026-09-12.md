## Why this enquiry exists

Current collection-first work identifies **two proven coherent archival blocks** with strong c.1850–1885 structure and potentially high onsite value:

1. **EBA 3 — Hafftersche Eisenhandlung, Weinfelden**;
2. **EBA 4 — Küderli & Co. (vormals Bär & Co.), Zürich**.

A third company cluster, **Maschinenfabrik Rauschenbach records in GFA 1**, is historically promising but its pre-1885 scale is not yet established by the public catalogue. ANTON clearly exposes one employee-register volume from 1871 and ten sickness-fund volumes from 1872, while several other early-looking records are catalogued as single dossiers spanning decades. Internal file lists / physical retrieval units are therefore the decisive missing evidence rather than more item searching.

The source-critical audit also shows that these blocks have different carrier/arrangement histories: Haffter has very strong bound business series and correspondence but a later arrangement history; Küderli is a multi-accession historical company collection; Rauschenbach combines a small proven bound serial core with present-day dossiers that can span widely separated dates.

A second catalogue-architecture problem remains open. Swiss ISIL lists for Eisenbibliothek:

- **66 manuscripts**;
- **3 estates (`Nachlässe`)**;
- c.300 graphic sheets;
- 80 maps of canton Schaffhausen.

Current ANTON separately exposes **EBA / GFA / GFKS / GFD**, while current Library reporting still includes separately listed collections/bequests. Historical boundaries have moved, so the ISIL figures cannot safely be added as independent collections without mapping them to the current catalogue architecture.

A third public-interface limit has been reached: current classification schemes exist and historical evidence establishes large nineteenth-century serial, materials/testing, company/product and world-exhibition environments, but exact current class membership and local holding runs are not reproducibly recoverable through the public routes used in this audit.

The enquiry should remain collection-level. Do not pitch Wedding, Martens, Sorby, microscopy, or a fixed thesis yet.

## Question rationale / what each answer changes

### Q1–5: EBA access and physical series

These determine whether Haffter/Küderli are genuinely non-substitutable onsite corpora and how many physical units can realistically be consulted in two to three weeks.

### Q6–8: Rauschenbach scale test

This is no longer an assumption that Rauschenbach is a large Tier-A block. The answer decides whether it returns to Tier A/O1 or stays a narrower company comparator.

Publicly controlled serial carriers are currently only:

- `GFA 1/806` — 1 bound employee-register volume;
- `GFA 1/809` — 10 sickness-fund volumes.

Broad one-file dossiers cannot be translated into pre-1885 volume without internal descriptive evidence.

### Q9: practical throughput

A strong corpus is not useful for a 2–3 week residency if retrieval/order limits make the planned comparison unrealistic.

### Q10–13 and Q17: current system mapping

This is a **catalogue-boundary problem**, not simply a digitisation-count problem. Historical/institution-level totals must be mapped into today's Library/EBA/GFKS systems before being used to claim a new corpus or onsite need.

### Q14–16: exports rather than more web brute force

The public web now provides enough morphology to know what is missing but not enough granularity to reconstruct it safely. Ask only whether an **existing export/list** can be shared. Do not ask staff to construct a bespoke bibliography.

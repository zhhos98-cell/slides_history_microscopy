## Why this enquiry exists

Current collection-first work identifies three archival blocks with strong c.1850–1885 structure and potentially high onsite value:

1. **EBA 3 — Hafftersche Eisenhandlung, Weinfelden**;
2. **EBA 4 — Küderli & Co. (vormals Bär & Co.), Zürich**;
3. **Rauschenbach operational/company records in GFA 1**.

The source-critical audit also shows that these have different carrier/arrangement histories: Haffter has very strong bound business series but a later arrangement history; Küderli is a multi-accession historical company collection; Rauschenbach combines bound operational registers with present-day dossiers that can span widely separated dates.

A second catalogue-architecture problem remains open. Swiss ISIL lists for Eisenbibliothek:

- **66 manuscripts**;
- **3 estates (`Nachlässe`)**;
- c.300 graphic sheets;
- 80 maps of canton Schaffhausen.

Current ANTON separately exposes **EBA / GFA / GFKS / GFD**, while current Library reporting still includes separately listed collections/bequests. Historical boundaries have moved, so the ISIL figures cannot safely be added as independent collections without mapping them to the current catalogue architecture.

A third public-interface limit has now been reached: the current Library page links 2024 classification schemes, and historical evidence establishes a large nineteenth-century serial environment, but the exact current classification hierarchy and complete local nineteenth-century periodical holding runs are not reproducibly recoverable through the public interfaces used in this audit.

The enquiry should remain collection-level. Do not pitch Wedding, Martens, Sorby, microscopy, or a fixed thesis yet.

## Question rationale / what each answer changes

### Q1–2: internal scans / remote reproductions

If extensive internal scans already exist, onsite necessity for that block decreases. If only metadata exists publicly and large series are not available remotely, onsite value increases substantially.

### Q3: EBA regulatory scope

Public service pages group library/archive use together, but the currently linked formal regulations are titled for the Corporate Archives of Georg Fischer Ltd. Do not assume the GFA `onsite only / no sending for consultation` rule applies verbatim to EBA without clarification.

### Q4–7: consultability, retrieval units and carrier structure

These questions determine whether the Tier-A blocks are feasible in a two- to three-week stay and whether present catalogue units correspond to physically useful research units.

The Rauschenbach carrier audit makes this especially important: its strongest serial carriers are bound registers (`GFA 1/806`, `/809`), while several correspondence/crisis records are present-day dossiers spanning broad or mixed chronology.

### Q8: staff structural knowledge

Open-ended only at the level of existing archival structure. Do not ask staff to build the historical argument.

### Q9–12 and Q15: current system mapping

This is a **catalogue-boundary problem**, not simply a digitisation-count problem.

Evidence already shows boundaries have moved:

- current ANTON root separates EBA / GFA / GFKS / GFD;
- GFKS 2 contains Schaffhausen maps;
- EBA 8 contains graphic/art material;
- EBA 8 records transfers from GF Kunstsammlung;
- historically named Eisenbibliothek Sondersammlungen can now appear as EBA fonds;
- current Library reports still retain a separately-listed collections/bequests category.

Therefore institution-level totals must be mapped into current systems before being used to claim a new corpus or onsite need.

### Q13–14: catalogue exports rather than more web brute force

The public web confirms that current classification schemes exist and that the nineteenth-century serial environment is large, but it does not presently yield a reproducible complete hierarchy or the full local holding-run/gap list.

Ask only whether an **existing export/list** can be shared. Do not ask staff to construct a bespoke bibliography.

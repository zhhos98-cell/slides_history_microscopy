## 1. Fischer has a thicker unpublished production corpus than the travel texts alone suggest

The Johann Conrad Fischer bibliography and Georg Fischer archive catalogue identify a cluster of unpublished production records that can be compared against the travel narratives:

- `GFA 1/94.1` — *Notizenbuch bezüglich Arbeiten in den Stahlwerken im Mühlental*, 1838–1845, 1 bound volume;
- `GFA 1/94.2` — *Fabrikationsbuch für die Werke im Mühlental*, 1839–1854, 1 bound volume;
- `GFA 1/94.3` — *Fabrikationsbuch*, 1850–1891, 1 bound volume;
- `GFA 1/94.4` — *Fabrikationsbuch mit Betriebsnotizen*, 1853–1854, 1 bound volume;
- `GFA 1/145.26` — *Notizen zur Gusstahlproduktion*, 1802–1854, 2 files;
- `GFA 1/148.8` — loose Fischer papers concerning cast steel / steel casting, 1805–1835, 7 files;
- `GFA 1/145.41` — Fischer note on pouring steel castings, dated 10 August 1846;
- `GFA 1/98` — *Industrie- und Gewerbeausstellungen*, 1804–1851.

The currently visible ANTON catalogue rows do not mark the above records as `Digitalisiert`. Treat this as a catalogue-state observation, not proof that no reproduction exists. They are now high-priority item-level archive checks because they can test whether categories seen in the travel texts — sample, batch, quality, experiment, casting, failure, comparison — also structure Fischer's production records at home.

The 10 August 1846 steel-casting note is especially valuable because it falls between the 1845 and late-1846 British journeys.

## 2. Great Exhibition 1851: travel narrative can be paired with exhibition-object documentation

The Fischer digital edition states that Johann Conrad Fischer exhibited ingots of several steel types, two daggers and four razors made of nickel-steel alloy at the Great Exhibition of 1851. It cites the *Official Descriptive and Illustrated Catalogue*, vol. 3, p. 1269.

The complete volume 3 is publicly downloadable in multiple public-domain scans (Wikimedia / Internet Archive / Smithsonian / Wellcome). This should be acquired and the Fischer entry extracted directly.

The Georg Fischer archive also identifies:

- `GFA 1/145.44` — *Werbeschrift von Johann Conrad Fischer für die Great Exhibition 1851, mit Tableau der Ausstellungsobjekte*; catalogue record currently carries a protection period into 2053/2054 because the record spans 1851–2023. Do not assume immediate access to the underlying historical component without staff clarification.
- `GFA 1/149.3` and `GFA 1/149.4` — 1851 London World Exhibition facsimile objects; marked `Digitalisiert`.

Working comparison:

**factory production record → selected material/object → exhibition catalogue entry → public display/jury judgement → later travel narrative.**

This is a stronger test of object reformatting than the travel diary alone.

## 3. Faraday relation is documented before 1845 and includes laboratory access

The Fischer TEI edition for 8 June 1825 describes Fischer going to the Royal Institution to meet Michael Faraday with a recommendation from de la Rive. Faraday receives him in the laboratory rather than only a visitors' room.

A modern materials-history study identifies the surviving recommendation as Faraday Correspondence Letter 257, Royal Institution manuscript `RI MS F3 B, 139–42`, de la Rive to Faraday, 28 May 1825.

This means the 1845 Faraday visit belongs to a relation at least twenty years old and should not be treated as a one-off encounter.

High-value control source:

- Faraday & Stodart, `On the Alloys of Steel`, *Philosophical Transactions* 112 (1822), 253–270 — free/public-domain full text.

Do not infer direct dependence of Fischer's metallurgy on Faraday merely from access/contact. The secure relation is recommendation → laboratory access → material inspection / exchange.

## 4. Maskelyne–Tutton preparation layer is much richer than one referee report

Royal Society catalogue material now gives a compact preparation corpus:

- `RR/12/397`, 28 Dec 1894 — Maskelyne referee report on Tutton's updated cutting/grinding/polishing apparatus; 4 manuscript pages; direct PDF download available;
- `AP/71/4` — Tutton's unpublished 1894 paper on the cutting/grinding/polishing instrument, including a photograph of the apparatus;
- `RR/12/253` — Maskelyne letter to Herbert Rix on Tutton's section-plate/prism and monochromatic-light papers;
- Tutton, `An Instrument for Grinding Section-Plates and Prisms of Crystals of Artificial Preparations Accurately in the Desired Directions`, *Philosophical Transactions* 185 (1894), 887–912;
- Tutton, updated cutting/grinding/polishing paper, *Proceedings of the Royal Society* 57 (1895), 324–330.

This allows comparison of manuscript submission, referee judgement, instrument photograph, published paper and later revised instrument.

Maskelyne's role should therefore be tested as a gatekeeper of both **preparation precision** and **adequacy of visual representation**, not simply as a mineralogist who used thin sections.

## 5. Maskelyne meteorite corpus is directly downloadable / readable

Key primary source:

- Nevil Story-Maskelyne, `On the Mineral Constituents of Meteorites`, 1870; Proceedings abstract pp. 146–157 and Philosophical Transactions paper, with Royal Society referee reports `RR/7/42` and `RR/7/43` digitised.

The Royal Society record for `RR/7/43` identifies the paper as containing two new minerals and possibly a new metal and makes the manuscript report downloadable.

This should be paired with Sorby's 1863–64 meteorite work to compare how two British microscopic material regimes approached heterogeneous extraterrestrial matter without assuming direct transmission.

## 6. Sorby has an unusually strong surviving object/archive layer

The Edyvean–Hammond catalogue confirms surviving glass-mounted metallurgical specimens at the University of Sheffield and South Yorkshire Industrial History Society and cites Sorby's diaries for 1862–64 plus letters in Sheffield archives.

High-value archive references surfaced in the catalogue include:

- Sorby diaries, 1859–1908 (with two gaps), University of Sheffield Special Collections;
- W. P. Beale to Sorby, 29 June 1864, Sheffield City Libraries Archives `SLPS 51-257`;

- H. Woodward to Sorby, 28 Sept 1864, `SLPS 51-260`;

The diaries/letters are not yet established here as remotely downloadable. They are archive leads for later co-presence/contact testing once Wedding's itinerary is extracted.

## 7. Wedding 1862 exhibition work has a clean downloadable primary source

`Official Catalogue of the Mining and Metallurgical Products; Class I. in the Zollverein Department of the International Exhibition 1862`, compiled under von Dechen's immediate direction by Dr Hermann Wedding, survives in multiple full digital copies.

Confirmed digital routes include:

- Brussels City Archives public PDF;
- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek / Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek digital copy, 106 pages;
- Google Books/Play free scan.

This source should later be compared to Mss 25 not merely for content overlap but for transformation of information:

**travel-note / site observation → selected statistics / objects / categories → official exhibition catalogue.**

That comparison may reveal what Wedding discarded, normalised or reformatted when moving from working notebook to institutional public representation.

# Bessemer licences / royalties — archival route for Brown and Cammell

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: ARCHIVAL ROUTING NOTE. This file separates three different record types that have been repeatedly conflated in secondary accounts: early lump-sum licence sales, later per-ton Bessemer royalties, and surviving formal licence agreements.

## 1. The £27,000 episode is already segregated

Bessemer's autobiography directly controls the 1856 first-licence episode. The £27,000 total belonged to the first malleable-iron licences sold immediately after Cheltenham to Dowlais, Govan, Butterley and a Welsh tin-plate manufacturer. Sheffield steelmakers were still standing aside at this stage.

See:
- `WEDDING_BESSEMER_27000_FALSE_LEAD_AUDIT_2026-09-12.md`

Therefore do not use £27,000 as the financial terms for John Brown or Charles Cammell.

## 2. Later Bessemer licensing operated through per-ton royalty

Bessemer later states in his autobiography that his royalty was **£2 per ton on all ingots of iron or steel made by his process**.

Source:
- Henry Bessemer, *An Autobiography*, chapter 18, part 19: https://www.bibliomania.com/2/9/71/118/21377/19.html

Near the end of the patent period, contemporary patent debate shows that the rail trade had a more complicated rebate structure. A 1869 discussion, quoting *The Engineer*, describes:

- nominal royalty on rails: **£2 per ton**;
- nominal drawback: **£1 per ton**;
- after scrap/crop-end/waste accounting, effective royalty on delivered rail estimated at about **£1 5s 6d per ton**;
- after expiry, only a small plant royalty would remain.

Routing source:
- *Recent Discussions on the Abolition of Patents for Inventions* (1869), citing *The Engineer* of 28 May: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/72096/pg72096-images.html

A later industrial history compresses John Brown's licence terms as £1/ton on rails and £2/ton on other steel. That shorthand is broadly compatible with a rebate regime but should not be treated as the exact contract wording until Brown's licence itself is located.

## 4. Revised archival target for Brown / Cammell

Do not search only for a document literally titled `Cammell Bessemer licence`.

Search three record families:

### A. Licence / patent agreements

Possible vocabulary:
- licence agreement;
- patent licence;
- agreement between Henry Bessemer / Robert Longsdon and Charles Cammell & Co.;
- permission to manufacture under Bessemer patents;
- assignment / grant / memorandum of licence.

### B. Royalty accounts / returns

Because later licensing was production-linked, potentially higher-yield records are:
- royalty account;
- tonnage return;
- Bessemer ingot return;
- rail tonnage declaration;
- patent royalty statement;
- payments to Bessemer / Longsdon;
- scrap / crop-end / waste drawback calculation.

### C. Company legal / deed / minute records

Known Cammell archive routes include:
- Sheffield Archives **MD8121** — Cammell Laird salaries journals, staff lists, registers of deeds, deeds, finance and legal papers, 19th–20th century;
- Sheffield Archives **WED/312–315** — Charles Cammell & Co deeds etc., 18th–19th century;
- Sheffield Archives **X306/6/4/1** — Plan of Cyclops Works, 1860s.

The public company guide has not yet exposed a specific 1861 licence reference inside these collections. Do not invent one.

## 6. Why this matters for Wedding

Wedding's 1860 notebook sits immediately before Cammell's adoption but inside an already functioning patent/licence economy. The access regime at Bessemer's Sheffield works and the later per-ton royalty regime together suggest that technical disclosure was institutionally connected to prospective transfer and measurable production.

This sharpens the project question from:

`Was the Bessemer process public or secret?`

to:

> **How were different layers of process knowledge disclosed, witnessed, inscribed and then converted into licensed production measured through tonnage and royalty?**

Wedding's notebook is evidence from the disclosure/inscription side of that chain; a Cammell licence or royalty ledger would provide the contractual/accounting side.

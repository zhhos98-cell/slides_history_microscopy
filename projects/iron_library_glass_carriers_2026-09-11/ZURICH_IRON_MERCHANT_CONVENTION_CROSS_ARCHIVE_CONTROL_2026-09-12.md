# Zürich iron-merchant conventions and profile documentation — cross-archive control

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** MAJOR EXTERNAL CONTROL / APPLICATION-HYPOTHESIS UPGRADE  
**Core result:** Stadtarchiv Zürich's Pestalozzi/Wiser iron-trade archive independently preserves a convention/correspondence/price/profile sequence that directly names `Schinz & Baer`, the predecessor represented by Iron Library `EBA 4` Küderli/Bär. This makes the Iron Library corpus part of a demonstrable inter-firm information system rather than an isolated merchant archive.

## 0. Why this matters

The previous hypothesis treated `EBA 4/5 Convention der Zürcher Eisenhändler (1854)` as potentially important but publicly opaque.

A separate corporate archive held by **Stadtarchiv Zürich**, `VII.382 Pestalozzi + Co AG`, proves that Zürich iron merchants were generating convention records from at least the early 1850s and that `Schinz & Baer` participated in later convention/cartel correspondence during the exact decades covered by the Iron Library Küderli material.

This does **not** prove the detailed content of `EBA 4/5`. It does prove that the title/date should be treated as a potentially network-level record, not a random one-off document.

## 1. Early convention sequence in the Pestalozzi/Wiser archive

Stadtarchiv Zürich finding aid `VII.382 Pestalozzi + Co AG, Firmenarchiv, 1788–1945` lists:

- `1.3` — **Protokoll über die Beschlüsse der vereinigten Eisenhändler in Zürich**, 22 December **1852**;
- `1.4` — **Convention (Vertrag)** of 22 December 1852; **Convention 1 September 1854 – 31 March 1855 and correspondence**; aggregate date range **1852–1901**.

Source:
- Stadtarchiv Zürich, `VII.382 Pestalozzi + Co AG`, finding aid PDF: https://amsquery.stadt-zuerich.ch/Dateien/21/D109662.pdf
- finding aid p.2.

### Immediate implication

Iron Library `EBA 4/5`, titled **Convention der Zürcher Eisenhändler**, date **1854**, now has a strong external cross-archive match in date and institutional language.

**Claim ceiling:** do not yet state that the copies are identical, that the same parties signed both surviving records, or what rules the 1854 convention contained. Those require document-level comparison.

## 2. Direct Schinz & Baer convention/cartel linkage, 1870–85

The same Stadtarchiv Zürich finding aid lists:

- `4.2.1` — **Korrespondenz zu Kartellabsprache mit Gebrüder Vogel, Schinz und Baer und Fäh und Schoch**, **1870–73**;
- `4.2.2` — **Convention zwischen den Herren Fäh und Schoch, Schinz und Baer, Gebrüder Vogel und Joh. David Wiser betreffend den Warenverkauf en détail und en gros**, **1873**;
- `4.2.3` — correspondence associated with the 1873 convention, **1873–75**;
- `4.2.4` — business correspondence between Zürich iron-trade firms **Schinz & Baer, Gebrüder Vogel, Faeh & Schoch / Julius Schoch & Co and J. D. Wiser**, chronologically arranged, **1879–85**.

Source:
- same finding aid, p.6.

### Consequence

The official EBA 4 history says that from **1870** Schinz & Baer shifted its supply emphasis from France to Germany. The external archive now independently shows that **1870–73 is also a period of explicit inter-merchant market coordination involving Schinz & Baer**.

Do not assume causation between supply shift and convention/cartel coordination. But treat 1870–73 as a **double hinge** to test:

`SUPPLY-GEOGRAPHY CHANGE` + `INTER-MERCHANT RULE/COMPETITION COORDINATION`.

K1 correspondence sampling should therefore prioritize 1868–72 even more strongly.

## 3. Direct external profile/price comparator, 1875–87

The Pestalozzi/Wiser finding aid has a dedicated section **Preislisten und physikalische Tabellen** and lists:

- `5.1` — price lists and protocols, **1853–73**;
- `5.2` — price lists, **1869–75**;
- `5.3` — price lists, **1875–79**;
- `5.4` — **Profile des Façon-Eisen-Lagers der Eisenhandlung Joh. David Wiser in Zürich**, **1879**;
- `5.5` — price lists, **1879–87**.

Source:
- same finding aid, p.8.

Later series include weight/load-bearing/dimension tables, confirming the firm's long-lived practice of linking product sale with quantitative reference information, but those later records must not be projected backward without inspection.

### Critical overlap with Iron Library

Iron Library EBA 4 preserves:

- `EBA 4/14` Profileisenalbum, **1877–85**, 3 vols;
- `EBA 4/6` price lists, **1878–80**, 2 files;
- `EBA 4/7` correspondence through **1872**.

Stadtarchiv Zürich preserves for a directly interacting competitor/co-conventioner:

- profile stock documentation in **1879**;
- price lists **1875–79** and **1879–87**;
- direct inter-firm correspondence with Schinz & Baer **1879–85**.

This is an unusually tight cross-archive chronological lock.

## 4. Revised historical object

The historical object should now be larger than `merchant profile album` but smaller than generic `standardization`:

> **the Zürich iron merchants' shared and firm-specific information infrastructure for making heterogeneous iron products governable as purchases, stock, prices, comparable profiles and competitive transactions, c.1852–1885.**

The key is the coexistence of two layers:

### Firm-specific layer

- supplier correspondence;
- product/profile albums;
- price lists;
- calculation/cash/sales books;
- stock identifiers and order references.

### Inter-firm layer

- conventions;
- price/sales conditions;
- cartel/coordination correspondence;
- shared or negotiated distinctions between wholesale and retail (`en gros` / `en détail`);
- potentially common price/product categories.

The research problem becomes the **interface between these layers**.

---

## 5. Do not moralize the word `standardization`

This cross-archive evidence is a warning against a one-directional efficiency story.

Formal engineering Normalprofile could make calculation and cross-producer specification easier. Merchant conventions could coordinate market conduct. Merchant profile albums could reduce heterogeneity for purchasing/order work. These operations may overlap in documentary form without sharing the same institutional purpose.

Therefore separate:

`TECHNICAL COMMENSURABILITY`  
`COMMERCIAL COMMENSURABILITY`  
`PRICE / TERMS COORDINATION`  
`COMPETITIVE GOVERNANCE`  
`FORMAL ENGINEERING STANDARDIZATION`.

The archival question is when one document or identifier crosses from one operation into another.

## 8. Stronger application question family

### A — institutional + documentary

**How did Zürich iron merchants coordinate competition while maintaining firm-specific systems for comparing suppliers, profiles, weights and prices between the 1850s and 1880s?**

### B — interface question

**How did collective market rules and private product-information systems interact as iron merchants shifted suppliers and confronted increasingly standardized rolled-iron profiles?**

### C — carrier question

**What work was done by conventions, correspondence, price lists, profile albums and transaction books respectively, and when did information move from one carrier into another?**

These are stronger than a generic history of `standardization` because they are anchored in a demonstrable multi-firm documentary network.

# Fellowship microscopy — BAM provenance addendum

Updated: 2026-09-11

Purpose: preserve new provenance findings relevant to the narrow Sheffield–Berlin microscopy fellowship project without altering `CURRENT_PROGRESS.md` or replacing the main narrow checkpoint.

## 1. BAM is now the first external provenance lead for Martens material

The present fate of Martens's 1884 Royal Mining Academy collection of 120 metallographic sections remains unresolved. Do not call the collection lost and do not claim it survives at BAM.

However, two independent BAM sources materially change the provenance search:

1. BAM publicly preserves and reproduces original Adolf Martens microscopic drawings from the 1878–1882 research sequence, including polished/etched iron and steel sections.
2. A 2025 official historical-research status report states that the BAM library contains a **Teilnachlass von Adolf Martens** for which there is **no inventory**; the library does not know where all portions not placed in display cases are located, nor the original or current full extent of the partial estate.

Therefore the defensible current position is:

> The fate of the 120-section collection is unresolved, while Martens-related surviving material at BAM is itself incompletely catalogued. BAM should be queried before inferring destruction or disappearance.

## 2. Martens notebook survives in BAM library

Peter D. Portella's BAM report `Adolf Martens and his contributions to materials engineering` (2006) reproduces pages explicitly captioned **`Notebook of Adolf Martens, BAM, library`**. The illustrated pages contain handwritten notes and drawn microscopic structures.

This is important for the fellowship project because Berlin microscopic practice can potentially be studied through a working documentary object, not only through published diagrams and later institutional histories.

Open questions for BAM:

- Does the notebook have a stable library/archive identifier?
- What date range does it cover?
- Is it formally part of the uncatalogued Martens Teilnachlass mentioned in the 2025 report?
- Does it contain numbered specimens, preparation notes, references to Wedding, the Bergakademie, or the 120-section collection?
- Are there additional notebooks, loose drawings, labels, specimen lists, photographic plates or collection documentation among the uncatalogued material?

## 3. Berlin glass already matters by 1880

Portella's discussion of Martens's 1880 microscopic work records that Martens's microscope arrangement allowed **direct exposure of photographic plates** and that he supplied practical advice on depositing appropriate **emulsions on glass plates**. At the same time, Martens regarded hand drawing of observed microstructure as especially powerful for gaining knowledge.

This keeps `glass` inside the narrow 1863–1885 chronology without turning the project into a later history of scientific photography.

Analytical distinction:

- Sheffield: glass-supported prepared metal sections, cover glass, then early photomicrographic reproduction.
- Berlin: prepared sections/looking-glass support; by 1880 a microscope explicitly designed with photographic glass-plate exposure as a representational option, alongside hand drawing.

Thus glass participates differently in **making visible**, **stabilising**, and **reproducing** microscopic evidence.

## 4. BAM original microscopic drawings

BAM's public historical materials reproduce original Martens drawings associated with the 1878–1882 microscopic studies, including Spiegeleisen and steel sections treated/etched in different ways.

These surviving drawings strengthen a bounded representation question:

> How was a transient microscopic field converted into a durable and inspectable evidential object: prepared section, notebook drawing, published engraving or photographic glass plate?

Do not expand this into a general history of scientific illustration.

## 5. Implication for the 120 Schliffe provenance search

Search order should now be:

1. BAM library / Martens Teilnachlass;
2. BAM historical collections / display material / old specimen documentation;
3. successor holdings of the Royal Mining Academy / Berlin mining and geoscience institutions;
4. TU Berlin University Archive only where institutional transfer evidence points there;
5. published catalogues and references documenting the collection after 1884 (including the 1900 specimen-no.-36 reference).

The 1900 citation establishes continued addressability of the physical collection but does not establish its later destination.

## 6. Sources to retain

- P. D. Portella, `Adolf Martens and his contributions to materials engineering`, BAM report, 2006. Relevant pages reproduce the Martens notebook in the BAM library and discuss the 1880 microscope, photographic-plate exposure, glass-plate emulsions and drawing.
- BAM historical image/gallery material reproducing original Martens microscopic drawings from 1878–1882.
- 2025 official historical research-status report stating that the BAM library has an uncatalogued Martens Teilnachlass of uncertain present extent/location.

## 7. Claim ceiling

Secure:
- BAM library holds at least one Martens notebook shown in an official BAM publication.
- BAM preserves original Martens microscopic drawings from his early metallographic work.
- An official 2025 report says BAM library has an uncatalogued Martens Teilnachlass whose full current location and extent are not known.

Open:
- whether the Martens notebook belongs formally to that Teilnachlass;
- whether the Teilnachlass contains documentation of the 1884 120-section collection;
- whether any of the original 120 sections survive at BAM or elsewhere;
- whether BAM's early drawings can be matched specimen-by-specimen to the 120-section catalogue.

# External source expansion — pass 4 — 2026-09-11

Status: controlled external-corpus research. This pass deliberately does **not** process Wedding Mss 23–25 notebook pages; that corpus is reserved for a separate page-level thread.

## 1. Major new sequence: 1885 visual failure → 1888 collective image display

A contemporaneous report in *The Engineer* (22 May 1885, p. 395) on Wedding's Iron and Steel Institute paper, `On the Properties of Malleable Iron Deduced from its Microscopic Structures`, states that the paper consisted largely of descriptions of appearances seen under the microscope and that **no illustrations were shown**. The reporter calls the result unsatisfactory because listeners could not readily draw conclusions from verbal description alone; Bauermann likewise regretted that the author had shown nothing.

This is a first-order statement of a communication/evidence problem:

**microscopic observation by one observer → verbal description alone → weak collective inspectability.**

Source route:

- *The Engineer*, 22 May 1885, p. 395, `THE IRON AND STEEL INSTITUTE`.
- Digital scan: https://repozytorium.biblos.pk.edu.pl/redo/resources/35944/file/scans/OCR_rezultaty/300000015956_A_v1_200dpi_q60.pdf

Three years later, a contemporaneous report on the 1888 Allgemeiner Bergmannstag in Vienna states that Wedding spoke on `Beurtheilung des Eisens aus seinem Kleingefüge` **while showing images in a darkened hall under magnesium light** (`mit Vorweisung von Bildern im verdunkelten Saale bei Magnesiumlicht`). The report says the presentation received lively general approval.

Source route:

- *Oesterreichische Zeitschrift für Berg- und Hüttenwesen*, vol. 36, 15 Sept. 1888, report on the Allgemeiner Bergmannstag in Vienna.
- Digital scan: https://opac.geologie.ac.at/ais312/dokumente/BHW_1888_489.pdf

Controlled interpretation:

> Between 1885 and 1888, Wedding's public microscopic argument moved from a setting where verbal description without displayed images was criticised as inadequate to one where images were deliberately shown to a group under controlled dark-room / magnesium-light conditions.

Do **not** yet call the 1888 method lantern-slide projection or photographic projection. The source currently establishes images + darkened hall + magnesium light, but the projection apparatus/media have not yet been identified.

This is a high-value question for visual epistemology: how did microscopic evidence become collectively inspectable rather than remaining tied to an individual eye at the microscope?

## 2. 1889: image-making and specimen preparation appear in the same title

A key Wedding article is now bibliographically secure:

Hermann Wedding, `Ueber Fortschritte in der Lichtabbildung des Kleingefüges von Eisen und die Herstellung von Schliffen`, *Stahl und Eisen*, vol. IX (1889).

The title itself is analytically important because it joins two operations that should not be separated:

- `Lichtabbildung` of iron microstructure;
- `Herstellung von Schliffen`.

Working bridge:

**prepared section ↔ making the microscopic structure into a light-image.**

Francis Scott Rice's 1897 bibliography independently lists this paper and the companion Martens contribution:

A. Martens, `Ueber die mikroskopische Untersuchung des Kleingefüges von Eisen`, *Stahl und Eisen* IX (1889), pp. 393–396.

The full 1889 *Stahl und Eisen* volume is remotely available through digitised library copies (Google Books/Play / HathiTrust routes surfaced in search), but this pass has not yet pinned Wedding's exact page range or extracted the article itself. That remains an immediate acquisition task.

## 4. Martens' 1884 120-section collection remained textually addressable

The 1884 collection remains central:

A. Martens, *Erläuterungen einer in der kön. Bergakademie zu Berlin befindlichen Sammlung von 120 Schliffen zur Darstellung des mikroskopischen Gefüges verschiedener Eisen und Stahlsorten*. Berlin, 1884.

A first-order discussion in *Stahl und Eisen* (1 Jan. 1900) refers explicitly back to this collection and to **specimen no. 36**. The writer notes that a gilding treatment on this foundry pig-iron specimen produced a bronze-coloured deposit on the softer constituent and a purple colour on the harder constituent, while discussing alternative polishing/etching methods.

Digital source:

https://sbc.org.pl/Content/694321/PDF/si240-1900-01-0001.pdf

Controlled conclusion:

> `No. 36` remained a usable textual/reference address sixteen years after the 1884 publication.

This demonstrates the persistence of the collection's numbered reference structure in metallographic discourse. It does **not** by itself prove that the physical specimen no. 36 still survived in 1900.

The 1884 booklet itself remains a high-priority missing primary source; no open full scan has yet been located.

## 5. The 1884/85 collection was commissioned by Wedding and used as an evidential base

The contemporaneous 1885 English reprint of Wedding's paper states that Martens had **at Wedding's request** prepared a collection of 120 sections for the Royal Mining Academy in Berlin, and that Wedding's subsequent investigation was based on this collection.

The preparation procedure described by Wedding includes:

1. removing small test pieces from larger masses by breaking, planing, filing or crushing;
2. rough grinding on a coarse emery wheel;
3. progressively finer grinding on cast-iron plates with emery;
4. cementing the specimen with resin/wax onto a thick piece of looking-glass;
5. using water during grinding to avoid loosening the cement through heat;
6. hand polishing with washed ferric oxide, putty powder, tripoli and related agents;
7. etching with highly diluted reagents, including platinum chloride and various acids;
8. controlled heating to generate differential colours;
9. in some cases faint gilding by Martens.

Wedding stresses that the **differences of colour**, rather than absolute colours, are characteristic.

The key relation is documentary:

**Wedding request → Martens preparation → 120-section collection → Wedding investigation/public argument.**

This makes the collection part of the evidential infrastructure of the 1885 paper rather than incidental institutional background.

## 6. 1885 Wedding–Sorby disagreement is about the making of valid microscopic evidence

The surviving Sorby specimen study reconstructs Sorby's response to Wedding as centring on preparation and illumination. Sorby argued that important differences between their results could arise because Wedding's sections had not been ground down appropriately with soft stone before the final polish and because direct/vertical illumination mattered.

Wedding, in turn, invited Iron and Steel Institute members to Berlin to inspect the Martens specimen collection supporting his work.

Analytical frame:

**Which combination of section preparation + surface treatment + illumination yields a valid microscopic object?**

The disagreement should therefore not be reduced to competing theories of iron structure.

There is an institutional-location discrepancy still to resolve:

- Wedding's 1885 reprint identifies the 120 sections as prepared for the **Royal Mining Academy of Berlin**;
- a modern Sorby reconstruction paraphrases Wedding's invitation in relation to the **Royal Prussian Mechanical Testing Institute**.

Do not conflate these institutions or infer a transfer of the collection until the original JISI discussion is checked.

## 7. 1886–88: visual publication and collective display intensify

A contemporaneous note in *Dingler's Polytechnisches Journal* reports well-executed colour images (`schön ausgeführte Farbenbilder`) of microscopic structure in a compound armour plate and compares their features with Wedding's 1885 material. It also refers to Wedding's later observations on burnt iron as communicated with excellent illustrations (`an Hand vorzüglicher Abbildungen`) in *Stahl und Eisen* in 1886.

This should be treated as a routing signal for a rapid intensification of visual publication after the poorly illustrated / non-displayed 1885 presentation.

The stronger current sequence is therefore:

- **1884** — Martens 120-section physical collection;
- **1885** — Wedding public microscopic argument; contemporaneous criticism that no illustrations were shown;
- **1886–87** — improved/colour microscopic illustrations appear in technical publication;
- **1888** — Wedding shows images collectively in a darkened hall under magnesium light;
- **1889** — Wedding publishes explicitly on both `Lichtabbildung` and `Herstellung von Schliffen`;
- **1890/91** — `Das Kleingefüge des Eisens`, `Mikroskopische Originalphotographien`, 19 plates (date still to control);
- **1891** — Martens publishes the microscopy equipment of the Königliche mechanisch-technische Versuchsanstalt;
- **1893** — Martens microstructure/Chicago material;
- **1899** — Martens/Heyn reflected-light microphotography work;
- **1904** — Materialprüfungsamt has separate cabinets for characteristic sections and more than 3,600 microphotographs.

This is currently the strongest non-genealogical history of evidential reformatting in the project.

## 8. 1904: physical sections and microphotographs are distinct institutional collections

Martens & Guth's 1904 account of the Königliche Materialprüfungsamt provides a clear institutional endpoint.

In the microscopy room:

- Martens' microphotographic apparatus stands on the east wall and can work with reflected and transmitted light;
- a collection cabinet contains drawers with zinc-sheet desiccators used to preserve `kennzeichnender Schliffe`;
- separate cabinets contain a collection of more than **3,600 Mikrophotographien / Lichtbilder**;
- additional microscopes include Martens/Zeiss and mineralogical/petrographic instruments;
- the darkroom was placed separately to keep the microscopy room dry.

Digital source:

https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/download/pdf/8127916.pdf

This gives a robust later architecture:

**prepared physical reference specimen collection ≠ microphotographic image collection.**

Do not assume the `kennzeichnender Schliffe` of 1904 are identical to the 1884 120-section collection.

## 12. Revised external-corpus question

The current sequence is no longer adequately described as `industrial travel → microscopy`.

A more precise question is:

> **How did metallurgical evidence become progressively more shareable, inspectable and addressable as it moved from prepared material surfaces to numbered specimen collections, public images, photographs and separate institutional image archives?**

The crucial operations are:

`SELECT → CUT/BREAK → GRIND → POLISH → ETCH/COLOUR → NUMBER → COLLECT → ILLUMINATE → DISPLAY → PHOTOGRAPH → REPRODUCE → ARCHIVE`.

The history concerns not only seeing structure, but engineering the conditions under which different observers could inspect, compare, dispute and revisit the same material claim.

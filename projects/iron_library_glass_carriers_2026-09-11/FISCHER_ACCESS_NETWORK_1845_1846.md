# Fischer 1845–1846 — access / testing / mediation network

Status: active working note, 2026-09-11.

Purpose: do not treat Fischer's named contacts as a generic social network. Code each person by the specific epistemic function they perform: opening a site, authorising access, testing material, judging quality, supplying information, transforming a sample, or converting an industrial process into a portable image.

Source basis: supplied machine-readable Fischer 1845 and 1846 texts. OCR/research texts only; not manually verified diplomatic transcriptions.

## Coding fields for later Wedding comparison

For every named person, record:

- date / place;
- person;
- institution / firm;
- prior relationship, recommendation or introduction;
- access function;
- what became visible / touchable / measurable because of that access;
- material object exchanged or tested;
- judgement / information produced;
- resulting onward introduction or route.

## High-value Fischer nodes

### John Gott — Leeds / Low Moor access logistics

Function: trusted old industrial/social contact; logistical broker.

Fischer wants to visit Low Moor while also seeing Gott. Gott makes both possible: he supplies a clerk to escort Fischer to the correct coach connection and organises the timing that gets him to Low Moor and back.

Analytical value: access to industrial observation is partly a product of older personal relations and logistical coordination, not simply of the traveller arriving at a factory gate.

### Low Moor recommendation -> Dawson / works proprietor

Function: formal recommendation converted into deep works access.

The recommendation gives Fischer a proprietor-level host who personally conducts him through the works for roughly two hours and supplies technical explanations plus a staged series of ore, limestone, coal, coke and iron samples.

Analytical value: the quality of the resulting observation is inseparable from the status and technical competence of the guide.

### Faraday — Royal Institution

Function: scientific interlocutor and material recipient.

In August 1845 Fischer visits Faraday at the Royal Institution. Faraday examines Fischer's cast-wrought-iron objects, a platinum piece melted from scraps in Fischer's experimental crucible, and related material. Fischer gives Faraday a similar experimental crucible for his use.

Analytical value: industrial objects and experimental apparatus move into a scientific-institutional relationship; the visit is not only conversation.

### Thomas William Brande — Royal Mint

Function: external testing authority, customer and credibility source.

Brande / the Royal Mint tests Fischer's steel bar, produces a favourable written judgement, requests additional bars of specified dimensions and discusses future supply. Fischer later uses Brande's letter when speaking with Sheffield manufacturers.

Analytical value: a portable material sample acquires an institutionally powerful written evaluation that can travel onward and alter subsequent industrial encounters.

### Im Thurn — London broker / institutional-social connector

Function: recurring London contact involved in railway business, introductions and practical navigation.

Im Thurn accompanies Fischer to the Register Office in 1845 and remains part of the London network through the 1846 journey.

### Faraday + Brande as access credentials — Croydon atmospheric railway

Function: reputational credential.

When Fischer seeks access to the Croydon atmospheric-railway Engine House, he names Faraday and Brande among his London friends. The person he is speaking with then gives him a card for the engineer Gregory. Gregory subsequently authorises railway staff to admit Fischer to the Engine House whenever he wishes.

Analytical value: named scientific/institutional relationships are convertible into technical access. The network does epistemic work.

This is exactly the kind of relation that must be tested in the Wedding notebooks: not merely 'who did he meet?' but 'whose name opened which door?'

### Sanderson — Sheffield

Function: long-standing industrial contact, factory access and quantitative comparator.

Sanderson gives Fischer extensive access to the cementation/cast-steel works, provides production data and later invites continued discussion. The coke-consumption comparison produces the reported `You beat us!` exchange.

### Huntsman family / works

Function: established Sheffield relationship and reciprocal viewing of works representations.

Fischer shows the Huntsman family Beck's coloured drawings of his Schaffhausen works; he is then invited into the hammer works and discusses changed furnace arrangements.

Analytical value: drawings of Fischer's own works operate as social and technical exchange objects.

### Rodgers & Son — Sheffield

Function: external skilled finishing + expert quality judgement.

Fischer supplies his own hardened razor-blade blanks. Rodgers & Son grind, polish and fit them, then provide a favourable judgement of their quality.

### Cammell & Co. — Sheffield

Function: industrial comparator / reciprocal technical discussion.

Fischer enters through a recommendation and then evaluates Cammell practice, including criticising a patented proposal based on his own experience.

### unnamed steel melter — Sheffield

Function: shop-floor technical authority.

While waiting for proprietors, Fischer holds an extended technical discussion with a melter about casting behaviour, crucible damage and plugs. The worker takes him into the crucible-making workshop and demonstrates manufacture directly.

Analytical value: epistemic authority is not confined to owners/engineers. Shop-floor workers can become the most technically informative guides.

### Butterley: Goodwin / absent Jessop / counting-house clerk

Function: negative control on access quality.

A recommendation gets Fischer into Butterley, but the intended technical director Jessop is absent. Goodwin assigns a young counting-house clerk as guide. Fischer explicitly doubts the reliability of the clerk's technical information.

Analytical value: formal admission is not equivalent to epistemically adequate access.

### Willat — optician / photographic-material supplier

Function: photographic materials and technical advice.

Fischer purchases photographic paper and receives information about different processes and their ease/reliability.

### Croucher — photographer

Function: image transformation and multiplication.

Croucher converts Beck's large coloured works drawing into a photographic negative and attempts multiple positive reproductions; weather and scale affect the result.

Analytical value: Croucher is a media operator in the comparison circuit, turning a site-specific industrial representation into potentially reproducible portable images.

## Network types now visible

The Fischer corpus distinguishes at least six functional networks:

1. **introduction/access network** — recommendations, cards, old acquaintances;
2. **technical explanation network** — owners, engineers, workers and guides;
3. **material test network** — Royal Mint, skilled Sheffield finishers, works test apparatus;
4. **sample circulation network** — Fischer's own steel/cast iron plus British ore/fuel/crucible specimens;
5. **representation network** — Beck drawings, Willat materials, Croucher photographic reproduction;
6. **credibility network** — Brande/Faraday names and institutional judgements become reusable credentials elsewhere.

## Wedding coding rule

When Wedding Mss 23–25 are indexed, every person should be tagged not only by identity but by function:

`introducer | gatekeeper | host | technical guide | worker | tester | judge | sample donor | correspondent | image-maker | institutional credential`

A single person may have multiple roles.

This should allow a direct comparison of whether Wedding's 1860–62 British journey relies on the same access architecture or a different, more institutionalised one.

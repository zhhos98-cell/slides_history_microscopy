## 1. Why the governing concept changes

The first synthesis used **reference-frame portability** as the governing variable. The exact-source and object-level afterlife passes show that this remains correct but is not yet precise enough.

A finder address was never automatically portable merely because it was numerical, external to the microscope, or printed on a standard slide. Portability had to be produced and maintained through:

- standard dimensions;
- reproducible slide placement;
- reference edges;
- orientation conventions;
- common or translatable graduations;
- stable stage/carrier geometry;
- local secondary marks;
- calibration among non-identical finder instances;
- documentary preservation of the reading convention.

The stronger term is therefore:

**calibrated portability**

or, when more explicit:

**reference-frame portability under calibration**.

---

## 2. The formation episode, 1855–1858

The early sources now reveal a compact technical debate rather than a single invention sequence.

### Bailey, 1855

Bailey identifies the defect of finders useful only on a particular microscope and explicitly wants a record independent of the original instrument and observer, suitable for a distant correspondent and for the lasting utility of collections.

The central operation is:

`instrument-specific locality -> transferable recorded locality`.

### Amyot, 1856

Amyot makes the transport problem materially explicit. Finder/carrier devices could also function as slide packing cases and be posted to correspondents. He therefore joins physical slide carriage and cross-microscope location recovery within one technical discussion.

### Microscopical Society Committee on Finders, 1856; Edwards, 1857

The Society converts individual experimentation into collective design criteria: a useful finder should work with different microscopes, avoid destructive or cumbersome registration, remain legible under ordinary use, and be cheap/simple.

Edwards then keys his design to the Society-recommended 3 × 1 inch slide.

Thus standard slide geometry becomes part of the relocation infrastructure.

### Maltwood, 1858

Maltwood relocates the reference frame into a substitute gridded slide. This does not eliminate dependence on geometry; it shifts that dependence from a single instrument to reproducible relations among specimen slide, finder slide, stage position, reference edges and orientation.

### Formation result

The 1850s problem can be stated as:

> Where should the spatial reference reside if a microscopic locality is to survive slide removal, posting, reinsertion, a different microscope, and a different observer?

That is already an historical problem of portability.

---

## 3. Portability becomes a collective engineering specification

The 1856 Committee on Finders matters because it shows that `universality` was not merely a retrospective label attached to successful devices.

Microscopists articulated design requirements for universality and usability before Maltwood became the durable named solution.

Those requirements include two different scales:

### Geometrical universality

Can the reference be reconstructed on another microscope or stage?

### Operational usability

Will the observer actually register a transient object while working, or is the procedure so disruptive that the location is lost before the address is made?

This second dimension is historically important. A theoretically exact address system can fail epistemically if its workflow cost prevents routine use.

---

## 4. Universality remains conditional in the afterlife

The Greville/Gregory material studied by Danielidis and Mann in 2002 prevents a triumphalist account in which Maltwood simply solves portability.

Historical Maltwood references could recover the exact individuals Greville had drawn, but the modern researchers found systematic differences between finder instances and used an empirical coordinate correction to translate the historical frame into their available finder.

Slide width and locating convention could alter the offset.

The important chain is therefore:

`historical address -> systematic discrepancy -> calibration/translation -> reconstructed locality -> exact historical individual`.

This shows that portability is maintained through **translation among reference frames**.

A finder does not abolish local geometry. It makes local geometry regular enough to be communicated, compared and, where necessary, calibrated.

---

## 5. Re-addressing is a historical operation

The branch now controls two especially strong cases of reference-frame migration.

### Bailey collection

`Bailey Universal Indicator address -> later Maltwood Finder address`

A collection created under one spatial convention is re-described under another.

### Greville/Gregory material

`historical Maltwood coordinate -> empirical modern correction -> recovered historical specimen`.

An old address is translated into a later physical reference frame.

These examples suggest that **re-addressing** should be treated as a scientific and curatorial operation in its own right.

A historical preparation can remain materially intact while its usable locality system changes. Re-addressing restores or migrates the relation that makes a particular microscopic individual recoverable.

---

## 6. Direct marking is not the opposite of finder technology; it is another portability strategy

The late-century source closure makes the contrast sharper.

Instrument- or stage-bound coordinates can be numerically precise but vulnerable to changes in geometry and zero.

Direct marking systems associated with Bridgman, Winkel, Fuess and others move the reference onto the preparation itself. A circle on the coverslip travels with the slide and therefore survives a change of microscope more readily than an arbitrary stage number.

But modern work on Gregory/Greville slides also shows the limitation: a diamond circle can preserve a region without preserving a fine-grained coordinate. Later researchers may still need a new benchmark and x/y system.

The relevant comparison is therefore not:

`primitive mark -> advanced numerical finder`.

It is:

`different reference architectures -> different survival properties under displacement`.

---

## 7. Klönne & Müller clarifies the persistence of instrument-bound solutions

The exact 1886 Bacteria Finder description proves that even after Maltwood, sophisticated instrument/carrier-bound coordinate systems continued to be developed.

Its two graduated motions, vernier and systematic strip-search procedure are not a failed precursor awaiting replacement by a universal grid. They optimize another task: exhaustive, precise search of minute objects within a controlled apparatus.

This matters because it breaks any linear story:

`stage finder -> Maltwood -> universal portability`.

Instead nineteenth-century microscopists continued to choose among:

- precision within a controlled mechanical frame;
- transferability through an external indicator;
- substitution-grid address;
- direct material marking;
- optical transfer.

No single architecture maximized every property.

---

## 8. Revised master argument

### Short version

> The microscope slide transported microscopic material; finder systems transported microscopic locality, but locality remained portable only while its reference frame could be reconstructed, calibrated or translated.

### Article-level version

> Nineteenth-century microscopic relocation was a problem of calibrated portability. Microscopists placed spatial reference in stages, carriers, transferable indicators, substitute finder slides, optical systems, or the preparation itself. Each architecture made some relations durable while leaving others vulnerable to changes of instrument, observer, orientation, scale and custody. The history of finders is therefore less a succession of locating gadgets than a history of constructing, maintaining and migrating portable microscopic addresses.

### Larger Slides-project version

> A microscope slide could circulate physically without carrying a recoverable observation. Standardized glass, labels and packing made preparations transportable; finder, indicator, marking and coordinate systems supplied the additional spatial infrastructure through which another observer could recover a particular locality within them.

## 9. Relation to `double fixing`

The repository’s broader `double fixing` claim can now be sharpened at microscopic scale.

### First fixing

Material is stabilized on glass.

### Spatial fixing

A relation between the preparation and a particular locality is stabilized through a coordinate, grid, indicator or mark.

### Re-addressing

When the original reference regime is lost, superseded or physically non-identical, the locality relation is translated into another reference frame.

Thus durability is not one act. It can be a sequence:

`fix material -> address locality -> displace -> calibrate/re-address -> observe again`.

---

## 10. A useful distinction for future writing: transport vs recoverability

The strongest bridge to a future `Transporting Slides` project is now exact:

### Physical transport asks

Can the preparation be moved without breakage, contamination or incompatibility?

### Observational transport asks

Can another observer recover the same scientifically significant locality?

The latter requires more than a portable piece of glass.

The Bailey/Amyot cluster demonstrates that nineteenth-century actors themselves connected slide exchange, correspondence and locality recovery. Edwards then connects that recovery to standardized slide dimensions. Maltwood creates a durable substitute-grid architecture. Greville’s afterlife shows that the address still requires calibration across generations of reference objects.

This is a complete formation-to-afterlife chain.

## 12. Historiographical claim after closure

The safest and strongest current formulation remains:

> Existing scholarship has studied microscope slides as material and epistemic objects, circulation and knowledge infrastructure, embodied microscopic practice, early American microscopy, and particular historical finder devices. In the bounded literature search completed here, no dedicated HPS synthesis was recovered that makes exact microscopic relocation, competing reference frames, calibration, and historical re-addressing its organizing problem.

This is a bounded search result, not a universal negative.

The originality lies in the **relation among cases and the problem they collectively expose**, not in claiming individual devices are unknown.

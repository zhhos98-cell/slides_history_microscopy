## 1. Klönne & Müller, 1886 — exact mechanism closed

### Source

*Journal of the Royal Microscopical Society* (1886), p. 127, description of **Klönne and Müller’s Pendulum Object-frame or Bacteria-finder**.

### Mechanism

The apparatus was devised to make very small objects readily findable and was described as adaptable to any microscope. It held the specimen slide in a movable frame and traversed the preparation through **two graduated motions**:

- a pendulum/swinging motion about a fixed point for one axis;
- a traversing screw for the other axis.

Position was read through:

- graduations associated with the circular slot governing the pendulum movement;
- a millimetre scale with vernier for the transverse movement.

The recommended search procedure was effectively raster-like: inspect a vertical strip using the pendulum motion, shift laterally by approximately one field width, then inspect the next strip.

### Classification

**Instrument/carrier-bound two-axis coordinate architecture.**

The Bacteria Finder does not mark the specimen directly. Its address is generated by the geometry and graduations of a mechanical carrier/frame.

### Evidence grade

**S — exact contemporaneous mechanism description.**

### Important guard

A later German technical discussion groups a Klönne & Müller apparatus with direct coverslip-marking systems. That reference appears to point to a different device or earlier apparatus family and should **not** be merged automatically with the 1886 Pendulum Object-frame/Bacteria Finder.

Current controlled distinction:

- `Klönne & Müller 1886 Bacteria Finder` = graduated mechanical coordinate frame;
- `Klönne & Müller c.1884 marking apparatus` = possible direct-marking family, identity/mechanism not yet closed.

Do not collapse these under one generic maker label.

## 2. A late-century critique of instrument-bound coordinates

A German technical discussion from the late 1890s gives unusually explicit reasons why stage- or stand-attached arbitrary scales can fail as portable references.

It notes that:

- different microscopes place the preparation differently relative to the optical axis;
- even on the same microscope, removing and remounting an apparatus can disturb the relation;
- arbitrary scale numbers produced on one adjustable stage have no necessary meaning on another mechanical system.

The source then contrasts such systems with apparatus associated with **Klönne & Müller, Schiefferdecker-Winkel, and Fuess** that mark the relevant locality on the coverslip, using ink or a diamond-drawn circle.

### Analytical consequence

This source articulates the late-century portability problem in actor-level technical terms:

`instrument-attached arbitrary coordinate` vs `mark carried materially by the preparation`.

The two architectures solve different problems:

- a mechanical coordinate frame can provide high-resolution numerical search;
- a direct mark is less dependent on preserving the original instrument geometry because the reference travels with the slide.

Direct marking is not automatically superior. Ink may disappear; a circle can provide only a region rather than a fine coordinate; and future observers may still need orientation or a secondary local reference.

### Evidence grade

**S/A for the critique as a contemporary technical comparison; case-specific grades remain separate.**

## 3. Fuess Object Finder, 1895 — classification upgraded, exact mechanics capped

### Controlled evidence

The Royal Microscopical Society apparatus/maker index securely identifies a **Fuess Object Finder** in 1895, p. 587.

A near-contemporary German technical source, discussing methods for recovering exact preparation localities, cites Fuess and places the device in a family that marks the relevant place on the coverslip with a circle in ink or with a diamond.

It cites contemporary 1895 references for the Fuess apparatus, including *Neues Jahrbuch für Mineralogie* and the *Zeitschrift für wissenschaftliche Mikroskopie*.

### Classification

**Direct material marking / coverslip target marking**, provisionally but strongly.

This now has enough evidence to move Fuess out of the earlier unresolved generic `object finder` bucket.

### Evidence grade

**A — identity is secure and near-contemporary mechanism-family classification is strong; the original JRMS p. 587 description has not been recovered in full in the current pass.**

### Evidence ceiling

Do **not** infer:

- exact arm geometry;
- exact diamond-holder construction;
- whether the circle was inscribed through a particular mechanical trajectory;
- dimensions or adjustment range;
- identity with any surviving Fuess object-marker unless object-level provenance establishes it.

The missing original p. 587 is now a **writing-detail residual**, not a discovery gap. Reopen only if a future draft requires an exact figure or mechanical reconstruction.

## 4. Klein II, 1888 — selection + marking link closed at the correct level

### Article identity

Ludwig Klein, “Beiträge zur Technik mikroskopischer Dauerpräparate von Süsswasseralgen II,” *Zeitschrift für wissenschaftliche Mikroskopie und für mikroskopische Technik* 5 (1888), beginning p. 456.

Bibliographic sources vary slightly over the terminal page; this does not affect the present mechanism claim.

### Contemporary review evidence

A review in *Hedwigia* 28 (1889) summarizes Klein’s continuation and explicitly reports two relevant elements:

- recommendation of **Winkel’s Markirapparat**;
- instructions for selecting **individual forms from mixtures**.

This is contemporaneous evidence that Klein’s permanent-preparation technique joined individual selection to a marking apparatus.

### Apparatus-family mechanism

Later Winkel/Zeiss catalogue evidence for the `Objektmarkierapparat` family describes a diamond used to scratch a circle on the coverslip around a desired microscopic locality so that it could subsequently be found again.

### Defensible chain

`mixed material -> selected individual form -> use/recommendation of Winkel marking apparatus -> materially marked locality -> later refinding`

### Evidence grade

- **S/A** for Klein II article identity and the 1889 review’s selection/marking statement;
- **A** for applying the later documented Winkel apparatus-family principle to interpret the kind of operation involved;
- **not S** for the exact 1888 mechanical details, because Klein II full text was not successfully recovered/read in this pass.

### Guard

Do not quote or paraphrase unexamined Klein 1888 prose as if directly read. Do not assert that every detail of the later Zeiss/Winkel apparatus was already present in the 1888 version.

The residual is now an **exact-wording/mechanical-detail residual only**.

## 5. The late-century field did not converge on one universal solution

The evidence now supports coexistence rather than a simple succession:

### Mechanical coordinate route

Examples:

- Okeden/Wright-type stage coordinates;
- Smith-type carrier coordinates;
- Klönne & Müller 1886 Bacteria Finder;
- Grunow’s controlled millimetre/coordinate practice.

Strength: numerical precision and systematic search.

Weakness: reference can remain bound to a particular geometry, zero, carrier, mounting relation or calibration.

### Material marking route

Examples:

- Bridgman;
- Francotte;
- Winkel;
- Fuess;
- marked Gregory/Greville coverslips in surviving collections.

Strength: the reference travels physically with the preparation and does not require the original mechanical coordinate frame.

Weakness: local precision may be coarse; marks can fade, be obscured or lose their interpretive key; later re-benchmarking may still be required.

### Substitute finder route

Maltwood and descendants place the reference in a removable standard grid rather than in the microscope or specimen itself.

Strength: potentially transferable among instruments.

Weakness: transfer still depends on common geometry, edge conventions, orientation and calibration among finder instances.

### Result

There is no single nineteenth-century transition from an inferior to a superior finder. Instead there is a persistent engineering choice about **where to locate the reference frame** and therefore **which kind of displacement it is meant to survive**.

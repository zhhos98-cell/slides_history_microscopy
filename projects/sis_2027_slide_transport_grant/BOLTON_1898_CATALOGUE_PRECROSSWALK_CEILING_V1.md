## 1. Controlled archive architecture

Archive-supplied finding aid establishes:

- `FZ/14/3/1` — Register of Slides Lent Out, May 1882–Jul 1887;
- `FZ/14/3/2` — *Catalogue of Slides, Journals, and Books belonging to the Society*, 1898 (printed).

The same collection also contains:
- `FZ/14/1/1` — General and Committee Minutes, 1 Aug 1877–29 Oct 1880;
- `FZ/14/1/2` — General and Committee Minutes, 5 Nov 1880–8 Feb 1900;
- `FZ/14/2/1` — Account and Subscription Book, 1880–1894.

## 2. Public evidence immediately before the lending register

Contemporary reporting in *The Microscopical News and Northern Microscopist* states that in summer 1881:

- the Society's slide collection was being re-arranged because of recent additions;
- Mrs Redmayne gave six dozen selected slides from the late John Thomas Redmayne's private collection;
- she placed the whole private collection in the hands of the Society President and Secretary so that they could select the most suitable slides;
- forty-eight choice diatom slides were also added;
- Society slides were lent to members for study.

This provides a direct chronological bridge into the lending register beginning May 1882.

## 3. Correction boundary

An earlier planning note incorrectly treated a twelve-slide Echinodermata series from F. W. Sharpus via W. R. Hughes as a Bolton accession anchor.

That series belongs to the **Birmingham Natural History and Microscopical Society**, not Bolton.

Therefore:
- Sharpus / Hughes / Echinodermata must not appear in the Bolton pre-field crosswalk;
- do not search `FZ/14` for Sharpus unless a separate Bolton-specific source independently appears;
- Bolton's controlled intake sequence begins with the Redmayne 1880–81 estate/display/selection material and the forty-eight diatom-slide addition.

## 4. Search result for the 1898 printed catalogue

A targeted public-web search for:

- `FZ/14/3/2`;
- Bolton Microscopical Society + 1898 + slides;
- Bolton library/Spydus references;

did **not** recover a digitised copy, transcription, OCR, or dependable item-level index of the 1898 catalogue.

The catalogue should therefore be treated as an **archive-only controlled target** at present.

Do not infer before inspection:
- the numbering system;
- whether maker names are recorded systematically;
- whether provenance/donor names are preserved;
- whether Redmayne's seventy-two selected slides are grouped or individually identifiable;
- whether the forty-eight diatom slides form a numbered block;
- whether numbers used in the 1882–87 lending register map directly to the 1898 catalogue.

## 5. The actual crosswalk to perform on site

The fieldwork task is not generic transcription of two documents.

### Step A — photograph `FZ/14/3/1` completely

Capture all lending transactions before attempting interpretation.

Fields:
- entry/page;
- date out;
- borrower;
- slide number/address;
- subject/taxon;
- maker/donor if present;
- quantity;
- return date;
- duration;
- annotations;
- custodian/officer signatures;
- missing/damage/replacement language.

### Step B — establish the register's address grammar

Determine whether lending entries use:
- sequential slide numbers;
- cabinet/drawer coordinates;
- series numbers;
- maker + subject only;
- catalogue page/item references;
- mixed address systems.

This must be learned from the register itself.

### Step C — sample diagnostic addresses into `FZ/14/3/2`

Prioritise:
1. repeatedly borrowed items;
2. unusually long loans;
3. entries with annotations;
4. diatom series;
5. items plausibly attributable to Redmayne;
6. any entry connected by minutes/accounts to acquisition or intervention.

For each diagnostic item record:
- same number/address present in 1898?;
- translated/new number?;
- maker retained?;
- subject retained/changed?;
- donor/provenance retained?;
- series relation retained?;
- evidence of survival versus merely catalogue persistence.

### Step D — reverse from 1898 into lending

Select several distinctive 1898 entries and search backwards through the lending register.

This tests whether the catalogue represents:
- a genuinely circulating historical collection;
- later additions that never appear in the register;
- renumbered older items;
- or a substantially reconstituted collection.

## 6. Redmayne-specific test

The strongest potential Bolton chain is:

`Redmayne private collection`
→ `whole collection temporarily placed with President + Secretary`
→ `72 slides selected`
→ `Society collection re-arranged`
→ `slides lent to members`
→ `named/numbered Redmayne slides in 1882–87 register`
→ `same or translated identities in 1898 catalogue`.

At present the first four steps are historically controlled and the archive structure needed for the last three is controlled.

The object-level identity across the lending register and 1898 catalogue remains **OPEN**.

## 7. Forty-eight-diatom test

The March/summer 1881 printed sequence records forty-eight choice diatom slides being added to the Society cabinet. Their donor/source remains open.

This is the second bounded intake group for fieldwork.

Test:
- resolve donor/source in `FZ/14/1/2` or accounts;
- determine whether a forty-eight-slide group/block received numbering or a cabinet address;
- test whether diatom entries appear as a coherent or dispersed lending group in `FZ/14/3/1`;
- test whether any stable or translated addresses persist in the 1898 catalogue.

Do not collapse the forty-eight diatom slides into the seventy-two Redmayne slides without explicit internal evidence.

## 8. Grant-method payoff

The absence of a public digital copy strengthens rather than weakens the justification for travel.

The funded Bolton visit has a precise non-duplicable task:

**reconstruct how one society converted a re-arranged slide collection into a lending system, then test whether loaned objects remained identifiable in its later printed catalogue.**

This is not research that can currently be completed from digitised sources.

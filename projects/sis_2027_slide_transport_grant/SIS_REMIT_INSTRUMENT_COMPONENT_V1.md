# SIS remit — microscope preparations as working components in an instrument ecology v1

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: GRANT-FRAMING AUTHORITY / AVOID ONTOLOGICAL OVERCLAIM

## 1. Purpose

The SIS application should not spend scarce prose arguing abstractly that `a microscope slide is a scientific instrument`.

The safer historical claim is narrower and better supported:

**Nineteenth-century microscopists, instrument makers and scientific-apparatus exhibitions treated microscopes, prepared microscopic objects, cabinets and documentary keys as interdependent parts of practical microscopy.**

The project studies the prepared slide as a **detachable working component within that historical instrument ecology**.

This keeps the proposal squarely within history-of-scientific-instruments questions without requiring an ahistorical ontology of `instrument`.

## 2. 1862 International Exhibition — instrument maker display joins microscopes, objects and cabinets

Howard Lynk's 2012 reconstruction of Smith, Beck & Beck's transparent injections gives page-specific contemporary routes to the 1862 International Exhibition.

### Official classification

Smith, Beck & Beck were entered as exhibitor 2964 in:

`Class 13 — Philosophical Instruments, and Processes Depending On Their Use`.

Primary catalogue route cited by Lynk:
*International Exhibition 1862 Official Catalogue — Industrial Department* (London: Truscott, Son, & Simmons, 1862), p. 48.

### Contemporary guide description

Robert Hunt's contemporary exhibition guide described the Smith, Beck & Beck case as combining:
- binocular and single microscopes;
- telescopes;
- object glasses;
- stereoscopes;
- **microscopic objects and cabinets**;
- a public `museum microscope` with **504 objects** attached for viewing.

Primary route cited by Lynk:
Robert Hunt, *A Complete Guide to the Contents of the International Exhibition of 1862*, 3rd ed. (London: Edward Stanford, 1862), p. 88.

Evidence status:
The page-specific contemporary references are secure bibliographic routes, but this project pass has not yet directly inspected Hunt p. 88 from a primary scan. Use the exact wording in publication only after direct page verification; for grant framing, paraphrase the documented configuration rather than overquote.

### Why this matters

The relevant historical configuration is not:

`instrument` versus `specimen`.

It is a commercial/exhibition system in which an instrument maker presented:

`microscope + object glass + prepared microscopic object + cabinet + viewing arrangement`.

The prepared objects were not incidental decorative holdings. They were sold, selected, mounted, demonstrated and viewed through instruments supplied by the same makers.

## 3. 1876 South Kensington Special Loan Collection of Scientific Apparatus — direct catalogue evidence

The official exhibition was explicitly catalogued as:

*Catalogue of the Special Loan Collection of Scientific Apparatus at the South Kensington Museum* (2nd ed., HMSO, 1876).

The catalogue directly places microscopes and prepared microscopic collections inside the same scientific-apparatus exhibition ecology.

### Consecutive official entries, p. 869

Under material loaned by the Royal Prussian Chief Mining Department, Breslau:

- **4218 — School Microscope**, from the Optical Institute of F. W. Schick, Berlin, with two eye-pieces and three object-glasses.
- **4219 — Collection of Forty Microscopical Preparations**, from the Institute of Rodig, Hamburg, with explanations by Prof. Dr. Ferdinand Cohn.

The catalogue then explains that the preparation collection served, with other teaching aids, elementary public-school instruction in the Prussian mining districts in physical and mechanical sciences and preparation for mining/engine work.

## 4. Möller 1868 — preparation + key can function as a reference apparatus

The main repository already directly controls the 1868 *Science-Gossip* report on J. D. Möller's `Diatomaceen Typen Platte`, exhibited at the Quekett Microscopical Club.

The described system is:
- an ordinary 3 × 1 inch glass slide;
- hundreds of diatoms arranged in a stable spatial order of squares/lines/positions;
- an accompanying book/key translating each spatial position into a taxonomic name.

Repository authority:
`data/analysis/moller_typenplatte_1868_print_key_reference_address.md`.

Analytical consequence:
The scientific function is carried by the coupled arrangement:

`prepared glass object ↔ stable spatial sequence ↔ external written key ↔ microscope use`.

If spatial order or key relation is lost, reference function is impaired even if the glass and specimens remain materially present.

The phrase `spatial reference apparatus` is the project's analytical description, not a claim that the 1868 author literally called the slide an apparatus.

## 5. Why the SIS project fits instrument history

The application can therefore frame its subject as an instrument-use and instrument-maintenance problem:

- prepared slides were made to fit microscopes and cabinet geometries;
- slides could be remounted when their carriers were incompatible with local formats;
- instrument makers sold/exhibited microscopes and prepared objects together;
- society cabinets organised preparations for repeated use with members' microscopes;
- temporary lending moved the same prepared object between different microscopes and custodians;
- labels, cabinet positions, registers and keys helped restore a returned object to subsequent scientific use.

The research question is thus not simply the history of a specimen collection.

It asks how a **detachable, reusable component of microscopical practice** was kept compatible with instruments, cabinets and users under changing custody.

## 6. Preferred grant language

### Strong and safe

`Prepared microscope slides occupied an unusual position between scientific specimens, collection objects and instrument components. Nineteenth-century instrument makers and scientific exhibitions routinely presented prepared objects alongside microscopes, object glasses and cabinets, while microscopical societies organised slides for repeated use with members' instruments. This project investigates the material and institutional practices that kept such preparations usable as they were remounted, catalogued, lent, returned and re-entered into society collections.`

### Shorter version

`The project treats the prepared microscope slide as a detachable working component of nineteenth-century microscopy, examining how societies kept slides compatible, identifiable and reusable as they moved between microscopes, cabinets and custodians.`

## 7. Language to avoid

Avoid categorical formulations such as:
- `microscope slides are scientific instruments`;
- `the slide was universally regarded as an instrument`;
- `Victorians classified slides as apparatus`.

The evidence supports integration within instrument practice/exhibitions, not a universal historical taxonomy.

Prefer:
- `working component of microscopy`;
- `instrument component` where context makes microscope use explicit;
- `prepared microscopic object` when preserving actors' vocabulary;
- `instrument ecology` as an analytical shorthand, not an actors' term.

## 8. SIS-specific payoff

This framing makes the archive programme legible to a scientific-instrument audience:

### Oxford RMS/HSM
Tests how a major instrument/microscopy society converted incoming preparations into durable cabinet/register addresses and how those objects relate to a surviving instrument-history collection.

### Bolton
Tests transaction-level movement of prepared slides out of a society cabinet into members' custody and back into use with their own microscopes.

### Quekett
Shows a mature cabinet of >2,000 freely circulating preparations functioning as a comparative/reference resource, while contemporaries recognised that the finished object alone did not carry all maker knowledge.

### Manchester
Tests the prehistory of circulating cabinets and a possible direct link to Thomas Armstrong, a Deansgate optical-instrument maker.

The grant therefore supports research on how scientific-instrument practice depended upon maintaining small prepared components across repeated material and institutional movement.

## 9. Evidence guard

The historical record is strongest when it describes **relations of use** rather than taxonomic labels.

The application should therefore demonstrate SIS relevance through what preparations did with microscopes, cabinets, makers, registers and users—not through a definitional claim about what category a slide `really was`.

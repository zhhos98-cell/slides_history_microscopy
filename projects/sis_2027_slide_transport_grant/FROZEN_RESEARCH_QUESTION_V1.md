# Frozen research question v1

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: QUESTION FROZEN / ANSWER REMAINS ARCHIVAL

## 1. Core question

**How were nineteenth-century microscope preparations kept usable and retrievable as they moved between carriers, cabinets and custodians?**

Expanded empirical form:

**What had to be changed, and what had to remain stable, when a microscope preparation was remounted, selected into a society collection, re-arranged in a cabinet, lent to another user, returned, and re-entered into collection order?**

This is now the governing question for the SIS satellite project. New sources may change the answer and case weighting, but should not reopen the project into a generic history of slide circulation.

## 2. Unit of analysis

Do not assume `the slide` is one indivisible object.

Historically relevant components/relations can include:
- specimen / prepared object;
- glass carrier;
- cover glass;
- mounting medium / cell / cement;
- label and maker attribution;
- cabinet position / number;
- catalogue or register address;
- temporary custodian / borrower;
- relation to text, drawing or reference series.

The research asks which of these actors treated as replaceable, and which had to remain or be translated for continued scientific use.

## 3. Why mobility still matters

The topic is not `transport` as logistics.

Movement matters because it changes material and institutional setting:
- imported slides may not fit a local cabinet and are remounted;
- circulating objects enter temporary custody;
- private collections are selected and re-arranged before becoming society property;
- society slides leave cabinets, enter members' homes and return;
- postal failure can separate glass slip from specimen/object;
- registers and catalogues make returning objects retrievable again.

Mobility is therefore the condition that makes reformatting, custody and return visible.

## 4. Secure pre-archive propositions

### Richardson 1862
A preparation could retain scientific identity after the original glass carrier was discarded and the specimen remounted for compatibility with another slide/cabinet format.

Historical fact is known in prior scholarship; this project uses it as an object-boundary problem rather than claiming discovery of the episode.

### Butterworth 1865
A circulating cabinet could build temporary custody and periodic replacement into its operating procedure.

Guard: Butterworth writes `objects`; do not make all contents glass slides by inference.

### Bolton 1880–87
The Bolton Microscopical Society did not merely possess slides. Contemporary reporting describes cabinet additions, selection from Redmayne's private collection, re-arrangement and lending to members; its surviving archive contains a dedicated `Register of Slides Lent Out, May 1882–Jul 1887` (`FZ/14/3/1`).

This makes society lending/custody directly testable at transaction level.

### Quekett 1879
A mature scientific club described a >2,000-slide cabinet whose preparations were freely circulated, while explicitly recognising that the finished object did not automatically carry the maker's knowledge of its origin and preparation.

### Oxford RMS
A major society collection, historical catalogues/registers, internal governance/financial records and a very large surviving cabinet exist together, but event-to-object and historical-to-modern address crosswalks remain open.

## 5. Historiographic exclusions

Do not claim novelty for:
- slides moving through postal networks;
- packing and safe transmission;
- postal breakage or mended slides;
- standardisation enabling exchange;
- mobility affecting scientific instruments generally;
- repair destabilising object identity generally;
- Richardson's German injections or their non-standard size as newly discovered facts.

These territories are already substantially occupied by Beiermann, Schaffer, Schickore, Baker, Lynk and related work.

## 6. Distinct contribution sought

The project shifts from **circulation as transmission** to **circulation as changing custody and material setting**.

It asks how ordinary scientific societies made heterogeneous preparations:
- selectable;
- cabinet-compatible;
- cataloguable;
- lendable;
- returnable;
- retrievable after return;
- and, where needed, repairable/remountable/replaceable.

The important institutional operation is not simply movement outward. It is the ability to make an object leave and then re-enter a collective order.

## 7. Minimum conclusion already worth an article

If archives produce no damage or repair evidence, the project can still establish:

**Scientific-society slide cabinets were not necessarily passive stores. They could operate as systems of temporary custody in which preparations were selected, re-arranged, lent, returned and made retrievable through registers and catalogues.**

Bolton can test this directly; Quekett provides a mature comparison; Oxford tests the same problem at much larger institutional scale.

## 8. Stronger conclusion if archives cooperate

If the records show remounting, replacement, loss, revised numbering or related intervention tied to continued use:

**Keeping microscope preparations in use required societies to preserve or translate working identities across changes of carrier, cabinet position and custodian.**

This is an upgrade, not a premise.

## 9. Strongest conclusion — evidence threshold high

Only if at least two independently controlled society cases show intervention plus continued/re-entered use:

**The nineteenth-century microscope preparation became a maintainable instrument component not through material immutability but through institutional practices that made component substitution compatible with continued scientific identity and access.**

Do not state this as settled in the grant application.

## 10. Core archive tests

### Oxford
Can public `presented / donated / received / exhibited` events be followed into internal custody, expenditure, registration and surviving cabinet/database addresses?

### Bolton
Can `FZ/14/3/1` reconstruct actual borrower–preparation–return cycles, and can diagnostic loaned slides be followed into minutes/accounts and the 1898 catalogue?

### Quekett
Can already established free circulation be tied to surviving circulation/loan/cabinet documentation or identifiable historical preparations?

### Manchester
Can the 1865–69 circulating-cabinet culture be tied to the later archive or to Thomas Armstrong the Deansgate instrument maker?

## 11. Article and grant titles

Grant-facing working title:
**Keeping Preparations in Use: Microscope Slides, Cabinets, and Scientific Societies in Nineteenth-Century Britain**

Article-facing working title:
**Changing the Glass: Remounting, Cabinets, and the Mobile Microscope Preparation**

The grant title is deliberately descriptive. The article title can carry the sharper material argument after archival results are known.

## 12. Stop rule

Do not add another archive, society or geographical node unless it can change one of:
- object boundary;
- temporary custody mechanism;
- lending/return procedure;
- carrier/cabinet incompatibility;
- remounting/replacement;
- register/catalogue re-entry;
- instrument-maker participation.

Otherwise, continue with the current cases and write from them.

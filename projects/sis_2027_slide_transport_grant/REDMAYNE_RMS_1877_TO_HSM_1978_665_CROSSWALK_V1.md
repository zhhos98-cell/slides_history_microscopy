The original volume is publicly digitised (Internet Archive / Biodiversity Heritage Library / Wikimedia Commons), but the current retrieval path did not expose p. 222 as machine-readable page text during this pass. Therefore:

## 3. What the accession number does and does not mean

Searches across the HSM library catalogue show that many RMS Collection books carry `1978-xxx` identifiers, including seventeenth-, eighteenth-, nineteenth-, and twentieth-century works. Some entries explicitly add `(purchase)` to the historical RMS provenance and others do not.

Therefore `1978-665` should be treated as an HSM catalogue/accession address associated with the transfer/cataloguing of the RMS library collection, **not** as the date on which the RMS itself acquired Redmayne's volume.

Do not infer from `1978-665` alone:
- that HSM acquired this exact volume in a uniquely documented 1978 transaction;
- that the RMS acquired it in 1978;
- that the current copy is automatically the exact physical copy donated in 1877.

## 5. Why this matters to the project

This is already stronger than a generic actor-network comparison.

Redmayne now supplies a controlled institutional contrast:

### During life
A microscopical publication enters the RMS reference/library collection.

### After death
His private slide collection is placed by Mrs Redmayne in the temporary custody of Bolton Society officers, selectively reduced to six dozen slides, re-arranged in the Society cabinet, and subsequently enters a documented lending system.

The comparison is therefore not simply `same person in two societies`.
It is:

`same microscopist's work → different institutional formats and custody regimes`.

At RMS/Oxford:
`publication / visual reference object → library collection → later museum/library address`.

At Bolton:
`private preparations → officer selection → society cabinet → lending register → later catalogue`.

This is directly relevant to the frozen project question: how scientific materials remained usable and retrievable as their institutional setting changed.

## 7. Consequence for article structure

Redmayne should remain a supporting thread rather than replace the project with biography.

His value is unusually high because he links several different material operations:
- slide making and exchange;
- photomicrographic publication;
- donation to reference institutions;
- posthumous selection from a private collection;
- cabinet re-arrangement;
- member lending;
- an object relationship with Thomas Armstrong's Manchester instrument-making network.

The article can use Redmayne to show that `movement` did not produce one generic fate for microscopical materials. Different institutions reformatted the same person's work into different usable forms.

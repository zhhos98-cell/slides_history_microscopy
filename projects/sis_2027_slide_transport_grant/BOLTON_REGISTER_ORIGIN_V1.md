# Bolton FZ/14/3/1 — register-origin problem v1

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: BOUNDED HYPOTHESIS / ARCHIVAL TEST REQUIRED

## 1. What is secure

Two chronologies must remain separate.

### Lending chronology
By summer **1881**, contemporary reporting already states that Bolton Microscopical Society slides were lent to members.

### Surviving-record chronology
The archive finding aid identifies:
`FZ/14/3/1 — Register of Slides Lent Out, May 1882–Jul 1887`.

Therefore:

**May 1882 is not the beginning of lending. It is the beginning of the surviving dedicated lending register.**

This distinction is mandatory in grant and article prose.

## 2. Society calendar

The Society's annual report printed after the 19 Nov 1880 Conversazione states:
- Society year began **1 October**;
- lectures and other work occupied members until **May**;
- the Society then `virtually closes` until September;
- the recess allowed members to pursue microscopical research during the summer.

The report lists general meetings through **14 May**, followed by a September meeting before the next formal year.

This supplies an institutional calendar independent of the later lending register.

## 3. Bounded hypothesis

The coincidence is historically suggestive:

`lending already operating in 1881`
→ `formal meeting season ends around May`
→ `surviving dedicated lending register begins May 1882`.

Testable hypothesis:
**the register may represent a formalisation of temporary custody at or around the annual transition from regular meetings to summer research/recess.**

Possible mechanisms include:
- slides remained with members for longer periods outside meetings;
- officers wanted a written record while normal meetings were suspended;
- a re-arranged cabinet acquired a stable numbering system requiring transaction records;
- the new register simply replaced an earlier informal/not-surviving recording practice.

None is presently established.

## 4. What public evidence does NOT show

The bounded public search has not located:
- a May 1882 resolution creating the register;
- a new lending bylaw;
- appointment of a dedicated slide librarian/curator;
- a complaint about missing or overdue slides that clearly triggered the register;
- a statement connecting the register to summer recess;
- evidence that lending itself began in 1882.

Do not invent any of these.

## 5. Exact archival test

### Minutes
Start with `FZ/14/1/2`, **April–June 1882**.

Look for:
- `slides`;
- `cabinet`;
- `loan / lend / lent`;
- `register / book / list`;
- `catalogue / index / number`;
- `rules / regulations`;
- `Secretary / President / custodian / librarian / curator`;
- `summer / recess`;
- `return / detention / overdue / lost`;
- `Society property`.

Only widen to Jan–Sep 1882 if the narrow window yields nothing.

### Register opening
Capture every cover/title/opening leaf and the first c.20 transactions before normal row transcription.

Ask:
- does the book begin with a rule/instruction or immediately with loans?
- are earlier loans backfilled?
- is there a mass opening batch in May?
- are slide numbers already stable at first use?
- is one officer clearly the recorder/custodian?
- are return entries contemporaneous?
- do the first entries cross the May–summer boundary?

## 6. Interpretive outcomes

### Outcome A — explicit formalisation
If minutes state that a register/book should be kept or lending rules changed:

Strong claim becomes possible:
`an existing lending practice was formally converted into a documented custody system in 1882`.

### Outcome B — no resolution, structured new register
If no minute exists but the register opens with systematic headings/stable identifiers:

Safe claim:
`by May 1882 lending had acquired a dedicated administrative record, although the decision/motive for creating it is not documented in the surviving public evidence`.

### Outcome C — backfilled or inherited loans
If the first pages contain earlier dates or already-outstanding material:

This would directly prove the register records a pre-existing lending system rather than inaugurating one.

### Outcome D — irregular notebook rather than formal register
If entries are ad hoc, unnumbered and weakly tied to returns:

Downgrade the language from `formalised custody system` to `surviving record of lending transactions`.

## 7. Article payoff

The chronology is potentially useful because it prevents a common analytical shortcut.

The story is not:
`Society creates lending register → slides begin to circulate`.

The controlled sequence is instead:
`Society already lends slides`
→ `private collection is selected and cabinet re-arranged`
→ `a dedicated transaction record appears later`.

This makes administration a historical response to an already operating material practice rather than automatically its origin.

## 8. Stop rule

Do not spend further public-web time trying to explain the May 1882 start unless a newly indexed exact report appears.

The question is now sufficiently bounded for the archive:
**What, if anything, happened in spring 1882 that turned an existing lending practice into the surviving dedicated register?**

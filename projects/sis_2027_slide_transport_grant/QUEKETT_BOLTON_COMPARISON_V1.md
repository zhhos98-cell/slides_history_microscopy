## 1. Why compare these two societies

Quekett Microscopical Club and Bolton Microscopical Society are useful not because both owned slides, but because the controlled evidence catches their cabinet systems at different institutional moments.

- **Bolton, 1881** exposes the **formation and reformatting** of a lendable society collection: a private collection enters officer custody, a subset is selected, the cabinet is rearranged, further slides are added, and Society slides are lent to members.
- **Quekett, 1865–79** exposes the **mature use problem** of a large circulating/reference cabinet: donations and exchange build the collection; by 1879 more than 2,000 slides are freely circulated; yet the President warns that the finished preparation cannot automatically transmit the preparer's knowledge of origin and preparation.

The comparison therefore separates two operations that generic `circulation` language would collapse:

`making a collection lendable` **vs.** `making a circulated collection instructive/reference-worthy`.

## 2. Bolton — collection formation under changing custody

Primary contemporary sequence, 1881:

1. Society already has a valuable slide collection.
2. Recent additions require the collection to be **re-arranged**.
3. Mrs Redmayne places her late husband's **whole private collection** in the hands of the President and Secretary.
4. Those officers select **six dozen / 72 choice slides** for the Society.
5. A further **48 choice diatom slides** are added.
6. Society slides are explicitly described as **lent to members** for home use during winter evenings.

Historical operations:
- temporary custody;
- selection authority;
- private → institutional transition;
- exclusion as well as inclusion;
- cabinet rearrangement;
- member lending;
- expected return/retrievability.

Core archival questions:
- What happened to the non-selected Redmayne slides?
- Were the 72 selected slides re-labelled, renumbered or grouped?
- Who supplied the additional 48 diatom slides?
- Were member loans recorded?
- Did each preparation have a fixed return address in the cabinet?
- Were loss, damage or replacement assigned to borrowers or officers?
- Does the 1898 `FZ/14/3/2` catalogue preserve the 1881 selection/rearrangement?

## 3. Quekett — mature cabinet as lending/reference infrastructure

Repository authority:
`data/analysis/quekett_club_cabinet_circulation_authority_upgrade_v1.json`.

Controlled chronology:

### 1865 founding programme
The Club's prospectus makes exhibition and exchange of specimens, a reference library, and a `comprehensive Cabinet of Objects` founding functions.

### 1867 Richardson donation
Contemporary Quekett-related reporting records that on 23 November 1867 the Club received **a box of slides from B. Wills Richardson, F.R.C.S.I., Dublin**.

This is especially relevant because Richardson had already described remounting imported German transparent carmine preparations in 1862. However:

**DO NOT identify the donated 1867 box with Richardson's remounted German injections.**

The current evidence only proves:
- Richardson practiced remounting at collection level in 1862;
- he donated a box of slides to Quekett in 1867;
- surviving QMC holdings include Richardson-labelled slides.

No item-level chain currently joins those three facts.

### 1868 exchange and cabinet growth
Quekett records include:
- German exchange material;
- 100 slides from Gould of North Bridgton, Maine, principally diatoms in sets;
- a carefully named donation valued as an identification authority;
- on 27 November 1868, **173 slides presented to the cabinet at one ordinary meeting**.

### 1879 mature cabinet
The President's Address states:
- the cabinet exceeded **2,000 slides** across animal/vegetable histology, entomology, botany, geology and crystallography;
- slides were **freely circulated**;
- they were called `interesting` rather than automatically `instructive`;
- a preparer who knew the object's origin, relations and crude state possessed knowledge unavailable to a casual first-time borrower;
- systematic series keyed to standard works such as Carpenter were proposed as a way to improve instructional value.

Historical operations:
- donation/exchange;
- cabinet incorporation;
- naming/classification;
- lending/circulation;
- comparison/identification;
- documentary/series supplementation.

## 4. The key contrast

### Bolton asks:
**How does a preparation become institutional property that can be lent?**

The visible work is upstream:
`private collection → selection → rearrangement → society cabinet → member loan`.

### Quekett asks:
**What can a borrower actually know or do with an institutional preparation once it circulates?**

The visible work is downstream:
`cabinet item → borrower → comparison/reference encounter → recognition that context is missing → proposal for names/series/books`.

Together they turn `circulation` into a lifecycle rather than a transport event.

## 5. What this comparison adds to the article

The two societies allow the paper to distinguish at least four forms of maintenance:

### Material maintenance
Remounting, replacing, cleaning or repairing a preparation.

### Collection maintenance
Selecting what belongs, rearranging cabinet contents, keeping fixed return locations, adding/removing objects.

### Custodial maintenance
Recording who temporarily possesses institutional objects and ensuring return.

### Epistemic maintenance
Keeping names, origins, series relations or accompanying literature sufficient for a circulated object to remain useful as a reference/instructional preparation.

The article should not assume these always travel together.

## 6. Stronger project formulation generated by the comparison

Instead of:
`How did transport affect microscope slides?`

Use:
**How did scientific societies turn privately made or acquired microscope preparations into objects that could leave a cabinet, enter temporary custody, and still return as usable and retrievable parts of a collective collection?**

This formulation:
- makes Bolton central;
- uses Quekett as a mature comparator;
- creates a clear Oxford archival test;
- leaves postal societies as specialized controls;
- avoids claiming that every important intervention was physical repair.

## 7. Oxford test created by Bolton + Quekett

Oxford RMS should now be searched for a lifecycle rather than keywords alone:

`meeting appearance`
→ `gift / purchase / temporary exhibition distinction`
→ `acceptance into Society custody`
→ `cabinet/register address`
→ `loan / temporary movement, if any`
→ `return / reclassification / material intervention`
→ `later retrievability`.

The strongest RMS evidence would show the decision layer connecting public meeting objects to durable collection status.

## 11. Article-level payoff

A possible central sentence now is:

**A slide collection did not become mobile simply because its contents could be carried. Societies had to decide which preparations belonged, where they returned, who could borrow them, and what information made them usable once they left the hands of their makers.**

That proposition is narrower than generic knowledge circulation and broader than repair alone.

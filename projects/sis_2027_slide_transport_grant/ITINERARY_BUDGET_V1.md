## Trip 1 — Oxford: 4 research days / 3 nights

### Purpose

Oxford is the principal archive stay. The History of Science Museum holds both the surviving RMS slide collection and the Society's internal archive. The July 2026 staff reply states that the internal archive is stored off-site and that requested material should be ordered at least one week before the visit.

The visit is designed to move from published evidence of slide circulation to the Society's internal administrative and material records. It is not a general survey of the RMS archive.

### Day 1 — ordinary meetings and circulation events

Primary target:
- RMS Internal Archive Box 31 — Minutes of ordinary and annual meetings, Jul. 1868–Feb. 1883.

Questions:
- when slides, preparations, boxes or instruments entered meetings;
- whether donations, loans, exchanges or returns were recorded;
- whether damage, replacement, packing, carriage or postage appears in the meeting record;
- whether particular slide series can be connected to known published circulation events.

### Day 2 — council decisions and material administration

Primary targets:
- Box 38 — Council minutes, Feb. 1863–Jul. 1880;
- Box 28 — Council minutes, Oct. 1880–Dec. 1893.

Questions:
- decisions about purchases, cabinets, storage, circulation and access;
- responsibility for custody, repair, replacement or insurance;
- payments or arrangements not visible in published proceedings;
- relationships between meeting display, society ownership and later collection management.

### Day 3 — financial and register layer

Primary targets:
- Box 41 — Ledger, 1841–96;
- Box 107 — Cash Book, 1860–71;
- Box 108 — Cash Book, 1872–78;
- Box 110 — Cash Book, 1879–85;
- Box 49 — Register of slides, 1897.

Questions:
- purchases of slides, glass, cabinets, cases, postage, carriage or repairs;
- names of suppliers, preparers or carriers;
- whether a formal slide register preserves older collection or circulation histories;
- whether expenditure can be tied to concrete changes in the Society's material infrastructure.

### Day 4 — instrument/collection cross-check and photography

Primary target:
- Box 103 — RMS Catalogue of microscopes, apparatus, etc., plus Donations to the Collection.

Secondary task:
- re-check diagnostic entries from Days 1–3 against HSM catalogue/object records where feasible;
- photograph permitted records systematically and close unresolved references rather than opening new archive series.

Expected Oxford output:
A source-controlled reconstruction of how one scientific society acquired, moved, stored and maintained slides as working instrument components, with at least one concrete chain connecting circulation practice to material or administrative design.

## Trip 2 — Manchester + Bolton: 2 research days / 1 night

### Day 1 — University of Manchester Special Collections

Collection:
- Manchester Microscopical and Natural History Society archive, GB133-MIC.

Access already established by correspondence:
- item-level catalogue is available;
- consultation is by appointment;
- most material requires at least three working days' notice;
- personal-research photography is permitted free of charge.

Research aim:
Use the Manchester records as a provincial comparison to RMS institutional practice. Prioritize item-level references for minutes, correspondence, accounts, catalogues/inventories and records of acquisition/exchange. Test specifically for slide circulation, boxes/cabinets, postal arrangements, damaged or replaced preparations and payments associated with moving or maintaining material.

### Overnight in Manchester

One night is budgeted so that Bolton can be consulted the following day without a second long-distance return journey from London.

### Day 2 — Bolton History Centre

Collection:
- FZ/14 — Bolton Microscopical Society.

Known anchor:
- 1898 `Catalogue of Slides, Journals, and Books`;
- archive staff supplied a fuller catalogue transcription in July 2026.

Research aim:
Test how a provincial society converted accumulated slides into a managed collection: acquisition, exchange, storage, cataloguing, lending/circulation and any handling or repair record. The 1898 catalogue is used as an endpoint from which earlier administrative records can be read backwards.

Operational note:
Bolton currently asks for archive requests by 3pm on the Friday before the intended consultation week. Plan and order references before travel.

Expected Manchester/Bolton output:
A controlled comparison between a large metropolitan scientific society and two provincial collections, asking whether transport and circulation generated similar material/administrative solutions or locally different ones.

## Trip 3 — Bristol: 1 research day, no overnight stay

Collection:
- Bristol Archives 44409 — Bristol Microscopical Society, minutes and papers, 1843–1883.

Current access information:
- the archive confirmed in August 2026 that the collection has a more detailed catalogue;
- current searchroom opening is Tuesday–Thursday, 9:30–16:30;
- advance booking is not mandatory but is useful because documents can be prepared in advance.

Research aim:
A bounded one-day comparison focused on the years overlapping the RMS and early club-circulation evidence. Order only the references most likely to record society acquisition/exchange, instrument or slide management, postal arrangements and collection maintenance.

Expected Bristol output:
A second independent provincial test of whether slide transport was merely episodic movement or part of routine society administration.

This self-funded component strengthens the application because SIS funds are reserved for costs that arise only because the project requires research outside the applicant's home city.

## Why this itinerary is proportionate

The itinerary is intentionally not a national census. Oxford supplies the institutional core and the strongest surviving archive/object conjunction. Manchester and Bolton form one geographically efficient comparative trip. Bristol adds one early provincial society archive reachable as a day return. London is used without charge to the grant.

The resulting evidence base is therefore:

RMS institutional archive + surviving slide/instrument collection
→ Manchester society archive
→ Bolton society archive and slide catalogue
→ Bristol early society archive
→ London comparison at applicant's own cost.

That is enough to test a historical mechanism across different institutions without spending the grant on repetitive travel.

## Why the fifth Oxford day is justified

Oxford is now not one research task but several linked tasks that require different archival layers:

1. calibrate public `presented / donated / received / exhibited` language against internal custody status;
2. examine council/meeting decisions and dated accession events;
3. work through ledger/cash-book/register material only around diagnostic events;
4. crosswalk historical collection addresses into later RMS/HSM catalogue structures;
5. inspect the surviving-object/library layer, especially Redmayne and any diagnostic slide cases generated during the first four days.

The fifth day is therefore not generic overflow. It protects the most difficult part of the project: **historical-event → internal address → current collection/object** crosswalks.

Railcard savings make this a more defensible use of grant funds than adding another city simply to increase geographical coverage.

### Day 1 — calibrate custody language

Targets:

Dated hooks include:

Question:

Output:

### Day 2 — Redmayne + accession/custody chains

- HSM currently preserves a matching Redmayne volume as **Royal Microscopical Society Collection [1978-665]**.

Task:
`1877 donation → internal RMS library/accession address → current HSM [1978-665]`.

Secondary dated hooks:
- 26 Apr 1882 — C. Baker / Naples preparations;
- 14 Mar 1883 — F. Jeffrey Bell / 19 Naples slides;
- 13 Jun 1883 — Charles Coppock / 5 slides;

### Day 3 — financial and material operations

### Day 4 — historical register/catalogue address layer

Targets:

Question:
Can earlier preparations/events be assigned a later institutional address?

Priorities:
- Redmayne library/object continuity;
- diagnostic Naples or other dated donation cases;

### Day 5 — surviving collection / crosswalk / unresolved high-value cases

This day is deliberately reserved for the task most difficult to predict before reading the archive.

Priority order:
1. current HSM/RMS object or library crosswalks generated on Days 1–4;
2. Small Worlds/cabinet checks only for already identified historical addresses;
3. close one or two event → register/catalogue → current-object chains rather than open new series;
4. photograph unresolved diagnostic records;
5. return to Box 49/103 or a council/cash-book entry only where the crosswalk requires it.

Success criterion:
At least one secure historical-event → institutional-address chain, with surviving current-object identity where the evidence permits.

The fifth day is expendable only if the first four days unexpectedly close the crosswalks early; it should not be replaced in advance by a lower-yield city.

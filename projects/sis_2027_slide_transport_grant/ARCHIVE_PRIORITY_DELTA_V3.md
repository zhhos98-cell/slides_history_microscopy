## New evidence forcing the change

Contemporary *Northern Microscopist* reporting now closes a non-postal Bolton mechanism more strongly than the earlier planning note recognized.

### 11 March 1881
The Bolton Microscopical Society unanimously agreed to augment its Society Cabinet with **48 choice diatom slides**.

### Summer 1881
The Society's slide collection was being **re-arranged** after recent additions. Mrs Redmayne placed the late founder John T. Redmayne's **whole private slide collection** into the hands of the Society President and Secretary so they could choose the most suitable material. They selected **six dozen / 72 choice slides**. A further 48 choice diatom slides had also been added. Crucially, the contemporary report states that the Society's slides were **lent to members** for use during the winter evenings.

This establishes, before archival work:

`private collection`
→ `temporary officer custody of whole collection`
→ `selection`
→ `Society cabinet re-arrangement`
→ `collective institutional custody`
→ `member lending / distributed use`.

The surviving archival endpoint `FZ/14/3/2`, the 1898 *Catalogue of Slides, Journals, and Books belonging to the Society*, gives a later register against which the 1881 transition can be tested.

## Revised conceptual role of Bolton

Bolton is no longer simply a comparator asking whether slides circulated.

The historical question is:

**How did a scientific society transform a dead founder's private collection into a selected, rearranged and lendable institutional cabinet?**

This case can illuminate:
- temporary custody;
- selection authority;
- private-to-collective ownership/custody transition;
- cabinet reorganisation;
- documentary registration;
- lending and return;
- potentially damage/loss/replacement if internal records survive.

It therefore directly addresses the project's shift from `transport` to **keeping preparations usable under changing custody**.

## Falsification update

Previous v2 said `no Bolton evidence beyond passive cabinet accumulation` would weaken the case. That condition has now been falsified by the public primary evidence: the cabinet was actively rearranged and its slides were lent to members.

The remaining archival uncertainty is narrower:
- how loans were recorded;
- how selected slides were classified/addressed;
- whether losses/damage/replacement occurred;
- whether the 1881 groups can be traced into the 1898 catalogue.

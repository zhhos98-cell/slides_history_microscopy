# Argument spine v3 — keeping preparations in use

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: CURRENT WORKING SPINE / SUPERSEDES V2 FOR CONCEPTUAL FRAMING

## 1. Why v2 needed revision

V2 correctly moved away from generic `circulation` and toward failure, custody and return to use. New Bolton evidence shows that an argument centred too narrowly on **repair after damage** would still miss an important class of operations.

In Bolton in 1881, the key acts were not repair:
- a deceased founder's private collection entered temporary custody of Society officers;
- officers selected six dozen preparations;
- the Society cabinet was re-arranged;
- an additional 48 diatom slides were added;
- Society slides were lent to members for use at home.

The historical common denominator is therefore broader than repair but narrower than circulation:

**preparations had to be reformatted materially and institutionally in order to remain usable under new conditions of custody, storage and movement.**

`Reformatting` is an analytical shorthand, not a claim that nineteenth-century actors used the word in this sense. Every section must name the historical operation actually performed: cutting, remounting, selecting, rearranging, cataloguing, lending, replacing, returning.

## 2. Central question

### Full research question
**How were nineteenth-century microscope preparations kept scientifically usable as they moved between mounts, cabinets, private collectors, scientific societies and borrowers?**

### Stronger diagnostic version
**What had to be changed—and what had to remain stable—when a microscope preparation entered a new material or institutional setting?**

This formulation allows the archive to answer with operations rather than forcing a predetermined theory of object identity.

## 3. Provisional claim

**The mobility of microscope preparations was not the intact transfer of finished objects. It repeatedly depended on acts of material and institutional reformatting: changing glass carriers, selecting and rearranging collections, assigning cabinet or register addresses, lending preparations into temporary custody, and returning them to use.**

Claim ceiling:
This is a research hypothesis across cases. It becomes a historical conclusion only if Oxford/Bolton/Manchester records document the institutional operations strongly enough.

## 4. Scale 1 — the individual preparation

### Richardson, 1862
German transparent carmine injections entered British collections on carriers that could be too broad or too short for local cabinet norms. Richardson cut some possible carriers with a diamond but preferred **remounting**, and stated that he had remounted all such preparations in his collection.

Operation:
`imported preparation → incompatible carrier → new carrier → local cabinet usability`.

Lesson:
The original glass was not always the non-negotiable identity-bearing part of a preparation.

### Northern postal-failure inverse
Postal damage could leave `nothing but the glass slip` while the object disappeared.

Operation:
`carrier survives → preparation fails`.

Lesson:
Material survival of the glass is neither necessary nor sufficient for scientific continuity.

## 5. Scale 2 — the circulating container

### Butterworth, 1865
The circulating cabinet fixes trays, quantities, restraints, custody duration, memorandum-book and carriage finance while the contents of members' trays are deliberately changed on return.

Operation:
`stable circulation apparatus + temporary custody → scheduled replacement of contents`.

Lesson:
A mobile collection could be designed around **persistent infrastructure and changing objects**.

The historical object of study is therefore not only the slide but the relation between slide, tray, box, record and custodian.

## 6. Scale 3 — private collection to society cabinet

### Bolton, 1881
After Redmayne's death:

`whole private collection`
→ `temporary custody by Society President + Secretary`
→ `selection of 72 slides`
→ `Society cabinet re-arrangement`
→ `addition of another 48 diatom slides`
→ `lending to members for home use`.

Operation:
**selection + rearrangement + institutionalisation + lending.**

Lesson:
Mobility across ownership/custody regimes could change a collection without changing the preparations physically. The work occurs at collection and documentary scale: deciding what enters, where it belongs, and how it can leave again temporarily.

This case prevents the article from collapsing into a history of breakage.

## 7. Scale 4 — the scientific society as custody machine

### Oxford RMS — archival hypothesis
Public proceedings already distinguish:
- explicit gifts/donations;
- objects `received`;
- temporary exhibitions;
- Conversazione material;
- maker/commercial-preparer donations.

Internal records can reveal whether these categories led to different custody outcomes.

The core question is:
**what happened between appearance at a meeting and durable inclusion in the Society's working collection?**

Operations to recover:
- acceptance/rejection;
- cabinet assignment;
- payment or purchase;
- labelling/classification;
- loan/return;
- remounting/repair/replacement;
- register translation;
- disposal or loss.

## 8. Postal societies become a control, not the centre

Postal clubs make movement unusually explicit. Beiermann has already established:
- packing systems;
- slide boxes;
- damage and liability;
- mending/beyond-repair cases;
- information accompanying preparations;
- postal reform and interoperability.

This project should use PMS/APMC to define what an **explicitly mobility-specialized organisation** looks like.

The new archival question is whether ordinary microscopy societies—whose primary institutional identity was not postal circulation—also had to build procedures of custody, selection, lending and material intervention around mobile preparations.

## 9. The historical contrast is not stationary vs mobile

Avoid a simplistic opposition in which private cabinet = stationary and postal box = mobile.

Private and society collections moved at several levels:
- imported commercial preparations entered domestic cabinets;
- private collections entered societies after gift/death/exchange;
- society cabinets lent preparations to members;
- meeting exhibits could be temporary or permanent;
- slides could be removed from old glass and enter new mounts;
- objects could return to a fixed cabinet after temporary circulation.

Better contrast:
**different custody regimes required different operations to keep preparations usable and retrievable.**

## 10. Four verbs that can structure the article

### REMOUNT
Richardson and other preparation-level material substitution.

### SELECT
Redmayne estate → Bolton Society: not every private object becomes institutional property.

### REARRANGE
Society cabinet order changes when collections are enlarged or translated into institutional classification.

### LEND / RETURN
Society property enters temporary personal custody and must remain retrievable, usable and accountable.

Repair/replacement can appear inside any of these, but need not carry the whole article.

## 11. Possible article structure

### Opening — a whole collection changes hands
Summer 1881 Bolton: Mrs Redmayne puts the whole collection in the hands of President and Secretary; they choose 72; cabinet is rearranged; members borrow slides for winter use.

Why open here rather than with breakage:
It immediately presents mobility as a **problem of custody and selection**, not transport technology.

### I. Changing the glass
Richardson 1862 and carrier incompatibility. A preparation can stay scientifically continuous through material replacement.

### II. Designing return
Butterworth 1865 + Manchester 1866/69. Circulating cabinets make custody duration, replacement and documentation explicit before the PMS.

### III. From private collection to lendable cabinet
Bolton 1880–81, with Redmayne/Armstrong regional actor links where safe.

### IV. What became Society property?
Oxford RMS event-led archival chains: donation, exhibition, receipt, cabinet/register, intervention and survival.

### V. The postal control
Use PMS/APMC compactly to show what happens when mobility itself becomes an organisation's explicit raison d'être; distinguish from Beiermann rather than repeat her evidence base.

### Conclusion
A microscope preparation was mobile not because the same finished object simply travelled intact. Its continued usability depended on repeated decisions about supports, selection, order, custody and return.

## 12. Strongest title candidates after Bolton evidence

### Grant-facing
**Keeping Preparations in Use: Microscope Slides, Cabinets, and Scientific Societies in Nineteenth-Century Britain**

Advantages:
- immediately legible;
- not overcommitted to transport;
- `scientific societies` explains Oxford/Bolton/Manchester travel;
- still clearly instrument/material-culture history.

### Article-facing
**Changing the Glass: Remounting, Cabinets, and the Mobile Microscope Preparation**

Advantages:
- concrete opening operation;
- allows material + institutional scales;
- `preparation` is analytically more precise than treating the slide as only glass.

### Alternative
**From Mount to Cabinet: Keeping Microscope Preparations in Use in Victorian Britain**

Most accessible, but less distinctive.

## 13. What the grant is actually buying under v3

Not `more examples of slides travelling`.

It buys the internal records necessary to move from visible public events to invisible institutional operations:
- who took custody;
- who selected;
- whether the Society purchased or accepted;
- where preparations were put;
- how loans were recorded;
- what happened on return;
- whether objects were repaired/remounted/replaced;
- how catalogue identity survived change.

This is why Oxford + Bolton now have a stronger intellectual rationale than a geographically broad tour.

## 14. Stop rule

If archives produce only lists of donations and lectures, do not inflate them into `reformatting`.

The article requires operations that actually transform custody, material format, collection order or retrievability.

If those are absent, fall back to the narrower pre-PMS circulating-cabinet genealogy, where custody/replacement mechanics are already explicit in print.

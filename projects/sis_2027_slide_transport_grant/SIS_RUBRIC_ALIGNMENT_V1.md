## 1. SIS criteria to answer directly

The September 2026 SIS grants page states that awards of up to £750 support **new research into the history of scientific instruments**, typically based on museum collections and/or archival materials directly relating to specific instruments. The project statement should explain:

1. why the topic is relevant to the history of scientific instruments;
2. how it relates to previous research;
3. how and when the research will be carried out;
4. whether relevant curators/archives have already been contacted;
5. details of specific historic instruments to be inspected where possible.

The expected outputs are a short SIS blog and a 2,000–4,000 word Bulletin article (or, by prior agreement, a peer-reviewed article plus Bulletin summary).

This file tests the project against those criteria rather than assuming fit.

## 2. Relevance to the history of scientific instruments

### Weak formulation — avoid

`This project studies how Victorian scientific societies circulated collections of microscope slides.`

Problem:
This can read as associational history, postal history or collection history with scientific instruments only in the background.

### Stronger and historically accurate formulation

**The project studies the microscope preparation as a working, detachable instrument component whose usability depended on the relation between specimen, glass carrier, mount, label, microscope stage, cabinet address and temporary custodian.**

A nineteenth-century prepared slide was not simply a stored specimen. It had to:
- fit the physical geometry of a microscope/cabinet system;
- remain optically and materially usable after mounting/remounting;
- carry enough identification to be selected and compared;
- be retrievable from a cabinet or catalogue;
- survive transfer between users or be reformatted where its carrier was incompatible;
- return to a collection in a form that could be used again with a microscope.

The central historical operation is therefore **keeping an instrument component operational through changes of material and institutional setting**.

Richardson's 1862 remounting episode supplies a direct material premise: a microscopic preparation could be transferred away from its original German glass support because the original carrier did not fit the local British cabinet/slide geometry. Bolton and Oxford then allow the research to ask how societies managed such preparations collectively rather than as one user's private objects.

### Instrument-history sentence for an application

A safe formulation is:

> Microscope slides were not passive illustrations but prepared components of the microscope system: their dimensions, mounting, labels and cabinet addresses determined whether specimens could be inserted into instruments, compared by successive observers, and returned to collective use.

Do not claim that every cabinet or catalogue is itself a scientific instrument. They are the institutional/material infrastructure that kept instrument components usable.

## 3. SIS precedent and remit fit

The SIS's own supported-project list demonstrates that its remit is not restricted to isolated brass instruments. It has funded, among other examples:
- Greek secondary-school science collections;
- collections associated with John Dalton;
- anatomical preparations and syringes;
- magic lanterns and slides in Leeds;
- scientific collections in schools;
- correspondence relating to an air pump;
- instrument trade and newspaper evidence;
- curatorial/institutional histories tied to scientific artefacts.

These are precedents for archive- and collection-based projects so long as the relation to scientific instruments/artefacts remains concrete.

This project should therefore foreground the **prepared microscope slide as historical instrument component**, with societies/cabinets as the mechanism under investigation.

### Distinct empirical problem

**What happens when a collection object is also a repeatedly mobile instrument component?**

The project moves from `circulation as transmission` to:

`temporary custody → re-entry into collection order → renewed instrumental use`.

Its distinctive archive test is transaction-level:
- preparation leaves a society cabinet;
- borrower/custody is recorded;
- object returns (or fails to return);
- address/identity remains usable or is translated;
- another observer can retrieve/use it later.

The novelty is not the abstract concept of mobility, return, repair or identity. It is the reconstruction of this operation for nineteenth-century microscope preparations in ordinary scientific societies.

## 7. Specific historic instrument/object problem

SIS explicitly asks applicants to provide details of specific historic instruments they wish to inspect where possible.

This project must not dodge that request.

### Specific material targets already controlled

1. **The Royal Microscopical Society slide cabinet at HSM, Oxford**
   - HSM states that more than three-quarters of its c.10,000 microscope slides are contained in one single cabinet on permanent loan from the RMS;
   - preparations in the cabinet date roughly 1860–1930;
   - exact current cabinet inventory/container identifier is not yet publicly closed and should be requested from HSM rather than invented.

2. **Selected nineteenth-century microscope preparations within the RMS/HSM collection**
   - final selection should be keyed to dated historical donation/register events or historical slide catalogues MS 5, MS 13, MS 18 / Box 49;
   - exact modern slide IDs should be identified pre-visit with HSM where possible.

3. **Redmayne, *Micro-Photographs from the Diatomaceae*, HSM `[1978-665]`**
   - not itself the central scientific instrument;
   - a supporting reference/publication object useful for testing the institutional address/custody of one microscopist's work across Bolton and RMS.

4. **Bolton Society slides as historical instrument components**
   - physical survival in current Bolton holdings remains unconfirmed;
   - do not claim surviving objects until archive staff identify them;
   - the exact historical instrument-component record is instead the lending register/cabinet catalogue, which documents repeated use of prepared slides.

### Application-risk statement

If the final application names only registers and minutes, it risks reading as society history.

Before submission, ideally obtain from Oxford:
- the cabinet's current object/container reference;
- 2–5 diagnostic nineteenth-century slide IDs linked to historical catalogue/event anchors.

This would make the material-instrument relation immediately visible to the SIS committee.

## 9. Tangible result/question

SIS emphasises a clear tangible result or question.

### Grant-safe research question

**How did nineteenth-century microscopical societies keep prepared microscope slides usable and retrievable as they passed between private collections, society cabinets and temporary borrowers?**

### Concrete intended result

Reconstruct at least one transaction chain at Bolton and one custody/address chain at Oxford:

**Bolton**
`collection intake → selection/re-arrangement → loan → return/repeated use → later catalogue`

**Oxford**
`historical receipt/donation → internal custody/register → later institutional/current address`.

This is falsifiable and achievable within a small grant.

## 10. Output scope

Do not promise the entire scholarly article to SIS.

A realistic Bulletin article can focus on:
- Bolton 1881 + lending register as empirical centre;
- Oxford RMS as larger institutional comparison;
- short Quekett/PMS contextual controls.

The broader article on mobile microscope preparations, carrier substitution and re-entry into collective order can remain a separate later publication.

## 11. Current weaknesses before application

1. **Oxford current object IDs are not yet specific enough.**
   Remedy: ask HSM for cabinet/container identifier and a handful of diagnostic slide IDs.

2. **Physical survival of Bolton Society slides is not established.**
   Remedy: do not promise inspection; make archival lending/crosswalk the Bolton task.

3. **Manchester could yield no continuity with the 1860s cabinet culture.**
   Remedy: keep it a bounded one-day test and do not make grant success depend on it.

4. **The project could drift into generic collection history.**
   Remedy: keep instrument-component operation central: dimensions, mounting/remounting, cabinet compatibility, labels/addresses and actual reuse with microscopes.

5. **The large article could exceed SIS Bulletin scale.**
   Remedy: define a narrow Bulletin output around Bolton + Oxford; treat broader synthesis separately.

## 12. Overall fit

Current assessment: **strong grant fit once Oxford object-level specificity is closed.**

The project already satisfies:
- new archival research;
- prior curator/archive contact;
- tightly bounded collections;
- a clear question;

The final application should be written as an **instrument-use and maintenance/custody project**, not as a survey of microscopical societies.

## 3. Pre-PMS anchors

### 1865 — Oldham circulating cabinet
John Butterworth describes an operating `Circulating Cabinet of Objects` among microscopists around Oldham with:
- member trays;
- six objects per tray;
- elastic retention;
- purpose-made box;
- one-week custody;
- replacement of member contents on return;
- travelling memorandum book;
- stamp-case and carriage finance.

### March 1866 — Manchester recruitment
*Science-Gossip* directs Manchester-area microscopists wishing to join a `Circulating Cabinet` to `Mr T. Armstrong, Deansgate, Manchester`.

### 1869 — Manchester Circulating Microscopic Cabinet Society
Contemporary reports name the society and record T. Armstrong exhibiting prepared slides.

Current identity status:
Thomas Armstrong the Deansgate optical-instrument maker is a high-confidence candidate for this actor, but direct identity closure remains open.

## 4. Later-society secondary leads

The Manchester Microscopical & Natural History Society's own historical reconstruction states:
- Manchester Microscopical Society was organized 1879/80;
- nineteenth-century Manchester contained several partly overlapping microscopical bodies;
- its 1890s account describes a `Mounting Section`;
- members frequently brought materials to share so that others could take them away and examine them at home;
- Annual Reports / Transactions survive and are the stated source base for much of that reconstruction.

These are **secondary leads**, not primary evidence for the current article.

Historical use if primary archive confirms them:
- temporary custody was not necessarily confined to a formally postal club;
- society meetings could feed take-home examination and preparation work;
- mounting/formatting practice may have been organizationally specialized.

Guard:
The secondary wording says `materials`, not necessarily microscope slides. Do not silently recode all take-home material as slides.

## 5. Exact archive questions

Request only material capable of answering one of these:

### A. predecessor history
Does the archive contain:
- historical notes;
- anniversary histories;
- early reports;
- predecessor-society references;
- member reminiscences
that name the Manchester Circulating Microscopic Cabinet Society, Leeuwenhoek Club, Manchester Scientific Students Microscopical Club, or T./Thomas Armstrong?

### B. material custody
Do nineteenth-century reports/minutes record:
- slides or specimens borrowed/taken home;
- circulation between meetings;
- named custodians/curators;
- slide cabinet/library rules;
- returns or missing material?

### C. Mounting Section
If a Mounting Section existed in the 1890s:
- when was it formed?
- what did it do?
- did it remount, prepare, label, repair or standardize Society/member material?
- did it operate on collection slides or only demonstrate craft to members?

### D. Armstrong
Search bounded actor strings:
- `Armstrong`;
- `T. Armstrong`;
- `Thomas Armstrong`;
- `Deansgate`;
- `Armstrong & Brother`.

Identity closes only if the society/cabinet actor is connected to full name, address, occupation, firm or another independently identified relation.

## 6. Stop rule

Manchester receives at most one focused research day in the grant itinerary unless the catalogue/archivist identifies a direct predecessor file.

Stop if:
- archive effectively begins with the 1879/80 Society and supplies no relevant predecessor chain;
- nineteenth-century records contain meeting lectures but no material-custody practice;
- Armstrong never appears in a document capable of closing identity.

Negative result does not damage the project because Bolton and Quekett already support ordinary-society lending/custody.

## 7. Upgrade condition

Manchester becomes a full article case only if one of these closes:
1. 1860s circulating-cabinet actor/body → later archive history;
2. T. Armstrong → Thomas Armstrong instrument maker;
3. later Society take-home material / Mounting Section → direct nineteenth-century primary record.

Otherwise Manchester remains a strong prehistory/context node rather than an institutional continuity claim.

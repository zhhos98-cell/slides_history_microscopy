# Manchester + Bolton date hooks v1

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: EVENT-LED ARCHIVAL WORKSHEET

## 1. Why these two places belong in one research trip

Manchester and Bolton should no longer be treated as two generic `provincial society` examples.

They now form a linked north-west research problem:

- pre-PMS circulating-cabinet practice is documented around Oldham/Manchester from 1865–69;
- the 1866 Manchester contact is `T. Armstrong, Deansgate`, strongly but not yet directly identified with optical-instrument maker Thomas Armstrong;
- the 1869 Manchester Circulating Microscopic Cabinet Society includes T. Armstrong exhibiting slides;
- Bolton founder John T. Redmayne later had a documented relationship with Thomas Armstrong of Armstrong & Brother Microscopes;
- after Redmayne's death, his private slide collection was placed in the hands of Bolton Society officers for selection into a Society cabinet that **lent slides to members**.

This makes the combined trip a test of how private preparations, instrument makers, circulating cabinets and society collections were joined by regional actors.

## 3. Manchester Hook B — 1869 Manchester Circulating Microscopic Cabinet Society

Primary report routes:
- *Monthly Microscopical Journal* (1869), pp. 62 and 174;
- *Quarterly Journal of Microscopical Science* 9 (1869), p. 216.

Controlled features:
- named organisation: Manchester Circulating Microscopic Cabinet Society;
- president Mr Horne;
- T. Armstrong exhibits slides of a salt of aniline;
- Horne exhibits santonine slides;
- J. C. Hope reads on fern structure;
- members show slides prepared since the preceding meeting on a previously specified topic.

Archive tests:
1. Is this Society named in later Manchester Microscopical Society historical records?
2. Are any rules, cabinet lists, meeting books or membership lists copied/preserved?
3. Is T. Armstrong expanded to an occupation/address?
4. Did `circulating cabinet` mean movement between members, or only a collective cabinet discussed at meetings?
5. Were preparations returned, replaced or retained after themed meetings?

## 4. Manchester Hook C — 18 December 1879 / 14 February 1880: foundation of the later Manchester Microscopical Society

Current society history records:
- setup meeting, 18 Dec. 1879, 27 attendees;
- first full meeting, 14 Feb. 1880, at the Mechanics' Institute;
- society explicitly intended to enlarge microscopy participation beyond a small private club;
- early presidents/officers include John Tatham, John Boyd, Thomas Brittain, W. Blackburn and G. E. Davies.

Evidence status:
This chronology is from the present Society's historical compilation, not yet from the deposited archival originals.

Archive test:
Use the earliest surviving minutes/reports in `GB133-MIC` to verify the foundation chronology and determine what material infrastructure existed from the beginning:
- slide cabinet/library;
- loan rules;
- microscopes/instruments;
- purchase/hire schemes;
- exchanges;
- curators/custodians;
- insurance or damage rules.

## 5. Manchester Hook D — regional actor bridge via Redmayne / Tatham / Armstrong

Known external leads:

- Redmayne advertised exchange of the album for a 1/4-inch objective or first-class microscopical slides;
- one executor of Redmayne's estate, John Francis Walkingame Tatham, later became President of the Manchester Microscopical Society (secondary biographical lead; verify before strong use).

Archive names to cross-search:

High-value result:

## 6. Manchester stop rule

If the deposited archive effectively begins with the 1880 Society and contains no recoverable evidence or institutional memory of the 1860s cabinet culture, do not narrate continuous organisational descent.

Manchester can still serve as:
- an 1880s society/material-practice case;
- a location where makers and microscopists overlapped;
- a regional comparator to Bolton.

But the 1865–69 circulating-cabinet sequence must remain a separate prehistory.

# BOLTON

## 7. Bolton Hook A — 19 November 1880: third annual Conversazione after Redmayne's death

John Thomas Redmayne died 10 September 1880.

Contemporary *Northern Microscopist* reporting of the Bolton Microscopical Society's 19 Nov. 1880 Conversazione describes a table of Redmayne's microscopes and work, including his photomicrographs, pathological preparations and rare diatoms. Society Secretary William Rideout also publicly marked Redmayne's role as founder and the loss caused by his death.

Archive tests around Sep.–Nov. 1880:
- what happened to Redmayne's Society offices/responsibilities after illness/death?
- did the Society discuss his private collection or possible transfer?
- were his microscopes/slides merely exhibited by his estate/widow, or already under Society custody?
- are transport/return/custody arrangements for the Conversazione recorded?

This date should be checked before jumping directly to the 1881 gift.

## 8. Bolton Hook B — 11 March 1881: Society decides to add 48 diatom slides

Primary source:
*The Northern Microscopist* 1 (1881), p. 92.

Controlled wording:
At the Friday 11 March meeting, the Society unanimously agreed to **augment the slides in the Society's Cabinet by the addition of 48 choice slides of Diatoms**.

Why this matters:
This is a recorded institutional decision, not merely a later inventory fact.

Archive tests:
1. Are the 48 slides purchased, donated or selected from an existing collection?
2. Who proposed them and who supplied them?
3. Was money spent?
4. Were cabinet positions or classifications altered?
5. Can the group be recognized in the 1898 catalogue?

Exact repository targets:
- FZ/14 records around March 1881;
- `FZ/14/3/2` (1898 *Catalogue of Slides, Journals, and Books*) for retrospective survival/addressing.

## 9. Bolton Hook C — summer 1881: Redmayne estate → officer selection → cabinet re-arrangement → member lending

Primary source:
*The Northern Microscopist* 1 (1881), p. 167.

This is currently the strongest non-postal society event in the project.

Controlled sequence:
1. Society already possesses a `valuable collection of Slides`;
2. collection is being **re-arranged** because of recent additions;
3. Mrs Redmayne gives **six dozen choice slides** from the late Dr Redmayne's private collection;
4. before selection, she places **the whole collection** in the hands of the Society's President and Secretary;
5. officers select the most suitable slides;
6. **48 choice diatom slides** have also been added;
7. the report explicitly says the Society's slides are **lent to members**, who will use them during winter evenings.

This gives an unusually complete custody transition:

`private collection / widow`
→ `temporary custody of whole collection by President + Secretary`
→ `selection of 72 slides`
→ `Society cabinet re-arrangement`
→ `Society ownership/custody`
→ `lending to members`.

Why this may become a main article case:
It shows a non-postal scientific society turning a deceased founder's private preparations into a managed, lendable collective resource. The relevant historical operation is not merely circulation; it is **selection, reclassification, cabinet maintenance and transfer of custody**.

Archive tests:
- What criteria defined the `most suitable selection`?
- Are exactly 72 Redmayne slides entered into a minute book/catalogue?
- Who supplied the additional 48 diatom slides?
- Was a new/reworked cabinet, tray or classification system purchased?
- Were loans recorded by member, date or subject?
- Who bore responsibility for loss/damage during borrowing?
- Did slides return to a fixed cabinet position?
- Can Redmayne items be identified in the 1898 catalogue?

## 10. Bolton Hook D — 1898 catalogue as endpoint

Exact archival reference:
`FZ/14/3/2` — *Catalogue of Slides, Journals, and Books belonging to the Bolton Microscopical Society*, 1898.

Use:
Do not treat the catalogue as merely a late list. Read it backwards against the 1881 decisions.

Tests:
- Does it identify Redmayne as maker/donor/source?
- Are groups/series/cabinet positions visible?
- Can 48-slide or 72-slide clusters be reconstructed?
- Does catalogue structure preserve the earlier selection/re-arrangement?
- Are loan marks, annotations, losses, replacements or condition notes present?

## 11. Why Bolton now ranks above a generic Bristol visit

Bolton already has public primary evidence for:
- an existing Society slide cabinet;
- a formal decision to add 48 slides;
- a whole private collection entering temporary officer custody;
- deliberate selection of six dozen slides;
- cabinet re-arrangement;
- **lending of Society slides to members**;
- a later surviving 1898 slide/library catalogue.

Bristol currently has only a promising archive chronology without equivalent controlled material-administration events.

Therefore, if the SIS itinerary must be shortened or budget reallocated, **Bolton should be retained before Bristol**.

## 12. Combined Manchester–Bolton research payoff

The regional comparison can now be posed precisely:

### Manchester
Did a culture of circulating cabinets, local instrument makers and coordinated preparation become part of later society administration?

### Bolton
How did a local society convert private slide collections into a selected, rearranged cabinet that was then lent to members?

Together they test two distinct routes from private microscopical practice to collective mobile material infrastructure.

## 14. Strongest current Bolton question

**How did seventy-two slides selected from a dead founder's private collection become part of a Society cabinet whose contents were lent out to individual members—and what records made that transfer durable?**

That is already a viable historical question even if no breakage/repair evidence emerges.

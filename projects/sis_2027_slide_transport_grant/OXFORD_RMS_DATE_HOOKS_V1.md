# Oxford RMS date hooks v1

Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: TARGETED ARCHIVAL WORKSHEET / PUBLIC-EVENT → INTERNAL-RECORD TEST

## 1. Purpose

The four-day Oxford visit should **not** be executed as a linear read through decades of RMS minutes and accounts. Public proceedings already provide dated events in which slides or prepared objects entered the Society. These events can be used as needles into the internal archive.

For each hook, test the same internal chain where possible:

`public meeting / donation / exhibition`
→ `ordinary-meeting minute wording`
→ `Council action, if any`
→ `ledger/cash-book entry, if any`
→ `cabinet / catalogue / slide-register entry`
→ `surviving HSM object or later historical catalogue address`.

The point is not to prove that every meeting exhibit became permanent Society property. The distinction between temporary exhibition, donation, custody, accession and later collection survival is itself part of the research problem.

## 2. Hook A — 12 January 1870: T. H. Browne, twelve bone-section slides

Public source:
*Monthly Microscopical Journal*, proceedings of the Royal Microscopical Society, King's College, 12 January 1870.

Controlled event:
Rev. T. H. Browne of High Wycombe presented to the Society **a box containing one dozen slides of carefully prepared sections of bone**. The meeting voted thanks.

Why this is useful:
- an explicitly **boxed** slide donation;
- bounded quantity: 12;
- precise date and donor;
- early enough to test how a slide group entered Society property before the later postal-society moment.

Internal targets:
- Box 31/earlier ordinary-minutes sequence as applicable for Jan. 1870;
- Box 38 Council minutes (Feb. 1863–Jul. 1880);
- Box 41 Ledger 1841–96;
- Box 107 Cash Book 1860–71;
- Box 103 RMS catalogue of microscopes/apparatus + Donations to the Collection;
- later slide catalogues/registers if donor/subject survives.

Questions:
1. Is the box accepted as permanent Society property or merely reported as a gift?
2. Is it assigned to a cabinet/category/address?
3. Does `box` remain part of the stored unit?
4. Are any costs, labels, cabinets or handling decisions attached to accession?
5. Does a later register/catalogue preserve Browne, bone sections, or a twelve-slide set?

Claim ceiling:
The public proceeding proves presentation to the Society, not present-day survival.

## 3. Hook B — early 1873: Alfred U. Allen, three `Distillation from the Vapour of Coke` crystal slides

Public source:
*Monthly Microscopical Journal* 9 (1873), pp. 125/130 vicinity; the donation list records three crystal slides titled `Distillation from the Vapour of Coke`, from A. Allen.

Context:
Alfred Uriah Allen was already exchanging this preparation in 1873 and later became a founder/secretary/editor of the Postal Micro-Cabinet Club / Postal Microscopical Society. Contemporary/reconstructed sources place the RMS donation in early 1873, before the postal club was formally organized later that year.

Why this is unusually useful:
This creates a bounded **pre-PMS actor bridge**:

`individual preparer/exchanger Allen`
→ `slide donation to RMS`
→ later `postal-cabinet organiser`.

Internal targets:
- ordinary minutes surrounding the donation list;
- Box 38 Council minutes;
- Box 41 Ledger;
- Box 103 donations catalogue;
- later slide register/catalogues.

Questions:
1. Did the RMS retain Allen's three slides as cabinet/reference material?
2. Was the gift recorded as three independent objects or a small set?
3. Is any later cabinet/register address attached to Allen or this preparation?
4. If retained, did a preparation already circulating through exchange channels become institutional reference property at RMS?

Date guard:
The exact RMS meeting date for the Allen donation has not yet been independently fixed from the page image. Use `early 1873 / MMJ vol. 9 pp. 125–130` as the locator until the archival/public page is checked. Do not invent a day.

## 7. Hook F — 13 June 1883: Charles Coppock, five commercial/prepared slides donated

Primary JRMS proceeding:
Meeting of 13 June 1883, King's College.

Controlled donation list:
Charles Coppock donated:
- three slides of *Bacillus tuberculosis* in human lung;
- one *Bacillus anthracis* in cow lung;
- one *Eozoon canadense* from Sutherlandshire.

The President immediately discussed the identification of the *Eozoon* specimen and the geological uncertainty attached to it.

Why this is strong:
- five precisely described slides;
- donor is a commercial microscopical preparer/instrument maker recently independent of R. & J. Beck;
- donation is immediately subjected to scientific scrutiny, not merely stored.

Internal targets:
- Box 28 Council minutes (1880–93);
- ordinary-meeting minutes immediately after Box 31 sequence as appropriate;
- Box 41 Ledger;
- cash book 1879–85;
- Box 103 donations catalogue;
- Box 49 slide register / later catalogues.

Questions:
1. Does the scientific doubt over the *Eozoon* alter how it is catalogued or retained?
2. Are commercial-preparer donations entered differently from institutional gifts?
3. Can any Coppock slide be followed into later RMS/HSM custody?
4. Does identification, relabelling or reclassification alter the object's institutional address without altering the glass?

This hook may prove more useful for **documentary maintenance** than physical repair.

## 9. Hook H — March 1884: J. Norman jun., two stickleback slides

Published RMS note, recovered through specialist source transcription:
In March 1884 `Mr. J. Norman, jun.` donated **two slides of sticklebacks** to the Royal Microscopical Society.

Actor context:
John Thomas Norman Jr. came from the Norman commercial slide-making business and described himself as an optician in period records. His later whereabouts are poorly documented; the 1884 RMS gift is one of the last known traces.

Internal targets:
- Box 28 Council minutes;
- Box 41 Ledger;
- cash book 1879–85;
- Box 103 donations catalogue;
- later register/catalogues.

Questions:
- did the two slides enter the Society cabinet?
- are they catalogued under `J. Norman jun.`, `Norman`, subject (`stickleback`) or another classificatory address?
- can present or historical object records separate junior from senior Norman production?

Use:
This is a compact maker → Society custody / attribution case.

## 10. Secondary early hook — 1875 Sorby / Castracane coal-ash objects

Public *Nature* report, April 1875:
H. C. Sorby reports requesting material from Count Castracane after reading about diatoms in English coal ash; he received **two mounted objects** of ash from coal shipped from Liverpool, intended for exhibition to RMS members.

Use:
Potential correspondence → shipment → mounted object → meeting route.

Guard:
Current public report establishes receipt by Sorby and intended/executed exhibition context, not donation to RMS. Treat as a custody comparator unless internal minutes establish Society ownership.

## 11. Search order onsite

### Day 1 morning — calibrate printed vs internal language
Start with three events whose public status differs:
1. 12 Jan 1870 Browne — explicit presented box/12 slides;
2. 9 Jun 1880 Naples/Waters — explicit donated 12 slides;
3. 11 May 1881 Crisp — merely exhibited selection.

Compare internal minute wording. This establishes whether published proceedings routinely omit custody/accession language.

### Day 1 afternoon — commercial/maker donations
4. 13 Jun 1883 Coppock five slides;
5. Mar 1884 Norman Jr two slides;
6. early 1873 Allen three coke-vapour crystal slides if exact date is closed before visit.

### Day 2 — Council decisions
Use the same events as entry dates into Box 38 / Box 28. Search a narrow window around each event before broadening.

### Day 3 — accounts and registers
Only chase events for which minutes/council records imply permanent custody, expenditure, reclassification, cabinet work or other intervention.

### Day 4 — object/register crosswalk
Take the **best two or three** internally documented chains and test them against Box 49, Box 103, HSM manuscript slide catalogues and the surviving/digital collection. Do not attempt a general RMS-cabinet inventory.

## 12. What would change the argument

### High-value find
A dated internal record shows a known donated/borrowed preparation being remounted, relabelled, replaced, repaired, assigned to a cabinet/register address, or returned after temporary custody.

### Medium-value find
A public `exhibition` can be shown internally to involve loan/return/custody rules, or a `donation` can be followed into a later register/object address.

### Negative but useful find
Public meeting records and internal minutes treat slides as transient exhibits with no maintenance trail. This would weaken the ordinary-society-maintenance hypothesis and push the article toward the pre-PMS circulating-cabinet / postal-specialisation fallback.

## 13. Current strongest Oxford question

Not:
`Did the RMS have slides?`

Not:
`Were slides transported?`

But:
**What happened institutionally between a slide appearing at an RMS meeting and that preparation becoming—or failing to become—a durable, retrievable part of the Society's working collection?**

That question can be answered even if no spectacular broken-slide repair is found.

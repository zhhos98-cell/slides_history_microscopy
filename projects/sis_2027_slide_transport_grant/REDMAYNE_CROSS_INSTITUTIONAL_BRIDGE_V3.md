## 1. Why Redmayne matters

John Thomas Redmayne connects several otherwise separate project cases without requiring a claim that the institutions formed one unified network. His materials and relations intersect Bolton Microscopical Society, the Royal Microscopical Society, Quekett Microscopical Club, and Thomas Armstrong / the Manchester optical-instrument world.

The historical value is not biography for its own sake. Redmayne's work entered different custody regimes: private exchange, society library/reference use, and posthumous selection into a lendable slide cabinet.

## 2. Bolton — private slide collection becomes society property

Controlled sequence:
- Redmayne helped found Bolton Microscopical Society in 1877;
- he died 10 September 1880;
- the Society's November 1880 Conversazione displayed his microscopes, photomicrographs, pathological preparations and diatoms;
- in 1881 Mrs Redmayne placed the whole slide collection in the hands of the Society President and Secretary for selection;
- six dozen slides were selected;
- forty-eight diatom slides were also added;
- the cabinet was being re-arranged and Society slides were lent to members.

Archive route:
- `FZ/14/1/1–2` minutes;
- `FZ/14/2/1` accounts/membership;
- `FZ/14/3/1` lending register, May 1882–Jul 1887;
- `FZ/14/3/2` 1898 slide/library catalogue.

Primary test:
Can Redmayne-origin slides be identified in the lending register and then in the 1898 catalogue?

If yes, the chain is unusually clean:
`private maker/collector → widow/officer temporary custody → institutional selection → re-arranged cabinet → member loan → later catalogue`.

## 3. RMS historical donation — direct primary control

The full OCR text of *The Monthly Microscopical Journal*, vol. 17 (1877), directly exposes printed p. 222.

The page heading reads:
`Donations to the Library and Cabinet since February 7, 1877:`

The donation list includes:
`Micro-photographs from the Diatomaceae. By [J.] Redmayne` with source/donor recorded as `Author`.

Source:
Internet Archive full-text derivative of identifier `monthlymicroscop17roya`, printed p. 222, OCR lines corresponding to the RMS donation list.

This directly closes the historical institutional event:
**In 1877 the Royal Microscopical Society recorded Redmayne's *Micro-photographs from the Diatomaceae* among donations to its Library and Cabinet, supplied by the author.**

The OCR renders the initial imperfectly, but the title and surname are unambiguous. Do not overquote the corrupted initial.

## 8. Thomas Armstrong / Manchester

A surviving copy catalogued by Christie's is inscribed by Redmayne to Thomas Armstrong of Armstrong & Brother Microscopes, Manchester. It also contains an additional microphotograph signed by Armstrong and J. G. Johnson.

Redmayne advertised the album as exchangeable for a quarter-inch objective or first-class microscopical slides.

Secure claim:

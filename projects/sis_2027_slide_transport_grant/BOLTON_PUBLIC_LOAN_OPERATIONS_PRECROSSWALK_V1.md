## 1. What contemporary printed sources now securely show

### 19 November 1880 — annual Conversazione and officer identities

A contemporary *Northern Microscopist* report identifies:

- **C. L. Jackson, F.R.M.S.** as President of the Bolton Microscopical Society;
- **William Rideout** as Honorary Secretary;
- the Society year as beginning on **1 October**;
- Redmayne's microscopes, photomicrographs, pathological preparations and rare diatoms displayed together after his death;
- Redmayne as the Society's late Treasurer and one of its founders.

Rideout's annual report states that the Society's active lecture/work season runs from October to May, followed by a summer recess and renewed work in September.

Historical significance:
The summer 1881 re-arrangement of the slide collection happened within the same institutional year that began 1 October 1880.

## 2. Summer 1881 — selection and lending

The contemporary summer notice says:

- the Society's slide collection was being re-arranged during the summer recess;
- Mrs Redmayne placed the whole private collection in the hands of the **President and Secretary** so that they could select the most suitable slides;
- six dozen Redmayne slides were selected;
- forty-eight choice diatom slides had also been added;
- Society slides were lent to members for winter study.

## 3. Probable selector identities — not yet direct-closed

Because the November 1880 report names **C. L. Jackson** as President and **William Rideout** as Hon. Secretary, and because the Society year is explicitly said to start on 1 October, the best current hypothesis is:

`President + Secretary selecting Redmayne collection in summer 1881`
≈ `C. L. Jackson + William Rideout`.

Evidence grade: **HIGH-PROBABILITY INSTITUTIONAL IDENTITY, NOT YET DIRECT-CLOSED.**

Do not state as fact until `FZ/14/1/2` confirms the 1880–81/1881 officer slate or names the selectors in the Redmayne decision.

## 4. Why this matters

If confirmed, the Redmayne transfer becomes more than an anonymous institutional action.

It becomes:

`Mrs Redmayne / private estate`
→ `Jackson + Rideout as temporary custodians/selectors`
→ `72 selected preparations`
→ `Society cabinet re-arrangement`
→ `member lending from May 1882 onward`.

This creates named responsibility at the moment private property was converted into a collective lending collection.

William Rideout is particularly useful as an archival search name because he is already the Society's named Hon. Secretary and therefore a plausible recorder/administrator of the collection transition.

## 7. Methodological payoff

The printed record and archive now divide neatly:

### Public sources establish
- collection transformation happened;
- named offices had custody/selection responsibility;
- slides were lent to members.

### Archive must establish
- who actually administered lending;
- how objects were addressed;
- how temporary custody ended;
- whether non-return/damage/intervention entered institutional records.

This division is useful in the SIS application because it demonstrates preliminary research without pretending the answer has already been digitised.

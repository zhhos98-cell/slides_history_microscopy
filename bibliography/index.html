This remains a bounded working bibliography rather than a claim to exhaustiveness. The second pass contains 39 research publications and 21 selected nineteenth-century printed sources. It now records publication language explicitly and retains original-language titles instead of treating English-language discovery systems as the field itself.

The multilingual pass promotes directly relevant Dutch, German, French, Spanish, and Italian material alongside English scholarship. It also screened Swedish, Polish, Czech, and Hungarian search routes; weak or merely adjacent results were left out rather than added to manufacture linguistic coverage.

Discovery and cross-checking use web-indexed Google Books and Google Scholar results, JSTOR, HOLLIS, iDiscover, KUBIKAT, national and university catalogues, Biodiversity Heritage Library, Wellcome Collection, society publications, publishers, DOI records, institutional repositories, and citation chaining. A portal name records a search route, not an asserted holding for every title.

Method note

Search across languages; promote by evidence.

The list privileges works that help interpret surviving slides and their documentary lives. Original-language search terms matter because preparation, permanent slides, teaching collections, and museum cataloguing have their own national vocabularies and bibliographic traditions.

Coverage remains asymmetric. A language is represented only when an item can be bibliographically anchored and materially connected to the project. Dealer catalogues, society transactions, periodicals, newspapers, museum registers, correspondence, and archival records remain evidence classes in their own right and are linked from cases or source datasets instead of being flattened into this bibliography.

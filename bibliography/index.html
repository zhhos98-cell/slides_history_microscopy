Microscope slides:
a working bibliography

Literature for following nineteenth-century slides as made objects: preparation and mounting, makers and trade, clubs and exchange, printed microscopy, public display, collections, material analysis, and conservation.

Web pass 01

1848–2025 · 44 verified entries

This is a bounded working bibliography rather than a claim to exhaustiveness. The first pass combines 31 research publications with 13 selected nineteenth-century printed sources. Entries are admitted when a stable bibliographic anchor can be checked against a publisher, DOI record, journal platform, library catalogue, society publication page, or open digital copy.

Discovery and cross-checking used web-indexed Google Books and Google Scholar results, JSTOR, HOLLIS, iDiscover, KUBIKAT, Biodiversity Heritage Library, Wellcome Collection, the Quekett Microscopical Club, publisher pages, DOI records, and citation chaining. Library discovery services expose uneven amounts of metadata to the open web, so a portal name here records a search route, not an asserted holding for every title.

Method note

Useful bibliography first; exhaustive accumulation later.

The list privileges works that help interpret surviving slides and their documentary lives. A general history of microscopy enters when it supplies a necessary institutional, technical, visual, or social frame. A title discovered in a catalogue is not treated as verified until its bibliographic identity is anchored elsewhere.

There are deliberate gaps. Dealer catalogues, society transactions, periodicals, newspapers, museum registers, correspondence, and archival records are evidence classes in their own right and should not be flattened into a secondary bibliography. They are linked from cases and source datasets when they become part of a specific evidentiary chain.

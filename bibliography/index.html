Research bibliography

Microscope slides:
a working bibliography

A deliberately narrow bibliography of physical microscope slides and slide-mounted preparations: making and mounting, makers and trade, circulation and exchange, collections and provenance, storage, restoration, and material analysis.

Scope

ca. 1850–2026

The admission rule is physical-slide first. Every row must put a microscope slide, a slide-mounted preparation, or the material system of slide + mount + coverslip + label at the centre of the work. Relevant subjects include making and mounting, named mounters and commercial supply, dedicated catalogues and subscription series of mounted objects, postal or institutional circulation, surviving slide and serial-section collections, object provenance, storage, deterioration, restoration, and chemical or material analysis.

General histories of microscopes, popular microscopy, societies, visual culture, laboratories, histology, or scientific periodicals are excluded when slides themselves are only incidental. The same rule applies to nineteenth-century manuals and trade catalogues: broad microscopy, histology, and instrument catalogues stay out unless the publication is substantially or specifically about mounting, permanent preparations, or prepared microscopic objects. Digital whole-slide imaging is outside scope.

Multilingual searching follows the same constraint. Original-language titles are retained where possible. Discovery and cross-checking use library catalogues, museum and society publications, publisher and DOI records, repositories, historical book platforms, and citation chaining. Data manifest ↗

Research literature

Slides as objects, collections, evidence and things in motion

Secondary scholarship, conservation literature, and collection studies admitted only when physical microscope slides are a primary object of analysis.

Historical printed sources

Mounting texts and publications built around physical slide series

A highly selective historical layer. Dedicated mounting manuals, permanent-preparation texts, slide-makers' technical papers, catalogues of mounted microscopic objects, and printed series issued with physical preparations belong here; general microscopy manuals and instrument catalogues do not.

Scope test

Could the work still exist if the slide disappeared?

If the answer is essentially yes, the item usually belongs elsewhere. A society history, instrument history, microscopy textbook, or cultural study may be useful to the wider project, but it is not part of this bibliography unless physical slides do the evidentiary work.

This narrower rule makes the bibliography useful as a research instrument. It can be mined directly for slide makers, mounting materials, price lists, subscription series, trade, circulation routes, surviving collections, serial sections, damaged mounts, reattributions, and object-level case studies without passing through a large field bibliography first.

Research bibliography

Microscope slides:
a working bibliography

A deliberately narrow bibliography of physical microscope slides and slide-mounted preparations: making and mounting, makers and trade, circulation and exchange, collections and provenance, storage, restoration, and material analysis.

1870–2026 · 40 verified entries

The admission rule is now physical-slide first. Every row must put a microscope slide, a slide-mounted preparation, or the material system of slide + mount + coverslip + label at the centre of the work. Relevant subjects include making and mounting, named mounters and commercial supply, postal or institutional circulation, surviving slide collections, object provenance, storage, deterioration, restoration, and chemical or material analysis.

General histories of microscopes, popular microscopy, societies, visual culture, laboratories, histology, or scientific periodicals have been removed when slides themselves are only incidental. The same rule applies to nineteenth-century manuals: broad microscopy and histology handbooks are excluded unless the publication is substantially or specifically about mounting permanent preparations. Digital whole-slide imaging is outside scope.

Multilingual searching continues under the same constraint. The current verified set includes English, French, German, Dutch, Italian, and Spanish material. Search routes also cover other European languages, but linguistic coverage is never a reason to admit an adjacent title. Original-language titles are retained. Discovery and cross-checking use web-indexed Google Books and Google Scholar results, JSTOR, HOLLIS, iDiscover, KUBIKAT, national and university catalogues, BHL, Wellcome Collection, museum and society publications, publishers, DOI records, institutional repositories, and citation chaining.

Export & reuse

The bibliography underneath the page.

Download the complete bibliography, or the current filtered view, directly from the same CSV rows that render this page. CSV, TSV and JSON preserve the project schema. CSL JSON, BibTeX and RIS provide portable citation-manager exports; the formatted citation and project note are retained so that no row-level evidence is silently discarded.

For Zotero, individual web-record capture remains the richest route when a publisher or repository exposes structured metadata. The CSL JSON and RIS exports are useful for batch intake; DOI and stable URLs are carried over where present.

Historical printed sources

Mounting manuals, preparation texts and slide catalogues

A highly selective historical layer. Dedicated mounting manuals, permanent-preparation texts, and catalogues of microscope preparations belong here; general microscopy manuals and the project’s large periodical corpus do not.

Scope test

Could the work still exist if the slide disappeared?

If the answer is essentially yes, the item usually belongs elsewhere. A society history, instrument history, microscopy textbook, or cultural study may be useful to the wider project, but it is not part of this bibliography unless physical slides do the evidentiary work.

This narrower rule makes the bibliography useful as a research instrument. It can now be mined directly for slide makers, mounting materials, trade, circulation routes, surviving collections, damaged mounts, reattributions, and object-level case studies without passing through a large field bibliography first.

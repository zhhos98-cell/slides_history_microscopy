## 1. *Hardwicke's Science-Gossip*, vol. 1 (1865): W. Gibson's proposal

Public full-text/scan routes:
- Wikisource volume index: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hardwicke%27s_Science-Gossip/Volume_1/Index
- public scan metadata is also available through Wikimedia/Internet Archive.

The volume index places `Proposal to London Microscopists` at printed p. 116 and `Quekett Microscopical Club` at p. 189. The same index shows that the first volume repeatedly reports the Society of Amateur Botanists (pp. 23, 46, 70), microscopy, microscopical societies, collecting and mounting objects, and exchanges.

On 1 May 1865, p. 116, W. Gibson proposed an association for London's amateur microscopists. He wanted easier communication and mutual assistance, monthly meetings in a central locality, and a low annual charge of five shillings; he explicitly proposed doing this `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists`. The editor appended an invitation for microscopists willing to cooperate.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE for Gibson's published proposal and the explicit Society of Amateur Botanists model. This source establishes the published organizational template; it does not by itself establish every subsequent founding action of the Quekett Microscopical Club.

## 2. *Science-Gossip*, Sept. 1, 1865: the Quekett Club as operating institution

A public Wikisource transcription of printed p. 216 (scan page 232) gives a notice under `NOTICES TO CORRESPONDENTS`:
- `Quekett Microscopical Club` ordinary meetings were to be held on the fourth Friday of every month at 32 Sackville Street, Piccadilly.

The same page is unusually useful for reconstructing the surrounding exchange infrastructure. It gives:
- an offer of Echinus spines `in exchange for other objects`;
- directions to the Society of Amateur Botanists, 192 Piccadilly, for exchange of mosses;
- notices about books on the microscope;
- queries based on submitted insects, galls, mosses, geological specimens and other natural-history objects;
- an editorial instruction that common names should accompany scientific names because the journal did not address scientific readers alone.

This page therefore places the newly operating Quekett Club inside a periodical economy of specimen exchange, identification, practical microscopy, books, vernacular/scientific naming and amateur correspondence. The institutional notice and the exchange notices share a page, but source ownership must remain distinct: the individual offers and editorial replies are not Club rules.

## 3. Society of Amateur Botanists inside the same first volume

The public Wikisource index records Society of Amateur Botanists reports at printed pp. 23, 46 and 70. A transcribed page reports its second annual meeting at 192 Piccadilly, with the President delivering an address/report on the year's proceedings. Correspondence pages direct readers wanting plant exchanges to the Society at the same 192 Piccadilly address.

This is PRIMARY evidence that *Science-Gossip* treated the Society as an active exchange/reporting institution in its first year, while Gibson explicitly named that Society as the organizational model for the proposed microscopists' association.

## 4. Cooke's 1866 editorial programme

A controlled modern article identifies Mordecai Cubitt Cooke's `Science-Gossip`, *Hardwicke's Science-Gossip* 2.1 (1866), p. 1, and reproduces its key editorial language. Cooke defended the journal's deliberately informal title through the image of admission to readers' `firesides` and described its purpose as discussing scientific subjects in ordinary conversational rather than savant language.

Evidence level here: CONTROLLED SECONDARY TRANSCRIPTION until the vol. 2 page itself is opened from a stable public transcription/scan. Do not quote the 1866 editorial in finished prose from the secondary source when the primary scan can be obtained.

The source nevertheless gives an exact primary acquisition target: vol. 2, no. 1 (1866), p. 1, `Science-Gossip`, M. C. Cooke.

## 5. Formation history: what is secure and what remains reconstructed

Secure primary sequence now controlled:
1. Society of Amateur Botanists is publicly active and reported in *Science-Gossip* vol. 1.
2. On 1 May 1865 W. Gibson publicly proposes a low-cost amateur microscopists' association `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists`.
3. By 1 Sept. 1865 *Science-Gossip* publishes the Quekett Microscopical Club's monthly meeting schedule and address.

A 2018 *British Journal for the History of Science* article reconstructs the founding community and notes that a month and a half after Gibson's proposal the QMC held its first meeting; it identifies Cooke, Hardwicke, Ruffle, Hailes, Bywater and others in the founding network. A 2023 *Annals of Science* article further reconstructs Cooke's WMC pupil network, Society of Amateur Botanists, *Science-Gossip* and QMC as a succession of popular-science/amateur institutional forms.

Evidence level for the named founding-network composition and WMC genealogy: CONTROLLED SECONDARY until the corresponding QMC minutes, Cooke memoir, Society proceedings, and WMC Magazine source are individually controlled.

## 6. Formation-date conflict for the Society of Amateur Botanists

Preserve a chronological discrepancy rather than normalize it:
- the 2023 reconstruction dates the former-WMC-pupil meeting that agreed to form the Society to 1862 and cites 1863 proceedings;
- a 1927 *Nature* society report, summarizing J. Ramsbottom's account, says the Society was formed in 1860.

The *Science-Gossip* first-volume report of a `Second Annual Meeting` on 21 December 1864/1865-era pagination is relevant but does not by itself settle which action counts as `formation` (informal group, constitutive meeting, first annual cycle, or formal society). Resolve only from the Society's own proceedings/minutes or contemporary notices with dates.

## 7. *Science-Gossip* New Series, vol. 4 (1897–1898): Lubbock Field Club at WMC

A contemporaneous society notice in the public scan lists:
- `Lubbock Field Club. Working Men's College, Great Ormond Street, Bloomsbury, W.C.`
- excursions on second Sundays;
- meetings on the following Mondays at 8 p.m.;
- named excursions including Theobald's Park / Cheshunt / Wormley and Totteridge / Shenley / Ridge.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE for the Club's public name, WMC address, excursion/meeting rhythm and the listed programme.

Critical source-ownership correction: nearby notices on the same page for `Mounting Slides`, `The Microscope`, and other topics belong to other societies printed in adjacent columns. They are not evidence of Lubbock Field Club microscope sessions.

Sutcliffe's separate statement that the Lubbock Field Club aimed at natural-science observation, excursions, discussion, `microscopic observations` and exhibition of specimens is sourced in her footnote to WMC Archive `LMA/4535/E/02/06/001`, not to this *Science-Gossip* notice and not to WMCJ. Keep these as two independent witnesses until the archive file is read.

## 8. Why this matters for the WMC line

The public sources now permit a narrower, falsifiable question than `did WMC teach science?`:

- Was Cooke's WMC teaching/pupil network one of the concrete social inputs into the Society of Amateur Botanists?
- Did the Society's low-cost meeting, excursion, specimen-exchange and mutual-assistance practices provide the explicit organizational model Gibson invoked in launching the amateur microscopists' association?
- How much of that 1860s associational grammar persisted independently at WMC, where a later Lubbock Field Club again organized field excursions and natural-history sociability?

Only the middle step is already explicit in the 1865 primary text: Gibson names the Society of Amateur Botanists as his model. The WMC-to-Society and Society/QMC-to-later-WMC continuity questions remain exact-source problems.

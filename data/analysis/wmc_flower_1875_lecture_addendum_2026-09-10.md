## 2. Revised Flower sequence

The currently controlled sequence is now:

- 1867: W. H. Flower, Conservator of the Royal College of Surgeons Museum, opens a WMC course of lectures on Human Anatomy;
- 1870: Flower is advertised in the WMC winter scientific programme while new class rooms are publicly shown;
- 1871: Flower, now identified in notices as Hunterian Professor, Royal College of Surgeons, gives WMC lectures on `Fishes`;
- 1873: the 1904 WMC internal retrospective names Professor Flower as president of the newly organized Natural History Society / Field Club;
- 1875: contemporary WMC advertisement again places Flower at Great Ormond Street, lecturing on `Elephants, Ancient and Modern`.

This changes the interpretation of his 1873 presidency. Flower was not simply an outside professional recruited at the moment of club formation; he had been repeatedly present in WMC's science programme for at least six years beforehand and remained present after the club's first phase.

## 3. Operation-level significance

Flower carries a recognizable professional package into WMC:

`anatomy / comparative anatomy + museum authority + zoological lecture + club presidency`.

This does not demonstrate transfer of Royal College of Surgeons specimens, cataloguing rules or museum administration into WMC. It does establish repeated access to a professional museum/anatomy practitioner across several WMC formats: formal course, public lecture and retrospective club office.

The WMC Natural History Society's 1873 emphasis on Museum study and the 1874 programme of Museum work/specimen mounting should therefore be read alongside, but not causally attributed to, this repeated Flower presence.

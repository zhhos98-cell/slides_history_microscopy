## 2. Existing repo evidence already makes transport a historical mechanism

The transport argument does not begin with the new archive enquiries.

### Postal Microscopical Society / logistics system

`docs/UK_US_RETURN_TO_CORE_2026-08-10.md` records the existing corpus result that Postal Microscopical Society circuits linked physical slides to manuscript books, notes, drawings, packing rules, postage, damage liability and valuation. The useful unit there is the `slide-plus-documentation-plus-logistics system`, not an isolated slip of glass.

This is a historical snapshot rather than the live router, but the factual synthesis remains part of the controlled repo history.

### *Science-Gossip* / reproducible circulation procedures

`data/analysis/wmc_science_gossip_periodical_operations_2026-09-10.md` controls John Butterworth's 1865 `Circulating Cabinet of Objects`: trays per member, six objects per tray, elastic bands, raised beads, a purpose-made box, one-week custody, replacement on return, a travelling memorandum-book, rules/comments, stamp-case and carriage finance. The source says `objects`; the repo guard forbids silently rewriting all of them as glass slides.

The same file controls specimen-request and fulfillment sequences in which printed addresses, stamped envelopes, finite stock, backlog and later dispatch structured the physical movement of natural-historical material.

### Quekett Microscopical Club / circulation and information loss

`data/analysis/quekett_club_cabinet_circulation_authority_upgrade_v1.json` is already S-level for nineteenth-century cabinet circulation. By 1879 the Club reported more than 2,000 slides and free circulation. Its President explicitly distinguished the preparer's knowledge of origin, relations and crude state from what a first-time borrower could infer from a finished preparation.

This matters for the transport question because movement expanded access while detaching the mobile object from part of its generative context. The repo already treats the resulting need for names, classification, series and books as a historical problem internal to circulation.

## 4. Grant-scale route

For a £750 Scientific Instrument Society grant, the most defensible route is not a nationwide survey. It is a deliberately small sequence in which each repository tests a different part of the same historical problem.

### Core visit 1 — Oxford HSM / RMS

Primary target: Boxes 31, 38, 28 and 49.

Questions:

- when did slides enter society-level registers or cabinet administration?
- do minutes record loans, exchanges, postal dispatch, boxes, carriage, breakage, replacement or remounting?
- can any historical register/cabinet address be connected to a current HSM `Small Worlds` slide address?

Why Oxford is unusually strong: the repo already has S-level collection survival and modern re-addressing architecture, while the new finding aid supplies the exact institutional records needed to test the nineteenth-century side.

### Core visit 2 — one provincial society, not all of them

Best candidates at present are Manchester or Bolton because access/catalogue structure is already unusually clear.

- **Manchester** is operationally efficient: item-level catalogue, three-working-day notice, free personal research photography.
- **Bolton** is historically attractive because a dedicated 1898 slide/journal/book catalogue already exists inside the Society archive and a handlist has been supplied.

Ealing and Bristol should remain reserve/comparator routes unless catalogue inspection reveals a bounded entry that changes the mechanism. Highbury should remain opportunistic London reconnaissance only.

## 5. What the SIS application can claim now

Safe claim:

> The project investigates microscope slides as transport-dependent scientific instruments: preparations whose use depended not only on mounting and optical examination but on boxes, supports, registers, postal/circulation rules and the documentary information that travelled with—or became detached from—the glass.

Current evidence already establishes circulating cabinets, postage/custody procedures, free society circulation and actor-recognized information loss. The grant would test how those published/corpus-level practices appeared in institutional records and slide registers.

Do **not** yet claim:

- that RMS minutes contain breakage or postal-shipping entries;
- that the 1897 RMS slide register maps directly onto the current HSM cabinet;
- that Bolton/Ealing/Bristol/Manchester records contain the desired transport evidence;
- that every circulating `object` in *Science-Gossip* was a microscope slide;
- that transport alone caused standardization of slide dimensions or mounting practice without a source-controlled sequence.

## 6. Relation to the Blaschka route

Blaschka transport remains a useful comparator outside this slide-core note because packing and shipment are already explicit in the Blaschka archive work. It should not be imported into the microscope-slide evidence layer as if it were a slide case.

For SIS budget design, the slide route currently has the cleaner marginal-use argument: grant money can buy inspection of unexamined, already identified institutional records, whereas substantial parts of the Blaschka shipment/packing corpus have already been obtained remotely and the unresolved Harvard reproduction target may exceed the £750 ceiling.

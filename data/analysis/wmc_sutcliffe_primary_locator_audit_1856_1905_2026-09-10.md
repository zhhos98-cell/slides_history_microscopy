## 1. 1856 College Museum statement — downgrade from apparent specificity

Sutcliffe writes that the College Museum was founded in 1856 through contributions of students and teachers and assembled fossils, minerals, shells and antiquities; she adds that objects and papers on scientific topics were presented and country walks complemented Natural History classes.

The paragraph appears highly specific, but the nearby note 42 points to D. A. Finnegan's general study of nineteenth-century natural-history societies and civic culture rather than to a WMC minute, report, catalogue or museum register.

Therefore the sentence should **not** be treated as though Sutcliffe has supplied a WMC primary locator for:

- exact foundation year 1856;
- student/teacher donation mechanism;
- fossils/minerals/shells/antiquities as a controlled collection census;
- object/paper presentation as a documented WMC Museum intake practice.

Revised status:

`WMC_MUSEUM_FOUNDED_1856 + EARLY_COLLECTION_CATEGORIES = MODERN_SECONDARY_RECONSTRUCTION__PRIMARY_LOCATOR_OPEN`.

This does not mean the Museum itself is doubtful. An independent Science Museum Group biography states that Harry Govier Seeley, a WMC student in the late 1850s, became secretary to the College Museum before going to Cambridge in 1859. Thus:

`WMC_MUSEUM_OFFICE_EXISTS_BY_LATE_1850s = INDEPENDENT_INSTITUTIONAL_BIOGRAPHY_STRONG`.

The exact foundation date and early holdings remain separate questions.

A bounded search of the uploaded BNA corpus for London-WMC museum/collection terms in 1854–60 did not recover a clear contemporaneous notice closing the 1856 foundation claim. This is a negative retrieval result, not an absence test.

## 1. Octavia Hill, 8 January 1860 — Cooke natural-history donation

Source: *Life of Octavia Hill as Told in Her Letters*, chapter 4, public-domain text on Wikisource.
URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Life_of_Octavia_Hill_as_told_in_her_letters/Chapter_4

First-order record:

- Date: 8 January 1860.
- Setting: a gathering at the Working Men's College.
- Actor as named by Hill: `Cooke`; no first name is supplied in the passage.
- Hill describes him as an `earnest young countryman`.
- Action: Cooke had presented a collection of butterflies and moths, `etc.`, to the College.
- Interaction: Hill asked him about names and habits of specimens in the collection.
- Historical consequence established by this witness: by January 1860 a person named Cooke had transferred a natural-history collection into the College and could discuss the specimens' names and habits with another participant.
- Identity ceiling: this witness alone does not distinguish Mordecai Cubitt Cooke from Ebenezer Cooke. Keep the actor as `Cooke [identity unresolved]` until another source supplies the bridge.

This hit materially changes the WMC intake. Natural history is no longer only a requested catalogue category or generic curricular possibility: a contemporary witness now places a Cooke-linked natural-history collection inside a College social gathering.

## 2. *Atlantic Monthly*, July 1861 — external observation of WMC, based on autumn 1859 participation

Source: `The London Working-Men's College`, *The Atlantic Monthly*, vol. 8, no. 45 (July 1861), public-domain full text via Project Gutenberg.
URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11154/pg11154-images.html

First-order records relevant to the Cooke/natural-history line:

- The author states that he became a member of the College in autumn 1859.
- He describes the College office as containing catalogues, circulars, and copies of the *Working-Men's College Magazine*.
- He states that the College published the Magazine monthly and that he read it to learn about the College's operation.
- His autumn-1859 curriculum description includes a geology class and states that there had been classes in botany and chemistry.
- He describes late-evening classes, drawing teaching including Ruskin, a library/club-room, practice classes, preparatory classes, and volunteer instruction.

Evidence function:

This source does not identify Cooke, but it supplies a contemporary institutional environment for the January 1860 Hill witness: natural-history subjects, a circulating College Magazine, a library/club-room, and social/class spaces were simultaneously active within the same College.

## 3. Evidence relation now permitted

- Autumn 1859: an external participant records geology, prior botany and chemistry teaching, College Magazine use, and the Great Ormond Street social/teaching environment.
- 8 January 1860: Hill records a College gathering at which `Cooke` had presented butterflies and moths and discussed their names and habits.

Do not yet infer:

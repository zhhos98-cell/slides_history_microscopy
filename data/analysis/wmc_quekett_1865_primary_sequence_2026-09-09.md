# WMC / Society of Amateur Botanists / Quekett — 1865 primary sequence

Updated: 2026-09-09
Status: PRIMARY_SEQUENCE_SECURE__WMC_ANTECEDENT_PENDING
Scope: bounded source bridge. This file reconstructs only the 1865 Society of Amateur Botanists → Quekett sequence from contemporaneous periodical and archive evidence. It does not infer the unresolved WMC → Society genealogy.

## 1. January 1865 — Cooke publishes the microscope as an accessible pursuit

In `What's Your Hobby?`, *Hardwicke's Science-Gossip* vol. 1, printed pp. 1–2, M. C. Cooke moves through possible self-selected natural-history pursuits and then asks whether the reader can purchase a microscope. He presents a moderately priced instrument as sufficient to open a natural world unavailable to unaided sight.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

Use: Cooke's own public framing of microscopy at the launch of the periodical. Do not infer a WMC origin for this language.

Public transcription: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Hardwicke%27s_Science-Gossip_-_Volume_1.pdf/18

## 2. 1 February 1865 — Society of Amateur Botanists uses microscopes and mounted specimens collectively

A *Science-Gossip* report of the Society meeting on 1 February records Cooke introducing microscopic fungi. Several microscopes and an excellent series of mounted specimens were provided; members spent the evening examining the material under Cooke's direction.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

This establishes before Quekett's proposal:
- a Society meeting organized around a microscopical subject;
- multiple microscopes available in the room;
- mounted specimens used collectively;
- Cooke directing the observation.

Evidence ceiling:
- ownership of the microscopes and mounts is not stated;
- `mounted specimens` must not be silently rewritten as `slides`;
- one documented meeting does not establish that all Society meetings used microscopes.

Public transcription: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Hardwicke%27s_Science-Gossip_-_Volume_1.pdf/86

## 3. 1 May 1865 — Gibson explicitly names the Society as organizational model

W. Gibson's `Proposal to London Microscopists`, *Science-Gossip* vol. 1, printed p. 116, proposes an association for amateur microscopists with easier communication, mutual assistance, monthly meetings in a central locality and an annual charge of about five shillings. Gibson explicitly proposes organizing it `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists` and says the new association should not be hostile to the existing microscopical society.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

Historical relation now safe to state: the Society was not merely later associated with Quekett personnel; Gibson publicly named it as the organizational model before the new Club was formed.

## 4. 14 June 1865 — inaugural Minute Book M1 defines institutional relation

Quekett Minute Book M1 opens with a meeting on 14 June 1865 at 192 Piccadilly. The manuscript page publicly reproduced by P. M. Greaves records Hardwicke, Cooke, Ruffle, Gibson, Ketteringham, Jaques, Highley, Godley, Hailes, Marks and Bywater among those present. Because several handwritten surnames are difficult and Greaves's printed transcription differs slightly, preserve spelling discrepancies rather than silently regularizing them.

Hardwicke moved and Bywater seconded M. C. Cooke as chairman; Bywater became secretary pro tem.

Cooke's preliminary statement recognizes the existing Microscopical Society of London's usefulness and efficiency while describing a general desire among amateur microscopists for an organization more specifically fitted to their requirements. The proposed body was explicitly not to be antagonistic. It would help the older Society by fostering microscopical pursuits and become a `nursery` for the parent Society, of which the proposed body would be the `offspring`.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / ARCHIVE IMAGE SECURE.

Historical consequence: the founding administrative language describes functional differentiation and incubation, not declared secession or institutional hostility. Any finished account that describes Quekett simply as an anti-RMS or anti-professional revolt would contradict the first recorded minutes unless supported by other evidence and qualified against this statement.

Spatial fact: this meeting took place at 192 Piccadilly, an address also used by the Society of Amateur Botanists in contemporary *Science-Gossip* notices. Co-location is secure; institutional identity or WMC genealogy is not.

Public archive guide: https://www.quekett.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/archives-lecture-2011-greaves.pdf

## 5. 7 July 1865 — general meeting converts proposal into a governed Club

*Science-Gossip* reports a general meeting for organization at St Martin's Schools, Charing Cross, on 7 July. The resolutions adopted:
- fixed the name `The Quekett Microscopical Club`;
- set ordinary meetings for the first Friday of each month at 8 p.m.;
- set annual subscription at ten shillings, payable in advance on 1 July;
- specified a President, two Vice-Presidents, a committee of twelve with four as quorum, a Treasurer and a Secretary;
- continued the provisional committee until officers could be elected;
- set the next meeting for the first Friday in August for elections and other business;
- directed prospective members before that meeting to Hardwicke, Treasurer pro tem, at 192 Piccadilly.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE from the contemporary *Science-Gossip* report.

### Proposal-to-institution change

The May proposal suggested roughly five shillings per year. The July organizational meeting adopted ten shillings. The movement from proposal to institution therefore involved a concrete financial thickening as well as formal offices, committee structure, quorum, payment date and election procedure.

Do not rewrite `low-cost association` as `five-shilling club`: five shillings belongs to Gibson's proposal; ten shillings is the adopted July subscription recorded in the contemporary notice.

Public scan route: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6c/Hardwicke%27s_Science-Gossip_-_Volume_1.pdf

## 6. September 1865 — routine public operation

By September *Science-Gossip* announces the Club's ordinary meetings on the fourth Friday of every month at 32 Sackville Street, Piccadilly.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

This creates an immediate follow-up question because the September routine schedule differs from the 7 July resolution specifying the first Friday. That difference may reflect a subsequent organizational decision, venue change or reporting context. Do not normalize the two schedules. Check Minute Book M1 between July and September.

## 7. What this sequence establishes

Primary-secure sequence:

`Jan Cooke hobby/microscope publication → 1 Feb Society collective microscopy + mounted specimens → 1 May Gibson explicitly names Society model → 14 Jun inaugural minute at 192 Piccadilly defines complementary amateur institution → 7 Jul name/subscription/governance adopted → Sep routine Club notice`.

The mechanism visible without relying on later reminiscence is therefore not merely a transfer of names. It includes:
- an existing amateur-botanical meeting practice using microscopes and mounted specimens;
- a published proposal for mutual assistance modeled explicitly on that Society;
- an inaugural administrative formulation distinguishing amateur requirements from the older Microscopical Society while rejecting antagonism;
- conversion of the proposal into fees, officers, committee rules, meeting rhythm and addresses;
- a periodical carrying the proposal, Society reports and operating notices across the transition.

## 8. What remains unresolved

This file does NOT establish:
- that the Society of Amateur Botanists was founded by WMC pupils;
- that it was founded by Trinity School pupils as an unqualified contemporary fact;
- that Quekett grew directly from WMC;
- that the February mounted specimens were glass microscope slides;
- that the 14 June founders shared one social class;
- that the September schedule can be substituted for the July resolution without checking intervening minutes.

## 1. Event identity

The Geologists' Association records an `Excursion to Plumstead and Crossness` on 5 July 1873, published in *Proceedings of the Geologists' Association*, vol. III, p. 265 onward.

Directors:

- Henry Walker, F.G.S.;
- William Whitaker, F.R.S.;
- Frank E. Houghton.

Report: H. Walker.

Public modern transcription/control: Earthwise / British Geological Survey-hosted historical Geologists' Association excursion corpus.

## 3. Fieldwork was an engineered observational procedure

The Geologists' Association report makes the event substantially more specific than `a geological walk`.

After reaching Abbey Wood, the party crossed to the Metropolitan Main Drainage Works at Crossness. Access was granted by J. C. Bazalgette. Under Frank Houghton's direction, the marsh had been opened to a depth of eight feet in advance, exposing the buried land surface.

The section exposed:

- alluvial deposit;
- yellow clay;
- blue silty clay;
- peat bed with tree stools still rooted in place.

The party examined preserved stumps and trunks, identifying oak and alder; an alder catkin was noted as unusually well preserved. Houghton also exhibited furniture made from oak recovered during drainage excavations. The party then visited the Crossness boring, linking the visible local section to the deeper geological succession.

This yields an operation sequence:

`permission / infrastructural access`
→ `section deliberately opened before visit`
→ `stratigraphic exposure`
→ `collective visual inspection`
→ `identification of organic remains`
→ `display of transformed excavated material`
→ `movement from surface section to deep boring`
→ `regional geological interpretation`.

This is a highly organized form of practical field pedagogy. It is materially different from an unstructured ramble.

## 4. Relation to WMC's 1873 Natural History Society and Field Club

The date is striking. WMC's 1904 institutional history retrospectively places formation of its Natural History Society and Field Club in the April–July 1873 programme window and describes the Society as combining Museum study with open-air rambles for practical geology and botany. The Plumstead–Crossness excursion occurred on 5 July 1873, at the end of that same window.

However, no controlled source yet states that the WMC students on the Geologists' Association excursion attended **as members of the new WMC Society**.

Therefore keep separate:

- `GA_EXCURSION = EVENT_ORGANIZER_SECURE`;

This separation is analytically useful. It shows that the same WMC field-science population could be embedded in more than one organizational infrastructure: WMC classes, WMC's own Society/Field Club, and an external metropolitan specialist association.

## 5. Relevance to the reallocation argument

The event reinforces the operation-level approach. `Fieldwork` did not belong exclusively to one club. Its organizational container could change while the practical act of collective observation persisted.

The 1873 WMC field-science ecology can now be represented as at least three partially overlapping forms:

1. internally organized WMC Natural History Society / Field Club Museum study and rambles;
2. WMC geological students participating in Geologists' Association excursions;
3. the older WMC Sunday-walking tradition, which had social and occasionally natural-historical dimensions but should not be collapsed into either scientific body.

Thus institutional history cannot be reconstructed reliably by tracking `walks` alone. Each event requires organizer, participants, observational task and documentary form.

## 6. Methodological significance

The official Proceedings and newspaper accounts perform different evidentiary work:

- Association Proceedings preserve directors, site permissions, sections, observed strata, specimens/materials and interpretive sequence;
- newspapers preserve the otherwise omitted fact that WMC geological students formed part of the party.

Neither source alone gives the complete historical event.

This is a useful source-method example for the broader project: institutional participation can disappear from a professional society's technical report while being visible in contemporaneous public reporting; conversely, newspapers can identify participants while truncating the actual observational procedure.

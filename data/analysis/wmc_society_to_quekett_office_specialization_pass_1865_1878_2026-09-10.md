## 1. Society operating package before Quekett

The already controlled 1864–65 Society of Amateur Botanists evidence establishes a package including excursions, specimen interchange, papers, publication, library, herbarium, governance and at least one collective microscopy session. Gibson's May 1865 *Science-Gossip* proposal explicitly says a London microscopists' association should be organized `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists`.

This controls **explicit copying of an organizational model**, but Gibson does not specify that every Society function was to be reproduced unchanged.

## 2. Quekett formalization redistributed operations into offices

The Quekett's own 1878 historical table, stated to be compiled from Annual Reports, lists differentiated offices and committee functions across 1865–1878. The headings include:

- Treasurer;
- Secretary;
- Secretary for Foreign Correspondence;
- Reporter;
- Librarian;
- Curators;
- Excursion Committee;
- Exchange (of Slides).

The historical value lies less in the mere existence of office-holders than in the taxonomy of work. Functions that the Society of Amateur Botanists had combined within a general excursion/exchange/library/herbarium/museum programme appear at Quekett as separately assigned responsibilities.

## 3. Contemporary Quekett journal closes one office at operation level

An early *Journal of the Quekett Microscopical Club* report for 24 April 1868 directly records Cooke acting as Secretary for Foreign Correspondence. In that capacity he reported and routed material connected with the Portland Society of Natural History, including distribution of forty-two circulars, correspondence, receipt of a publication for the library, and an exchange arrangement in which publications were supplied in return for microscopical slides.

This is unusually useful because it shows the office **doing** the operation rather than merely appearing in a list.

Sequence:

`external society communication`
→ `foreign-correspondence secretary`
→ `circular distribution / correspondence`
→ `library receipt`
→ `publication-for-slide exchange`.

Thus `foreign correspondence` is not a ceremonial title; it is a routing mechanism for texts, objects and institutional relations.

## 4. Slide custody becomes explicitly specialized

Modern reconstruction by Geoffrey Belknap, grounded in the early QMC committee/journal record, identifies Frederick Ruffle with microscope-slide curatorship and William Jacques with the library, while Cooke handled foreign correspondence and Robert Hardwicke combined treasurer/publisher functions.

Where possible, individual office-holder claims should still be cited back to the early QMC journal/committee record rather than Belknap alone. The broader structural claim is already supported by the Club's own compiled table: slide exchange, curatorship, library and correspondence became named organizational functions.

## 5. Analytical consequence: institutionalization as division of epistemic/material labour

A stronger formulation than `Quekett became more formal` is:

> The Quekett institutionalized amateur microscopy by allocating recurrent material and documentary operations to offices. Correspondence, library custody, slide exchange, curatorship, reporting and excursion organization became distinct responsibilities. Formalization therefore meant a division of epistemic and material labour, not merely the adoption of rules and fees.

This has a direct comparative value for WMC. In 1873–74 WMC natural-history activity is described through Museum study, field rambles, specimen mounting, talks and display; at Quekett, closely related operations are redistributed among named offices and committees. The comparison should remain operational, not genealogical.

## 6. Relation to Cooke

Cooke is particularly important because he appears in multiple organizational roles across the sequence:

- WMC botany/microscope teaching;
- Society of Amateur Botanists organization and collective microscopy;
- *Science-Gossip* editorial routing;
- chair at the first recorded Quekett organizational meeting;
- Quekett Secretary for Foreign Correspondence.

The continuity is therefore strongest at the level of **a person repeatedly performing or redesigning routing/teaching/association operations**, not at the level of one institution directly becoming another.

## 7. Current evidence ceiling

Safe:

- Gibson explicitly cited the Society of Amateur Botanists as the organizational model for the proposed microscopists' association.
- Quekett subsequently differentiated correspondence, library, curatorial, excursion and slide-exchange functions into named offices/committees.
- Cooke's 1868 foreign-correspondence activity can be observed directly in the Club journal as circulation of circulars, publications, correspondence and slides.

Hold:

- every Quekett office was copied from an equivalent Society office;
- slide exchange was already a separately administered Society of Amateur Botanists office;
- Quekett directly inherited WMC administrative structures;
- office specialization proves professionalization in a sociological sense without a comparison of recruitment, payment and authority.

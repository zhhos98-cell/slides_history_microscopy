# Working Men's College museum / collection practice — bounded source pass

Updated: 2026-09-09
Status: MATERIAL_INFRASTRUCTURE_HYPOTHESIS__MIXED_EVIDENCE_LEVELS
Scope: WMC museum, collections, specimens and natural-history teaching from the mid-1850s through the Cooke/Grugeon line. The purpose is to test whether later Cooke and field-club evidence entered a pre-existing material system, not to assume that all natural-history objects belonged to one continuous collection.

## 1. College Museum by the late 1850s

A current Science Museum Group institutional biography of Harry Govier Seeley states that he studied English and mathematics at the Working Men's College in the late 1850s and became secretary to the College's museum; in 1859 he entered Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge and soon worked for Adam Sedgwick at the Woodwardian Museum.

Evidence level: CONTROLLED INSTITUTIONAL BIOGRAPHY. This independently establishes a museum office and a named museum secretary in the late 1850s, but the underlying WMC appointment record has not yet been opened.

Marcella Pellegrino Sutcliffe's historical reconstruction dates the College Museum to 1856 and states that students and teachers contributed fossils, minerals, shells and antiquities; she also describes scientific objects/papers being presented and country walks complementing Natural History classes.

Evidence level: CONTROLLED SECONDARY. The exact WMC primary locator behind the 1856 foundation date, collection categories and contribution practice remains to be recovered.

## 2. 8 January 1860 — Cooke natural-history donation

Octavia Hill's letter dated 8 January 1860 describes a gathering at the Working Men's College and an `earnest young countryman of the name of Cooke` who had presented a collection of butterflies and moths, etc., to the College and then explained some of their names and habits to Hill.

Evidence level: CONTEMPORARY PRIMARY / SECURE for a person called Cooke, the donation of a natural-history collection to the College, and Cooke's explanation of specimens. Identity remains unresolved because the letter gives no first name.

Source-ownership limit: the letter says the collection was presented `to the College`; it does not state that it entered the College Museum, a cabinet, a teaching collection or any particular administrative custody. Do not convert the museum context into an accession claim.

## 3. 1861 — Cooke physiological botany and microscope target

Nicholas E. C. Smith's 2023 reconstruction states that Mordecai Cubitt Cooke was employed by WMC in 1861 to teach an evening class of Physiological Botany and emphasized use of the microscope in his teaching plan. Smith supplies an exact primary locator: `Report of General Meeting`, *Working Men's College Magazine* 3 (1861), p. 173.

Evidence level: CONTROLLED SECONDARY WITH EXACT PRIMARY LOCATOR. Await Gale page before primary promotion.

If the page confirms the wording, it will place microscope-mediated botanical teaching inside the same College in which a museum office and a Cooke natural-history donation are independently attested. It will not by itself prove that the donated specimens or museum holdings were used in Cooke's class.

## 4. 1866 — formal physical-science teaching

The College's own 1866 report names `Mr. Grugeon` on the Council as a certificated teacher of Botany under the Science and Art Department of the Privy Council. The report also shows Botany, Physiology, Geology, Chemistry and related physical-science subjects in the College's educational structure; its autumn 1866 programme includes Botany, Natural Philosophy and Zoology.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

This establishes that by 1866 WMC's natural-history/material context was accompanied by formal physical-science instruction and a certified botany teacher. It does not establish continuity from Cooke's 1861 class without actor/document bridges.

## 5. Later museum/collection targets

Smith's reconstruction states that Cooke was invited to become Curator of the WMC Museum in 1875. This remains CONTROLLED SECONDARY until the WMC invitation/minute/report is opened.

Separately, later secondary reconstruction points to a 1873 Natural History Social and Field Club whose botanical walks involved collecting plants and displaying them on return to the College, and to the 1890s Lubbock Field Club whose archival aims include microscopic observations and specimen exhibition. Those lines are controlled in `wmc_grugeon_field_club_continuity_pass_2026-09-09.md` and must not be silently folded into the Museum.

## 6. Current material question

The source problem is now narrower than `did WMC have a museum?`:

- What objects entered the Museum, by whom, and under what catalogue/custody terms?
- Did natural-history classes use museum or donated objects?
- Did Cooke's 1861 physiological-botany teaching use specimens or preparations in addition to the microscope?
- Did Grugeon's later teaching, field collecting and plant display feed the same Museum or another temporary/class collection?
- When Cooke was reportedly invited as Curator in 1875, what collection was he being asked to curate?

No continuity claim should be promoted without an accession, catalogue, minute, report, named office, object list or explicit retrospective connecting the episodes.

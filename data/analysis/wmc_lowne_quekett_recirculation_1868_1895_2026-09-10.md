Scope: bounded person-operation pass for Benjamin Thompson Lowne (1839–1925). This file tests whether the Cooke / Society of Amateur Botanists / Quekett story should be treated as a one-directional succession away from WMC. The evidence instead shows a mature Quekett microscopical actor entering formal WMC science teaching in 1873 and returning repeatedly to WMC for more than two decades. This supports recirculation between institutions without implying a formal institutional merger or a single continuous course.

## 1. Lowne was already producing microscopical morphology inside Quekett before WMC botany in 1873

The early *Journal of the Quekett Microscopical Club* contains B. T. Lowne's work before his 1873 WMC teaching appointment.

### 27 November 1868 — proboscis of the fly

Lowne's paper `On the Proboscis of the Fly` discusses the relation of anatomical parts and explicitly warns that specimens compressed in mounting can misrepresent their natural relations. He recommends examination of specimens mounted without pressure and with parts preserved in their natural relation.

Operation-level significance:

`specimen preparation / mounting condition`
→ `preservation or distortion of anatomical relation`
→ `microscopical interpretation`.

This is not merely microscope use as visual enlargement; the mode of preparation determines the morphology visible to the observer.

### 22 October 1869 — microscope and animal classification

A Quekett meeting records Lowne's paper `On the Aid Derivable from the Microscope in the Classification of Animals`.

At the same meeting the Club also reports material donations including microscope slides sent by M. C. Cooke. The co-presence of classificatory argument and slide circulation belongs to the Club's already differentiated material/documentary machinery; it does not prove that Cooke's donated slides were used in Lowne's paper.

### 1870 — blow-fly monograph

Contemporary review literature identifies Lowne's *The Anatomy and Physiology of the Blow-fly* as based on his own dissections, investigations and microscopical demonstrations. A later Quekett review explicitly states that the work `owes its origin to the Quekett Microscopical Club`.

Evidence consequence:

`LOWNE_QMC_MICROSCOPICAL_MORPHOLOGY_BY_1868_70 = PRIMARY/CONTEMPORARY_SECURE`.

By the time he appears in WMC Botany, Lowne is therefore not a generic external lecturer who happens to know natural history. His recent scientific work has been materially and institutionally formed through microscopical preparation, observation and discussion in Quekett.

## 2. July–October 1873: the strongest reverse-flow node

A contemporary *Nature* report of the Quekett annual meeting held on 25 July 1873 records B. T. Lowne among the Club's Vice-Presidents. The report gives the Club's membership at roughly 570 and records its annual election of officers.

The WMC 1904 jubilee history, reconstructing the administrative transition under R. B. Litchfield and explicitly referring to the October 1873 programme, states that the new/repaired scientific teaching staff included:

- S. D. Darbishire — Physiology;
- **B. T. Lowne, F.R.S.A. — Botany**;
- J. L. Lobley — Geology.

These two controls overlap within the same year:

`25 July 1873: Lowne = Quekett Vice-President`
→ `October 1873: Lowne = WMC Botany teacher`.

`LOWNE_QMC_OFFICE_AND_WMC_FORMAL_BOTANY_1873 = MULTI_SOURCE_STRONG`.

Evidence ceiling: the WMC statement is a 1904 internal history reproducing/using the 1873 programme, not the programme image itself. The Quekett office is controlled contemporaneously through *Nature*.

## 3. Why this changes the genealogy model

Nicholas E. C. Smith's 2023 reconstruction usefully follows a line outward from WMC through Cooke, former pupils, the Society of Amateur Botanists, *Science-Gossip* and Quekett. The Lowne case shows that this should not be visualised only as outward institutional succession.

By 1873 a mature Quekett officer and microscopical morphologist is teaching formal Botany inside WMC. The more accurate directional model is therefore at least bidirectional:

`WMC Cooke/Grugeon relation`
→ `Society of Amateur Botanists operating package`
→ `Quekett institutionalization`

but also:

`Quekett microscopical actor/practice`
→ `WMC formal Botany teaching`.

The second arrow is person-level and practice-level. It does not prove that WMC formally adopted Quekett's curriculum, rules, collections or slides.

A safe article-scale formulation is:

> The relation between WMC and metropolitan amateur microscopy was not simply genealogical or centrifugal. Within less than a decade of Quekett's foundation, one of its Vice-Presidents, B. T. Lowne, was teaching botany at WMC while carrying a recent body of work in microscopical morphology and specimen preparation. Practices and practitioners could therefore return to the institution from which part of the earlier Cooke story had emerged.

## 5. Recirculation is stronger than `network`

The useful historical operation is not merely that Lowne belonged to several scientific bodies. His locations matter because each institution assigns a different function to him:

| Site | Lowne operation / office |
| --- | --- |
| Quekett | microscopical anatomical investigation and discussion |
| Quekett | Vice-President / governance office |
| WMC | formal Botany teaching in 1873 programme |
| WMC | subsequent course/public scientific lecturing |
| WMC | chairing Flower lecture |
| Middlesex Hospital / medical setting | professional anatomy/physiology identity appearing in WMC publicity |

This supplies a concrete recirculation mechanism:

`specialist microscopical society`
→ `actor acquires/expresses technique and office`
→ `actor enters adult-education class/lecture platform`
→ `scientific practice is re-hosted under another institutional form`.

No generic `scientific network` vocabulary is needed unless a specific relation cannot be described more precisely.

## 6. Relation to WMC's practical/technical distinction

Lionel Jacob's 1904 account says WMC's scientific provision had not developed equally with its literary work and distinguishes the College from institutions able to provide technical instruction. Yet the same section describes contemporary Botany as microscope-intensive, with about an hour and a half of a two-and-a-half-hour lesson spent at the microscope and students learning by direct observation; Field Club Sunday walks serve as an auxiliary to Botany; Physiology is described as especially laboratory-based; Geology draws on Museum mineral collections; Electricity includes practical work.

Lowne's 1873 entry into Botany helps supply a historical prehistory for this mixed regime. Practical/microscopical work and `technical instruction` were not synonymous categories in WMC discourse.

Do not infer that Lowne himself established the exact 1904 microscope-heavy class routine; the later teacher/successor identity remains open.

## 7. Henry Newey: possible later Botany successor, not yet identified with Grugeon's prize pupil

The BNA export supplies an exact later name absent from the current repo synthesis:

- *Echo*, 18 Oct. 1898, `BL/0004596/18981018/079`: **Mr Henry Newey** is announced for Botany at Working Men's College, Great Ormond Street.
- *Star*, 1, 15 and 22 Nov. 1898: `H. Newey, Botany` appears repeatedly at WMC on Tuesday evenings.

The repeated weekly listing makes this look like a class/series rather than a single popular lecture.

The 1904 WMC history separately states that three of Grugeon's pupils won the Queen's Prize at South Kensington and that one of them was `his successor at the College to-day`, followed immediately by the description of the then-current microscope-heavy Botany lesson.

# Working Men's College — public periodical and institutional-print source map

Updated: 2026-09-09
Status: PUBLIC-WEB SOURCE MAP; bounded WMC/Cooke intake only.

This map lists publicly discoverable Working Men's College periodicals, institutional prints, and a small set of contemporary external periodical witnesses relevant to the Cooke / natural-history / teaching line. It does not reopen the frozen microscope-slide census.

## A. College periodicals

### 1. *The Working Men's College Magazine*

- Publisher: Macmillan & Co.
- Began: January 1859.
- Contemporary/secondary evidence indicates it ran to early 1861; the exact terminal issue and volume structure should be taken from the Gale title pages when supplied.
- Open Library catalogue describes it as monthly, indexed, and preserved as three online volumes reproduced from the Goldsmiths' Library of Economic Literature, Senate House Library, University of London.
- Current full-scan route identified: Gale reproduction. A stable unrestricted public full scan has not yet been located.
- Public metadata: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL32291568W/The_working_men%27s_college_magazine
- Research priority: HIGHEST for 1859-61 Cooke / natural-history / teaching activity.

### 2. *The Working Men's College Journal*

- Began: February 1890 according to a historical account of the College.
- Publisher: Working Men's College, London.
- WorldCat records the title as a journal/magazine of the College.
- A Google Play / Google Books scan is publicly readable for *The Working Men's College Journal: Conducted by Members of the Working Men's College, London*, vol. 10, issues 167-188 (1907), 510 pages, offered free.
- Public scan entry: https://play.google.com/store/books/details/The_Working_Men_s_College_Journal?id=FC4BAAAAYAAJ
- Exact early-volume coverage still needs mapping.
- Research priority: HIGH for retrospective institutional memory and Ebenezer Cooke.

### 3. Ebenezer Cooke, `The Early Art Classes` (Working Men's College Journal)

- A controlled secondary citation identifies Ebenezer Cooke, `The Early Art Classes (part I)`, *Working Men's College Journal*, 1905-1906, p. 88.
- The same citation quotes Cooke stating that the new drawing methods that began at the College had spread widely through school drawing teaching.
- This is a source target, not yet a controlled primary-text transcription.
- Public secondary locator: https://arthistoriography.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/levi-and-tucker.pdf
- Research priority: HIGH for the Ebenezer Cooke / Ruskin / WMC teaching branch.

## B. College institutional print, openly scanned

### 4. Working Men's College report, 1866

- Caption title: *The Working Men's College, founded 1854, 45 Great Ormond Street, Bloomsbury, W.C. Principal, Rev. F.D. Maurice, M.A. ...*
- Publisher: the College.
- Date: 1866.
- Extent: 8 pages.
- Report dated and signed by F. D. Maurice, October 1866.
- Public-domain scan identified through Internet Archive / Wikimedia Commons.
- IA identifier: `workingmenscolle188work`.
- Open Library record: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25104695M/
- Research priority: MEDIUM-HIGH for curriculum, governance, and institutional self-description.

### 5. *The Working Men's College, 1854-1904: Records of Its History and Its Work for Fifty Years*

- London/New York: Macmillan, 1904.
- 344-page public-domain scan available through Internet Archive / Wikimedia Commons.
- Includes chapters on early art teaching, social life, the transition period, College clubs, education, and the new home.
- Public scan page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Working_men%27s_college,_1854-1904;_records_of_its_history_and_its_work_for_fifty_years_(IA_workingmenscolle00davirich).pdf
- Internet Archive identifier: `workingmenscolle00davirich`.
- Research priority: HIGH as a retrospective internal source and index to names/events, but claims should be checked against contemporary sources where possible.

## C. Contemporary external periodicals and print witnesses

### 6. *The Atlantic Monthly*, July 1861, `The London Working-Men's College`

- Public-domain full text available through Project Gutenberg.
- The author describes joining the College in autumn 1859, paying the membership fee, reading the College Magazine, and observing the curriculum and social arrangements.
- The article states that the 1859 curriculum included geology and that there had been classes in botany and chemistry; it also describes the College office as holding catalogues, circulars, and copies of the *Working-Men's College Magazine*.
- Public full text: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11154/pg11154-images.html
- Research priority: HIGH as a contemporary external witness to the institutional ecology in which a Cooke natural-history donation would have operated.

### 7. *Nature*, 13 October 1870, W. Rossiter, `Working Men's Colleges`

- Letter in *Nature*, vol. 2, p. 475.
- Rossiter identifies himself as a member of the Great Ormond Street College and the younger South London college and comments on their educational spirit.
- DOI/public page: https://doi.org/10.1038/002475a0
- Research priority: MEDIUM; relevant to comparative institutional self-description, not presently a Cooke source.

### 8. *The Leader*, 4 November 1854, coverage of the WMC opening

- Nineteenth-Century Serials Edition preserves public OCR/page access to coverage of Maurice's inaugural lecture and the opening of the College.
- Public record: https://ncse-os.kdl.kcl.ac.uk/periodicals/l/issues/cld_04111854/page/5/
- Research priority: MEDIUM for the founding scene and stated educational objects.

## D. Non-periodical but immediately relevant public primary witness

### 9. Octavia Hill letter, 8 January 1860

- In *Life of Octavia Hill as Told in Her Letters*, Hill describes a Working Men's College gathering and an `earnest young countryman of the name of Cooke` who had presented a collection of butterflies and moths, etc., to the College and explained their names and habits to her.
- Public text: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Life_of_Octavia_Hill_as_told_in_her_letters/Chapter_4
- This is a direct contemporary witness to a Cooke-linked natural-history collection entering the College.
- Identity status: UNRESOLVED. The passage does not give a first name. Do not assign it to Mordecai Cubitt Cooke or Ebenezer Cooke without an independent bridge.
- Research priority: CRITICAL.

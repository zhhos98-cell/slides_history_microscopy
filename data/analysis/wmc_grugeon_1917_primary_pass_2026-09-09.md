# Alfred Grugeon / Working Men's College — 1917 primary-source retrospective pass

Updated: 2026-09-09
Status: RETROSPECTIVE_PRIMARY_TEXT_SECURE__UNDERLYING_GRUGEON_MS_PENDING
Scope: bounded WMC/Cooke/Society/Lubbock source control. This note records what John Ramsbottom's 1917 notice actually states and separates it from later biographical databases and modern reconstruction.

## Source

John Ramsbottom, `Alfred Grugeon (1826-1913)`, *Journal of Botany, British and Foreign* 55 (1917), pp. 193–194.

Public full-text source: Internet Archive item `journalofbotanyb55trim`, full-text OCR corresponding to the public-domain scan.

The notice is signed `J. Ramsbottom` and ends by stating that for most of the facts he relied on Grugeon's unpublished manuscript `Botanical Reminiscences`, a paper read before the Lubbock Field Club in 1896, together with the *Working Men's College Journal* for 1913, both lent by C. E. Britton, who also supplied information.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE for what Ramsbottom published in 1917; RETROSPECTIVE for events of 1860–1893. The underlying 1896 Grugeon manuscript and 1913 WMC Journal remain exact-source targets.

## 1. Grugeon joins WMC because Cooke is teaching botany

Ramsbottom states that Grugeon had attended botany classes in Bunhill Row in 1849, continued buying botanical books, and made progress independently until 1860. He then writes that, hearing that M. C. Cooke was giving botany lessons at the Working Men's College, Grugeon joined the institution.

Ramsbottom further states that Grugeon soon sat the South Kensington botany examination and gained a prize.

Evidence consequence: the earlier controlled statement `Grugeon took botany lessons at WMC` can now be promoted beyond modern biographical-index level. Ramsbottom explicitly links Grugeon's entry into WMC in 1860 to Cooke's botany teaching.

Evidence ceiling: Ramsbottom does not on these lines specify the exact WMC course title or repeat the microscope wording attributed to the 1861 *Working Men's College Magazine* report. Those remain dependent on the incoming Gale page.

## 2. Cooke resigns; Grugeon carries on the WMC class with Cooke's help

Ramsbottom writes that in the following session Cooke resigned; because no other teacher could be obtained, Grugeon was persuaded to carry on the class. He adds that, with Cooke's help, Grugeon soon passed the examinations necessary to become a certificated teacher in botany.

He then says Grugeon continued with the botany classes at the Working Men's College from 1862 to 1888 when he left London; the next sentence says that on his return in 1879 he became Curator of the Museum and later resumed botany teaching. The internal chronology here is awkward (`to 1888` followed by `on his return in 1879`) and must not be silently repaired from the OCR or later summaries. Check the page image and Grugeon's underlying reminiscence before using a continuous-year formula in finished prose.

Evidence consequence: Cooke → Grugeon is no longer merely a shared-institution inference. Ramsbottom explicitly represents Grugeon as taking over the class after Cooke's resignation and obtaining certification with Cooke's assistance.

## 3. Grugeon as original Society of Amateur Botanists member

Ramsbottom states that Grugeon was `one of the original members` of the Society of Amateur Botanists, which Cooke formed `from amongst his old students`.

This materially changes the genealogy control:
- one original Society member is now retrospectively identified as a man who had joined WMC specifically to attend Cooke's botany lessons;
- the source explicitly connects Cooke's old students to the Society;
- it still does **not** state that every original member was a WMC student, nor does it name WMC in the Society-formation sentence itself;
- it gives no Society formation date in this passage.

Ramsbottom then attributes to Grugeon a recollection that some Society members became jealous of Cooke's position and that Cooke later transferred his energies to the new Quekett Club.

Evidence level: RETROSPECTIVE PRIMARY-TEXT WITNESS grounded largely in Grugeon's own 1896 reminiscence. Preserve the jealousy statement as Grugeon's attributed recollection, not as independent motive diagnosis.

## 4. Grugeon and Cooke in the field

Ramsbottom records that Grugeon accompanied Cooke on a week's tour in North Wales in 1865 and says Grugeon was surprised to discover that collecting microfungi for fascicles was intended to cover Cooke's expenses.

Ramsbottom also says Grugeon explained Cooke's interest in fungi through Cooke's purchase at a herbarium sale of an old continental set of microfungi, which Cooke examined but found too deteriorated for identification beyond the host leaves.

Use: this is a concrete Cooke–Grugeon field/specimen relation and a rare retrospective glimpse of specimen collecting as publication/economic practice. Do not generalize it into the financial basis of all Cooke collecting.

## 5. Grugeon's material teaching practice

Ramsbottom states that Grugeon was a wood turner by trade and used his lathe skill to construct `very ingenious models` for his botanical classes. He also notes a special chuck for spiral turning.

Evidence consequence: WMC botany teaching can now be connected, at least retrospectively, to teacher-made physical models rather than treated only as lecture/text instruction.

Exact next question: locate any surviving description, illustration, inventory or museum/classroom record for Grugeon's botanical models.

## 6. Lubbock Field Club and WMC afterlife

Ramsbottom describes Grugeon as intimately connected with WMC and a mainstay of its social life. He states that Grugeon was president of the College's `Lubbock Field Club` from its foundation in 1893 until his death.

He also says Grugeon contributed papers on botany and natural history to the College magazine, and that his teaching at the College lasted over thirty years. A memorial fund of £30 was used to purchase botany books for the library, and the botany classroom was called the `Grugeon Laboratory`.

Evidence consequence:
- the 1893 formation date for the Lubbock Field Club now has a specific published retrospective source;
- Grugeon provides a personnel bridge from Cooke's WMC botany teaching to later WMC botany and the Lubbock Field Club;
- personnel continuity is secure at retrospective level; continuity of specific practices (microscopy, specimens, excursions, museum custody) still requires the intervening WMC records.

## 7. What this source now permits

Safe, with Ramsbottom 1917 explicitly attributed where appropriate:
- Grugeon joined WMC in 1860 after hearing that Cooke was giving botany lessons there.
- After Cooke resigned in the following session, Grugeon carried on the class and obtained botany-teacher certification with Cooke's help.
- Ramsbottom identifies Grugeon as one of the original members of the Society of Amateur Botanists and says Cooke formed it from old students.
- Grugeon later became WMC Museum curator and continued/resumed botany teaching.
- Ramsbottom places Grugeon as president of the Lubbock Field Club from its foundation in 1893.
- Grugeon used self-made physical models in botanical teaching.

Still unsafe as unqualified claims:
- `The Society of Amateur Botanists was founded entirely by WMC students.`
- a single formation date for the Society of Amateur Botanists.
- `Cooke's WMC class directly became the Society.`
- `The Lubbock Field Club directly descended institutionally from the Society of Amateur Botanists or Quekett.`
- a repaired continuous chronology for Grugeon's teaching based only on the awkward 1917 sentence.

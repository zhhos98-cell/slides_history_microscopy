## 1. Early WMC Museum role

The Science Museum Group's institutional biography of Harry Govier Seeley states that in the late 1850s he studied English and mathematics at the Working Men's College and became secretary to the College's Museum. It further records that he worked in the British Museum library, where Samuel Pickworth Woodward encouraged his geological study.

In 1859 Seeley entered Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge and soon worked for Adam Sedgwick at the Woodwardian Museum, where he lectured, catalogued fossils and began field studies of the Cambridge Greensand.

## 4. Relation to the material-processing argument

The 1904 WMC history separately reconstructs early Museum work as involving donated rocks/fossils, expert naming, cabinet finance, arrangement and display. Seeley's early secretaryship places a named future professional geologist inside that institutional milieu.

By 1874 the Natural History Society's programme includes:

- Seeley on method in geology;
- Museum work;
- specimen mounting;
- recurring monthly work.

This does not establish that Seeley supervised the Museum work or mounting session. It does establish a programme in which methodological geology and material Museum operations are deliberately adjacent.

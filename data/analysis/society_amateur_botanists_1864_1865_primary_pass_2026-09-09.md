Scope: bounded control of the Society of Amateur Botanists immediately before and during the formation of the Quekett Microscopical Club. This file records contemporaneous *Science-Gossip* notices and does not resolve the WMC/Trinity social-origin conflict by inference.

## 1. Second Annual Meeting — 21 December 1864, reported 1 January 1865

*Hardwicke's Science-Gossip* vol. 1, p. 23, dated 1 January 1865, reports the Society of Amateur Botanists' `Second Annual Meeting`, held at 192 Piccadilly on Wednesday evening, 21 December.

The President delivered an address incorporating a report of the year's proceedings. The report states:

- Saturday-afternoon excursions had taken place during favourable weather;
- the annual full-day excursion was to Darenth Wood, Kent;
- interchange of specimens had so far been largely confined to members, with a stated hope of extending exchange to other societies with similar objects;
- papers had been read with tolerable regularity;
- some papers had been solicited for publication in the *Journal of Botany*, where two or three had appeared;
- the Library and Herbarium were gradually increasing in extent and usefulness;
- membership totalled forty-seven, fifteen elected during the year;
- the Treasurer reported a balance above £5 despite the `very low rate of annual subscription`;
- officers were unanimously re-elected;
- a Council of four members was constituted to handle business so that meeting evenings could be left for papers and subjects brought forward by members.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE for what *Science-Gossip* reported contemporaneously about the Society's 1864 operations.

## 2. What the `plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists` concretely meant before Gibson's proposal

Gibson's 1 May 1865 `Proposal to London Microscopists` explicitly proposed organizing the new microscopists' association `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists`.

The December 1864 annual report allows that phrase to be unpacked at first-order level. Before Gibson's proposal, the Society already combined:

- recurring field excursions;
- a larger annual excursion;
- member specimen exchange with aspiration to inter-society exchange;
- member papers;
- publication channels into a botanical journal;
- a growing Library;
- a growing Herbarium;
- low annual subscription;
- elected officers;
- a small Council handling business separately from substantive meeting time.

Do not reduce Gibson's `plan` to low fees or monthly sociability alone. The contemporaneous Society report shows a more developed package of excursion, exchange, collection, publication, library/herbarium and governance practices.

Evidence ceiling: Gibson does not enumerate which components he specifically intended to copy. The Society's operating package and Gibson's explicit model statement are separate primary witnesses that can be compared, not silently fused into a claim that every Society practice was transferred to Quekett.

## 3. Chronological implication of `Second Annual Meeting`

The report calls 21 December 1864 the Society's `Second Annual Meeting`.

This proves that an annual-meeting cycle existed by December 1864 and implies a preceding annual meeting, but it does not by itself establish whether the Society was `formed`, `constituted`, or informally active in 1860, 1862, 1863, or another date.

Keep distinct:
- Ramsbottom 1927: Trinity School pupil rambles → constitution as Society in 1860;
- later botanical report / modern indexing: formation or proceedings from 1862;
- contemporaneous 1864 report: `Second Annual Meeting` and a mature operating structure.

The word `Annual` is evidence about meeting sequence, not a warrant to back-calculate a formal foundation date without the first annual meeting record or constitution.

## 4. Society address and Hardwicke infrastructure

The annual meeting was held at 192 Piccadilly. *Science-Gossip* later directs correspondents interested in Society membership or exchange to the same address.

In September 1865 the periodical also gives its own editorial address as 192 Piccadilly, while directing moss-exchange desiderata to the Society there. This spatial overlap places publisher, periodical and Society communication in the same address system.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE for addresses and published routing instructions.

Do not equate co-location with institutional identity. Hardwicke's publishing premises, the Society, *Science-Gossip*, and the early Quekett organizing meetings overlap spatially but remain distinct actors and documents.

## 5. 1865 microscopical practice inside the Society

A later *Science-Gossip* report in the same first volume records the Society meeting of 1 February 1865 on microscopic fungi: several microscopes and an excellent series of mounted specimens were provided, and members spent the evening examining them under Cooke's direction.

Evidence level: PRIMARY / SECURE.

Combined with the December annual report, this shows that the Society's pre-Quekett operating repertoire included both botanical field/specimen/library practices and at least one collective microscopical session with multiple instruments and mounted preparations.

Evidence ceiling: do not rewrite `mounted specimens` as glass slides without source identification; do not infer that microscopy dominated ordinary Society meetings.

## 6. Later 1865 public routing

By September 1865 *Science-Gossip* correspondence notices still direct moss exchanges to the Society of Amateur Botanists at 192 Piccadilly, while a separate notice announces Quekett Microscopical Club ordinary meetings at 32 Sackville Street.

This is useful because it shows both organizations publicly coexisting after Quekett began operating. The Society was not simply renamed Quekett in the periodical's routing layer.

## 7. Historical consequence

The Society → Quekett bridge now has a first-order operational antecedent:

`Dec 1864 Society annual report: excursions + specimen exchange + papers + Journal of Botany publication + library + herbarium + low subscription + council → Feb 1865 collective microscopes/mounted specimens → May 1865 Gibson explicitly proposes microscopists' association on Society plan → June–August Quekett organization → Sept 1865 both Society and Quekett remain separately routable in Science-Gossip.`

This sequence supports transfer/comparison of associational practices without requiring an unbroken institutional genealogy from WMC.

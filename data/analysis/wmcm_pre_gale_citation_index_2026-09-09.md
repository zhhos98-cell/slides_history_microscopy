## Serial control

- Title: *The Working Men's College Magazine*.
- Publisher: Macmillan & Co.; Macmillan's later bibliographical catalogue describes it as published under the direction of the Council of the London Working Men's College.
- Open Library catalogue: monthly; three online volumes; reproduction from the Goldsmiths' Library of Economic Literature, Senate House Library, University of London.

### Terminal-date control: strong 1861 case, unresolved 1862 citation anomaly

The current working inference is that the magazine ran for three volumes from January 1859 through January 1861, but the Gale title pages must decide the matter.

Evidence pointing to January 1861:
- a detailed historical account states that the magazine commenced in January 1859 under John Ludlow and `ended in January, 1861` under Litchfield, adding that no College periodical followed until the *Working Men's College Journal* in February 1890;
- a 1921 bibliography lists *The Working Men's College Magazine* as `3 vols., 1859-1861`;
- modern WMC scholarship cites material explicitly as `WMCM (January, 1861)` and treats that issue as part of the terminal phase;
- Open Library independently describes three volumes, compatible with the 1859-61 run.

Evidence producing the anomaly:
- J. F. C. Harrison's 1954 *A History of the Working Men's College 1854-1954* places an epigraph on its preliminary page and attributes it to `The Working Men's College Magazine, January 1862`;
- a later WMC institutional report repeats the same quotation with the same January 1862 attribution.

This is presently a citation anomaly, not evidence of a controlled fourth volume or confirmed 1862 issue. It may be a Harrison miscitation later copied by the College, but do not label it a typo until the supplied serial itself is checked. The first Gale intake action must record all three volume title pages, terminal issue date, and any bound supplements/indexes before normalizing the range.

- Contemporary external evidence: *The Leader* of 8 October 1859 lists *The Working Men's College Magazine*, no. 9, among current periodicals; an *Atlantic Monthly* observer who joined WMC in autumn 1859 describes the magazine as monthly and says he bought the year's volume and used it to read up on the College's working.

## Exact primary locators already recovered

### No. 1 / January 1859, p. 6

F. D. Maurice, introductory lecture on the studies/study of the Working Men's College. Multiple later scholarly citations identify the piece in no. 1 (January 1859), pp. 1–8; one secondary quotation points specifically to p. 6.

Use: founding educational programme and disciplinary language. Not a Cooke-specific target.

### No. 2 / February 1859, Supplement p. 32

Marcella Pellegrino Sutcliffe cites `Working Men's College Magazine, n. ii (Feb. 1859), Supplement: 32` in her reconstruction of early WMC educational/civic language.

Use: exact early-serial locator; substantive proposition must be identified from the scan before use.

### January 1861, p. 25: iii — exact secondary citation

Sutcliffe gives `WMCM (January, 1861), 25: iii` in the notes to her reconstruction of the College's educational debate. The colon may encode an internal section/subdivision rather than ordinary pagination; verify the printed hierarchy from the scan.

Use: terminal-issue calibration as well as substantive navigation.

### 1861 / vol. 3, p. 173 — `Report of General Meeting`

Nicholas E. C. Smith supplies this exact locator while reconstructing Mordecai Cubitt Cooke's 1861 WMC employment in Physiological Botany and the role of the microscope in Cooke's teaching plan.

Priority: CRITICAL. This is the first substantive page to open when Gale vol. 3 arrives, after title-page/issue-boundary control.

### 1861 pages 7, 8, 28, 60, 126 — Litchfield material, secondary-citation layer

A recent study repeatedly cites `Litchfield (1861)` in the *Working Men's College Magazine* at pp. 7, 8, 28, 60 and 126 for formulations concerning fellowship, liberal knowledge, science/literature apart from trade utility, social intercourse and the College's aims.

These page references are useful navigation points but the article/title and volume/issue ownership must be verified from the scan before citation. Do not treat the modern author's page references as sufficient bibliographic control.

## Date-window targets

### January 1860

Octavia Hill's letter of 8 January 1860 describes a WMC gathering at which a person called Cooke, who had presented butterflies and moths etc. to the College, explained their names and habits. Search the issues immediately before and after 8 January for meeting reports, donor lists, Museum/collection notices and Cooke references.

Identity remains unresolved until the magazine or another source supplies a first-name bridge.

### 1861 Cooke class window

After p. 173, search surrounding issue(s) for appointment notices, schedules, class reports, General Meeting records and later follow-up that may explain the reported forty-lesson plan and Cooke's departure.

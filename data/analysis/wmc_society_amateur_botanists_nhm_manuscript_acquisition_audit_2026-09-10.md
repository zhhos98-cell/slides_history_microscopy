The `(B)` designation belongs to the Botany holdings in the catalogue.

## 2. 1931 acquisition provenance

*Nature*, 31 January 1931, reporting recent British Museum (Natural History) acquisitions, states that the Department of Botany had purchased the Society's manuscript Transactions, two volumes, 1863–64, **from the library of Sir George Watt**.

The same accession notice describes the Society as formed among old students of M. C. Cooke's artisan evening botany classes at Trinity Schools, Lambeth, and names later-known participants including James Britten, William Thiselton-Dyer, Berthold Seemann, Worthington G. Smith and Henry Trimen.

Evidence levels must remain separate:

- purchase of two manuscript volumes in 1931 from Sir George Watt's library = `ACCESSION_CONTEMPORARY_SECURE`;
- the 1860s origin story printed in that 1931 acquisition notice = `MUSEUM_RETROSPECTIVE_GENEALOGY`, useful but seventy years later.

## 3. Ramsbottom 1927 independently supplies the Trinity genealogy and operating package

A *Nature* report of J. Ramsbottom's Linnean Society paper, published 15 January 1927, states that Cooke's Trinity School evening botany pupils went on country rambles; outsiders later joined; in 1860 they constituted themselves as the Society of Amateur Botanists. Ramsbottom's account defines the Society's purposes as:

- excursions;
- interchange of specimens;
- communication of papers;
- library;
- herbarium;
- museum.

It gives alternate-Saturday excursions and alternate-Wednesday meetings, first at the Metropolitan Club, Edgware Road, then above Robert Hardwicke's Piccadilly shop. It further says Gibson's 1865 *Science-Gossip* proposal for an amateur microscopists' association `on the plan of the Society of Amateur Botanists` led to Quekett, many Society members joined it, and the older Society declined.

A 1927 *Journal of Botany* / *Naturalist* report reproduces substantially the same account.

Status: `RAMSBOTTOM_1927_TRINITY_GENEALOGY = RETROSPECTIVE_PRIMARY-PRESENTATION / PUBLIC_REPORT_SECURE`.

## 4. Consequence for the WMC genealogy

The repository also controls Ramsbottom's 1917 notice of Alfred Grugeon, based heavily on Grugeon's own 1896 `Botanical Reminiscences`, which states that Grugeon joined Cooke's WMC botany class, later carried that class on, and was one of the original members of the Society Cooke formed from his old students.

The combined evidence makes a simple `Society founded by WMC pupils` formulation untenable as a source-safe statement. The stronger current model is:

- Cooke taught botany in more than one institutional setting;
- a Trinity School pupil/rambler cohort is repeatedly named in Ramsbottom's later historical reconstruction and in the 1931 museum acquisition notice;
- at least one retrospectively identified original member, Grugeon, came through Cooke's WMC teaching;
- `old students` need not refer to one school or one class cohort;
- the Society may have drawn together overlapping teaching constituencies before or during formal constitution.

Current formulation:

`SAB_CONSTITUTIVE_CORE = TRINITY_HEAVILY_ATTESTED_RETROSPECTIVELY__WMC_GRUGEON_MEMBER_BRIDGE_SECURE_RETROSPECTIVELY__MIXED_CONSTITUENCY_HYPOTHESIS_OPEN`.

Do not force a WMC-versus-Trinity binary until the manuscripts/minutes expose membership and constitutional chronology.

## 5. Ramsbottom 1932 is now a Tier-1 exact secondary/retrospective source

Geoffrey Belknap (2018) gives an exact locator:

John Ramsbottom, `The President's Address: the Society of Amateur Botanists and the Quekett Microscopical Club`, *Journal of the Quekett Microscopical Club*, series 2, 16(98), 20 September 1932, pp. 214–230.

This article is unusually important because:

- Ramsbottom was Keeper of Botany at the British Museum (Natural History);
- the Museum had purchased the Society manuscript volumes in 1931, immediately before this address;
- the address appears after the 1927 Linnean paper and may preserve expanded information derived from the newly acquired manuscript corpus or related records.

The full 1932 article has not yet been recovered in the current public-web session. Quekett confirms that its journal has an unbroken digital run and historic issues are available digitally, but the freely indexed BHL/IA holdings surfaced here do not expose series 2 volume 16 cleanly.

## 6. Quekett as selective institutionalization rather than direct WMC succession

Contemporary and early institutional evidence now supports a multi-stage transformation:

`Cooke teaching constituencies`
→ `Society of Amateur Botanists: excursion + specimen interchange + papers + library/herbarium/museum`
→ `Gibson explicitly proposes London amateur microscopists' association on Society plan`
→ `Quekett formalizes offices, fees, committee and calendar`
→ `specialized custody/communication offices appear`.

A 1878 Quekett historical table compiled from annual reports shows a differentiated office structure including Treasurer, Secretary, Secretary for Foreign Correspondence, Reporter, Librarian, Curators and Exchange (of Slides) functions. Early journal material directly confirms Cooke acting as secretary for foreign correspondence and routing publications/exchanges through that office.

This is a more precise genealogy than `Quekett grew directly from WMC`: specific organizational operations are copied, redistributed and specialized.

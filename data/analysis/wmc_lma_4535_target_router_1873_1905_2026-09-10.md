## 1. Collection-level structure now directly controlled

The AIM25 / London Archives description of `LMA/4535` confirms a large Working Men's College archive including:

- Directors Minutes, 1857–1878;
- Council Minutes, 1854–1997;
- Executive Committee Minutes, 1863–1989;
- College / Corporation ledgers spanning the nineteenth century;
- student lists for the early College;
- building plans and institutional papers;
- a substantial body of records generated by student clubs.

This materially changes acquisition strategy. The current live questions can be routed to named record types rather than phrased as a generic request for `anything on microscopy or natural history`.

## 2. Target A — 1873 Natural History Society / Field Club

Question:

What was formally constituted in 1873, by whom, under what room/custody arrangements, and how did the Society relate to Museum work and earlier/later walking groups?

Priority series:

1. Council Minutes, 1873–74;
2. Executive Committee Minutes, 1873–74;
3. Directors Minutes, 1873–74;
4. student-club records under the natural-history / field-club group;
5. room/programme records if separately catalogued.

Search names/terms:

`Natural History Society`, `Field Club`, `Natural History Social and Field Club`, `Museum`, `museum work`, `room`, `Tuesday`, `Flower`, `Litchfield`, `Grugeon`, `Ebenezer Cooke`, `Fotheringham`, `Fleming`, `Savage`, `Slade`.

Reason: the 1904 internal history already supplies a retrospective formation narrative and a direct room-loss explanation. The archive request should test the administrative mechanism, not merely re-prove society existence.

## 4. Target C — Lubbock Field Club constitution / aims

Sutcliffe gives the exact archive locator `/4535/E/02/06/001` for the Lubbock Field Club aims combining natural-science observation, excursions, meetings, papers/questions, microscopic observations and exhibition of specimens.

This item is high-value because it can close direct institutional wording for:

- microscopy;
- specimen exhibition;
- natural-history observation;

Request the complete item, including cover/title, date, constitution/rules, amendments, officers and any attached programme. Do not rely only on the quoted extract in Sutcliffe.

## 5. Target D — 1905 natural history / `byways` debate

WMCJ 9, no. 152 (Aug.–Sept. 1905), pp. 141–143 is the exact printed locator for the argument that natural history occupied recreative `byways` associated with country walking, contrasted with technical educational `highways`.

Archive request should be used only if the Journal volume itself cannot be recovered digitally. Priority alternatives:

- bound WMCJ vol. IX / no. 152;
- editorial or lecture manuscript if indexed;
- Council/Executive records only if drafting requires the administrative response to the argument.

Do not turn the printed essay into a Council policy without evidence of adoption.

## 6. Target E — room allocation and laboratory transition

The 1873 Society room-loss episode and the 1898–1907 laboratory-building sequence make spatial allocation itself a historical variable.

If the article requires this layer, target:

- room-use schedules / term programmes;
- Great Ormond Street building plans;
- Executive/Council discussion of rooms, laboratory and museum;
- equipment gifts/purchases where directly tied to named rooms.

Question:

How were science practices displaced or stabilized by access to rooms, cabinets, Museum space and later a dedicated laboratory?

Do not ask for the whole architectural archive without a named spatial problem.

## 8. Stop rule

The archive is large and substantially capable of indefinite expansion. Use `LMA/4535` only for questions that can change one of the following:

- institutional identity/constitution;
- office or person succession;
- object/specimen custody;
- room allocation;
- microscopy/specimen wording;
- laboratory/museum organizational relation.

Do not reopen general WMC administrative history merely because the series exist.
